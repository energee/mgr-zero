## Sign in and sign out [#sign-in]

<Screen name="Sign in" />

1. Enter your **Email** and **Password**.
2. Select **Sign in**.
3. MGR opens **Today** for the brewery you're a member of.

Wrong credentials show **Incorrect email or password.** For passwordless entry, enter a valid **Email**, leave **Password** blank, and select **Email me a link**. An empty or invalid email must be corrected before requesting a link. This does not create a new account. **Sign in** still requires both email and password. Signing in, by password or emailed link, opens **Invitations** first when a pending invitation is waiting for you; see [Accept an invitation](#getting-started). Signed in but not on a brewery? MGR shows **No brewery yet** with **Sign out**; ask an admin for access. On a hosted installation, **Create brewery** is also available to signed-in accounts. Entry cards sit near the bottom of a phone screen and center on wider screens. If your session ends while you work, **Session expired** confirms that eligible queued readings remain on this device; select **Sign in to retry** as the original user and brewery. Without JavaScript, that URL shows the sign-in card directly. Open **Me** in the header when you want **Sign out**.

<Screen name="Reset password" />
<Screen name="Set new password" />

**Forgot password?** on the sign-in card opens **Reset password**. Enter your **Email** and select **Send reset link**; the page always says a link is on its way if that email is on an account, so it never confirms whether one is. The link in the email opens **Change password**, which shows the account and asks you to **Choose a password** and **Save password**; if the password is refused (for example, it matches your current one), the page says why and you can try again; a used or timed-out link lands back on Reset password saying so. Signed in already? **Change password** in **Me** opens the same page.

## Accept an invitation and create a brewery [#getting-started]

<Screen name="Accept invite" />

Open the invitation email, enter **Your name** and **Choose a password** (at least eight characters), then select **Join**. The shared entry card shows the verified brewery and your assigned role. Staff open Today; buyers open their customer portal. The link is single use. A used, expired, or wrong-audience link opens **Invite expired**, with **Reset password** and **Back to sign in**; contact the brewery if access is still missing. Validation keeps your entered name for correction without filling the password back in. An existing account receives a pending invitation instead of immediate access; their password stays unchanged.

<Screen name="Account invitations" />

If you already have an MGR sign-in and someone invites that email to staff a brewery, signing in sends you to **Invitations** first; you can also open it yourself at `/invitations`. The page reminds you that joining grants access to the brewery shown and does not change your password. Each pending invitation names the brewery and the role offered; select **Accept invitation** to join. Accepting switches you to that brewery and opens Today with your new role active. Consent expires seven days after the invitation is sent; an expired or revoked invitation needs a new one from the brewery. Replaying an old accepted invitation never restores removed access. With nothing pending, the page says **No pending invitations. Ask the brewery for a new invitation if yours expired.** If acceptance fails, MGR asks you to check you're signed in with the invited email and ask the brewery whether it expired or was revoked.

<Screen name="Create brewery" />

On hosted MGR, a signed-in account can open **Create brewery** from **No brewery yet**. Enter **Brewery name**, a valid **Timezone** such as America/New_York, and an optional **TTB registry number**. The timezone field suggests common US zones and still accepts another valid IANA zone. **Create brewery** creates the brewery and makes you its first admin, then opens Today. Validation errors keep your entries for correction. Dedicated installations hide this entry and refuse brewery creation from the page and the API alike.

<Screen name="First-run checklist" />

An admin sees **Set up** while the brewery has neither a location nor a brand. Adding either one returns Today to its usual work list. Add locations, optionally open **Import CSV**, add a brand, optionally **Invite staff**, and record opening inventory. Importing and inviting staff are optional; they do not hold the checklist open.

## Search and pick records [#search]

<Screen name="Search" />

**Search** in the header finds app pages and records. Before you type, it lists the last five records you opened from it as **Recent**; until you have opened one, it shows a hint to type a page name, record name, or document number. Recent records last until you reload the page, switch brewery, or sign out. Page names such as `invoice` and `catalog` show matching destinations in a separate **Pages** group, using the same role-visible navigation as the sidebar. Each page shows one line saying what you do there; a page listed on **More** shows the same line on its card. Pages remain available while record searches load or fail. Record search covers SKUs, orders, invoices, lots, customers, purchase orders, and batches. Type a name to match on its first letters, or a document number (ORD-0231, INV-1042, PO-0142, B-0012, a lot code) to match it exactly; an exact number sorts first. Results group by kind; use the Up and Down arrow keys to move between matches and press Enter or click to open one. You only ever see records your role may read and open: Brewer searches only batches, Warehouse does not see lots or batches, and Sales does not see purchase orders or batches. The sheet distinguishes loading, failed, offline cached, and valid empty results.

SKU fields in **New order** open their own picker. For a wholesale order with a customer chosen, it lists the active SKUs priced for that customer; otherwise it lists the brewery's SKUs. Typing filters that list by name. It always starts from that full list; it does not move SKUs you picked before to the top or save past searches.

Shared forms use the same text fields and pickers throughout MGR. Number-field minus/plus buttons change the value by one, keeping its decimal part; you can also type decimals where the field allows them. Reading cadence uses whole hours. Optional numbers can stay blank. Quantities with a unit beside them keep that unit visible.

Forms tell you what they need. On New order, New batch, New PO, New transfer, New vessel, the route builder, Repack, and the Replenishment forms, an unavailable save button has a line beside it naming what is still missing, such as *Still needed: a vendor and a quantity above zero on each line.* The line changes as you fill the form and disappears once the button is available. A picker with nothing to choose says so under it and links to the page where that record is made, such as **No vendors yet.** with **Add a vendor**. When your role cannot make that record, the note names the role that can instead of linking. A picker whose choice depends on another field says so too: a chosen location with no bins says **This location has no bins.** A typed value MGR cannot accept is marked at its own field once you leave the field, with what to enter instead underneath, such as *Enter a quantity above zero.* On a line, the message names the line and marks the field that is wrong: the SKU, the quantity, or both.

## Know what your roles can do [#roles]

A member can hold more than one staff role. You get everything each role allows: Sales + Brewer can manage customers and brew batches. Adding Taproom never takes away another role's access. Admin already includes every staff permission, whether held alone or alongside other roles. Staff roles do not grant access to a customer's portal account; portal membership stays separate.

*MGR checks your roles when you submit an action. A button can still be on the page even when your roles cannot finish it. The same limits apply everywhere, including the API: customers, orders, invoices, prices, and compliance filings are readable only by the roles listed for them below.*

| Role | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Admin** | Every staff screen and action that exists. |
| **Sales** | Catalog; customers and pricing; create, submit, confirm, adjust, and cancel orders, plus credits; view inventory; invoices, including recording and voiding invoice payments; replenishment suggestions and creating the replenishment order; brand approvals and state registrations, the brewery’s licenses, the monthly report and its filed snapshot, and lot trace. |
| **Warehouse** | View inventory and record movements; keg pools and keg events on **Kegs**; view orders, pick, and ship; build delivery routes, and depart, confirm each stop, and return a route you are the assigned driver of; view replenishment suggestions (write controls are available only to Admin and Sales); schedule, pick a tank for, start, and close packaging runs, and repack; vendors, materials and contracts; purchase orders (draft, mark sent, receive), material counts, and drafting orders from Planning. Catalog, Customers, the Invoices list, and Team open if you type their address, but they are not in Warehouse's navigation. Locations opens the same way; there Warehouse can add, rename, or remove a location's bins, though only Admin can add a location or edit its name and uses. |
| **Taproom** | Today links the tap board, weekly count, and four-week variance with their last observed facts. Beer shows stock at this brewery’s taproom locations and opens the same taproom tools. More offers your own Units and Chat preferences. You can link or unlink your Slack account, mute personal reminder reasons, and select an existing verified personal delivery destination. Warehouse stock, lot history, Work, Search, business records, shared channels, brewery quiet-hour changes, and direct inventory writes are unavailable. |
| **Brewer** | Today; linking your Slack account from App Home; Recipes and versions; Batches, vessels, the Prep pull list, and recording brew day; Cellar transfers and fermentation readings; packaging runs (schedule, pick a tank, start, and close), but not **Repack**, which is Admin and Warehouse only; vendors, materials, contracts and material counts, and reading Planning (drafting the orders is Admin and Warehouse). See [Navigate MGR](#navigation). |

A permission error is the end of that attempt. Ask an administrator. Hitting submit again will not change your access.
</Callout>

If you open the portal (`/portal`) with a login that is on no customer account, MGR shows **Portal is for customers**: "The portal is for customer accounts. You are signed in as staff." Select **Back to Today** to return to the staff app, or **Sign out** to sign in with a different login. ## Taproom variance [#taproom-variance]

<Screen name="Variance by brand" />

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Variance by brand** from Taproom, Beer, or Taproom Today. Choose an owned taproom location and 4 or 12 weeks. The report compares whole completed count periods whose ending dates fall in that brewery-local window, with exact exclusive starting and inclusive ending times shown. Sold depletion is the actual used in the comparison; destroyed volume is displayed separately. The first count has no starting observation, so its actual depletion is shown without a comparison. A period may begin before the selected window.

Actual is the saved count's depletion. Expected is reconciled POS quantity times its frozen serving size; editing today's pour or package size never changes an existing expectation. A later reconciled sale can update a closed period's expected amount and variance, but cannot change counts or stock. Variance is expected minus actual.

The shared **Variance by brand** screen puts the location and the **4 weeks** / **12 weeks** tabs under its title, then the brand totals, followed by excluded and unattributed volumes and the **Count periods** list with each period's coverage. When no count period ends in the window, the page shows one message, **No completed count periods in this window**, and no empty period list. A window whose only count is a first count shows the same message and lists that first count under **Count periods**. **Recurring brand trend** says **Not available yet**: the report totals each brand over the whole window, not week by week, and MGR does not infer a repeated shortage from those totals. A period without a comparison says why in plain words: a first count has nothing to compare against, or no usable POS sales data covers it. Other periods say **Comparable**, with their coverage.

Without usable POS observations, comparison rows are empty and expected stays blank. A dash means a value is unavailable; read zero alongside coverage and excluded consumption. Mapped lines still contribute when other items need mapping, with coverage and mapping gaps shown. Overlapping taps split each sale equally as an estimate; shares assigned to kegs outside inventory are excluded while count-derived actual stays intact. Sales without a matching open interval remain explicitly unattributed. Guest names do not identify POS sales, so guest yield is unavailable. Square connection and sales sync live under [Point of sale](#settings); they supply expected amounts only and never post stock.

## Taproom physical counts [#taproom-counts]

<Screen name="Weekly count" />

**Weekly count** uses the shared explorer layout: expected consumption first, then the checked print action and one remaining-package stepper per stock bucket, followed by the saved receipt and recent counts. Type a whole quantity or use minus/plus; blank remains uncounted, and zero must be explicit. **Correct count** opens the same correction sheet from an eligible Admin receipt; its reason and quantities lock during an uncertain retry.

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Taproom** under Beer to prepare, record, and read physical counts. Choose a taproom location, then enter remaining whole packaged units for every displayed bin, SKU, and lot identity, including zero quantities. A partially full keg counts as one until it is gone; estimated fill levels never change the inventory ledger.

The page uses today's date in the brewery's timezone and holds the revision and prior-count identity from the prepared count. The expected card links the last saved count by date; a first count shows no link. A first count's saved receipt says there is nothing to compare against. Every displayed bucket needs its own observation. A tracked lot and **untracked** stock are separate buckets; there is no automatic lot allocation. Admin and Warehouse see existing stock labels when one is available. Taproom sees the full worksheet row number on every bucket instead of raw lot identifiers on screen.

**Print current stock labels** uses the same captured count revision for Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom. The printed worksheet includes only current positive Taproom stock, keeps each row's position from the full count worksheet, and names a tracked lot by its current code or says **Untracked**. MGR rechecks the revision immediately before opening the browser's native print flow. If stock or the prior count changed, printing is refused and the page tells you to reload and review; entries and an uncertain count retry remain in place. An empty positive-stock projection says there is nothing current to print. Full lot history remains outside this projection as Admin and Sales compliance data.

A matching count still saves its server date and time, author, and all observations. Its receipt says **matched · no movement** per unchanged bucket. For each shortage, choose **Sold** (the default) or **Destroyed**. Sold includes comps, staff pours, and unexplained shortages and uses the Taproom sale channel. Destroyed posts only the missing units as destruction without channel, tax, or destination-state facts. One choice covers each bucket; allocations and samples are not supported. Matching Sold does nothing; matching Destroyed is refused. The choice freezes during an uncertain retry; the receipt shows the movement reference and frozen depleted BBL. POS setup and sales do not affect this posting. The page lists the newest 50 saved count headers for the selected taproom and can reopen each complete durable receipt, including counts larger than catalog list limits. Stock changes or a draft held past the brewery's local midnight require **Start fresh recount**, which clears entries and loads a new revision and server-owned date. Counts above recorded stock, incomplete counts, and a second count on the same date are refused. A taproom with no recorded stock shows no buckets. **Record count** still saves a count of zero for today. Your next count compares against it.

The page also shows expected consumption while the next count is still a draft. It uses the latest saved count as the starting observation and the server's current time as the end, and identifies that saved count and both timestamps. Expected amounts are grouped by brand because several package SKUs may belong to one beer; your physical entries remain separate by bin, SKU, and lot. As you complete every bucket for a brand, the comparison shows draft actual depletion from the snapshot's package volumes and expected minus actual. A missing bucket keeps draft actual and difference blank. This is a draft estimate; the saved count receipt remains authoritative. When expected is blank, the card says why in plain words: this is the first count, no usable POS sales data covers the period, the data covers only part of it, or some POS items still need mapping. A complete empty observation may show zero. Coverage bounds, partial coverage, unmapped or ignored lines, shares excluded because a tap was outside inventory, and poured volume without an active matching tap remain visible. **Refresh expected** updates only this comparison; it never posts stock, allocates lots, or replaces the count form's stock revision. If that refresh finds a newer saved-count baseline, the comparison becomes unavailable and the form stays locked until **Start fresh recount** loads the new snapshot and clears entries. Late POS reconciliation may change the aligned comparison until the count is saved.

If the page cannot tell whether a submission reached the server, including a timeout or `5xx` response, it freezes every quantity and the request ID. Select **Retry unchanged count** to recover the original result without posting twice. A later refusal cannot unlock an already uncertain request. A definitive first validation error leaves the fields editable for a corrected new attempt.

Admin can select **Correct count** on the latest saved count, once, when one or more sold quantities were counted too low. Enter the corrected whole units, no more than the stock recorded before the count, and a reason. Destroyed shortage lines cannot be corrected. In a mixed count, the form sends only increased sold lines and preserves every original bucket, including destruction, in the effective receipt. It cannot reduce a count, exceed the recorded-before quantity, correct a historical count, or correct an earlier correction. Warehouse and Taproom can read the corrected receipt and its author, time, and reason, but only Admin sees the action.

The original header, observations, and ledger rows remain unchanged. MGR appends an exact compensation for each changed depletion and, when the corrected amount still falls below recorded stock, a smaller replacement depletion using the original package volume, channel, tax treatment, state, bin, SKU, and lot. The corrected count keeps the original observation time, appears as one logical occurrence in history, becomes the next count's baseline, and updates variance without counting unchanged lines twice. Its signed ledger entries belong to the time when Admin made the correction; an earlier filed compliance snapshot stays byte-for-byte unchanged. If the response is uncertain, **Retry unchanged correction** reuses the frozen reason, quantities, payload, and request ID.

## Tap intervals [#tap-intervals]

<Screen name="Tap board" />
<Screen name="Kick keg" />
<Screen name="Swap keg" />

**Tap board** uses the shared vessel-style tiles for owned kegs and a separate guest-keg row, with **Swap** and **Kick** on each. **Tap keg** opens the same entry sheet in the explorer and application. Closing fill is **Empty**, **About 25% left**, or **About 50% left**; opening fill remains a separate coarse observation. A tile says **opened full** for a full keg, and a history row prints the closing fill chip it was closed with. **Swap keg** keeps the outgoing captured SKU available for **Same own SKU**, even if it is no longer in the active SKU chooser. The recent-activity rows show the two most recently opened current intervals; closed intervals remain in recent history. These actions never post finished-goods depletion.

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Tap board** from Beer, Taproom, or Taproom Today. Choose an owned taproom location. **Tap keg** opens a keg with its packaged keg SKU, or with an explicit guest label and positive nominal size in BBL. Own keg size comes from its format and is frozen by the server for that interval. **Tap keg** and **Swap · one record** stay unavailable until the sheet is complete. A line next to the button names what is still needed, such as a packaged keg SKU or a guest keg label. A guest field you leave empty or at zero is marked with the same need. **Kick keg** is always ready, because its reason starts at **Kicked empty**. A new tap starts on the first keg SKU in the picker. The picker lists active keg SKUs whose keg format has a volume. When there are none, the picker says so. A member who may create SKUs (Admin or Sales) gets an **Add a keg SKU** link to Catalog. Anyone else is told that an admin or sales member adds keg SKUs. A guest keg can still be tapped. A guest always shows as not in inventory; an own keg also receives that flag when its taproom has no positive stock. Tapping never changes inventory.

Opening fill is **25%**, **50%**, **60%**, or **Full**. The **Tap number** is optional and can repeat. Two kegs can share a number, and a keg with no number sorts last. Kick closes an interval with a reason and estimated remaining fill: empty, about 25%, or about 50%. Swap closes and replaces it in one action. Omit the incoming keg to reuse the outgoing own SKU; a guest replacement always requires an explicit name and size. An omitted replacement number leaves the new interval unnumbered.

Retry an unchanged action with the same request ID to recover its original result, even after another action. A new action against an already closed interval is refused and reports the safe closer handle, or **staff**, plus the closing time. Open taps includes every interval, with unnumbered entries last; recent history returns the latest 50 closed intervals with safe @handles and times. These fill estimates do not change counts or inventory. No POS yield or remaining-volume bar is supplied.

The board refreshes its open rows and recent history every 30 seconds and when you return to the tab. A sheet with unsaved edits stays open and unchanged. If MGR cannot tell whether a tap, kick, or swap reached the server, its fields and request ID freeze for **Retry unchanged**. A later refusal cannot unlock an uncertain request. An already-closed conflict reloads recent history and shows the safe closer handle and time when available.

Guest labels are never matched to POS items, so a guest row never shows numeric yield. When there is no usable POS numerator, the board shows what is open, when, and by whom without inventing a bar. Tap, kick, and swap create no finished-goods movement; the weekly physical count owns depletion.

## Navigate MGR [#navigation]

Orders and New order reuse data already loaded in this tab. While visible, Orders checks for changes every five seconds; New order checks its customer, location and SKU options every 30 seconds. Checks pause in hidden tabs and resume immediately when you return or reconnect. **Last checked** shows the most recent successful check (the oldest option-list check on New order); **Updating** keeps existing data and your typed draft visible. A connection pause or failed refresh identifies **last-known data** rather than claiming it is current; failed reads offer **Try again**. Slow or interrupted connections can delay updates beyond the normal interval. Saving changes marks cached data for refresh. Switching brewery or signing out discards that cache. The first visit fetches data on demand with a short loading message, not placeholder rows. Ask MGR loads its conversation and history when you first open the drawer, rather than when you enter the site.

**Last checked** uses the active brewery's timezone, not your device's timezone. Planned and requested dates remain calendar dates.

<Screen name="Today" />

The brewery name in the header is the brewery whose records you are looking at. Navigation has up to four sections: **Today**, **Beer**, **Work**, and **More**. On a phone they sit as tabs along the bottom, and tapping the tab opens that group's primary page. On a wider screen they sit in a left rail; the toggle beside the brewery name collapses it to a narrow strip of icons, each naming itself when you point at it, and the page widens to fill the space. A list or setup page's browser tab names it after its menu entry, such as **Orders · MGR** or **Deliveries · MGR**. A page with no entry of its own, like Team, uses the entry it sits under: **Settings · MGR**. A single record's page, such as one order or one batch, reads just **MGR**.

Scroll within the page to reach lower fields and actions. The header, collapsed **Ask MGR** handle, and phone tabs stay outside that scrolling area, so they do not cover packaging controls or other page actions, even in a short window.
Related records appear as simple separated rows. The row's named action or arrow says what it will open or do; a switch or quantity control changes only that row.

- **Beer** opens a landing that counts each area (finished-goods shortages, taproom SKUs below par, kegs on tap, occupied tanks, material shortages, kegs out) and holds **Inventory**, **Taproom**, **Taps**, **Cellar**, **Materials**, **Shortages**, and **Kegs** as allowed by role. **Materials** opens materials on hand, where each material is edited and counted. **Shortages** counts the materials that planned work is short of and opens **Planning**, which lists them.
- **Work** opens everything in motion: Today's rows plus the open purchase orders and delivery routes your role may open, sorted by due date, with chips (**All**, **Orders**, **Transfers**, **Batches**, **Packaging**, **Purchase orders**, **Deliveries**) that filter it. Apart from **All**, each chip uses the same name as its sidebar entry. The chip you pick is remembered on that device. Select **New order** (Admin and Sales) to open the New order form. Work holds **Orders**, **Pick**, **Replenishment**, **Transfers**, **Batches**, **Packaging**, **Purchase orders**, and **Deliveries**.
- **More** opens the list of your setup and office pages, each with one line saying what you do there: **Invoices**, **Catalog**, **Menu**, **Customers**, **Price groups**, **Recipes**, **Vendors**, **Compliance**, **Planning**, **Import**, **Settings**, **Chat**, **Sale channels**, **Locations**, and **Units**. Taproom's More list is **Chat** and **Units**. Settings is Admin's; for every other role, the back link on **Units** returns to **More**.

Taproom does not have Work. Its Beer section opens the live Taproom count, Taps board, and brand variance pages, and an empty Today links to those same jobs. Links your role cannot use are simply not shown. Menu appears under More for Admin and Warehouse, while a tab with nothing you can open does not appear.

Admin opens **Import** from **Settings** or the first-run checklist. See [Import CSV](#import).

Brewer does not have permission to open Taproom, Taps, or Variance by brand. Orders, Transfers, Batches, Packaging, Purchase orders, and Deliveries each carry their sidebar name as the page heading, with the Work tabs beneath it. The tabs use the same names. These tabs open the corresponding lists and include only areas your role may open. On a phone the tabs may not all fit; the strip fades at the edge that hides more, and you can swipe it sideways. The strip opens scrolled so the current page's tab is in view.

On the **Work** landing itself, chips filter the rows in place. **All** restores your role's default kinds. **Nothing in motion** means the selected filter has no matching rows. Work lists orders that need a next step, batches with an overdue fermentation reading, open purchase orders, and open delivery routes. Stock transfers and packaging runs are not listed on Work; their chips say so, and their own pages open from the tabs. A taproom transfer is an order, so it appears under **Orders**.

**Sort a table** by selecting a column heading. The first selection sorts it from lowest or earliest to highest or latest, the second reverses it, and the third returns the table to its usual order. An arrow beside the heading shows the direction. Dates and amounts sort by value, and empty cells always sort last. Order status sorts in lifecycle order, from Draft to Cancelled. Each table remembers its last sort on this browser and device. On a phone a table becomes a list with no headings, so it keeps any sort chosen at a wider width in the same browser. Orders and Invoices sort only the page of records shown, not their whole history. Monthly compliance and price-group tables keep their fixed order and have no sortable headings.

Dates read **Sep 10, 2026** on every screen. Date fields you pick from a calendar show the same format. Day headers read **Thu, Sep 10**. Timestamps read **Sep 10, 2026, 8:10 AM**, without seconds.

**Me** in the header shows the email you signed in with, your brewery and role, and offers **Guide**, **Change password**, and **Sign out**. **Guide** opens this staff guide on a phone or a wider screen. When your account belongs to more than one brewery, Me also lists them with **Switch** beside each one that is not current. Me is a centered dialog at every screen size. Other forms open as a panel from the bottom of the screen on a phone and as a centered dialog on a wider screen. The panel sits above the bottom navigation and Ask MGR drawer, so forms with a pinned footer keep their Save or Record action visible while you scroll the fields. On a phone, page actions such as **New order** or **Save** span the column. On a wider screen, create actions sit on the title row and other actions hug their label instead of stretching across the page. Sign-in and other entry cards stay a narrow column, so their buttons still span that card.

## Catalog: brands, formats, and SKUs [#catalog]

<Screen name="Catalog" />

*A brand is the beer, such as Hazy IPA. A format is the physical shape it ships or pours in, such as a half-barrel keg or a case of 16 oz cans. A SKU is one brand in one packaged format. SKUs hold inventory and appear on wholesale orders. A pour is a serving from the tap, not a SKU: it lives on the beer's price group, and a sale draws from keg stock. Admin and Sales can create brands, formats, and SKUs. Warehouse opens **Menu** under More and reads Catalog at its address without catalog mutation controls. Catalog is not reachable by Brewer.*

**Read the brand list.** Each brand shows its **Brand**, **Style**, **ABV**, **SKUs**, and **COLA**. A staff-only alias appears under the name. **Not set** means no style or ABV is saved; a saved ABV of zero reads **0%**. **SKUs** counts the brand's packaged SKUs plus the pours on its price group, active or not, so it is not a stock or orderable count. Admin and Sales select a brand record to open it. For a role that cannot open the brand page, such as Warehouse alone, the name is plain text. On a wide screen the brands are a table; on a phone each brand lists the same facts under its name.

**Read a brand's COLA.** The **COLA** column shows the COLA approvals saved on the brand; every staff role that reads Catalog sees it. **None** means no COLA is recorded for the brand. One saved COLA shows its TTB ID and its approval date, or *approval date unknown* when none was entered. Two or more show only how many are on file, such as **2 on file**. Formula approvals and state registrations are not counted. Catalog shows only what was saved; MGR does not contact TTB. Admin and Sales open the brand to see each COLA or add one under **Compliance**; other roles see the Catalog cell.

**Find a brand.** Above the brand list, **Search brands or aliases** finds brands whose name or alias contains what you type; press Enter to search. **Sort** orders the list by **Name** (A–Z), **ABV** (highest first; brands with no ABV come last), or **Newest** (most recently added first). **Category** shows one category's brands, or **All categories**; it appears once the brewery has a category. Sort and Category are drop-down lists beside the search on a wide screen and under it on a phone; choosing an option reloads the list at once and keeps the search and the other setting. The search, sort and category stay in the page address, so a link or Back returns to the same list. While a search is on, **Clear search** below the box removes it and keeps the sort and category. When nothing matches, Catalog reads **No brands match**; select **Clear search** or choose **All categories** to see every brand.

**Below the brands.** **Price groups** shows how many price groups the brewery has and opens them for Admin and Sales. **Water profiles** opens the source and target water profiles for Admin and Brewer; Brewer reaches Catalog only alongside another role. A row you cannot open shows no link and no **Open** arrow. With several roles you get every link any one of them opens.

### Create a brand

<Screen name="Brand" />

**Manage categories**, beside the **Category** field, lets Admin and Sales add a category, edit its name with **Save name**, or choose **Delete → Confirm delete**. Renaming immediately updates every brand using that category. Deletion is allowed only when no brands use it; first reassign those brands or save them as **Uncategorized**. Category changes save independently of **Save brand**. A success notification confirms each addition, update, or deletion. **Save brand** also shows **Brand saved** after a successful save. Adding a category selects it for the current brand; save the brand to keep that assignment. Duplicate and blank category names are refused. Existing category values are preserved, including values supplied by imports. New breweries can add their first category here.

1. Open **Catalog** and select **New brand**.
2. Enter **Brand name** (required), **Style** (optional; a new style is added to the brewery's list), and **ABV** (optional percentage from 0 to 99.99; type `6.5` for 6.5% or step it with **−**/**+**; a value outside that range is refused with *ABV must be a percent from 0 to 99.99*). **Description** and **Hops** are optional notes. **Category** uses the brewery’s shared list; choose **Uncategorized** to clear the assignment. **Price group** is the row of the [price grid](#pricing) this beer sits on; leave it **Unpriced** and none of its SKUs can be ordered until you choose one. When the brand has a recipe, a line under the field reads its cost per barrel (from the most recently brewed version, or the newest if none has been brewed, using each ingredient’s last receipt cost) against the groups’ ceilings: *Suggested group 2 · recipe cost $48.10/bbl* with **Use**, which only fills the field; **Save brand** still commits. *Above every ceiling* means no band covers it, and *Recipe cost unknown* counts the ingredients that have never been received and names up to three, so no number is shown.
3. Optionally enter an **Alias**: an informal name staff also use, such as a series number. A beer named "Appaloosa" might carry the alias "Animal #14". Only staff see it: it shows under the name in Catalog and beside it in brand pickers ("Appaloosa · Animal #14"), and search finds the brand's SKUs by it, but buyers, the portal, Square and SKU names only ever show the brand name. A note under the field says so. Clear the field to remove it.
4. Select **Save brand**. Brand names must be unique within the brewery. The page moves to the new brand's own address, where **Sells as** and **Compliance** appear; until the first save they read *Save the brand to add SKUs and compliance.* In **Catalog** the brand lands alphabetically and its **SKUs** column reads **0**. Every switch under **Sells as** starts off.

**New brand** opens the brand's own page rather than a dialog, and so does selecting a brand's name in **Catalog**. Catalog lists the brands, not their packages. The brand page starts with all saved values; changing one field keeps the others. The title shows the saved name and alias, and changes only when you save. **Save brand** keeps you on the page; use the **Catalog** link above the title to go back. On a wide screen the form is on the left (the name and style fields, then **Selling** with **Price group**, then **Sell sheet**, ending in **Save brand**), and **Sells as**, **Pours**, and **Compliance** are on the right; on a phone they follow the form. **Save brand** saves only the form: SKUs, compliance records and categories save on their own. Badges under the title show the brand's state at a glance: **COLA pending** (select it to jump to **Compliance**), the price group or **Unpriced**, the category, and the active SKU count. The brand page is for Admin and Sales; other roles see the catalog without brand links.

**Compliance** lists what this brand holds: its COLA or formula approvals and its state registrations, each with **Edit**. A brand with no COLA shows *COLA · pending* until one is added. **Add approval** takes the approval kind, its identifiers, and an optional **Date approved**. A COLA asks for the **TTB ID** that TTB assigned, such as `25318001000034`, and an optional **Serial number**, your own applicant serial such as `260135`; both are kept exactly as typed, leading zeros included. A formula asks only for its **Formula number**. On a COLA, **Open COLA on TTB’s public registry** opens TTB’s public page for that TTB ID in a new tab; without a TTB ID the sheet says to enter one. The link is built from the TTB ID alone and does not confirm the COLA is approved or that TTB’s site is up. **Find COLA on TTB** looks up this brand’s COLAs on TTB’s public registry, under the TTB registry number in **Settings**; without one, it asks you to add it there. It lists the COLAs whose fanciful or brand name contains the brand’s name, newest first, with TTB ID, serial, class and TTB’s completed date. **Use** fills the **TTB ID**, **Serial number** and **Date approved** from TTB’s completed date. Check the COLA on TTB before **Save approval**; nothing is saved until then. COLAs completed more than 15 years ago are not found. When no COLA matches, the sheet says so; when TTB does not answer, it shows an error instead of an empty list. A COLA does not expire, so no expiry is asked for. The brand lists a COLA by its TTB ID, with its serial when one is on file. The same kind and TTB ID or formula number on the same brand is one record, so saving it twice is refused. **Add registration** takes the two-letter state and an optional number and expiry; one record exists per brand and state, so saving again updates it, and **Edit** keeps the state fixed. The brand is stated on both sheets, never chosen. A new brand must be saved before its compliance can be recorded. The brewery’s own state licenses are not brand facts; they live under [Compliance → Licenses](#compliance).

### Create a format

<Screen name="Formats" />

Each format's name opens it. On desktop, the format table keeps names, basis, and package contents left-aligned, with volume right-aligned for comparison. On a phone, each format lists the same **Basis**, **Volume**, and **From** under its name. Price groups remain a comparison grid with formats across the top; scroll that grid horizontally on a phone. Other wide tables also scroll within their own region, with quantities and amounts right-aligned and descriptive text left-aligned.

<Screen name="Format" />

1. In **Catalog**, scroll below the brand list and pricing links to **Formats**, then select **New format**. The Catalog screen preview shows the same section. Formats are shared across brands; each packaged SKU selects one.
   Selecting **Can** or **Bottle** requires a positive **Size of one container** and a whole **Containers per package** count. The form explains any missing size, count, or required name while creation is disabled.
2. Choose a **Container**. For a standard case, choose **Can**, enter **16 oz** for the size and **24** containers per package.
3. For kegs, choose **Keg size**. Standard sizes calculate beer volume automatically. **Custom size** accepts a volume. For cans and bottles, enter **Size of one container** and **Containers per package**; MGR calculates the total. A single can uses a count of 1.
4. Review **Beer per package** and the suggested name. Expand **Rename · optional** to use your own name. Select **Create format** to save and open its detail page. Packaged format names must be unique within the brewery.

Catalog shows one entry per brand. Open the brand and switch a packaged format on under **Sells as** (see *Choose what a beer sells as* below). Glass sizes are not SKUs: add them on the beer’s [price group](#pricing) with **Add pour**. A pour’s serving size is fixed once a POS mapping, menu, or sale uses it; a rename still saves. Pours are not individually stocked and cannot enter wholesale orders or packaging materials. The taproom menu offers the group’s pours for each beer on that group.

Select the format's name, then **Edit format**. The same sizing fields are prefilled; existing names and custom volumes are preserved. Adjusting a legacy keg's custom volume keeps its existing fleet-size classification. Once a SKU, another package, packaging materials, or a POS mapping, menu, or sale uses a format, its volume, units per case, and serving ounces are fixed and a change is refused with **format is in use**; renaming still saves. Create a different format when another package needs a different definition. Recorded movement volumes, closed packaging yield, and consumed material quantities stay as recorded.

**Packaging materials · optional** is in the format editor. After the format exists, expand it to add trays, labels, or other materials. If none exist, follow **Add a material** and return. Choose distinct materials, enter positive quantities per complete package in each material’s base unit, and select **Used up** or **Return to stock** under **When unpacked**. **Save materials** saves the complete material list separately from **Save format**. Removing every row requires the explicit clear confirmation. Materials are optional and do not block creating formats or preparing imports. Warehouse can view definitions; Admin and Sales can edit them.

For packages built from smaller packages, expand **Build from other packages** and enable calculated volume. The **Package contents** picker appears immediately: choose smaller formats and their quantities, and enter the required format name. **Create format** saves the definition and contents together, then opens the finished format. If creation fails, neither is saved and your entries remain for correction or retry. The parent stores no volume: its total is always derived from the saved child quantities. Only packages with their own volume are eligible; already-composed packages cannot be nested. Existing unfinished formats can be completed through **Edit format → Package contents → Save contents**. A format with a typed volume, a poured format, or a format already used as a child cannot have its components replaced here. Clearing contents leaves no volume, so the format cannot hold stock until contents are added again.

Admins can open a packaged format, select **Edit format → Delete format**, and confirm its removal. **Delete format** sits beside **Save format**, with destructive styling. Deletion removes that format's contents and packaging-material assignments, leaving the smaller formats and materials themselves intact. Formats referenced by SKUs, other packages, prices or history cannot be deleted; the confirmation shows the refusal. Cancel leaves the format unchanged.

### Water profiles

<Screen name="Water profiles" />
<Screen name="Water profile" />

Brewer and Admin use **Water profiles**. Admin opens it from Catalog or Settings. Admin and Brewer also open it from the **Water profiles** row under the Recipes list. The **Water profiles** row on a recipe’s Water sheet opens it in a new tab, so the unsaved version stays put. The back arrow goes to Catalog for Admin and to Recipes for Brewer. **Water profiles** keeps the water you start from and the waters you aim at: a name and six ions in parts per million (ppm, the same as mg/L): calcium, magnesium, sodium, sulfate, chloride and bicarbonate. **Add water profile** and **Edit** open the same sheet, and **Save water profile** saves it. Every ion is required and cannot be negative, and two profiles cannot share a name. A profile is not marked as a source or a target. A recipe version’s Water sheet can pick its source and its target from this list, so one profile can serve either role. Nothing is calculated here: the page records a water report or a target. You enter the salt additions yourself.

### Choose what a beer sells as

<Screen name="SKU" />

A saved brand's page has **Sells as** at the top of its right column. It lists every packaged format with a switch. On means the beer sells in that format. Each row also shows the format's price on each sale channel for the beer's price group, such as *DTC $22.00 · Wholesale $18.00*. A row reads **Unpriced** when no channel has a price for it, and every row does when the brand has no price group.

1. Open the brand from **Catalog**.
2. Under **Sells as**, turn on the switch for a format, such as **Case**.
3. The switch flips at once. The first time, MGR creates the SKU, named *Brand · Format*, with no UPC and no keg pool. After that, the SKU is available in inventory, pricing, and order forms.

A brand has at most one SKU per format. Admin and Sales can flip the switches. All switches are disabled for a moment while one saves. If the save fails, the switch flips back and the error shows under the switches.

**Switching off** makes the SKU inactive. It is never deleted: stock, orders, and history keep it. Switch it back on to sell it again. That reuses the same SKU with its UPC, keg pool, and batch share.

**Package contents.** A format that is inside another format, such as a Four Pack inside a Case through **Package contents**, has no switch of its own. While its parent is on, it shows as a read-only line under the switches, such as **Four Pack · 6 per Case**, with its prices. If the beer already has a SKU in that smaller format, that format keeps its switch.

**Pours** lists the price group's pours, each with its serving size and prices. They're read-only here. A pour is a serving from the tap, not a SKU. **Edit on price group** opens [Price groups](#pricing), where you add and edit pours and set prices. A brand with no price group lists no pours.

**One-way kegs** (disposable shells you buy and never get back) are a keg format of their own, not a keg pool. Create a format for the shell's size, add the shell as a packaging material on it (category Packaging, counted in each, with its supplier as default vendor), and choose **None** for the keg pool on each SKU. Each keg packaged then draws one shell from stock, Planning orders shells from that supplier ahead of packaging runs, and no deposit is charged. Keep returnable kegs on their own format so they never draw a shell. Instead of a deposit, each one-way keg shipped carries the brewery's **One-way keg fee** (set in [Settings](#settings)) as its own invoice line. The fee is never refunded. The fee in force when the order is submitted is frozen on the line, so a fee changed later does not reprice it, even when the invoice is written at delivery. A line submitted while the fee was zero has no fee frozen on it, and picks up the fee then in force if staff later adjust the order. A submitted order's page lists these fees and the keg deposits under **Keg charges**. A fee of zero adds no line.

**Edit SKU** sits beside the switch on any **Sells as** row that has a SKU, on or off. It changes **Active**, **UPC**, and the **Keg pool** for keg formats. A format switched on for the first time has no keg pool, so open **Edit SKU** to choose one, or leave it at **None** for no deposit. A pool must be in service and the format must have a keg size. Its kind sets the container source automatically. A retired pool already linked to a SKU stays linked; it cannot be newly selected. Clearing or changing the pool affects future charges, not existing invoice deposits or refunds. Orders already submitted retain their deposit rows. Adjusting a confirmed or picked order can add a missing deposit to retained keg lines. Unticking **Active** does the same as switching the format off. Deactivating it removes it from New order choices and the portal catalog, and prevents adding it to new orders; recorded inventory and order history remain. Reactivate it to sell it again at its current grid price. UPC is 8, 12, 13 or 14 digits and unique within the brewery; clear the field to remove it. A SKU's own UPC overrides its [price group's](#pricing) code for that format. A SKU with no UPC of its own scans as its group's code, and has no barcode when the group has none for that format. The brand and format stay fixed, and provider mappings are preserved.

**Batch package share (%)** on **Edit SKU** is the share of a batch's beer volume normally packaged into this SKU. Create the SKU first, then edit it to set a percentage above 0 and at most 100. All package shares for one brand, including inactive SKUs, must total no more than 100%; they need not use the whole batch. Leave the field blank to remove its projection. Only active SKUs with a share create projected demand on [Planning](#purchasing).

**BBL per unit** is the barrel volume of one inventory unit and lives on the format, so every SKU in that format shares it. One US beer barrel is 31 gallons (3,968 fl oz). Half-barrel keg = `0.5`. Sixth-barrel keg is about `0.16666667`. A 24 × 16 oz case is `384 ÷ 3968`, about `0.09677419`. Set it on the format before creating a SKU for it: once a SKU, another package, packaging materials, or a POS mapping, menu, or sale uses the format, its BBL per unit is fixed.

Catalog shows this as **Volume** in brewery units: common keg fractions stay in bbl, smaller exact totals use whole gallons, and anything under a gallon uses fluid ounces. So `0.5` reads as **½ bbl**, a 24 × 16 oz case as **3 gal**, and a can as **16 oz**. Composed formats show the calculated total from their saved contents (four 16 oz cans show **64 oz**), with those contents under **From**. The format detail and each brand’s SKU list show the same derived total. Formats without a volume or saved contents show **—** in Catalog. A volume that converts to no whole unit stays in bbl. MGR still stores and calculates all beer volume in bbl.

An empty Catalog says **No brands yet** and an empty Formats section **No formats yet**.

Wholesale Shop shows the packages the brewery has designated for wholesale (½ keg, ⅙ keg, case, bottle), grouped under the beer name. Buyers do not see warehouse counts. Staff make that designation on the packaging plan. Prices come from the buyer's [sale channel](#pricing) and the beer's price group; a package with no price for that buyer does not appear on Shop.

Admin and Warehouse see **Record movement** at the top of the page. Admin and Sales see **Add SKU in Catalog** there too. It opens Catalog, where SKUs are made.

A negative **Available** is red and has **Shortfall** under it. Select **Shortfall** to see what competes for that SKU in [Taproom replenishment](#replenishment). Select a SKU’s name to open its SKU detail.

**Bins.** A bin is a named place inside a location: a walk-in, a cold room, a dry rack. Every location starts with three, **Cold**, **Dry**, and **Walk-in**, and always keeps at least one; add, rename, or remove them from [Locations](#locations). A bin that has ever recorded stock cannot be removed, even at a zero balance, because the movement history still names it; rename it instead. A bin a POS menu uses cannot be removed either, so the menu, its prices, and its public menu address stay intact; configure that location's menu against another bin first. When shipping, choose the actual source bin and lot for each quantity; tracked lots stay identified through shipment and returns.

<Screen name="SKU detail" />

A SKU’s name on Inventory opens its SKU detail, separate from **Edit SKU** in Catalog. It shows on-hand by location, global ATP and allocations, and immutable paginated movement history, including inactive and zero-stock SKUs. Locations and bins retain their recorded identities; untracked lots are labeled **Untracked**.

**Reverse movement** is available to Admin and Warehouse only for a standalone `adjustment` or `loss` that has not already been reversed. The original remains; **Reversal recorded** and **Original movement** link the two entries. Removing a prior positive adjustment is refused if that exact bin/lot no longer holds enough stock. Retry an unchanged failed form to recover the same result.

Sales sees history without correction controls. Shipments retain **Return shipment** through their order workflow. Taproom count depletion uses **Correct count** on the eligible latest saved count; other compound entries keep their original correction owner. A generic adjustment is not a count correction.

### Record a movement

<Screen name="Record movement" />

1. Select **Record movement** from Today (Admin and Warehouse), or open **Inventory** and select **Record movement**. The Today shortcut opens the same form. If **Record movement** on Today or **Open as form** in Ask MGR opened the form, closing it clears the form from the page address. Reloading the page then does not reopen it.
2. Choose a **SKU**, **Location**, and **Bin**. Picking the location preselects its first bin alphabetically; change it if the stock sits elsewhere. With no SKUs yet, the picker says **No SKUs yet**. Admin gets **Add a SKU**, which opens Catalog. Warehouse is told that an admin or sales member adds them there.
3. Choose a **Kind** from the table below. No kind is preselected, and **Record movement** stays unavailable until you choose one. Until the form is complete, **Still needed:** beside the button names what is missing, such as **a kind** or **a quantity above zero with at most two decimals**. The preview appears once nothing is missing. A form opened with **Open as form** in Ask MGR arrives with its proposed kind chosen.
4. For **Depletion**, pick a **Channel**. The list is your brewery's own: every brewery starts with **Wholesale**, **Taproom**, **DTC**, and **Export**, and you edit that list under [**Sale channels**](#channels). **Taproom** is preselected. The channel decides how the removal is taxed, and that treatment is frozen onto the entry: editing the channel later never rewrites what is already recorded. Shipments set their own channel, not this form.
5. Enter a positive **Quantity** in SKU units, to two decimal places if needed. The kind decides whether stock is added or removed; for **Adjustment**, choose **Add stock** or **Remove stock**. Zero, a negative number, or a third decimal place is refused: once you leave the field, it says **Enter a quantity above zero, with at most two decimal places**. Samples and festival removals require a two-letter **Destination state**. Any other entry in that field shows **Enter a two-letter state, such as PA** once you leave it. An inactive SKU remains available in history but cannot receive a new movement. A removal (including **Remove stock** on an adjustment) cannot take more than the selected bin holds: MGR refuses it with **insufficient selected bin and lot stock**, or asks you to **choose the recorded lot** when the bin's stock is lot-tracked. Check the signed quantity and estimated volume in the preview.
6. Add an optional **Note**, then select **Record movement**.

| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opening balance** | Initial stock when you start MGR | Positive |
| **Production in** | Finished beer in from production | Positive |
| **Adjustment** | Difference found by a verified physical count | Positive or negative |
| **Depletion** | Taproom sale or consumption | Negative |

| Wholesale shipment | Written by shipping from the order workflow | Negative |

**Movement recorded** appears only after the server confirms the save. It shows the committed quantity, frozen barrel volume, bin, state/channel, note, timestamp and reference. A failed or uncertain response does not show this receipt; retry the unchanged form to recover the same save. **Done**, or closing the receipt, returns to the ledger. When the form opened from a shortcut link, Back does not reopen it. Open the SKU’s detail from its name on Inventory to reverse an eligible standalone adjustment or loss; other entries retain their correction workflow.

An assisted **Record movement** proposal is still only a proposal. Review its signed quantity, frozen barrel volume, selected bin and lot stock before and after, channel tax treatment, destination warning, and available correction, then confirm it in the same conversation. The confirmation is bound to you, this brewery, that conversation, and the exact proposed fields. MGR locks the inventory ledger and the displayed catalog and location facts while it checks the preview; changed stock, package volume, labels, tax, or registration warning rejects the save and asks for a new preview without recording anything. A retry of the same confirmed request shows the original result once in that conversation, even after the preview expires or stock changes, and does not record the movement again. If the assistant proposes any other change, or a movement proposal arrives incomplete, the drawer shows an error with nothing to confirm and records nothing. Ask again in the conversation, or open **Record movement** on Inventory. The error clears once the assistant replies again.

A slim handle for the **Ask MGR** drawer sits at the bottom of the staff shell. Select or drag the handle to open the drawer to a compact chat height, again to expand it to the top of the page, and again to return it to the handle. Opening the drawer loads your latest conversation with its 40 most recent messages, or starts one if you don't have one yet. While that's in progress, the header shows **Retry setup** instead of **New chat**. A pulsing bar shows that setup is running. The message box opens when setup finishes. If setup fails, an error appears with **Try again**. If setup takes longer than 15 seconds, the error reads **Ask MGR could not start**. **Try again** and **Retry setup** both restart setup. Neither creates a second conversation. Once your conversation loads, the header switches to **New chat**, which starts a separate private conversation. An admin chooses the AI model that answers under [Settings](#settings). The composer uses Vercel AI Gateway, streams answers as they arrive, and formats them with lists and emphasis where useful. Ask naturally about inventory or search for an SKU, order, invoice, lot, customer, purchase order, or batch. MGR reads only tools exposed to your current brewery role, marks live results with their observation time, and asks a focused follow-up instead of inventing a missing SKU, unit, quantity, location, or lot. **Stop response** cancels a stream. If sending a message, starting a new conversation, or recording a movement fails, **Try again** retries that same action. Press Command-K or Control-K to open the drawer and focus the message box, Enter to send, and Shift-Enter for a new line. Enter that confirms an input-method candidate (for example Japanese or Chinese) does not send.

Assistant replies are length-limited and do not load images.
Stopping a response also cancels its active generation request.

Ask MGR may first show **Opening your conversation…** with a pulsing bar while its chat interface loads. **Outbox** remains available during this step, even offline. If the interface cannot load, the drawer says **Ask MGR could not load** and keeps the rest of MGR and your outbox available. Reconnect, then select **Reload page** to load chat again. Reloading does not discard locally queued readings.

For inventory movements, chat can prepare but never execute the write. No commit button exists until the server returns a canonical preview. **Open as form** carries the known fields to the ordinary inventory form, **Dismiss** records nothing, and **Commit movement** is the only action that records the preview. Conversation history is stored for only the signed-in person in the current brewery. If Gateway is unavailable or unconfigured, the composer shows an error and the ordinary application forms remain available.

**Outbox** shows only fermentation readings captured by this signed-in person for the current brewery. A reading is saved locally with its exact observed time, parsed values, tank, role and request ID before MGR tries the network. Retry sends that exact attempt; returning under another account or brewery never sends it, and a role change disables Retry. **Fix as new reading** keeps an uncertain original while opening its fields for review under a fresh request ID. **Discard** asks for confirmation naming the reading and removes only the selected row; bulk discard names every row. Inventory movements, picks and transfers need current server state, so MGR reports an offline failure and never queues them. If this browser holds a saved reading MGR can no longer read, MGR sets it aside unsent, keeps every readable reading, and says so at the top of **Outbox**; re-enter the reading if it is still needed, then **Dismiss** the notice to delete the set-aside copy.

MGR freezes barrel volume when each entry posts. Ordinary entries use quantity × the format’s BBL per unit; exact reversals negate the original stored volume, and shipment returns retain their source volume. Movements cannot be edited or deleted. See [Errors and corrections](#errors-corrections) before you try to fix inventory.

Empty sections say **No inventory recorded yet** or **No movements recorded yet**.

## Customers and ship-tos [#customers]

*Admin and Sales can create and edit customers and ship-to addresses. Warehouse can read these pages at their addresses; customer, ship-to, and invitation controls are hidden. The list shows name and alias, type, home state (or the country, outside the US), sale channel, and payment terms.*

Above the list, **Search customers** finds customers whose name or alias contains what you type (press Enter); the search stays in the page address, **No customers match** means nothing does, and **Clear search** below the box shows every customer again.

### Create or edit a customer

<Screen name="Customer detail" />

1. Open **Customers**. Select **New customer**, or select an existing customer's row and then **Edit** beside the customer name.
2. Enter **Name** (required). Optionally enter an **Alias**, a second name staff know the account by. The alias is for staff only: buyers never see it. **Search customers** finds the customer by it, and the customer list shows it beside the name. A note under the field says so.
3. Choose **Type**: **Distributor**, **Retailer** (default), **Brewery**, or **Other**.
4. Choose the **Country** (United States is preselected). For a US customer, enter the required two-letter **State**. MGR uppercases it as you type and refuses a code that is not a US state, DC, or US territory. A customer in another country has no state, so the field is hidden.
5. Choose a **Sale channel** (required; Wholesale is preselected). It decides the customer's prices and where shipped orders are recorded.
6. Optionally enter **License number**. Choose **Payment terms**: **Due on receipt**, **Net 15**, or **Net 30** (default), the same list vendors use. Each new invoice freezes its due date from its issue date plus 0, 15, or 30 days. Changing these terms later does not move existing invoice dates.
7. Select **Create** or **Save**.

The customer form has no ship-to fields. Add ship-to addresses on the customer's page after you create the customer; **New customer** says so above **Create**. See **Create or edit a ship-to** below.

Customer detail shows **Tax treatment**. Choose **Inherit sale channel** to use the channel default, or select a customer override; editing another field preserves the selected treatment. **Orders** opens only this customer’s orders, and status filters keep that customer selected. **Clear** returns to all customers.

On desktop, **Account details**, **Ship-tos**, and **Portal users** sit together, with **Trading terms** and **Customer activity** alongside them. **Edit** stays beside the customer name. **New ship-to** belongs to the Ship-tos section; **Delete customer** is separated below the account sections. The same sections stack on smaller screens.

### Delete an unused customer

Admin can open a customer and select **Delete customer**. The confirmation names the customer and explains that its ship-to addresses will also be deleted. **Cancel** keeps everything. Confirming returns to Customers after deletion.

Customers with orders, invoices, keg records, portal access, invitations, or a QuickBooks link cannot be deleted. MGR shows the reason and preserves the customer and its addresses. This does not merge customers or remove their history. Sales and Warehouse cannot delete customers.

### Create or edit a ship-to

<Screen name="Ship-to form" />

Select the customer name, then **New ship-to** or **Edit**. Choose the **Country**: a new ship-to starts in the customer's country, and an edited one keeps its own. For a US address, **Label** and **State** (a two-letter US state code, uppercased) are required. **Address**, **City**, and **Postal code** are optional until you know them; a postal code you enter must be a 5-digit ZIP code or ZIP+4, such as 97201-1234. Existing orders keep their chosen destination. Ship-to state is the destination used when a wholesale shipment leaves inventory.

An invitation interrupted before its email was sent stops blocking after 15 minutes. Existing sign-in accounts get no email. They see the invitation in [Invitations](/invitations) the next time they sign in, and access is not granted until they accept there, within seven days.

**Portal users** lists each buyer who can sign in for this customer, by email, and says **No portal users yet** when there are none. Admin and Sales can select **Remove access** beside a buyer and confirm. The buyer loses this customer's portal at once, even if they are signed in: their next page or action is refused. Their orders, invoices, and sign-in account stay. To restore access, send a new invitation and have the buyer accept it. Replaying an old accepted invitation never restores removed access.

## Price groups [#pricing]

<Screen name="Price groups" />
<Screen name="Price group" />

*Admin and Sales manage prices. Order lines snapshot their price when the order is created.*

Price groups are the rows of the price grid, with formats across the top. Each [sale channel](#channels) has its own tab above the grid, and the grid shows the selected channel; the first channel shows when you open the page, and the address remembers the tab you choose. Put each brand on a group (**Catalog** → **Brand** → **Price group**) and fill the cells; every SKU of that brand then sells at its format's cell on the customer's channel. An empty cell means unpriced, and an order for that SKU is refused. Clear a cell to unprice it. A group a brand sits on, a pour belongs to, or a cell is filled for, cannot be deleted.

1. Open **Price groups** and select **New price group**. The sheet is the same one a group's name opens. Enter a required **Group name**. **Position** is required: it is the group's row in the price grid, a whole number where lower numbers sit higher and the lowest is the top row, pre-filled after the last row. **Cost ceiling ($/bbl) · optional** comes next, with the previous group's ceiling below it for reference. Then select **Save price group**. It stays disabled until the group has a name, and the line beside it reads *Still needed: a group name.* Name and position must each be unique within the brewery.
2. On that group, **Add pour** and enter **Serving size · oz** (for example 14); expand **Rename · optional** to call it something other than the default *14 oz pour*. Pours belong to the price group, not the beer. Every beer on the group uses them. A pour stays on the group it was added to; it cannot move to another group. The grid adds a column by that name. To price the same pour on every group, give it the same name on each (for example **Half**, with each group's own serving size). A group with no pour of that column's name shows **+ add** there instead of a price; it opens **Add pour** for that group with the name filled in. **Remove** on a pour asks you to confirm, then deletes it. Removal is refused while its price cells are filled, or while a Square mapping, menu line or recorded sale uses it; clear the cells and mappings first. **Edit pour** beside **Remove** changes the serving size or name later; the serving size is fixed once a POS mapping, menu, or sale uses it, but a rename still saves. A beer's page lists its group's pours under **Pours** read-only, with **Edit on price group** linking here.
3. Choose a channel's tab, then type a price in dollars (stored as whole cents) into a cell. Pressing Enter, Tab, or selecting elsewhere saves it; Tab moves to the next cell, so you can fill a row without opening anything. Every SKU of every brand on that group sells at that price, in that format, on that channel. An unpriced cell is empty and shows **—**. Text that is not a dollar amount is not saved and reads *Enter dollars, like 12.50* under the cell.
4. To unprice a filled cell, delete its amount: nothing is saved yet, and a **Clear** button appears beside the cell. **Clear** asks you to confirm, then empties the cell; those SKUs stay unpriced on that channel until you save a price. Type the amount back instead to keep it.
5. Under **Barcodes**, each packaged format has a **UPC** row: select **Add** (or **Edit**), enter the code, and **Save UPC**. Every SKU of every brand on that group scans as that code for that format, unless the SKU has its own UPC. One code may cover several formats, for example the can label's code on both the Four Pack and the Case. A format with no code reads **none**; that is normal for kegs and raises no warning. Save it blank to clear it. A code is 8, 12, 13 or 14 digits.
6. To delete a group, open it by name, select **Delete**, and confirm. Its barcodes go with it.

Opening an existing group also shows a **Prices** line summarizing where it's priced already, for example `$132.00 on Wholesale · ½ bbl keg, and 3 more cells`, or **none** if nothing prices it yet.

A cost ceiling is dollars per barrel of recipe cost. Position sorts the rows, and the ceilings are read in that order. A brand's page suggests the first group, from the top row down, whose ceiling is at or above the brand's recipe cost; groups with no ceiling are skipped (see [Catalog](#catalog)). Saving a ceiling never moves a brand or changes a price. A brewery with no groups yet says **Add a price group, then put each brand on one from Catalog.**

## Staff orders [#orders]

Orders show up to 50 records per page, newest first. **More** opens older orders only when another one exists; **Newest** returns to the latest page. Status and customer filters remain applied while paging. Changing a filter starts at its newest orders.

**Status** under the Work tabs picks **All states** or one state: **Draft**, **Submitted**, **Confirmed**, **Picked**, **Shipped**, or **Cancelled**. The choice is kept in the page address, so a link, a refresh, or Back keeps it. ### Create an order

<Screen name="New order" />

2. Choose **Kind**: **Wholesale** (default) or **Taproom transfer**.

Once a wholesale customer is chosen, the SKU list offers only active SKUs priced on that customer's sale channel; a transfer offers every active SKU. If a line's SKU has no price for the customer, saving names that SKU and says it is not active and priced for this customer. **Add line** adds a row. **Remove** takes one away, but at least one row stays. Availability is shown only when supplied; a missing ATP figure does not mean zero stock.

A successful empty list says **No active SKUs are priced for this customer**; check **Price groups** or choose another customer. **No matching SKUs** means your search does not match a loaded choice.

   Changing customers keeps entered quantities and SKU selections. A selection absent from the new customer's options remains visible with an **unavailable** warning. Choose an available SKU or remove that line before saving; no entered unavailable selection is silently dropped. Compatible selections stay valid. **Save draft** waits for the first pricing read and remains disabled while any selected SKU is unavailable. The server checks eligibility again when saving.

The button stays disabled until the required fields are chosen: for wholesale, a customer, a ship-to, a source location, and at least one line with an available SKU and a quantity above zero; for a transfer, a destination instead of the customer and ship-to. The order page reads as one document. The header names the order number, the customer (or **Taproom transfer**), the status, and the next step. The main verb comes first, in reading and tab order: a confirmed order shows **Record pick**, then **Add or remove lines**; a picked order shows **Ship** (or **Complete transfer**), then **Record pick** and **Add or remove lines**. While picked beer is staged for restocking, **Put back** comes first instead and the fulfillment verbs turn secondary, because the beer must go back before anything ships. Admin and Sales see **Cancel order**, a quiet red action at the bottom of the page, after the history; it still asks for a reason and confirmation.

The pending restock note (naming what to put back) and the **changed after pick** note sit directly under the header. **Order details** lists the fulfillment source, the ship-to (or **Pickup**), the requested ship date, the customer PO, and the note when one was entered. A fact with no value reads **Not specified**. A taproom transfer has no ship-to or PO, and the page does not display its destination.

**Price summary** sits beside the details on a wider screen and before the lines on a phone. It is in US dollars and never includes tax:

- A submitted, confirmed or picked wholesale order shows **Ordered items**, **Returnable keg deposits** and **One-way keg fees**, then **Order amount · before tax**. This is the ordered quantities at the prices and keg charges captured on the order. It is not an invoice or a balance due.
- A shipped order shows the same ordered amount and says it is not the final invoiced amount. The invoice bills what actually shipped.
- A cancelled order shows **Cancelled order amount · before tax**, as history rather than an amount to collect. A draft cancelled before it was ever submitted shows an **Items estimate** instead, because its keg charges were never set.
- A draft shows an **Items estimate** only. Keg deposits and fees read **Not finalized until submission**, because they are set when the order is submitted.
- A taproom transfer says it is not a customer charge and shows no amount.
- As a safeguard, if keg charge data is ever missing from the order, the page shows the ordered items only and says **Order amount unavailable** rather than a total that leaves charges out.

Each line amount is the ordered quantity times the captured unit price, rounded to the cent. The ordered-items subtotal is the sum of those rounded line amounts. A one-way keg fee is charged per keg, so it is multiplied by the line's quantity. A deposit uses the amount saved on the order.

**Ordered items** is a table on a wider screen and a list of labeled lines on a phone. Each line shows ordered, picked and shipped quantities, brewery-wide ATP, the captured unit price, and the line amount. A quantity not yet counted reads **Not recorded**. Missing ATP reads **Not supplied**, never zero, and brewery ATP is not stock at the fulfillment source. A short reason shows under its line as **Short:**, and a line picked above its ordered amount or with negative ATP carries a warning in words. **Keg charges** lists each deposit and one-way fee with the line it belongs to. **History** lists each event in the brewery's time zone and who made it: staff by @handle, a portal buyer by email (Admin and Sales; others read **a user not on the team** for a buyer, as everyone does for a removed staff member); an empty history says **No events yet**. An empty order list says **No orders yet**, or names the status or customer filter that found nothing. A shipped or cancelled order has no further action controls.

Admin and Sales also see a separate **Cost and margin** section on a shipped order, below the keg charges and apart from the price summary: what the beer it shipped cost, and the margin on it. Cost is the per-package cost frozen when each shipped lot was packaged (see [Inventory](#inventory)); beer that came back through a credit memo or a checked-in refusal is taken out of both cost and revenue. Revenue is the shipped quantity at the order line prices, so an order whose invoice is deferred to delivery already shows its margin. Margin is a percent of that revenue. A shipped unit with no cost (opening-balance stock, historical runs, or a lot with no receipt cost) is counted but not priced: the line then reads *N units without cost, margin withheld*, and the cost shown is only the costed part. Warehouse does not see the line.

Shipping asks for each line's actual **source bin and lot**. Select **Add source** for every bin/lot used and enter its quantity; the sources must sum to the shipped quantity, and zero lines have no sources. Untracked legacy stock is labeled explicitly. A selected source must have enough stock when the shipment commits. An internal transfer also requires its destination bin and preserves the same lot on both legs. Shipping keeps one invoice line per order line even when several sources supplied it. On-delivery shipping moves the beer once; delivery confirmation creates only the invoice.

After shipping, the confirmation shows the recorded quantities and the invoice link when one was created. **On delivery** shipments say the invoice is deferred. Refreshing this page keeps the recorded confirmation.

## Invoices and credit memos [#invoices]

Invoices show up to 50 records per page, newest first. **More** opens older invoices only when another one exists; **Newest** returns to the latest page.

<Screen name="Invoices" />
<Screen name="Invoice" />

*Admin and Sales open the Invoices list from **More**. Warehouse can open it by entering the address directly and sees the invoice created by a wholesale shipment from **View invoice** right after shipping. Only Admin and Sales can return beer against an invoice or mark a buyer's question answered.*

Each row's number is its one list destination on a wide screen and a phone. It opens the invoice; push, retry, re-push, and write-off are chosen and confirmed there. Selecting a list link performs no accounting write. **Needs mapping** marks a failed push that Admin or Sales can fix from the invoice. Accountant-edited rows show the frozen local subtotal under the current total. **Open in QuickBooks** is available to Admin and Sales on invoice detail and list rows when the pushed invoice identity matches the current connected company and connection. A list row shows it only when that link is verified; the reason it is unavailable stays on invoice detail. It opens the corresponding invoice in QuickBooks, where you may need to sign in. An unpushed or deleted invoice, disconnected company, missing identity, or connection mismatch shows why the link is unavailable. Credit memos have no supported staff destination here; find them in QuickBooks separately. Reconnecting cannot reuse a previous connection’s invoice link. This staff action is separate from a buyer payment link. **Email delivery status** is also unavailable: MGR can’t tell whether QuickBooks emailed an invoice, so it never claims one was emailed.

The register keeps document and customer identity separate from money, payment, and accounting. Each row names its kind, **Invoice** or **Credit memo**, under the number, and shows the document's **Total** separately from the current **Balance**. The balance comes from QuickBooks, or from payments recorded in MGR for an invoice QuickBooks never received (see [Record a payment in MGR](#record-payment)). A numeric zero always displays as `$0.00`; an unavailable balance is not treated as zero. The due day and payment meaning are separate from accounting: a balance settled without cash says **Settled / no cash payment recorded**, not **Paid**. On a wide screen these facts are table columns; on a phone the same labeled facts follow the number link. Outstanding invoices past their due day are marked **Overdue** in the brewery's time zone; an invoice due today is not overdue. An invoice QuickBooks never received (never pushed, rejected by QuickBooks, or at a brewery that never connected) has a balance from MGR, so it is marked **Overdue** past its due day until payments cover it or Admin writes it off. Credit memos, invoices whose balance is unknown, invoices with no payment due, and settled, paid, written-off, voided, or deleted invoices are not marked overdue. For Admin and Sales, the **Accounting** column is empty on an ordinary row and lists only exceptions, such as **Needs mapping** or a failed push; other roles see the captions described below the table. Existing invoices that lacked a due date use the customer's current terms because historical terms were not recorded. This backfill does not re-push invoices to QuickBooks. Select the invoice number to open the invoice, described below. Every wholesale order with returnable kegs is charged the pool's deposit when it is submitted, whether staff entered it or a buyer placed it; a portal order keeps the deposit price the buyer reviewed. If Admin or Sales later adjusts its lines, a retained keg line keeps that frozen deposit price while its deposit quantity follows the new order quantity; a newly added keg line freezes the deposit configured at adjustment time. A pool with a zero deposit remains a valid no-charge keg, while a returnable SKU missing its required keg format or pool is refused before the order changes. Its invoice adds a separate deposit line for each charged returnable-keg quantity actually shipped. A one-way keg line instead adds a **One-way keg fee** line for the kegs shipped, or for the kegs the customer kept when the invoice is written at delivery. The fee line is not listed on the Beer return page: returning the beer never credits the fee. An empty list says **No invoices yet**. A payment on an invoice in QuickBooks is recorded in QuickBooks. MGR records a payment only on an invoice QuickBooks never received.

Roles other than Admin and Sales see only **Not pushed** or **QuickBooks status available to Sales** in the Accounting column, unless the invoice is written off, voided, deleted, predates the books, or has payments recorded in MGR.

**Paid** is a payment status, not a settlement date. For an invoice in QuickBooks, MGR does not supply an actual payment date for one or multiple payments. An invoice paid in full by payments recorded in MGR is marked paid on the day its last payment was received, and **Payments** on the invoice lists each payment's received day. A partial cash payment remains due until the balance reaches zero; credit-only closure is **Settled**, not **Paid**. In the register, a balance closed with a mix of partial cash and credits shows **Settled** with the cash amount, not **Paid**. MGR does not display a settlement date for an invoice in QuickBooks.

An invoice reads as a document. Its number and state in words (such as **Unpaid**, **Partially paid**, **Settled**, **Paid**, **No payment due**, **Voided**, **Written off**, or **Credit memo**) head the page, with the customer under them. **Issued** and **Due** are separate facts; an invoice without a due date says **Not set** for Due. Beside them on a wide screen, and before the lines on a phone, the **Price summary** is the one place that shows the invoice's total. It adds the stored line amounts by line kind: **Items**, **Keg deposits** (refunds included), **One-way keg fees**, and **Adjustments** for anything else, so the rows always add up. **Total** is the QuickBooks total once QuickBooks has one, even zero, and otherwise the sum of the lines. When a QuickBooks sync reports that accounting edited the invoice there, the summary labels **Current invoice total** and shows **Frozen MGR line subtotal** beside it; the original lines do not change, and the difference is reviewed in QuickBooks. An invoice QuickBooks taxed but nobody edited instead shows a **Tax (QuickBooks)** row, so the rows add up to **Total**. A voided invoice reads **Total** $0.00 with no balance. A credit memo shows a negative **Credit memo total**, which is not money the customer owes. Under the total, an invoice that is still owed shows the **Outstanding balance** from QuickBooks and any **Cash received in QuickBooks**. For an invoice QuickBooks never received, the outstanding balance is the total minus payments recorded in MGR, and a **Paid in MGR** row shows their sum. A balance QuickBooks has not supplied reads **Unknown**, never $0.00. A paid invoice shows its balance only when it is known: from QuickBooks, or $0.00 with a **Paid in MGR** row for an invoice paid by payments recorded in MGR. Credit memos and voided, deleted, or written-off invoices show no balance due. **Line items** lists each line's description, quantity, unit price, and amount: a table on a wide screen, labeled records on a phone. The amount is the stored line amount, not recalculated from quantity and price. **Payments** lists each payment recorded in MGR, newest received first, with its amount, received day, method and reference; a voided payment shows **Voided** and its reason. Admin and Sales see **Record payment** there only on an invoice that can take one (see [Record a payment in MGR](#record-payment)), so it is hidden for a QuickBooks invoice, a pending push, a push to a previous QuickBooks company, and a paid, written-off, or $0.00 invoice. **QuickBooks and accounting** states the invoice's accounting status once, then **Open in QuickBooks** and any permitted push, retry, re-push, or write-off action. Only mappings that need action are listed. While the invoice has a payment recorded in MGR that is not voided, it reads **Payments recorded in MGR · not sent to QuickBooks**, with no push action and no mapping rows. **Buyer questions** follow, unanswered first. **Return** sits last, under **Correct a shipment**, because it corrects a shipment rather than collecting payment.

Orders picked up before the brewery's **Books opened on** date (see [Settings](#settings)) can be brought over from another system. Each one is recorded as picked up and paid on its pickup day, at the prices given when it was imported. Its invoice is never sent to QuickBooks and reads **not in QuickBooks (before the books opened)**, with no push or retry. It carries no stock, keg deposit or one-way keg fee, so it changes no inventory or keg balance. It never appears on a route or as overdue. It cannot be returned or credited.

QuickBooks reconciliation is manual. Admin and Sales choose **Sync QuickBooks** from **Invoices**; Admin can also start it from **Settings → Accounting**. **Invoices** shows the sync only while QuickBooks is connected. Otherwise its QuickBooks row says **Not connected** or **Reconnect required**, above a note that payment status may be stale until QuickBooks is reconnected and synced. Admin chooses **Connect** on that row to open the page that connects or reconnects QuickBooks. Sales sees **Admin must connect** instead. A saved batch waits until QuickBooks is reconnected; **Retry saved sync** then appears on **Invoices** again. While the sync is shown, it lists the last successful sync and who ran it beside the button. The latest attempt appears only when it did not finish or was superseded. The latest recorded failure stays visible beside the sync. The operator appears by full name, or by email when no name is set. Until a whole batch succeeds, displayed payment status may be stale. A provider failure never advances the last-success time.

A sync re-reads every open and part-paid invoice. It also re-reads an invoice for 90 days after a sync first sees it paid in full, voided, or deleted. After those 90 days, MGR stops reading that invoice. A later QuickBooks edit, reversed payment, or reopening of it does not reach MGR. An invoice closed by credit alone (**Settled**) is re-read on every sync.

**Retry saved sync** resumes your own unfinished batch, even after a reload. It preserves the request identity and invoice set. Another operator cannot retry your request. If a newer sync has superseded that batch, the retry records that terminal outcome without changing payment status. Then choose **Sync QuickBooks** to start a new batch. A superseded batch never advances the last-success time. Reconnect QuickBooks first when its authorization requires recovery. A historical failure remains visible separately after a successful retry. Successful sync history begins when this tracking feature is installed; MGR does not invent timestamps for older syncs.

Admin and Sales can run the QuickBooks invoice sync. It freezes the eligible invoices for that request, reads each one in the same connected company, then updates the whole batch together. If one read or update fails, none of that batch is applied; retrying the same request reads the same frozen invoice set, and a completed retry returns its recorded result. If two syncs overlap, a result from the older read cannot replace the newer invoice state. The sync updates current balance, total, tax, payment state, and edit marker. Staff and portal screens show that current QuickBooks total, while the original local lines and pushed snapshot remain unchanged as audit history, so an edited total can differ from the visible line sum. A partial payment stays due; a payment that is later reversed makes the invoice due again. MGR reads linked Payment details once per batch and records only positive cash that QuickBooks assigns to that invoice. A zero-dollar Payment created by applying credit records no cash and shows the invoice as settled rather than paid; a single-invoice mixed cash-and-credit settlement records only its cash portion. If one Payment covers multiple invoices and its cash is less than their combined applications, MGR records no cash from that ambiguous Payment instead of counting the same cash more than once. Voided and deleted invoices keep any earlier payment-confirmation marker as history but no longer appear paid. MGR compares only fields it originally sent, so a QuickBooks default such as a derived due date is not reported as an accountant edit. MGR reports a real accountant edit and stops; it never overwrites or pushes the invoice automatically.

Admin opens **Settings → Accounting** to connect, reconnect, inspect safe connection health, manage mappings and choose whether future QuickBooks invoices allow bank transfer, card, or both. Admin and Sales can open **Fix mapping** from an invoice and enter an exact customer or item ID they verified in the connected QuickBooks company; MGR never selects a record from a matching name. After a mapping saves, reopening it shows the ID the server retained. The payment choices are copied when the next invoice is pushed and do not change invoices already there. Every push, exact retry, corrected push, or re-push first opens a confirmation that names the invoice and the operation. Closing it sends nothing. Selecting its **Confirm** action sends it; an exact retry still reuses the previously saved request. QuickBooks payment, edit, void, and deletion status remains visible after disconnect. After sync confirms an invoice was voided or deleted in its original QuickBooks company, Admin may mark it **Written off** with a reason even while QuickBooks is disconnected. Admin can also write off an unpaid invoice QuickBooks never received (see [Record a payment in MGR](#record-payment)). Credit memos never offer write-off. That status records who acted and when inside MGR; it does not change QuickBooks, record cash, or create a credit.

Accounting shares the explorer's connection, online-payment status, and **Bank transfer (ACH)** / **Card** switches. The QuickBooks status reads **Connected**, **Not connected**, or **Reconnect required**. While QuickBooks is connected, **Online payments** says customers pay through each invoice's QuickBooks payment link: the portal's Pay button opens it, and when QuickBooks has none the customer is asked to contact you. While both **Bank transfer (ACH)** and **Card** are off, it reads **Off**: new invoices get no online payment option, and invoices pushed earlier keep theirs. While QuickBooks is not connected, it reads **Not available** and says it needs a QuickBooks connection. If QuickBooks is not turned on in MGR, **Connect QuickBooks** is unavailable and the page says so. The payment switches sit under **Push defaults**; before QuickBooks is connected they show as one locked row. Select **Save push defaults** while connected; saved settings remain read-only during recovery. If a refresh credential has expired or QuickBooks rejects it as revoked, MGR shows **Reconnect required** and the authorization error. Admin can reconnect or disconnect. Rarely, if MGR is interrupted while renewing its QuickBooks access, the next QuickBooks action shows **Reconnect required**, and an Admin reconnects. A temporary provider failure leaves the connection state unchanged. **Customers missing a portal login email** says how many customers have no portal login email, or **All customers have a portal login email**, and offers **Review**. **Disconnect QuickBooks** opens a confirmation sheet, including while the status reads **Reconnect required**. Disconnecting deletes MGR's stored credential first, then asks QuickBooks to revoke it. If QuickBooks confirms, you land on Connect QuickBooks; if it does not, the sheet stays open and says the credential is deleted but revocation was not confirmed, so remove MGR's access in QuickBooks or reconnect. Closing the sheet changes nothing.

The **QuickBooks mappings** list separates customers, SKUs, the deposit/refund item, and the one-way keg fee item. **Map** or **Change** opens **Mapping conflict** from Accounting, or **Fix mapping** from an invoice's mapping list. Each sheet identifies the local record and accepts the exact QuickBooks customer or item ID, without changing leading zeroes or guessing from names. **Save mapping** is unavailable while the ID is blank or a save is pending. Closing discards unsaved edits. A company claimed by another brewery must be disconnected there first; a mapping cannot override that claim. A QuickBooks customer maps to one MGR customer per company: saving an ID another customer already uses in the same company is refused with "That already exists. Use a different name or value." Remap the other customer first.

For received materials, Admin also maps each **Vendor** by its exact QuickBooks vendor ID and sets one **Purchase account** for the entire brewery. Use the expense or inventory asset account your accountant chose; there are no per-material account mappings. Both mappings belong to the connected company, and connecting a different company clears them. A missing vendor or purchase account mapping blocks a receipt Bill and links back to this list.

While connected, invoice detail lists only the mappings that need action for the current QuickBooks company: a missing customer, SKU, keg-deposit, or one-way keg fee mapping. A mapped customer or item is not listed, and an invoice with a payment recorded in MGR that is not voided lists none. **Fix** opens the mapping list; a missing deposit or fee mapping requires Admin in Accounting. An old company's ID is not a match. Missing local records show **Mapping unavailable**. The QuickBooks status, buyer questions, and permitted confirmation actions remain visible alongside these rows. The balance is in the price summary.

When a buyer asks about an invoice from the portal, the question appears under **Buyer questions** on the invoice with their words and the day they asked, and as an **Answer** row on Today for Admin and Sales. Reply by phone or email, then select **Mark answered**; that clears the Today row and nothing on the invoice changes.

Returns preserve the selected shipment lot and its original recorded volume even if the format definition later changes. A damaged return writes that same volume back out as loss atomically.

### Record a payment in MGR [#record-payment]

<Screen name="Record payment" />
<Screen name="Void payment" />

Admin and Sales can record a payment received outside QuickBooks, such as cash or a check, on an invoice QuickBooks never received. That includes an invoice never pushed, one QuickBooks rejected, and every invoice at a brewery that never connected QuickBooks. MGR's payments then own that invoice's balance, paid state and overdue mark.

On the invoice, select **Record payment** under **Payments**. Enter **Amount ($)**, **Received on**, **Method** (**Cash**, **Check**, **ACH**, or **Other**), and an optional **Reference** such as a check number. The amount must be more than $0.00 and no more than the balance due. A payment can be partial. The received day cannot be after today in the brewery's time zone. A day before the invoice was issued is accepted, for a payment taken at order time. When payments reach the total, the invoice reads **Paid**, marked on the day the last payment was received. The customer portal then shows it as paid too.

A payment is never edited or deleted. To correct one, select **Void** beside it, enter a reason of up to 500 characters, and select **Confirm void**. The payment stays on the invoice, marked **Voided** with its reason, and no longer counts toward the balance. If the invoice drops below its total, it is no longer paid. Record the correct payment again if one was received.

At a brewery connected to QuickBooks, **Record payment** warns that the invoice will not be sent to QuickBooks while it has payments recorded in MGR. While it has a payment that is not voided, the invoice offers no push and lists no mappings. A push of it, from MGR or the API, is refused with the reason "payments for this invoice are recorded in MGR, so it is not sent to QuickBooks". Once every payment on it is voided, it can be pushed again.

Some invoices with no QuickBooks number still cannot take a payment, and keep an unknown balance:

- An invoice whose QuickBooks push never finished (**push result unknown**). Retry the push first. If the QuickBooks company or connection changed while that push was pending, the push can never finish, so the invoice can be neither pushed nor paid in MGR.
- An invoice pushed to a previous QuickBooks company. Push it to the current company, and record its payment in QuickBooks.

Paid, written-off and historical invoices take no payment, nor does a $0.00 invoice, which reads **No payment due**. Credit memos take no payment, and a credit memo does not lower the balance of the invoice it credits. An invoice that will never be paid, including one a credit memo credits in full, stays **Overdue** past its due day until payments cover its total or Admin writes it off. On an invoice that can take a payment, Admin sees **Write off** under **QuickBooks and accounting**; enter a reason of up to 500 characters and select **Confirm write-off**. The invoice then reads **Written off** and is no longer overdue. A partly paid invoice can be written off for the rest of its balance: its payments stay as they are and can still be voided, but it takes no new payment. A write-off records who acted and when inside MGR; it records no cash and creates no credit. MGR has no void for an invoice QuickBooks never received.

### Return beer and correct the invoice

<Screen name="Return and credit" />

1. Open the invoice and select **Return**, under **Correct a shipment** at the bottom, to open the Beer return page.
2. Enter the quantity coming back for each applicable line. Leave unrelated lines at zero. A return cannot exceed the remaining uncredited quantity. The invoice's keg deposit lines are listed too: enter the number of kegs whose deposit you are refunding, in whole kegs.
3. For beer lines, split the returned quantity under **Original shipped source**. The source amounts must sum to the line quantity; a source cannot be returned more than was shipped. Choose **Return to**, the destination **Return to bin**, and a reason: *unsold* and *wrong item* put the beer back as sellable stock at that location; *damaged* puts it back and writes the same quantity to loss in the same step, so broken beer never lands on a pick list.
4. Select **Return shipment**.

For older invoices that have no shipment, choose the return location; MGR records untracked stock in its first bin. Shipment-backed invoices always require the original source selection.

The preview uses the selected destination and the invoice's captured beer and deposit prices. A deposit refund moves no stock and needs no source or bin, and it does not record the empty keg. Full kegs returned with their beer post **Returned** automatically; for a deposit-only refund, record the **Returned** event in [Keg fleet](#kegs) when the empty keg comes back. A refund of deposits alone still asks for a return location and reason, which it does not use. After saving, MGR returns to the original invoice.

MGR creates a separate credit memo at the prices frozen on the invoice lines, never today's price, with a keg deposit refund line for each refunded deposit (which lowers that customer's deposit held on **Customer keg balance** and the portal Account page), records the return-in (and, for damaged, the loss) against that credit memo, and notes the correction on the originating order's timeline. A paid invoice can still be returned; the credit sits as available credit. Credit memos cannot themselves be credited. An invoice that is **Written off**, or voided or deleted in QuickBooks, is no longer owed: it shows no **Return**, and MGR refuses a return or credit memo against it. MGR also refuses a return or credit memo against an order recorded before the books opened, because no stock left with it.

## Locations [#locations]

<Screen name="Locations" />
<Screen name="Location detail" />

**Locations** (open `/locations`; Settings links here) lists every location with what it is used for. Admin sees **Add location**: a name and its **Uses**: any of *Warehouse*, *Taproom* and *Storage*. **Uses** takes more than one answer. The line under it starts *Choose one or more*. *Warehouse* starts selected. A place is often more than one: a building that pours pints, holds pallets and ships orders is all three, so select all three. At least one use is required. A note in the form explains bins: they are the rooms and racks inside a location where stock sits. Every new location starts with **Cold**, **Dry** and **Walk-in**. Names are unique within the brewery. Admin returns to **Settings**; other staff return to **Beer**. **All finished goods inventory** opens the global inventory, not a location filter. ## Stock transfers [#transfers]

<Screen name="Transfers" />

**Transfers** (Work tab; Warehouse and Admin) move stock between two of the brewery's locations without an order or an invoice. **New transfer** takes a source and destination location, the bin at each end (the first bin is preselected), and SKU lines with quantities; **Create transfer** creates it ready to pick and opens the new transfer. A wholly blank spare line is ignored. Every other line needs a SKU and a quantity above zero. Until it has both, **Create transfer** stays unavailable with the missing pieces named beside it, and once you leave the line's quantity, the missing SKU or the bad quantity is marked with what to fix under the line. An entered line is never silently left out. With no SKUs, the SKU picker links Admin to **Catalog**; for Warehouse it says an admin or sales member adds them in Catalog. A chosen location with no bins says so under its bin picker and links to **Locations**, and **Create transfer** asks for a location that has a bin. There is no draft step. Materials and empty kegs can also move through the API. The document is numbered TRF-0001 upward and walks **Record pick** (quantities default to what was requested), then **Receive**. A pick cannot exceed what the source bin holds: lines drawing the same stock from the same bin count together, across every lot of it (the lots are chosen at **Receive**), and picking more is refused with **insufficient selected bin and lot stock**, which names what the bin holds; nothing is recorded and the transfer keeps its status. Receiving writes the paired stock movements in one step, out of the source bin and into the destination bin, so on hand changes place and nothing counts as a removal; the fleet total for kegs is unchanged. A same-location pair is refused: open **Locations → Location bins → Move stock** instead. **Move stock** needs some stock in the location's bins and a second bin. With nothing stored, the page shows its empty state instead. With stock in a lone bin, **Move stock** shows as unavailable and says to add a bin first. Admin and Warehouse choose stock and its source bin, a different destination bin, and a positive quantity no larger than the source bin holds for that stock. Materials use base units; empty kegs require whole units. Each finished-goods or material lot is listed separately from **Untracked stock**; choose the identity actually being moved. The selected lot is preserved on both entries. A bin move writes paired rows without a document. Neither a bin move nor **Receive** takes out more than the source bin holds, whether finished goods (lot or untracked), materials, or empty kegs: the whole move or receipt is refused with **insufficient selected bin and lot stock**, which names what the source bin holds, and nothing is written. Untracked stock is checked on its own: when it falls short and the bin holds lot stock, MGR asks you to **choose the recorded lot**. Receiving checks the source bin as it stands at receipt, so stock that left the bin after the pick blocks it. A pick does not hold the stock for its transfer: two transfers can pick the same stock, and the one received second is refused. Record the pick again with what the bin holds, or cancel it and create a new transfer. Until a transfer is received, **Cancel transfer** closes it out with a required **Reason**: a pick never takes stock out of the source bin, so nothing moves, the picked quantities are cleared, and the transfer shows *cancelled* with the reason. A received or already-cancelled transfer cannot be cancelled. In the list, a received or cancelled transfer has no verb; select its row to open it.

## Compliance [#compliance]

<Screen name="Compliance months" />

**Compliance** (More; Sales and Admin) reviews TTB periods monthly, quarterly, or annually and records each filing you make with TTB: pick **Monthly**, **Quarterly**, or **Annual** at the top. Monthly lists the last three months, Quarterly the last four calendar quarters (January–March, April–June, July–September, October–December), and Annual the last two calendar years, each plus every period of that length already filed. If the brewery has a **Books opened on** date in [Settings](#settings), periods that start before it are left out of the list. When that leaves a tab with no period, the tab says so, names the books-opened date, and names the first period MGR can report, which appears once it starts. Opening a period that starts before the books opened shows **Before the books opened** with the date instead of a report; Admin gets an **Open Settings** button, and Sales, who cannot open Settings, is asked to have an admin change the date; a period already filed still shows its snapshot. A period marked **Not filed · Ready to review** opens for review; the current period is marked **In progress · File after the month ends** (or quarter, or year) and can be reviewed but not recorded as filed until its last day has passed. A filed period shows **Filed** with its filing date and taxpaid barrels, or **Needs amendment** after a basis correction. Below the periods sit the **Licenses** link and, under **Lot trace**, every packaged lot. MGR reports TTB periods here; other jurisdictions and date ranges are available through the API. A TTB filing always covers one whole calendar month, quarter, or year; any other TTB range, such as February 1 through April 30 or a partial month, can be reviewed but not recorded as filed.

Destination-state totals show **Removed (bbl)**, **Returned (bbl)**, signed
**Adjustments (bbl)**, and **Net (bbl)** alongside invoiced beer **Sales**,
**Credits**, and **Net sales**. **Net (bbl)** is **Removed** less
**Returned**, plus signed **Adjustments**; **Net sales** is **Sales** less
**Credits**. Deposits are excluded from beer sales. Movement dates and invoice dates remain separate.
**Export state transactions** downloads the exact transactions, source IDs,
bbl, and cents behind those totals. Returns retain the original shipment state;
later address edits do not reassign sales. Transactions without a recorded destination (shipments, returns,
adjustments, invoices, or credits) appear under **Unassigned** for review. MGR does not infer their state. Export
sales, and returns or credits against them, are not flagged Unassigned just
because they have no US destination.
Saved snapshots retain their own export. Older snapshots saved without these
transactions say so. State tax calculations and filing forms remain external.

<Screen name="Monthly compliance" />

A period is generated from the inventory ledger when you open it, so nothing is stored until you record the filing. Each finished-goods movement counts on its effective date: **Packaged on** for a packaging close, otherwise the day it was recorded. Historical packaging closes keep their original recorded day, so previously reported periods do not change. Cellar losses count on the day they were recorded. The table shows each package class (**Kegs**, **Cans**, **Bottles**) in barrels: **Begin** + **In** − **Out** = **End**, and every class must balance. Figures print to two decimals; the printed cells always add up and a period's printed end is always the next period's printed begin, so an **In** or **Out** cell can differ by 0.01 from its movements rounded on their own. Zeros print 0.00. Beer in process is what the tanks hold now, not at the period end. Below the table, **Packaged** is production into finished goods, then each removal class (taxpaid, export, samples, losses, and so on) by the tax treatment recorded when the beer moved, so editing a sale channel or format later does not change historical classification, and taxpaid removals by destination state. The removal classes are printed so they add up to the printed **Out** plus any cellar removals, so one class can differ by 0.01 from its own movements rounded alone.

**Review cellar losses** lists each generic loss posted in the period: a batch's completion loss and each cellar transfer loss, labelled **completion loss** or **transfer loss** after the batch number. A batch can have several. Each row shows the barrels recorded, any earlier reattributions, and the exact barrels left to reattribute. Admin and Sales can reattribute part of the remainder to **Sample**, **Taproom**, or **Destruction**. Samples require the two-letter destination state. Taproom freezes the current Taproom channel tax treatment even if its display name changes. Taproom volume is a removal for consumption on premises. It counts once under that treatment (**Taxpaid removals** for the default Taproom channel), appears in the Cellar · Taproom breakdown, and files like any other removal. Keep an uncertain form open: the amount, target, state, and request stay frozen until **Retry unchanged reattribution** recovers the saved result. The original remains in history and total volume does not change. The separate **Cellar removals breakdown** is explanatory and is already included once in the removal totals; never add it again. **Loss (bbl)** entered on a cellar transfer is a generic loss in the period of the transfer: it counts once in the loss removal, appears in this review, and can be reattributed like a completion loss. Corrections post in the period when they are saved and never rewrite an earlier filed snapshot.

**MGR does not file with TTB or the state**, and step 3, **Record the filing**, says so above the button. File your report there yourself, for example on Pay.gov, then record it here. **Save filed snapshot** asks you to confirm, since a period is filed once, then records the filing in MGR: it stores the figures as they stand, with an optional note, and marks the period filed. Reopening it shows the snapshot rather than regenerating. While the report does not balance, the button is off and the line beside it names each package class that does not balance and any movement type with no report column, in the same words as the notes under step 2. A note about transactions with no recorded destination does not hold the button. Once a period is filed, step 3 says the snapshot records the figures as filed. Until the period ends, step 3 says **You can record the filing after** the period **ends** in place of the button. A month can be filed once, and a period overlapping a filed one is refused. The report itself is a fixed shape, so a movement recorded later into a filed month appears in the next generation of a *different* month, never in the snapshot. A packaging close cannot be dated into a filed month.

<Screen name="Report does not balance" />

Saving the first filed snapshot locks the ordinary **Books opened on** field, including an empty value. If that basis is wrong, Admin opens **Settings → Correct reporting basis**, enters the corrected date (or clears it to count all history), and supplies a **Correction reason**. Turn on **I understand affected filed periods need amendment outside MGR**, then select **Record correction and flag amendments**. MGR records the old and new basis, the actor and time, and the reason permanently. A stale form is refused: refresh and review the current basis before trying again. If the response is uncertain, the fields freeze; **Retry unchanged correction** recovers the original request rather than creating another correction.

Every affected filed period is marked **Needs amendment** in Compliance, including later periods whose opening balances or destination-state facts change and periods that start before the corrected date. Unaffected periods are not newly flagged. Open a flagged period to compare its unchanged original filed snapshot with the corrected report figures recorded at each correction, newest first. Each corrected report retains its own destination-state totals and supporting export. The history identifies the old and new basis, actor, time and reason. A period starting before the corrected date cannot be generated under that basis; consult your compliance owner about its external filing. File any amended TTB or state report outside MGR. ## Keg fleet [#kegs]

<Screen name="Keg fleet" />

**Kegs** (Beer tab; Warehouse and Admin) is the brewery's returnable keg fleet: pools and where their kegs are. One-way kegs are materials and do not appear here. The page shows **No keg pools yet** until you add one.

### Keg pools

A pool is one group of kegs you count together: **Owned**, **Leased**, or **Pay per fill**. **Add keg pool** takes the name, the kind, the vendor for a leased or pay-per-fill pool, the per-fill cost, and the deposit charged per keg. Under each money field the form says which pools it applies to. **Per-fill cost** is required for a **Pay per fill** pool. Leave it blank for an **Owned** or **Leased** pool. A blank cost stays blank and is not saved as $0.00. **Deposit per keg** is charged on wholesale orders for each keg from the pool; it starts at 0.00, which means no deposit. **Edit** on a pool row changes its name, vendor, costs, or deposit, or turns **In service** off to take it out of service; a pool out of service accepts no more events. An owned pool has no vendor, so its Vendor row reads **None · the brewery owns these kegs**. The kind cannot change after creation, and a per-fill cost or vendor once set can be changed but not removed; on **Edit**, the per-fill help says so.

### Record a keg event

**Record keg event** posts one line to the keg ledger. Its **Record keg event** button stays at the bottom of the form while the fields scroll. Choose the pool, the size, the reason (**Acquired**, **Retired**, **Shipped**, **Returned**, **Lost**, or **Found**), the location and bin the kegs are at (the first bin is preselected), and how many kegs. The **Reason** picker says under it what the chosen reason does to the kegs:

- **Acquired** and **Retired** add kegs to or remove them from the fleet at that bin. No customer.
- **Shipped** and **Returned** move empty kegs to or from a customer; the customer is required. Shipped names the bin they left from, Returned the bin they came back into. The fleet total does not change.
- **Lost** removes kegs from the fleet. Without a customer they come out of that bin; name the customer when they were lost out at a customer, and they come off that customer's balance instead (they already left the bin when shipped). **Found** adds kegs back to a bin and never names a customer: a keg found at a customer is a shipped keg.

Shipping returnable keg beer posts **Shipped** automatically; full kegs returned with their beer post **Returned** automatically. The physical pool, size, and quantity are saved when shipped, including zero-deposit kegs. Later catalog edits do not change the returned shell’s pool or rewrite the deposit charge. Later empty-keg pickups and deposit-only refunds remain separate. If recorded shells are short, shipping still succeeds and Shipment done names the pool, size, and location that is short, for example *Owned ½ bbl · Warehouse: 2 kegs short*. Keg fleet shows the same warning for each pool, size, and location below zero; record Acquired kegs to reconcile.

For manual keg events, the quantity is always positive; the reason is the direction. Retired, shipped, and lost at a bin cannot exceed what the bin holds; returned and lost at a customer cannot exceed what that customer holds of that pool and size. A typo is refused rather than driving a bin or a customer negative. A **Note** is optional and shows next to the event in Keg event history. Kegs moving between bins or locations are recorded through **Transfers** or a bin move, not here; both refuse to take out more kegs than the source bin holds. Empty kegs only: beer coming back with a keg, and its credit, is [Return shipment](#invoices). A keg deposit refund is a separate credit memo: open the invoice that charged the deposit, select **Return**, and enter the kegs on its deposit line.

**Record keg event** needs at least one location and one pool in service. Until then the page shows **Record keg event** as unavailable and says what is missing, such as *Recording a keg event needs a keg pool in service.* Take every pool out of service and the same message returns until you add one or turn one back on.

Each pool row shows the kegs each bin holds per size, so 36 in the taproom and 40 in storage read as two rows; kegs out at customers have left their bin and are listed under **Customer keg balance** for each customer holding some. The fleet includes every bin balance and sums each customer's holdings across all pools and sizes, even for a large fleet. The page hides bin balances of zero and customers with nothing out. If holdings change while loading, reload rather than relying on partial totals.

### Keg report

<Screen name="Keg report" />

**Keg report** (from Keg fleet; Warehouse and Admin) shows whether the fleet is working or sitting and who to chase. The headline is kegs out at customers over the fleet total (acquired or found, minus retired or lost), then the same split per pool and size. The age table groups unreturned kegs into 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, and over 90 days with the deposit at the pool's rate for each bucket. The ledger counts kegs rather than serials, so a return or a loss closes the oldest open shipment first; a keg's age is the shipment it is still attributed to. Each customer holding kegs is listed with how many are over 90 days and their oldest open shipment; select the row to open that customer's keg balance. Under **Deposit mismatches**, each customer, pool, and size where a deposit was refunded and the kegs on deposit differ from the kegs out plus the kegs lost at that customer is listed and flagged; select the row to open that customer's balance. With no keg pool in service the page says **No keg pools**; with a pool but no kegs recorded as acquired yet it says **No kegs recorded yet** and asks you to record kegs acquired on Keg fleet. Both offer **Open Keg fleet**, where you add a pool or record kegs acquired.

<Screen name="Customer keg balance" />

**Customer keg balance** lists, for one customer, the kegs out per pool and size and the deposit held for them: deposits invoiced less deposits refunded on credit memos (zero when no deposit was ever invoiced). An invoice or credit memo that is **Written off**, or voided or deleted in QuickBooks, no longer counts, and neither does a refund of a deposit whose invoice no longer counts. Kegs out is shipped minus returned minus lost. Refunding a deposit alone and recording the empty keg's **Returned** event are separate steps, so the two can disagree. Once a deposit on a row has been refunded, the row is flagged when the kegs on deposit differ from the kegs out plus the kegs lost at that customer, and reads, for example, **4 out · 3 on deposit**. A lost keg keeps its deposit, so it is not a mismatch. Shipping charges the deposit and posts its **Shipped** event. A row with no refund is never flagged as a deposit mismatch. A deposit still held with no keg out is listed too. Record the missing keg event in Keg fleet, or refund the deposit with **Return** on the invoice that charged it, and the flag clears. It shows **No kegs currently out** once everything is back.

<Screen name="Keg event history" />

**Keg event history** is the ledger newest first. Narrow it with **Keg pool** and **Customer**; **Customer** opens a search and lists only customers with a keg event. Each has an **All** choice, and the two combine. A combination that finds nothing shows **No matching events**. With no filter set, an empty ledger shows **No keg events yet**. The choice stays in the page address, so a link, a refresh, or Back keeps it. Nothing here can be edited; a wrong entry is corrected by recording the opposite event.

current recipe and make changes. Admin and Brewer can open current and older
versions only within their brewery. A `?v=` number that is not a saved version opens the not-found page.

**New recipe version** sits on the title row. It opens a draft that starts as a copy of the current version: change what you need and save. The process numbers sit inline: **Pre-boil volume**, **Boil time**, **Whirlpool** minutes, temperature and rest, **Knockout temp**, **Brewhouse efficiency** and **Yeast attenuation** (both as percents). Boil and whirlpool minutes are whole numbers, and a pre-boil volume, when entered, is above zero; the form refuses anything else before saving. Ingredients are listed on the draft itself: the **Add ingredient** row opens a sheet to add one, and each ingredient's **Edit** opens it to change or delete. With no materials yet, the sheet's **Material** picker says **No materials yet.** with **Add a material**, which opens **Materials**. In that sheet, **Material** is a search: type part of a name, and each choice reads its name and category. The quantity label names the material's unit, as in *Quantity per bbl (lb)*. Three rows each open a sheet: **Mash schedule** (ordered rests, each a type, a name, a temperature and minutes; the longest rest between 144 and 162 °F is recorded as the version's mash temperature; the sheet names it, as in *The 152 °F rest is recorded as the mash temperature.*), **Fermentation schedule** (stages with a temperature and days), and **Water** (the source profile, a target profile from Water profiles, mash and sparge volumes, target mash pH, and salts or acids each with an amount, a unit and one stage: *Mash*, *Sparge* or *Kettle*). Water with no source picked reads **No source**. In a sheet, tap a step, stage or addition to edit or delete it, and use its up and down arrows to move it. You enter every addition and its amount yourself. **Suggest additions** and the ion read-out are shown as unavailable: salt suggestions are not part of this release. Nothing is written until **Create recipe version**. While the button is disabled, a line above it names what the draft still lacks, such as *Add a mash step.*

A draft you leave unsaved is kept in that browser tab. Coming back to it shows *Restored your unsaved draft* with **Start over**, which returns to a fresh copy of the current version. If someone saved a newer version meanwhile, the notice says your draft is based on an earlier one. Saving clears the kept draft. A new recipe's first draft is not kept.

*Admin and Brewer.*

Open **Recipes** from the **More** navigation to see every recipe, alphabetical, titled with its current version (*Hazy IPA v4*) and the brand it brews. A recipe with no version yet says **No version yet** instead. On a recipe, **Brand · optional** sets or changes the brand it brews. Saves when you pick, and *Brand saved* confirms it; choose **Not decided** to clear it. Batches already scheduled keep the brand they were given. Select **New recipe** to open a recipe with no version yet: a **Name**, an optional **Brand**, and an optional note sit above the first version's rows and fields. The form says *Pick the brand this recipe brews, or leave it undecided. Packaging always asks for the brand.* A recipe takes its style from the brand. One **Create recipe** saves the recipe and its first version and opens it. Tap anywhere on a row to open the recipe.

A recipe version is the process spec a brewer follows: its mash schedule, brewhouse efficiency and yeast attenuation, an optional boil length, and its ingredients. Each one is a material, a quantity per barrel, the stage it enters at (*Mash*, *Boil*, *Whirlpool*, *Fermentation*, *Dry hop*, *Packaging*, or *Other*), and an optional timing in minutes. A version cannot be edited once it is saved; select **New recipe version** to write the next one from a copy of it, so what a past batch was brewed from stays true.

Only mash-stage ingredients predict gravity, and only when every one of them has an **Extract potential** on its material (see [Purchasing](#purchasing)); until then no prediction is shown, rather than a misleading 0.0. In its place the page names the first ingredient that blocks it and how to fix it: set the material's **Extract potential** under Materials, then create the next version (a draft picks the new value up at once). Give a mash ingredient that adds no extract, such as rice hulls, a potential of 1.000. Quantities are converted from the material's unit (lb, kg, oz or g) to pounds per barrel; a mash ingredient kept by volume or count, such as a syrup in gallons, has no weight to convert, so no prediction is shown, and the page says which ingredient and unit. As you add ingredients, MGR previews the version's predicted OG, FG and ABV live, using the same calculation the record reads back afterward. Nothing is stored, so a later edit to a material's typed extract potential can never move an old version's prediction; a new version snapshots the value it saw at the moment it was saved. Predicted OG and FG are shown in whatever unit you chose under [Gravity unit](#gravity-units).

### Batch totals in packs

**Show totals at (bbl)** sits under **Ingredients** when the version has a mash, boil or whirlpool ingredient. Type a batch size and each of those ingredients shows its batch total. When its material has a pack size, the total also reads in packs, such as *22 bbl → 220 lb = 4 × 50 lb + 20 lb*. The pack size is the material's **Base units per purchase unit**, set under [Materials](#purchasing) (for a 55 lb sack: Purchase unit *each*, Base units per purchase unit *55*). A material with no pack size, or a total under one pack, shows just the amount. The batch size is for this page only: it isn't saved and doesn't change the recipe. Until you type one, the page says *Enter a batch size to see totals in packs.*

A total that already comes out in whole packs shows **Whole packs**. In a draft, any other total offers **Round to N × size** down and up, such as **Round to 4 × 50 lb** and **Round to 5 × 50 lb**. A total under one pack offers only **Round to 1 × 50 lb**. Choosing one rewrites that ingredient's per-barrel amount so the total at that batch size is exactly that many packs. It's a draft edit like any other: nothing is saved until **Create recipe version**. A saved version shows the totals but offers no rounding.

On a wide screen each group is a table; on a phone each batch is a short list of labeled facts. Work tabs show only areas your role may open. Only history closed before the brewery's **Books opened on** date (see [Settings](#settings)) can be recorded this way, so these batches never appear on the Cellar map, on Today, or in a report. They carry their knockout, no loss written off at completion, and their packaging as closed runs and lots with the quantities filled. That packaging put no stock on hand: these lots appear in **Packaging** history and in a lot trace, which names the batch, but never in inventory, on an order, or in a report. They cannot be corrected with **Correct turn**; fix them by importing again.

Select a vessel's name, or **New vessel**, to open the same full-page **Vessel detail** as Cellar. It takes a **Name**, a **Type** (**Fermenter**, **Brite**, **Barrel**, **Kettle**, or **Other**), and a **Capacity (bbl)**. A new vessel's page shows only these facts; once the vessel is saved, its Vessel detail also shows its occupancy and readings. The capacity must be above zero: once you leave the field, a capacity of 0 or less is marked under it with *Enter a capacity above zero.* Until the vessel has a name and a capacity above zero, **Save vessel** stays unavailable and the line beside it says what is still needed. **Save vessel** returns to Cellar. A vessel's contents are never entered directly: they always derive from its open occupancy.

Only an admin adds, edits, or deletes a vessel. **New vessel**, **Add vessel**, **Save vessel**, and **Delete vessel** appear only for an admin. A brewer opens Vessel detail to see the occupancy and readings, with the name, type, and capacity shown as text.

**Delete vessel** asks you to confirm first. A vessel holding beer cannot be deleted: transfer the beer out or complete its batch first. A vessel that has held beer is retired instead of deleted. A retired vessel leaves the Cellar map and is no longer offered for a transfer or a brew day. MGR refuses any beer into it, with the message *vessel is retired*. Its batch history keeps it, and the **Vessels** table lists it as **Inactive**. Its Vessel detail says it is retired and has no **Delete vessel**. Its name is free again, so a replacement tank can take the same name. A vessel that never held beer is deleted.

### Prep: pull the ingredients [#prep]

<Screen name="Brew prep" />

A planned batch has two tabs: **Prep** and **Brew day**. Prep is the pull list you work through before the brew. Brew day is where you log the brew and record it. The batch opens on **Brew day** once Prep has saved pulls, and on **Prep** until then. Select a tab to switch.

Both tabs sit under the batch title, followed by one **Planned** line: the planned barrels, date and note. **Reschedule or cancel** at the bottom of either tab opens **Reschedule** and **Cancel plan**.

Prep works like the order [pick sheet](#pick-sheet). Lines are grouped under **Mash**, **Boil** and **Whirlpool**, the stages that use them. Each line starts at the recipe plan for the batch's planned barrels. The amount reads as whole packs plus loose pounds, such as `4 × 50 lb + 20 lb`. A pack is the material's purchase unit, and its size is the material's **Base units per purchase unit**, set under [Materials](#purchasing). A material with no pack size is counted in its base unit alone.

Touch only the exceptions. Each line has a packs field and, for a material with a pack size, a loose field beside it. A line planned at less than one pack shows only its loose field. Type what you actually pulled. Packs take decimals, so `4.4` sacks of 50 lb is 220 lb. A partly used bag is just loose pounds: MGR doesn't track open bags. A blank field counts as zero. Under each line's name you see its plan, its lot and its bin, such as *plan 220 lb = 4 × 50 lb + 20 lb · C-2 · Malt room / A2*. An untracked material reads *untracked* in place of a lot.

A line is amber while it's short (pulled less than plan) or has no source chosen.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Short** | Shows on a line pulled below plan. It jumps to that line's first amount field so you can enter what you really pulled. |
| **Choose lot** | Shows on a line with no source. It opens **Source**, this material's stock by lot, location and bin with the amount available, such as *C-2 · Malt room / A2 · 1000 lb available*. Pick the exact lot and bin you're pulling from. It's disabled when the material has no stock anywhere. |
| **Vessel** | The tank you plan to knock out into. Optional. Brew day's Knockout starts from it. Retired tanks aren't offered, and a tank retired after you saved Prep shows as not chosen. |
| **Later, in cellar** | Counts the recipe's additions you don't pull for brew day, such as *Fermentation 1 · Dry hop 6*, or says *None*. Add those in [Cellar](#cellar) when the time comes. |
| **Print pull list** | Prints the page so you can pull on paper. |
| **Done pulling** | Saves the list and opens the Brew day tab. On a phone it stays at the bottom of the screen, beside **Print pull list**. |

A material held in exactly one bin starts with that source already chosen. A line that already has a source has no **Choose lot** on Prep. To pull it from a different lot or bin, use **Edit** on the Brew day tab.

**Done pulling** saves each line's lot, bin and pulled amount, plus the vessel. It moves no stock and reserves nothing, so you can pull the day before. Saving again replaces the whole list. A line pulled to zero with no source is left off it, and the Brew day tab starts that line at zero. **Done pulling** stays disabled while a line is pulled above zero with no source, or while a packs or loose amount isn't a number at zero or above.

To fix a saved pull's amount, change it and select **Done pulling** again. To change a saved lot or bin, use **Edit** on the Brew day tab. Nothing on Prep touches inventory, so there's nothing to reverse.

If saving is refused, the message shows above **Done pulling** and your entries stay. MGR refuses Prep for a batch that's already brewed (*batch 202 was already brewed on 2026-10-22*) or cancelled (*batch is cancelled*); there's nothing left to prepare, so go back to **Batches**. It refuses a retired tank: *vessel FV-3 is retired; choose another tank*. It also refuses a lot on a material that isn't lot-tracked, or no lot on one that is: *choose a lot exactly when the material is lot-tracked*.

### Brew day: log and record the brew [#brew-day]

<Screen name="Brew day timeline" />

Brew day runs in process order: **Mash**, **Lauter**, **Boil**, **Whirlpool**, **Knockout**. Each stage heading shows how many of its ingredients you've confirmed, such as *2 of 4 confirmed*, and the recipe's target for it, such as *152 °F · 60 min · pH 5.3* for Mash. A stage the recipe sets nothing for shows no target. On a phone one stage is open at a time: the first one with an ingredient you haven't confirmed. Tap another stage's heading to open it. On a wider screen every stage shows.

Each ingredient sits in the stage the recipe gives it, so there's no stage to choose. Lines start from the saved Prep pulls. A line Prep left off starts at zero. Before Prep is saved, every line starts at plan. Either way, its source is already chosen when the material sits in only one bin. A short line reads *pulled …* before its plan and is amber until you confirm it.

- **Confirm all**, at the top, confirms every line at the amount and source it shows. Use it when the pull went to plan, then **Edit** any exception.
- **Confirm** marks the ingredient as in the brew. It turns to **Pulled** and the line goes green. A confirmed line stays amber while it's pulled above zero with no source, or pulls more than its lot and bin hold. Tap **Pulled** to undo it.
- **Edit** opens the same packs and loose fields as Prep, plus **Source** when the material has stock to pick from. Change the amount or the exact lot and bin there.

Process readings sit in their stage. All are optional, and a blank one stays unrecorded. Minutes are whole numbers.

| Stage | Readings |
| --- | --- |
| Mash | **Mash water gallons**, **Mash pH** |
| Lauter | **Sparge water gallons**, **Pre-boil barrels** |
| Boil | **Boil minutes** |
| Whirlpool | **Whirlpool °F**, **Whirlpool minutes**, **Whirlpool rest minutes** |
| Knockout | **Knockout °F** |

Knockout holds the facts you only know at the end of the brew. **Vessel** is the tank it knocked out into, and starts at the tank chosen on Prep. **Knockout barrels** must be above zero, and starts at the planned barrels. **Brewed on** starts at today. Once all three are set, Knockout shows the loss baseline, such as *Knockout 22 bbl → FV-3*. Until then it reads *Choose a vessel, positive barrels and a brew date*.

When nothing is pulled above zero, **Confirm no ingredients used** appears below the stages. Switch it on to record a brew with no ingredients.

**Record brew day** stays disabled until:

1. every ingredient is confirmed;
2. every amount is a number at zero or above, and every line pulled above zero has a source;
3. the lines pulled from each lot and bin together fit what it holds;
4. something is pulled, or **Confirm no ingredients used** is on;
5. Knockout has a vessel, barrels above zero and a brew date.

On a phone, **Edit** and **Confirm** sit on their own line under each ingredient, and **Record brew day** stays at the bottom of the screen.

**Record brew day** is the step that uses stock. In one step it freezes the plan and actuals, takes the confirmed amounts out of their lots and bins, stamps the brew date and opens the vessel's occupancy. Insufficient stock refuses the whole action. It is refused if the vessel is already occupied, including a backdated day that falls inside a stretch the vessel was full but has since been emptied. A vessel emptied earlier the same day (in the brewery's time zone) can be brewed into with that day's date: the new occupancy starts when the vessel was emptied, not at midnight. It is also refused when the knockout barrels exceed the vessel's **Capacity (bbl)**; the message names the vessel and what it holds.

Confirmations and edits on Brew day aren't saved until you record. Leave the page and they start over from the saved Prep pulls. Changing an amount here doesn't change the Prep list.

Brew day has no **Add ingredient**, and you can't swap in a different material. The recipe's planned water additions are listed under **Mash** as a reminder, such as **Gypsum** with *40 g planned · add with Correct turn after recording*, but they aren't recorded with the brew. Record the brew, then open **Correct turn** (below) on that turn and use its **Add ingredient** to add them.

### After the brew [#after-the-brew]

<Screen name="Brew day" />

A brewed batch's page shows its current open vessel and total confirmed knockout. **Add brew turn** records another brew of the same batch into that tank, with its own actual ingredients, process and date. The first batch brew date stays unchanged. The remaining recipe plan prefills the next turn; confirm the actual quantities. Another turn is refused after a transfer, started packaging, closure, a dependent filed report, a date before the first turn, or a capacity excess. Normal later additions do not prevent another turn. The frozen record retains its original knockout, sources and process observations even after the occupancy closes or master data changes. Older batches without a frozen record explicitly say it is unavailable. **Correct turn** selects the current revision of that turn, requires a reason and appends a revision, exact material reversals and any knockout measurement delta. It never changes the original record. Corrections are refused after later additions, transfers, started packaging, closure or a dependent filed report.

**Add brew turn** and **Correct turn** use the older one-page brew form, with a row per ingredient rather than the timeline. Confirm each **Material source**, **Stage**, and **Actual quantity** before recording. Choose the exact lot and bin, and enter stock units for water additions rather than assuming a conversion. Use **Add ingredient** for substitutions or other actual use, and **Remove ingredient** to drop a line. If none were used, remove the rows and select **Confirm no ingredients used**. Optional process observations record measured values; blanks stay absent. A new turn goes into the same tank, so its **Vessel** is fixed. A correction also fixes **Brewed on** and asks for a **Correction reason**.

The gravity field is labelled and read in whatever unit you chose under [Gravity unit](#gravity-units). Changing the unit never changes a saved reading. If what you type there is not a gravity MGR can read, the field says so and **Save reading** stays disabled until you correct it or clear the field. A mistyped gravity is never quietly dropped from an otherwise saved reading.

## Packaging [#packaging]

Closing a run freezes package cost from its batch ingredient receipts divided by knockout volume (as corrected on the brew record), charged by barrels drawn, plus its standard packaging BOM at the used source receipt costs. Draw loss is absorbed; packaging material loss is not. Later brew, packaging-material, or receipt corrections do not change that cost. Later ingredient additions affect only later closes. A blend receiver currently includes only its own ingredients and knockout; incoming beer cost is not apportioned. Historical runs are not backfilled, and repack children without original output costs remain uncosted.

Open an unstarted run to **Reschedule** its planned date or **Cancel plan** and confirm. Admin, Brewer and Warehouse can change these plans. Cancellation retains the run and planned outputs in history, removes their material and beer demand, and stops the run from blocking batch completion. It moves no beer or stock. Started or closed runs cannot be cancelled or rescheduled. An active old plan continues to count as demand until it is cancelled.

MGR refuses insufficient exact-source stock and never silently substitutes another lot. **Review current material plan** refreshes the BOM revision and balances while retaining entered actuals. If the BOM changed before closing, review it again. The close freezes the reviewed BOM, material names and units, source identities and actual quantities with the output lot in one transaction. MGR records **Barrels drawn** rounded to three decimal places. A close whose packages exceed that recorded draw by more than 0.0005 bbl is refused; check the drawn amount and package counts. Equality and differences up to that rounding tolerance are allowed. A refusal leaves the run open and records no lot, finished stock or material consumption. A close whose packages add up to more beer than the tank held is also refused, and so is one that brings the batch's packaged total, across all its runs, past what the batch had after recorded losses; the message gives both figures, so check the counts. A closed run cannot be reopened. The tank itself stays open afterward; emptying it is a separate Cellar transfer, which MGR accepts only once no started run still draws from that tank.

A closed run shows its committed **Lot**, **Packaged on**, **Barrels drawn**, **Actual outputs**, and **Material record history**. Actual quantities come from the saved close, not the plan; a missing recorded value says **Not recorded**. **Correct material actuals** requires a reason and appends a replacement record with exact reversals of the previous usage and loss. It preserves the original record, finished output quantities and tank draw. Correction is refused after finished goods are sold, transferred, repacked or adjusted, after batch completion, or when a filed report depends on the run.

If a close or material correction response is uncertain, its exact input and request ID stay saved in this browser tab across navigation and reload. **Unresolved requests → Saved request** shows the original input. Use **Retry saved request** to recover it, even if the run now shows closed; retry never creates a second close or correction. The open form also offers an unchanged retry. Both close and material correction stay frozen for that run until recovery or deliberate discard; other runs remain available. A changed material plan definitively refused on the first send permits review and a corrected new close, but a later refusal cannot prove an earlier uncertain send did nothing.

Before **Discard saved request → Confirm discard**, check the closed run's lot, actual outputs and material history. Discard only removes this tab's retry; it sends nothing and does not undo saved work. Recovery belongs to the original signed-in person and brewery, and sends nothing automatically. Closing the browser tab clears its recovery storage. These packaging actions are not queued for offline use.

**Repack** also opens directly from `/packaging/repack` (which redirects to `/packaging?repack=1`). The form keeps **Confirm repack** disabled until its required selections and quantity are filled, and names what is missing beside it. When no SKU can be broken, **Break** says a SKU breaks when its format is made of another format. For Admin it links to **Catalog**, where a format's contents are set; for Warehouse it says an admin or sales member sets them there. A chosen location with no bins says so under **Bin** and links to **Locations**.

**Repack** (Admin and Warehouse only; a Brewer does not see this button), next to Schedule run, breaks a composed unit (a case) into the units it is made of at one location and bin. It is a shape change, never a production or a removal. Choose the **Package to break down** (only composed SKUs are offered), the **Location** and **Bin**, the **Lot** the cases come out of, and the **Packages to repack**. **Lot** lists that SKU's lots in the bin with how many each holds; **Untracked stock** is stock recorded without a lot, and the note under **Lot** says so. It is not a new lot, and it cannot stand in for lotted stock. When the bin holds only one lot of that SKU and no untracked units, that lot is already chosen. The broken units leave that lot, and the units they break into keep it, so the lot's trace still reaches them. A bin holding lotted stock refuses a repack with no lot chosen (**choose the recorded lot for this removal**), and a repack of more than the lot holds is refused with **insufficient selected bin and lot stock**. The "into" side is not typed: the component SKU and its count are derived from the case format's composition, and the tape shows both legs and the barrels they conserve before you **Confirm repack**. Repack doesn't record damage, and the sheet says so: the count it adds always comes from the composition. The case format's packaging comes off or goes back on the same bin in whole units for a counted material: what is used up rounds up and what returns to stock rounds down. A repack that uses up more packaging than the bin holds is refused with **insufficient selected bin and lot stock**, and nothing is recorded. A composed SKU whose format has no single component record cannot be broken and says so in place of the button.

## Purchasing: vendors, materials, and purchase orders [#purchasing]

<Screen name="Purchase orders" />

Purchasing is four pages. **Vendors** and **Materials** hold the definitions, **Purchase orders** (Work tab) is where an order lives from draft to received, and **Planning** shows what committed work is short of and drafts the orders to cover it.

### Vendors and contracts

<Screen name="Vendors" />
<Screen name="Contracts" />

**Vendors** (More tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists your suppliers. **Add vendor** and **Edit** take a name, optional email and phone (7 to 15 digits; spaces, dots, dashes and parentheses are fine), payment terms (**Due on receipt**, **Net 15**, or **Net 30**, the default; the same list customers use), and a **Lead time** in days. The lead time is the vendor's, not the material's. When Planning lists a material short and it resolves to this vendor, its buy-by date is the needed-by date less this lead time. Once a vendor has received orders, the row also shows the observed average from sent to last receipt, with how many orders it rests on (n=3 is weak evidence) and how it was sent, because a date someone typed for an order phoned in is not the same evidence as one MGR stamped.

Above the list, **Search vendors** finds vendors whose name contains what you type (press Enter), and **Contracts** below then lists only those vendors' contracts; **No vendors match** means nothing does, and **Clear search** below the box shows every vendor again.

Below the vendors, **Contracts** lists commitments: one vendor, one material, a quantity in purchase units, an optional price, window, and **Contract number**. A contract with a number shows it as the row's label; without one, the row falls back to the material name. A contract is not an order. You order against it all year, so each row shows four numbers: **committed**, **received**, **on order** (placed but not yet arrived), and **available**, which is committed less both. Available is the one to decide against; received is the one accounting reconciles. A contract never stops you ordering: exceed it and the extra lines are ordinary spot lines.

**Add contract** needs a vendor and a material to exist first. With no vendors yet, the form's **Vendor** picker says **No vendors yet. Close this form and choose Add vendor.** With no materials yet, its **Material** picker says **No materials yet.** with **Add a material**, which opens **Materials**.

### Materials

<Screen name="Materials on hand" />

**Materials on hand** (Beer tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) shows quantities summed across every bin, including inactive materials, marked **inactive**. With no materials yet, the list says to add one first, then receive a purchase order to put it on hand. A material that isn't lot-tracked can also be counted in. A lot-tracked material comes on hand only by receiving a purchase order, because a count needs an existing lot. Each row offers **Edit**, which changes the material itself, beside **Count**, which records what is on hand at one bin. **Add material** and **Edit** take a name, a kind (malt, hop, yeast, adjunct, chemical, packaging, other), **Base unit** (what recipes and counts use), **Purchase unit** (how you order it) and **Base units per purchase unit**. For hops bought in 44 lb boxes, set Base unit to *lb*, Purchase unit to *each*, and Base units per purchase unit to *44*. The form also takes whether it is **lot-tracked**, an optional **default vendor**, for malt and adjunct an optional **Extract potential** (specific-gravity style between 1.000 and 1.050, so 1.037 for a malt of 37 points per pound per gallon; recipe predictions read it, and leaving it blank on an edit keeps the one saved), and whether it is **Active** (available to recipes and purchase orders; on by default). Units are refused once anything stores a quantity of the material: movements, a recipe version's ingredients, a format's packaging materials, a purchase order line, a contract, or a transfer line. Each of those was written in the units the material had then, and a recipe's predicted gravity would otherwise change under it. A lot-tracked material asks for a lot code at every receipt of a counted quantity; an untracked one never does. **Lot-tracked** cannot be turned on while the material has stock recorded without a lot: saving says "material has stock without a lot: count it to zero before turning on lot tracking". Count that stock to zero at each bin first, then turn tracking on.

Above the list, **Search materials** finds materials whose name contains what you type (press Enter); **No materials match** means nothing does, and **Clear search** below the box shows every material again.

Stock totals include every recorded material-and-bin balance, even in a large inventory. If MGR cannot read the complete list, reload before using its totals. A failed stock read is not a zero balance.

**Count** on a material row opens a cycle count for one bin: pick the location and bin, type what you counted in base units, and **Record count**. The count is always recorded, even when it matches. Only the variance posts, as a count adjustment. When the material holds several lots, a shortage comes off the earliest best-by lots first and an overage lands on the newest lot; a shortage crossing two lots writes one adjustment per lot. The live preview shows the system total and variance for the selected bin. Enter base units only: automatic whole-roll conversion is not available.

### Purchase orders

Admin and Warehouse can use **Cancel purchase order** with a **Closure reason** on a draft or sent order with no receipts. After a receipt, use **Close short** with a reason when the vendor will not supply the remainder. The reason, actor, and closure time remain recorded. Closed orders leave the open Work and purchasing lists, and their unreceived remainder stops counting as on order. Existing receipts and stock remain unchanged; receipt corrections do not reopen the order. Corrected receipt expectations still use the ordered amount less other effective receipts, not the closed order's zero operational remainder. New receipts on a closed order are refused.

<Screen name="New PO" />
<Screen name="Receipt" />

**Purchase orders** (Work tab; Admin and Warehouse) lists open orders: drafts, sent, and partially received. Each open row names its next action. A received or cancelled order has none, so its row only opens it. **New PO** opens a full page with a vendor, an optional expected date, and lines: a material, a quantity in purchase units, an optional unit cost, and on a lot-tracked material an optional **Expected lot**, the lot the vendor named when you ordered. Use the quantity stepper or type a decimal quantity, and **Add line** for another material. With more than one line, **Remove** under a line drops it. Every line needs a material and a quantity above zero, and a unit cost, when entered, of zero or more. Once you leave a line's quantity or unit cost, a quantity of zero or less is marked with *Enter a quantity above zero.*, and a negative unit cost with *Enter a cost of zero or more.* **Save draft** stays unavailable until you fix or remove that line, and the line beside it names what is still missing, such as *Still needed: a vendor.* With no vendors or materials yet, the **Vendor** or material picker says so and links to **Vendors** or **Materials**. Changing a line's material clears its previous expected lot. The expected lot is advisory: it creates nothing, and receiving prefills from it. **Save draft** numbers the order PO-0001 upward and returns to the purchase-order list.

A draft's page offers **Mark sent**. Choose how it went out, **From my mail client** or **Phoned, faxed, or vendor portal**, and confirm. MGR does not send email: it records that you sent the order today. The status then reads **Marked sent** with the date, never *Delivered*, because nothing here can know that. Marking is one-way; a sent order cannot go back to draft.

A sent order's page is the receiving form. Choose the location and bin everything lands in and the received date, then for each line still due type what you **counted**. Lines default to what is still due; type over them. Over and short are both allowed and both recorded. A lot-tracked line asks for the **lot code off the package** (prefilled from the expected lot when the order named one; if the vendor shipped another lot, type what the box says) and an optional best-by date. A lot-tracked line counted 0 needs no lot code, and no lot is recorded for it. A preview shows counted base-unit quantities and over/short differences before you commit. **Recent lots** is marked unavailable; read the lot from the package rather than expecting suggestions. **Receive purchase order** writes the receipt, its lines, the lots, and receipt movements into that bin in one step; only what you counted posts to inventory. The saved **Receipt** page opens after receiving and survives refresh. It shows posted base-unit quantities, any recorded lot reference, over/short amounts, and the current **Still owed** balance across all receipts; its back link returns to that purchase order. Open an earlier receipt from the purchase order's **Receipts** list. New receipts preserve the material name, units, conversion, and lot facts recorded at receipt; older receipts explicitly mark missing snapshots. The order becomes **partially received** until every line is met, then **received**. What each line still owes is worked out from the receipts every time, so a second receipt against the same order simply closes the remainder. **Show received and cancelled** at the foot of the list reveals finished orders.

Open a saved receipt and choose **Correct receipt** (Admin or Warehouse). Enter a **Correction reason**, every corrected counted quantity, and the actual lot code and best-by date. The original receiving bin and receipt date stay fixed. The original receipt remains in history; MGR reverses its exact stock movements and appends a replacement receipt. Only the latest revision contributes to **Still owed**, so reducing a completed receipt can reopen the PO as partially received. **Original receipt** and **Corrected receipt** link the history. Correcting an older receipt does not make its cost newer than a later delivery's cost.

Correction is refused if stock in the receipt bucket has been used afterward, if changed lot facts affect downstream use or a shared lot, or if another correction already replaced the receipt. Reload to see the current revision. If another inventory operation keeps material stock busy, MGR says the stock is busy; retry the correction. Historical zero-count receipts without a frozen conversion or receiving location cannot be corrected safely; MGR explains the missing fact. A zero replacement count is allowed for a receipt whose original facts are known and posts no new positive stock movement.

### Post a receipt Bill to QuickBooks

Admin can choose **Post Bill to QuickBooks** on the latest saved receipt. MGR posts one Bill for that receipt, not the purchase order. Each positive counted line uses its recorded receipt movement's unit cost times its base-unit quantity, rounded to cents. The PO's ordered quantity and later price changes do not replace those receipt costs. A missing cost blocks posting; enter the price before receiving rather than guessing an invoice amount.

Before sending, MGR saves the exact Bill, connected company, and durable request ID. **Retry exact Bill** first searches for that original Bill after a lost or uncertain response. If it finds the Bill, it records the existing identity without posting. If there is no match, it resends the unchanged request with the same ID, so QuickBooks returns the original result rather than duplicating it. A failed or ambiguous lookup leaves the receipt pending. Only a definite QuickBooks rejection offers **Post corrected Bill** after you fix the mapping; that explicit action creates a new request and preserves the rejected snapshot. A rejected Bill never posted, so the corrected Bill goes to the currently connected company, even after a reconnect; a pending Bill stays with its original company. Changing a mapping never silently rewrites a pending Bill.

Correcting a posted receipt does not edit its Bill or create a vendor credit. The replacement receipt says **Bill posted, adjust in QuickBooks** and retains the original Bill identity. A correction while a Bill is pending also retains that frozen payable. Before any Bill attempt, post only the latest receipt revision. Record and adjust vendor payments only in QuickBooks; MGR does not sync Bill payments back.

### Planning

<Screen name="Planning" />

**Planning** (More tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists each material that planned work is short of. Demand combines unbrewed recipe ingredients, open packaging runs, and projected packaging for uncovered orders and batches with package shares. Packaging uses the format's bill of materials, rounding counted materials up per package line as a close does. Supply is on hand plus what is already on a sent purchase order, so a gap is never ordered twice. A draft is not supply until it is marked sent. Each row shows need, on hand, on order, the needed-by date, and the vendor the gap resolves to (a contract with commitment still available, else the material's default vendor). A resolved vendor with a typed lead time also shows the **buy by** date; one with no lead time typed says so instead, and the row still drafts, just with no date to judge it against. A row whose needed-by date has already passed starts with **overdue** and gets the attention dot. A row past its buy-by date is out of reach and is left out of the draft; a material with no contract and no default vendor cannot draft at all until you set one.

<Screen name="Planning empty" />

Planning lists only shortages. With nothing short it says **No material shortages**, which does not mean any batch or packaging run is scheduled. Under that sentence it links to **Batches** and **Packaging**, where that demand is planned, and to **Materials**, where each material gets the default vendor a gap needs to draft. Batches is Brewer's, so Warehouse sees only the Packaging and Materials links.

**Projected packaging** is included in the same material row, marked **includes N projected**, and drafts like firm demand. For a planned or brewing batch, MGR divides its planned volume among the brand's active SKU package shares and rounds down to whole packages. Needed-by is the brew date (the planned date until brewed) plus **Days to package** from Settings. Closed and cancelled batches project nothing. Any non-cancelled run linked to the batch directly or through its source tank replaces that batch's entire projection, not just the run's units; closing the run does not restore it. Cancelling the run restores the projection if no other non-cancelled run links the batch. A tankless run cannot identify which batch it replaces until its source tank is picked.

For orders, MGR projects only the open order quantity not covered by finished goods, open packaging runs, or projected batch units of the same SKU. Standing allocations hold stock ahead of orders but never project on their own. For example, 100 packages on hand, 50 standing and 80 ordered projects 30, while no stock, 50 standing and 10 ordered projects only 10. Needed-by is the earliest requested ship date among that SKU's open order allocations, or today if none has one. Receiving finished goods or scheduling enough run output removes the order projection; an order and the batch expected to supply it are not added twice.

**Draft N purchase orders** (Admin and Warehouse) writes one draft per vendor for every buyable row, quantities rounded up to whole purchase units, priced from the contract for what its available commitment covers and left blank beyond that. The label says how many before you commit, and the page then opens the Purchase orders list so you can review and mark them sent.

Successful saves confirm at the top right; failures remain beside the affected form. Below the forms, rows open **Locations**, **Team**, **Accounting**, **Point of sale**, **Chat**, and **Import**. **Source water** opens the existing **Water profiles** catalog to manage source and target measurements. **Point of sale** shows a truthful **Not connected**, **Connected**, or **Reconnect required** Square state. If Square is not turned on in MGR, **Connect Square** is unavailable and the page says so. Admin connects or disconnects Square, refreshes catalog and sales, and maps each observed Square location to one MGR location. Point of sale also lists **Sales coverage**, which counts only completed sync windows, and **Recent Square facts**; open one to inspect that retained sale or return and its source revisions. **Taproom sales in QuickBooks** sits here because Square, not MGR, can send taproom sales to QuickBooks through Square's own connector. It opens a sheet of the same name with an explanation and a link to MGR Accounting; it does not detect, configure, or acknowledge that connector.

**Menu** is available to Admin and Warehouse. With no mapped Square location, the page names the missing step. If Square is not connected, never or since a disconnect, it says **Square isn’t connected**: Admin gets **Set up Square**, which opens Point of sale settings, and Warehouse is asked to have an admin reconnect it. If Square is connected but no location has synced yet, it says so: Admin gets **Set up Square**, where **Sync Square catalog** fetches them, and Warehouse is asked to have an admin sync and map one. If Square locations were found but none is mapped, it says **No Square location mapped**: Admin gets **Map Square locations**, which opens Square locations, and Warehouse is asked to have an admin map one. Point of sale settings are admin-only, so Warehouse never gets a link. Pick a mapped Square location, configure its exact MGR bin and sales channel, inspect derived in-stock and off-register rows, edit a nullable location price override, choose website publication, and publish the complete location menu or one brand item. An all-out-of-stock menu remains publishable so MGR can retire its previously owned Square items, and off-register rows still open their item detail. If saving the website checkbox fails, it returns to the stored value. An unknown publication result always shows its saved attempt identity and asks you to retry that exact attempt. Only a confirmed Square success says **Published**; a rejection or supersession keeps its terminal attempt identity and offers a new corrected attempt. A superseded sync says the catalog or sales were not refreshed and starts a fresh request next time. A permission or conflict refusal asks you to resolve that condition and starts a new sync request afterward; only an uncertain transport result retries the exact request.

Admin can connect, reconnect, or disconnect; Admin and Sales can see the safe connection status needed for invoice work. An Admin who returns from QuickBooks without a brewery cookie uses their verified default brewery membership; a stale or foreign brewery cookie is refused. After code exchange, MGR makes an authenticated CompanyInfo read through the callback company's realm-scoped URL before claiming it; a denied request or invalid CompanyInfo response reports QuickBooks unavailable and stores no connection. Accounting shows the CompanyName from that read as the connected company, with its QuickBooks company ID as a separate field, and the connection status ends in **sandbox** or **production**, read from the configured QuickBooks API. A connection made before company names were stored shows **QuickBooks Online** until it is reconnected. If QuickBooks delivers the same callback twice, the second one exchanges no second code. If the first one already connected, the second reports the connection as completed instead of failed. If the first one is still connecting, the second returns to Accounting with no message. The connection status there is as it was when the page opened. Refresh the page to see whether the first one connected. A disconnect stops local work, releases the QuickBooks company for another brewery, and removes the local credential before MGR attempts remote revocation. It keeps the disconnected brewery's history and that company's customer, item, and invoice mappings so reconnecting the same verified company while it is unclaimed can resume without remapping. That reconnect preserves the logical connection identity while rotating the credential version, so an older refresh cannot overwrite the new credential. Connecting a different company creates a new identity and clears the old mappings and invoice sync fields. Historical portal quotes keep their original company identity as stale audit evidence and cannot use the replacement company's credential. One current QuickBooks company connection cannot belong to two MGR breweries. If Intuit is unavailable, health shows **Not connected** and remote revocation is unresolved. If OAuth completion does not finish, MGR reports QuickBooks unavailable and requires a fresh reconnect instead of replaying the one-time code; any credential Intuit already issued for that attempt is revoked, best effort, so an unstored connection does not stay live at QuickBooks. If MGR stops partway through a connect, a daily cleanup marks the attempt for reconnect and revokes that credential the same way. MGR skips that revoke when the connection was actually saved, or when that QuickBooks company already has a saved MGR connection for this or another brewery, because Intuit's revoke would disconnect that company's live connection.

Portal review uses QuickBooks sales tax only when the connection actually granted that scope and the customer, every ordered SKU, any keg deposit, and the shipping-source address are mapped for the same QuickBooks company. If entitlement, mapping, address, or the provider is unavailable, buyers see **Tax pending** and can still order; the later invoice remains authoritative.

Admin can connect, reconnect, inspect redacted connection health, refresh the complete location and catalog snapshot, and disconnect. The callback accepts only the initiating actor while they are still a current Admin of the selected brewery, and verifies the Square seller through its locations before storing the credential. If a code was exchanged but seller verification or local adoption fails, MGR first records a cleanup fence and then revokes only the newly issued, unadopted access token; this preserves any existing brewery connection for that seller. It records whether cleanup was confirmed or unresolved without exposing the credential or provider response, and sends no revoke if the fence could not be stored. A rejected current access or refresh authorization changes health to **Reconnect required**; a temporary Square outage leaves the connection in place for retry. Disconnect stops local work and purges the local credential before asking Square to revoke the full authorization. While that full revocation is in flight, health shows **Reconnect required** and a new Square connection cannot finish. A confirmed revoke ends **Not connected**; an unavailable revoke remains **Reconnect required** with unresolved remote cleanup, and **Disconnect Square** then says the credential is deleted but Square did not confirm revocation, never that Square is disconnected. **Retry revocation** on that page asks Square again by seller (merchant) id, since MGR no longer holds the token; a confirmed retry ends **Not connected**, and another failure stays unresolved so you can retry again, remove MGR's access in your Square account, or reconnect. A **Reconnect required** connection that still holds its credential offers **Disconnect** beside **Connect** on Point of sale; disconnecting it deletes that credential and attempts revocation the same way. Retrying the same in-progress disconnect does not send another blind provider request, and a late finish from the old request cannot change a newer credential. An older refresh or catalog response cannot replace facts after a reconnect or disconnect.

If the same Square seller is now connected to another MGR brewery, an old connection's **Retry revocation** sends no seller-wide revoke, so it cannot disconnect that brewery. The old connection keeps its unresolved cleanup status and the instruction to remove MGR's access in Square manually. Coordinate with the current brewery before removing that access: Square revocation affects the seller's full MGR authorization.

Each successful Square catalog sync finishes all provider pages before it changes availability, and advances the catalog generation atomically with the committed local snapshot. Publication cannot begin while a newer catalog fetch for the current seller is unfinished, including while that sync installs a refreshed credential; retry after it settles and publication captures the generation from the committed snapshot, so it never starts a Square write from the old rows during that gap. The arriving snapshot supersedes unresolved publication from an older view. A stale provider page cannot downgrade a newer locally recorded provider version or hide a variation published after the sync began. Only the newest sync for the captured seller and credential generation may commit. Retrying the same completed request returns its recorded result without contacting Square again. An OAuth reconnect, including to the same seller, terminally closes unfinished work from the prior credential before installing the new one; a normal token refresh keeps its active sync and advances that attempt to the refreshed credential. Catalog sync keeps new and unmapped locations and variations, and retains deleted variations and their mappings as unavailable history. Admin explicitly maps one observed Square location to one unclaimed MGR location. A location with observed POS coverage cannot be remapped in place because that would rewrite the meaning of history. Before coverage exists, remapping succeeds but explicitly clears that location's menu because its old bin cannot follow it; configure the menu again for the new location. Replacing a seller is likewise allowed only without retained sales history and terminally closes unfinished old-seller catalog work, clears every old-seller menu and current catalog ownership before the replacement can publish, and retains publication history. A late catalog response from that old seller returns its recorded superseded result and cannot write into the replacement seller. Admin and Warehouse map a variation to either the brewery's packaged SKU or a poured format on a price group, or mark it ignored, once it has sold (see **Also in Square** on Menu). A poured format names no beer, so a sale on one counts toward the beer of MGR's own Square item. For a variation MGR did not publish, it counts toward the group's only beer, or else the one beer on that group on tap at the sale's location when it was sold. When several of the group's beers are on tap, or none is, the sale stays unreconciled and adds no expected volume. Parent items and variations remain separate identities, so pint and half-pint stay distinct and guest or event items are never matched by name. Mapping changes reconcile only unresolved sales; existing expectations keep their frozen serving size. Square connection, catalog, and mapping operations never create inventory movements. The weekly physical count remains the source of depletion.

Admin can run **Sync Square sales**. The first successful run searches the seller's complete order history by updated time; later runs overlap the previous watermark by 72 hours so delayed and revised orders are seen. The start, end, location batches, and page cursor are durable: a failed page leaves the window incomplete and a retry resumes it without claiming coverage. Concurrent runs can finish in either order without moving the completed watermark backward. A completed empty window is recorded as observed for every Square location, including an unmapped location; choosing that location's MGR mapping later attaches the already observed coverage.

Each sale, return, and revision stays as an immutable source fact identified by the Square connection, order, line, kind, and order version. MGR also freezes the complete line set for that order version; if Square later returns different contents without advancing the order version, sync refuses the page and retains its prior checkpoint and coverage. The newest complete order version supplies current expected consumption while older expectations retain their original serving interpretation for history. Itemized linked returns reduce expected consumption once, up to the original line quantity; exchange sale lines add their own expectation. Custom-amount refunds, unlinked returns, measured quantities, unsupported count precision, and excess returns remain visible as unsupported facts and do not become beer volume. A sale received before its catalog entry remains queued by variation identity, and later catalog mapping reconciles it without rewriting the fact, including when that variation has since been deleted. Sales sync never creates an inventory movement or changes a physical count.

Admin and Warehouse can configure one menu for each mapped Square location by choosing that location's MGR bin and Taproom sale channel. The menu derives every brand with positive active packaged-keg stock in that exact bin, then offers the poured formats on the brand's price group. Each beer has its own row for a shared pour, so its override and website publication apply to that beer only. Stock in another bin or location never makes a row available, and hand-entered availability or Square's physical count is never used. A poured format inherits its channel price until you set a location-specific override; clear the override to follow later channel-price changes again. Each location keeps its own selection and overrides. Items MGR publishes to Square are mapped when they publish, so they need no step. A Square item MGR did not publish (a guest beer, food, merchandise, or anything made by hand in Square) appears under **Also in Square** once it has an accepted sale at any Square location, even before that location's menu is configured. Choose a SKU or pour for it and **Save mapping**, so its sales count as expected consumption, or choose **Ignore this variation**. An item that has not sold asks for nothing. A mapped or ignored item moves to **Mapped or ignored** below; pick a different choice there and select **Change mapping** to correct it or to stop ignoring it.

Website publication is explicit per menu row. The public feed returns only the location name, safe beer and format labels, serving ounces, current price, availability, and a content version for currently stocked published rows. It never returns brewery, stock, bin, channel, SKU, format, connection, or provider identifiers. The version and ETag stay fixed while that visible content is unchanged and change with its stock, price, label, or publication state. Responses use a 60-second shared-cache window with five minutes of stale-while-revalidate, so changes can remain visible in an edge cache for up to that window.

Publishing a Square menu is available to Admin and Warehouse. **Publish changes** freezes the complete derived brand change set for the selected location and reserves every brand action before its first Square request, including a prior-owned brand that now needs to leave that location. A standalone item publish cannot take one of those brand actions while the menu request is being created. Retrying that exact menu request traverses only the saved manifest, so a brand added or removed afterward cannot add, omit, or change a provider write. MGR keeps one durable Square parent item for each brand's poured menu group, with pint, taster, and other poured formats as that item's variations. Two beers on one price group each get their own item, with their own variation of each shared pour. Publishing one brand item sends that whole brand item for one Square location; adopting an existing Square item also names the observed Square parent and one mapped variation. A sales mapping alone never gives MGR permission to rename, reprice, hide, or delete that Square item or variation. An existing provider identity becomes writable only after MGR created and durably recorded it or an operator explicitly adopted the observed mapped item and variation. If that poured format already has a recorded Square variation, adoption must select that same variation; selecting a different mapped variation is refused before MGR records or sends the publish. MGR creates a new parent only when the brand has at least one currently stocked, priced format, and it never adopts or changes another seller-owned item by matching its name. A publish preserves the Square item's taxes, modifiers, category, inventory setting, unrelated seller variations, and prices at other locations. An out-of-stock format is removed only from the selected Square location and keeps the same provider identity for a later return; the parent remains visible there while any owned or unrelated variation still belongs there. When MGR's own item is left at no Square location, MGR also archives it in Square, and a later return restores that same item, so a beer keeps one Square item and one sales history. Catalog sync still treats an archived item as available. To take a beer off Square yourself, archive it in Square; never delete it, because a deleted item cannot come back and loses its link to past sales.

If Square's reply is lost, retry the unchanged publication so MGR sends the same saved request for that brand item. A changed local price does not alter that uncertain request. Before any provider write, MGR refreshes an expired Square access credential and supersedes the older saved attempt so its frozen generation cannot be reused. If Square rejects the current authorization, MGR records **Reconnect required** and terminally settles that publication; a temporary provider failure leaves the saved attempt recoverable for exact retry. If Square reports that its version changed, MGR rejects the saved attempt and requires an explicit conflict retry, which first reloads the current item before preparing a new request. When one brand in a menu has a definite rejection, MGR records the menu as rejected, preserves every completed and failed brand outcome, and releases its unfinished brand actions so a corrected menu publish can start; retrying the rejected request returns that exact result without another Square call. Temporary or unknown failures keep the saved menu recoverable instead. A newer connection credential or catalog sync supersedes an unresolved older attempt, so current work can continue and a late older reply cannot overwrite it. A definite deleted, missing, or malformed owned item or variation rejects that attempt. A disconnect or loss of the Admin or Warehouse role blocks provider access before the request is sent. If the original operator loses that role, MGR preserves their unfinished publication, marked as ended because their role changed; another current Admin or Warehouse operator can then begin a new publication, while late work from the original operator cannot change Square or local ownership.

Admin and Sales can map each customer and SKU to its ID in the connected QuickBooks company; Admin also chooses the QuickBooks item used for returnable-keg deposit charges and refunds, and the item used for one-way keg fees. An invoice with a one-way keg fee line is refused with "map the one-way keg fee item" until that item is set; reconnecting a different company clears it, like the deposit item. Pushing an invoice freezes its customer, address, email, line quantities, captured prices, exact cent amounts, payment-method defaults, mappings, company connection, request body, and Intuit request ID before contacting QuickBooks. Credit memos use QuickBooks' CreditMemo document with positive credited quantities and amounts, including keg-deposit refunds through the configured deposit item. MGR refreshes an expired access credential before sending. When several actions need a refresh at the same moment, one refreshes and the others wait for and use its result, so QuickBooks never sees two refreshes of the same credential. If QuickBooks returns unauthorized for a credential that appeared current, MGR refreshes once and retries the identical saved body and request ID. An unavailable or expired refresh credential leaves the push pending for honest recovery. If any other response is uncertain, retry the same action so MGR sends the identical saved body and request ID. Once that request has completed, retry by the same currently authorized staff member returns its durable result without contacting QuickBooks again, even if the connection, mappings, catalog, or invoice status changed later; the remote document ID already stored on the attempt remains authoritative. A disconnect blocks a pending retry until the same company is verified and reconnected; then MGR resends the frozen request through the preserved logical connection. A different-company reconnect cannot retarget or finish the old request. A rejected push remains rejected on exact replay even after its mapping changes or a later corrected attempt succeeds. It needs an explicit corrected attempt after its mapping is fixed. Recreating a document is available only after sync confirms that the prior QuickBooks document was deleted; both cases keep the prior attempt in history.

**Sync now** also checks pushed credit memos in the connected QuickBooks company. A confirmed deletion stops that credit's beer money from reducing newly generated state totals and CSV exports. A deleted keg-deposit refund also stops reducing deposits held in Customer keg balance and Keg report; the separately recorded physical keg return stays unchanged, so a mismatch may become visible. Recreating the deleted credit in QuickBooks makes its original credit money count again in current totals. Its original lines and amounts, push history, physical returns, and already filed reports stay unchanged. An unavailable or malformed QuickBooks response does not prove deletion; retry the same sync. Applying a credit or leaving it with zero balance is not cash collected and does not mark it paid or voided.

For an eligible live invoice with a current positive QuickBooks balance, the customer payment handoff reads a fresh QuickBooks link only after checking the signed-in customer, invoice, current company connection, payment settings, accounting permission, credential generation, and original pushed document. MGR checks the same connection snapshot and positive balance again after the QuickBooks read. A reconnect, credential rotation, or loss of accounting permission while the read is in flight makes that link unavailable. A zero or unknown current balance is also unavailable; a reversed payment with a new positive balance is eligible, and the next live sync clears its payment-confirmation marker. The link is never stored or shown in an MGR page; an unavailable or unverified link leaves the customer on a **Payment unavailable** page with instructions to contact the brewery.

## Sale channels [#channels]

<Screen name="Sale channels" />

<Screen name="Channel" />

*Admin only. Everyone who records or reads a movement sees the channel names; only Admin can add, rename, or remove one.*

A sale channel is a named way your brewery sells beer. Every brewery starts with four: **Wholesale**, **Taproom**, **DTC**, and **Export**. Open **Sale channels** from the **More** navigation to see each channel and its tax treatment. Only Admin adds, edits, or deletes channels; Sales and Warehouse see the list read-only, and their back link returns to **More**.

1. Select **Add channel**, enter a **Channel name**, choose a **Tax treatment**, and select **Save channel**.
2. To rename a channel or change its treatment, select **Edit** on its row. The sheet is titled with the channel's name, for example **Edit channel · DTC**. Change the fields and select **Save channel**.
3. To remove a channel, select **Delete** on its row and confirm.

The tax treatment (**Taxable**, **Export**, **Vessel supplies**, **Research**, or **Transfer in bond**) is the default for removals recorded on that channel. With **Export** selected, the sheet explains it: sold for shipment outside the US, not taxed. The treatment is separate from the channel's name, so a channel named Export can still carry any treatment. A customer may override it on their own record, and the treatment that applies is frozen onto each movement the moment it is recorded. Editing a channel later never restates a month you have already filed.

Every channel can be renamed: no name is load-bearing, because an order carries its own channel from the moment it is created.

One removal is refused. Admin sees this rule above the list, and the confirmation repeats it: A channel used by any movement, customer, order, price cell or Square menu cannot be deleted. Rename the channel instead, or clear its cells, move those customers, and pick another channel for that Square menu first.

## Gravity unit [#gravity-units]

<Screen name="Units" />

*Everyone. Only Admin sets the brewery default; the personal preference is yours alone.*

Changing your gravity unit only changes how gravity is shown and typed. Your readings are not changed.

Open **Units** from the **More** navigation. Gravity is the only unit you choose there. **Brewery default** (Admin only) is what everyone here sees unless they choose otherwise. **Your preference** is yours: choose **Plato (°P)**, **Specific gravity (SG)**, or **Use brewery default** to go back to following the brewery. **Preview** shows one reading converted into the unit you see, such as *A reading of 12.5 °P shows as 1.050 SG.* The choice applies wherever MGR shows or asks for a gravity: the fermentation reading form and log, and the predicted OG and FG on a recipe.

## Errors and corrections [#errors-corrections]

A bookmarked staff page that your role cannot read opens **No access**, names the required roles, and offers **Back to Today**. Order controls follow your role: Sales confirms, adjusts and cancels; Warehouse picks, ships and puts back; Admin can do both.

**A form reports an error.** Read the message, fix the visible fields, and submit again. Your entries stay until the form succeeds or you close it. Permission errors need an administrator. **That already exists** means another record already uses that name or value (for example, a second location with the same name, or a second SKU for the same brand and format); change it or edit the existing record. **A number is out of range** means a figure is too large or too small for its field; enter a realistic value. A number larger than MGR can store, such as a water ion above 999,999.9 ppm or a price or cost above $21,474,836.47, is refused with a message that names the field and its limit.

**MGR says there are too many requests.** Wait for the stated retry time, then retry. Ask MGR messages count toward the same limit as other actions; after the stated time, select **Try again** in the drawer. If the action's result was uncertain, use **Retry saved request** to recover the frozen input and original request ID safely. The same action on the same record waits until the original outcome is recovered or you discard the saved request.

**Your signed-in account or brewery changed while an action was pending.** MGR refuses the action before it writes anything. If the earlier response was uncertain, return to the original account and brewery and use the unchanged retry; do not start a replacement request until you have checked the record.

**A list says it "changed while loading".** A long list, such as Transfers, Packaging runs, the Pick sheet or Batches, loads in parts. If someone saves a change to it while it loads, MGR shows "… changed while loading. Reload and try again." instead of a list with rows missing or doubled. Reload the page.

**A page fails to load.** Select **Try again** once. If it still fails, give your administrator the brewery or customer name, page, approximate time, and the action you tried.

**A record seems missing.** Confirm the brewery shown in the navigation, clear any order-status filter, and check the exact brand, SKU, customer, or location name.

**A page says "Not found".** Opening an order, customer, or invoice link that matches nothing in your brewery shows a Not found page with a link back to Today, not an error. The record was removed, the link is wrong, or it belongs to a brewery you are not signed in to. Switch brewery, or ask the person who sent the link.

**Inventory is wrong.** Do a physical count and figure out what happened. Record an adjustment only for a verified count difference. Never disguise a sale, sample, loss, destruction, festival removal, return, or transfer as another type. Put a note on the correction.

**A saved Taproom count was too low.** Ask Admin to open the latest saved count and use **Correct count**. Keep the signed compensation and replacement entries with that count; do not add a generic inventory adjustment.

**A shipped order or invoice is wrong.** Do not add an unrelated inventory movement. Use **Return** on the invoice so the credit and the returned stock stay linked to the shipment history.

## Import CSV [#import]

<Screen name="Import" />

Admin opens **Settings → Import** or **Import CSV** on the setup checklist. Under **What are you importing?**, choose the kind, then upload a CSV with a header row and 1–5,000 data rows, map columns, and review the editable preview. Before you upload, the page lists the chosen kind's columns and marks each one required, optional, or required for a US address. A reference column, such as `saleChannelId`, is marked **name or ID**: it takes an existing record's name or its ID. A header named exactly like a listed column, with the same capitals, maps on its own. Map any other header in the next step. Quoted commas, newlines, escaped quotes, a quote inside an unquoted value (such as `16" keg`), blank lines, Excel's `sep=,` first line, and a leading byte-order mark are supported. Malformed quoting, duplicate or missing headers, unequal row widths, and empty files show an error before anything is staged.

The complete API request is limited to 8 MiB. Most 5,000-row files fit; files with unusually large notes or other cells may need to be split into smaller batches.

The lookup lists show each existing record's label and ID. Map these exact fields (optional fields follow the semicolon):

| Kind | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Customers | `name`, `type`, `saleChannelId`, `state` (US only); `licenseNumber`, `paymentTerms`, `country`, `alias` |
| Ship-tos | `customerId`, `label`, and for a US address `state`; `address1`, `address2`, `city`, `zip`, `country` |
| Products / SKUs | `product`, `formatId`; `sku_name`, `style`, `abv`, `upc` |
| Channel prices | `saleChannelId`, `priceGroupId`, `formatId`, `unitPriceCents` |
| Opening balances | `skuId`, `locationId`, `binId`, `qty`; `note` |

Reference cells accept an existing record's name or UUID in the same ID column, including `formatId`. Names are trimmed and matched case-insensitively and exactly. Missing or ambiguous names block the row; use the UUID when several records share a name. A bin name is looked up within the row's location, and a channel-price pour format within the row's price group. Product imports use packaged formats only. Create missing reference records first: an import does not infer or create them. Customer `type` is distributor, retailer, brewery, or other; `paymentTerms`, when given, is `due_on_receipt`, `net15`, or `net30` (an empty cell means net30); state is two uppercase letters. `country` is a two-letter country code such as JP; leave it blank for a US row. A row outside the US has no state, and a ship-to row outside the US needs only its customer and label. Prices are nonnegative whole cents. Opening quantity is a positive count of SKU units with at most two decimal places, and the bin must belong to the location. An existing product is reused without changing its metadata. Duplicate customer or SKU creation is blocked; channel prices update the existing price cell. Rows import in parallel, except where two rows could collide: rows with the same customer name, the same product, or the same price cell are applied in file order. For the same customer name or product, the earlier row wins. For the same price cell, the last row sets that cell's price. A Products / SKUs file with any UPC is applied entirely in file order. A Channel prices file is also applied entirely in file order when a reference column mixes names and UUIDs, so a name and its UUID cannot reorder writes to the same cell.

The shared preview keeps the horizontal **Upload**, **Map**, **Preview** and **Commit** steps and a compact row/record/match/state table. Expand **Edit row** to correct that row's fields on either phone or desktop, or go back to mapping. The import kind is fixed after upload; go **Back to upload** to choose another kind. **Import ready rows** (or **Post opening balances**) stays disabled if every row is invalid. Only rows the preview marks ready are sent; rows it marks blocked stay out of the batch, so fix them before importing or import them later. Each sent row commits independently, and a row the server still refuses comes back blocked with its error. Results number each row as the preview did, so skipped blocked rows leave gaps in the numbering. A failed product/SKU row leaves none of its new dependent records behind.

Keep the page open until results are recovered. If the connection fails, some rows may have committed: **Retry same batch** returns the original committed and blocked outcomes without posting opening stock twice. **Correct blocked rows in a new batch** carries only blocked rows into a fresh editable preview. Even if lookup data changes, retrying the unchanged old batch returns its original blocked result. Never upload committed opening balances as a new batch; that would add the stock again. Reloading this browser tab restores the unresolved batch and its original row numbers. Retry is always explicit. If you have checked the imported records and the batch cannot be recovered, select **Discard saved request**, then **Confirm discard**; MGR drops the saved batch without sending it and returns to the preview, or to upload after a reload. Check which rows were saved first, and never re-import committed opening balances. Closing the tab clears its recovery data.

Once results show, **Import another file** returns to upload so you can import the next kind, such as ship-tos after customers or opening balances after SKUs. It clears the finished batch, file, and mapping, and reloads the reference ID lists so records the last batch created can be used. It appears only after results are recovered; an unresolved batch must be retried or discarded first. Any blocked rows from the finished batch are left behind, so correct them with **Correct blocked rows in a new batch** before moving on.

## Closing your account [#closing]

To close a brewery account, an administrator first disconnects QuickBooks, Square and Slack in **Settings**, then asks MGR support to close it. Closing ends sign-in for every staff member and buyer and cancels open invitations. Your records are not deleted: MGR keeps them for 3 years after closing, for brewery recordkeeping. Ask for an export of your records, one spreadsheet file per kind of record, before or after closing.

## Actions not yet available [#unavailable]

Do not hunt for hidden controls for these:

- public account sign-up
- resending or revoking an invitation
- editing a purchase order after it is saved, or sending one by email from MGR
- deleting a customer that has history, a single ship-to, inventory history, orders, invoices, or credit memos
- recording a payment on an invoice QuickBooks received (record it in QuickBooks; see [Record a payment in MGR](#record-payment) for the invoices that take one here)
- adding an ingredient on a planned batch's **Brew day** tab (record the brew, then use **Correct turn**), or changing a saved or prefilled source on **Prep** (use **Edit** on Brew day)
- a printed lot trace packet
- an order-confirmation warning from licenses and registrations, or deleting an approval, registration, or license
- filing a state or other non-TTB report, or a period other than a calendar month, quarter, or year, from the interface (the API accepts any non-TTB jurisdiction and range)

Ask the person who configures your brewery when one of these is required.

This manual describes controls that exist now. If the application and this guide disagree, report the screen, action, and exact message to your MGR administrator.

## Screen walkthrough [#walkthrough]

The [screen explorer](/docs/screens-explore) is a drawing of MGR, including screens that are not available yet. Settings links to Accounting, Point of sale, and Chat for administrators. Connection and disconnection actions show their resulting screens. Chat preview tabs switch only the preview, and keep connection and delivery controls visible. Menu location tabs keep you on Menu, and each published item links to its price override sheet. Browser Back and Forward restore the selected screen and its walkthrough trail, including the page behind an open sheet. These frames do not change connected services.

### Portal fulfillment warehouse

<Screen name="Settings no warehouse" />

In **Settings**, under **Wholesale fulfillment**, Admin can see the current **Portal fulfillment warehouse**, choose a warehouse directly in Settings, and **Save warehouse**. Only locations whose **Uses** include **Warehouse** are offered. Until you choose one, **Save warehouse** says *Still needed: a warehouse.* If no location is a warehouse, the picker says so. Follow **Edit locations**, add **Warehouse** to a location's **Uses**, then come back and choose it. If the saved warehouse later loses its **Warehouse** use, the picker starts empty again. This source is used for new portal orders; other brewery settings do not change it.

For cross-location stock transfers, receiving shows the recorded lots at each source bin. Enter the quantity from each source so it totals the received line; finished goods and material transfers preserve that lot on both legs. Manual inventory movements also offer the recorded lot in the selected SKU/bin. Select it for samples, losses or other lot-specific changes; historical untracked balances stay untracked.

For a material **Cycle count**, choose the location and bin and enter the counted quantity in the material's base unit. Select **Preview count** to see every affected lot, its before-and-after quantity, and the signed adjustment. Shortages use earliest best-by lots first; overages go to the newest lot. **Record count** saves exactly that reviewed allocation. A matching count still records the observation without adjustment movements.

Editing the quantity or bin clears the preview. If stock or lot details change, preview again before recording. A busy stock message means another inventory operation is active; retry the preview. If recording receives no trustworthy response, the form freezes its quantity and allocation. Use **Retry unchanged count** to recover the original result. Counts do not convert rolls, cases or other purchase units, and lot allocation cannot be edited manually.

Customer account details and trading terms are edited directly on **Customer detail** with **Save customer**. Staff without edit permission see the same fields disabled. **Units** uses the shared choice buttons; selecting a unit saves it, and a failed save restores the previous choice.

### Recover an uncertain form submission

If a response is lost, **Unresolved requests** shows the saved input and **Retry saved request**. This includes staff and buyer invitations. Retry recovers the original operation; it does not submit subsequent field edits. A saved request blocks the same action on the same record. Row actions (prices, invoice pushes, menu items, mappings, deletes, and edits to an existing row such as a ship-to, bin, license or team member) are scoped to their row, so you can still save another price cell, push another invoice, or edit another row. Other actions, such as recording a movement or creating an order, wait until the saved request is retried or discarded, even for a different bin or customer. Different actions on the same page can still run while a request is unresolved. A request still being sent from this tab is not listed until its outcome arrives. If you have checked the record and know the outcome, select **Discard saved request**, then **Confirm discard**. MGR stops offering that retry and sends nothing; the record then accepts a new submission. Check the result before discarding, because a discarded request may already have been saved. Reloading the same browser tab preserves recovery. Return to the original signed-in account and brewery to recover its requests. Nothing is sent automatically. Keep the tab open until recovery finishes. CSV imports use **Retry same batch** on the Import page. Existing order, count, completion and Ask MGR recovery controls retain their own workflows.

If Square authorization is denied or fails, **Point of sale** says it was cancelled or could not finish and that the existing connection was not replaced. Use **Connect** to try again; when a connection already exists, **Try connecting again** opens the reconnect flow.


### Batch completion in-tank acknowledgement

Completing a batch lists the beer still in each open tank. When that total reaches max(0.05 bbl, 0.5% of the batch baseline), turn on the switch that names the barrels and tanks being written off as loss; **Complete batch** stays disabled until you do. Below-threshold residue needs no acknowledgement. If someone changes tank volume after you reviewed it, completion is refused with the new figures: reselect the batch and confirm again. Existing loss reconciliation and eight-decimal precision remain unchanged. **Retry unchanged completion** keeps the exact acknowledgement and request; this does not add reopening or undo.
