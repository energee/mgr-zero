You're in by invitation. There is no public sign-up and no password-reset button on this screen.

## Sign in and sign out [#sign-in]

<Screen name="Sign in" />

1. Enter the **Email** and **Password** from your invitation.
2. Select **Sign in**.
3. MGR opens **Today** for the brewery you're a member of.

Wrong credentials show **Incorrect email or password.** Signed in but not on a brewery? MGR shows **No brewery yet** with **Sign out**; ask an admin for an invitation. If your session ends while you work, the next action returns you to Sign in with **Your session ended. Sign in to retry.** Open **Me** in the header when you want **Sign out**.

<Screen name="Reset password" />

**Forgot password?** on the sign-in card opens **Reset password**. Enter your **Email** and select **Send reset link**; the page always says a link is on its way if that email is on an account, so it never confirms whether one is. The link in the email opens **Set new password**, which shows the account and asks you to **Choose a password** and **Save password**; a used or timed-out link lands back on Reset password saying so. Signed in already? **Change password** in **Me** opens the same page.

## Search and pick records [#search]

<Screen name="Search" />

**Search** in the header opens one search across SKUs, orders, invoices, lots, customers, purchase orders, and batches. Type a name to match on its first letters, or a document number (ORD-0231, INV-1042, PO-0142, B-0012, a lot code) to match it exactly; an exact number sorts first. Results group by kind; use the Up and Down arrow keys to move between matches and press Enter or click to open one. You only ever see records your role may read, and if nothing matches, the list says **No matches · change the term**.

## Navigate MGR [#navigation]

<Screen name="Today" />

The brewery name in the header is the brewery whose records you are looking at. Navigation has up to four sections: **Today**, **Beer**, **Work**, and **More**. On a phone they sit as tabs along the bottom, and tapping the tab opens that group's primary page. On a wider screen they sit in a left rail.

- **Beer** opens a landing that counts each area (finished-goods shortages, tanks with beer, material shortages, kegs out) and holds **Inventory**, **Cellar**, **Materials**, and **Kegs**. Taproom and Taps are shown locked until they are built.

The chip you pick is remembered on that device. It holds **Orders**, **Pick**, **Replenishment**, **Transfers**, **Batches**, **Packaging**, **POs**, and **Deliveries**.

Links your role cannot use are simply not shown. Planned areas stay hidden until they are built (Taproom, Taps, Menu, Import), so a tab with nothing you can open does not appear.

Sign-in and other entry cards stay a narrow column, so their buttons still span that card.

## Catalog: brands, formats, and SKUs [#catalog]

*A brand is the beer, such as Hazy IPA. A format is the physical shape it ships or pours in, such as a half-barrel keg or a case of 16 oz cans. A SKU is one brand in one packaged format, the thing inventory, pricing, and orders refer to. Admin and Sales can create brands, formats, and SKUs. Warehouse has no link here (the planned **Menu** entry is hidden until built), though the page is readable at its address. **New Brand**, **New Format**, and **New SKU** still appear for Warehouse, but submitting any of them returns a permission error. Catalog is not reachable by Brewer.*

### Create a brand

1. Open **Catalog** and select **New Brand**.
2. Enter **Name** (required), **Style** (optional; a new style is added to the brewery's list), and **ABV** (optional percentage; type `6.5` for 6.5%). **Description**, **Category**, and **Hops** are optional notes. **Price group** is the row of the [price grid](#pricing) this beer sits on; leave it **Unpriced** and none of its SKUs can be ordered until you choose one.
3. Select **Create**. Brand names must be unique within the brewery. The brand lands alphabetically and starts as **No SKUs yet**.

### Create a format

1. Under **Formats**, select **New Format**.
2. Enter **Name** and choose **Basis**: `packaged` holds stock; `poured` is a glass served at the taproom and never holds stock.
3. For a packaged format choose **Package type** (`keg`, `can`, or `bottle`), the **Keg size** for kegs, and optionally **Units per case** and **BBL per unit**.
4. Select **Create**. Format names must be unique within the brewery.

### Create a SKU

1. Find the brand and select **New SKU**.
2. Choose a packaged **Format**. **Name** is optional and defaults to *Brand · Format*; **UPC** is optional.
3. Select **Create**. A brand has at most one SKU per format. After that, the SKU is available in inventory, pricing, and order forms.

**BBL per unit** is the barrel volume of one inventory unit and lives on the format, so every SKU in that format shares it. One US beer barrel is 31 gallons (3,968 fl oz). Half-barrel keg = `0.5`. Sixth-barrel keg is about `0.16666667`. A 24 × 16 oz case is `384 ÷ 3968`, about `0.09677419`. Set it on the format before you record inventory.

Catalog shows this as **Volume** in brewery units: common keg fractions stay in bbl, smaller exact totals use whole gallons, and anything under a gallon uses fluid ounces. So `0.5` reads as **½ bbl**, a 24 × 16 oz case as **3 gal**, and a can as **16 oz**. A volume that converts to no whole unit stays in bbl. MGR still stores and calculates all beer volume in bbl.

An empty Catalog says **No brands yet.** and an empty Formats section **No formats yet.**

Wholesale Shop shows the packages the brewery has designated for wholesale — ½ keg, ⅙ keg, case, bottle — grouped under the beer name. Buyers do not see warehouse counts. Staff make that designation on the packaging plan. Prices come from the buyer's [sale channel](#pricing) and the beer's price group; a package with no price for that buyer does not appear on Shop.

## Inventory and movement history [#inventory]

<Screen name="Finished goods" />

*Admin, Sales, and Warehouse can view inventory. Admin and Warehouse can record movements.*

<Cards>
  <Card title="On hand">The sum of recorded movements for one SKU in one bin at one location. ATP stays per SKU.</Card>
  <Card title="ATP">Available to promise: on hand minus open reservations for that SKU. It can go negative.</Card>
  <Card title="Movement log">The 50 newest entries: time, type, SKU, location, signed quantity, and note.</Card>
</Cards>

**Bins.** A bin is a named place inside a location: a walk-in, a cold room, a dry rack. Every location starts with three, **Cold**, **Dry**, and **Walk-in**, and always keeps at least one; rename or remove the rest to match the building. A bin that has ever recorded stock cannot be removed, even at a zero balance, because the movement history still names it; rename it instead. Beer that ships on an order leaves from the location's first bin alphabetically, so name the bin you pick from so it sorts first.

### Record a movement

<Screen name="Record movement" />

1. Open **Inventory** and select **Record Movement**.
2. Choose a **SKU**, **Location**, and **Bin**. Picking the location preselects its first bin alphabetically; change it if the stock sits elsewhere.
3. Choose a **Type**. The default is `opening_balance`.
4. For `depletion`, pick a **Channel**. The list is your brewery's own — every brewery starts with **Wholesale**, **Taproom**, **DTC**, and **Export**, and you edit that list under [**Sale channels**](#channels). **Taproom** is preselected. The channel decides how the removal is taxed, and that treatment is frozen onto the entry: editing the channel later never rewrites what is already recorded. `sale_removal` takes a channel too, but shipping sets it, not this form.
5. Enter **Qty** in SKU units, to two decimal places if you need them. Positive quantities add stock. Negative quantities remove it. Zero is rejected.
6. Add an optional **Note**, then select **Record**.

| Type | Use | Normal sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `opening_balance` | Initial stock when you start MGR | Positive |
| `production_in` | Finished beer in from production | Positive |
| `adjustment` | Difference found by a verified physical count | Positive or negative |
| `depletion` | Taproom sale or consumption | Negative |
| `destruction` | Beer you meant to destroy | Negative |
| `loss` | Unrecoverable finished-goods loss | Negative |
| `sample` | Beer pulled as a sample | Negative |
| `festival_removal` | Beer pulled for a festival | Negative |
| `return_in` | Sellable beer back into inventory | Positive |
| `sale_removal` | Wholesale shipment from the order workflow | Negative |
| `taproom_transfer` | Transfer shipment: a negative source entry paired with a positive destination entry | Negative and positive pair |

`sale_removal` and `taproom_transfer` are written by shipping, not by this form.

<Callout type="warn">
**Samples and festival removals:** these need a destination state, and the form has no destination-state field. Do not fake it with another type. Use your brewery's approved recording procedure.
</Callout>

MGR calculates and stores barrel volume for every movement from quantity × the BBL per unit of the SKU's format. Movements cannot be edited or deleted. See [Errors and corrections](#errors-corrections) before you try to fix inventory.

Empty sections say **No inventory recorded yet.** or **No movements recorded yet.**

## Customers and ship-tos [#customers]

*Admin and Sales can create and edit customers and ship-to addresses. Warehouse has no link here, but the page is readable at its address: **New Customer** and **Edit** still appear, but submitting either returns a permission error. The list shows name, type, home state, sale channel, and payment terms.*

### Create or edit a customer

<Screen name="Customer detail" />

1. Open **Customers**. Select **New Customer**, or **Edit** beside an existing customer.
2. Enter **Name** (required).
3. Choose **Type**: `distributor`, `retailer` (default), `brewery`, or `other`.
4. Enter the required two-letter **State**. MGR uppercases it as you type.
5. Choose a **Sale channel** (required; Wholesale is preselected). It decides the customer's prices and where shipped orders are recorded.
6. Optionally enter **License number** and **Payment terms**, such as `net30`.
7. Select **Create** or **Save**.

### Create or edit a ship-to

<Screen name="Ship-to form" />

Select the customer name, then **New Ship-To** or **Edit**. Required: **Label**, **Address 1**, **City**, **State** (two letters, uppercased), and **Zip**. **Address 2** is optional. Ship-to state is the destination used when a wholesale shipment leaves inventory.

Empty lists say **No customers yet.** or **No ship-tos yet.** You cannot delete a customer, and you cannot invite a portal user from this page.

## Price groups [#pricing]

<Screen name="Price groups" />

*Admin and Sales manage prices. Order lines snapshot their price when the order is created.*

Price groups are the rows of one grid per [sale channel](#channels), with formats across the top. Put each brand on a group (**Catalog** → **Brand** → **Price group**) and fill the cells; every SKU of that brand then sells at its format's cell on the customer's channel. An empty cell means unpriced, and an order for that SKU is refused. Clear a cell to unprice it. A group a brand sits on, or a cell is filled for, cannot be deleted.

1. Open **Price groups** and select **Create price group**. Enter a required name and a **Position** (where the row sits in the grid), optionally a **Cost ceiling**, and **Save price group**. Name and position must each be unique within the brewery.
2. Select a cell in a channel's table, enter the price in dollars (stored as whole cents), and **Save price**. Every SKU of every brand on that group sells at that price, in that format, on that channel.
3. **Clear** on a filled cell empties it again.

A cost ceiling only sorts the rows and suggests a group for a beer; nobody is moved automatically. A brewery with no groups yet says **Add a price group, then put each brand on one from Catalog.**

## Staff orders [#orders]

<Screen name="Orders" />

*Admin and Sales create and manage orders. Admin and Warehouse record picks and shipments. Admin, Sales, and Warehouse can view the order list and detail.*

`draft` → `submitted` → `confirmed` → `picked` → `shipped`

An order can become `cancelled` any time before shipping. The Orders screen lists newest first and can filter by any status.

### Create an order

<Screen name="New order" />

1. Open **Orders** and select **New Order**.
2. Choose **Kind**: **Wholesale** (default) or **Taproom transfer**.
3. For wholesale, choose the **Customer** and one of that customer's **Ship-to**s. For a transfer, choose the destination in **To location**.
4. Choose the source in **From location**. Both location selects list every location of the brewery, not just taprooms or warehouses, so you pick a warehouse source and a taproom destination yourself.
5. Optionally choose **Requested ship date** and enter **PO number**.
6. Add one or more lines. Each needs a SKU and a quantity greater than zero. **Add line** adds a row. **Remove** takes one away, but at least one row stays.
7. Select **Create**. The button stays disabled until the required fields are chosen: for wholesale, a customer, a ship-to, a from location, and at least one line with a SKU and a quantity above zero; for a transfer, a destination instead of the customer and ship-to. The result is a draft. Wholesale line prices are captured from the cell where the customer's sale channel meets each beer's price group. Later price changes do not rewrite those lines.

<Callout type="info">
On a new brewery with nothing to choose yet, the form says **Before creating an order, add a customer, a location, and a SKU.** with links to **Customers** and **Catalog**, and **Create** stays disabled until those exist.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn">
A wholesale order fails if an ordered SKU has no price: its brand is on no price group, or that group's cell for the SKU's format is empty on the customer's sale channel.
</Callout>

### Move an order through fulfillment

<Screen name="Confirm order" />

A submitted order's **Confirm** on Today, Work, or the Orders list opens a review two taps from Today: the customer, the fulfillment source, each line with its ATP, and a note when confirming would oversell (that stays your call). **Confirm order** confirms it and returns to Orders; **Cancel order** asks for a reason first.

<Screen name="Complete transfer" />

A picked taproom transfer shows **Complete transfer** on its order page: the same step a wholesale order ships with, minus the invoice. Each line moves at its picked quantity from the source to the taproom.

<Screen name="Order" />

| Action | When shown | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Submit** | Draft | Status becomes submitted and the event timeline records it. |
| **Confirm** | Submitted | Status becomes confirmed and stock is reserved. Negative ATP shows as a warning (**ATP negative for …**) but does not block confirmation. |
| **Adjust lines** | Confirmed or picked | Replace the full line set and give a required **Reason**. Reservations update. Changing a picked order adds a **staged — needs restocking** badge until it is picked again. |
| **Record pick** | Confirmed or picked | Enter a nonnegative picked amount per line. Values default to the current picked amount, or the ordered amount if nothing has been picked yet. Short picks and zero are allowed. Status becomes picked and the restocking badge clears. A count below ordered shows **Short** beside the line, which opens the short-line sheet instead of saving silently. |
| **Short line** | Confirmed or picked | From **Short** on a pick line (Warehouse or Admin). Enter the count and a required reason, then choose one resolution: **Adjust order to N** makes the count the order, shrinks the reservation so ATP recovers, and the customer sees the line as adjusted; **Keep N owed** records the count and leaves the remainder owed, so the order stays picked but keeps its Pick row on Today until every line reaches its ordered quantity. Either way a *short pick* event is recorded with the reason. A count at or above ordered is refused as not short. |
| **Ship** | Picked | Enter a shipped amount per line (defaults to picked; every line must be listed, held-back lines at 0). **Carrier** and **tracking** are optional. A transfer adds the same stock to its destination. One transaction writes the shipment, the stock movements, fulfills reservations, and, for a wholesale order, the invoice. Tick **Invoice on delivery** to ship without an invoice; the shipment remembers that choice and the invoice is raised when the delivery is confirmed. Shipping less than was picked flags the order for restocking, so the held-back beer shows on Today as **Put back**. |
| **Cancel** | Any unshipped order | Give a required **Reason**, then **Cancel order**. Reservations are released. Cancelling after picking leaves the restocking badge as a physical reminder. |
| **Confirm delivery** | Shipped, on a route stop | Opens from Today's delivery row or the route's **Resume** (the route's assigned driver, or Admin) at the stop's own page: customer, ship-to, shipped lines, and the invoice timing saved at ship. Type the receiving name and press **Delivered**: the stop is stamped delivered and, when the shipment was **Invoice on delivery**, the invoice is raised now from the shipped quantities at the order's prices. Nothing moves in stock; shipping already did that. A delivered stop shows the name and the invoice number instead of the button, and confirming it again is refused as already delivered. |
| **Put back** | Any order flagged for restocking | Opened from Today's **Put back** row (Warehouse or Admin). The page lists each line's staged quantity: everything picked on a cancelled order, or the picked excess on an order adjusted down. **Put back N** clears the flag and records a *restocked* event; nothing moves in the ledger, because the beer never left the warehouse. An order whose flag is already clear says so instead of offering the button. |

The order detail shows the fulfillment source, customer and ship-to for wholesale orders, PO, requested date, ordered/picked/shipped quantity, captured price, current ATP, and a time-stamped event history. When restock is pending it names what to put back. The current detail page does not display a taproom transfer's destination, and it does not show the order note. A note entered by a portal customer is visible only on the portal's own order detail. An empty order list says **No orders yet.** An empty timeline says **No events yet.** A shipped or cancelled order has no further action controls.

## Pick sheet [#pick-sheet]

<Screen name="Pick sheet" />

*Admin and Warehouse can open Pick from the Work tab. The page also opens for Sales when the address is entered directly, but it is not linked in Sales navigation.*

Open **Pick** to see confirmed and picked orders grouped by requested ship date. Orders without a date sit under **Unscheduled**. Each card shows ordered and previously picked amounts and links to the order detail, where picking is recorded.

## Invoices and credit memos [#invoices]

<Screen name="Invoices" />
<Screen name="Invoice" />

*Admin and Sales open the Invoices list from **More**. Warehouse can open it by entering the address directly and sees the invoice created by a wholesale shipment from **View invoice** right after shipping. Only Admin and Sales can return beer against an invoice or mark a buyer's question answered.*

The list shows each invoice or credit memo with its customer, total, and due or paid date. **Open** an invoice to see the customer, line quantities, captured unit prices, amounts, and total. An empty list says **No invoices yet.** Payment is display-only; there is no record-payment action, and the QuickBooks rows stay locked until accounting sync is connected.

When a buyer asks about an invoice from the portal, the question appears on the invoice as **Buyer asked about this invoice** with their words, and as an **Answer** row on Today for Admin and Sales. Reply by phone or email, then select **Mark answered**; that clears the Today row and nothing on the invoice changes.

### Return beer and correct the invoice

<Screen name="Return and credit" />

1. Open the invoice and select **Return**.
2. Enter the quantity coming back for each applicable line. Leave unrelated lines at zero. A return cannot exceed the remaining uncredited quantity.
3. Choose **Return to location** and the **Reason**: *Unsold* and *Wrong item* put the beer back as sellable stock at that location; *Damaged* puts it back and writes the same quantity to loss in the same step, so broken beer never lands on a pick list.
4. Select **Return shipment**.

MGR creates a separate credit memo at the prices frozen on the invoice lines, never today's price, records the return-in (and, for damaged, the loss) against that credit memo, and notes the correction on the originating order's timeline. A paid invoice can still be returned; the credit sits as available credit. Credit memos cannot themselves be credited.

## Locations [#locations]

<Screen name="Locations" />

**Locations** (open `/locations`; Settings links here) lists every warehouse and taproom. Admin sees **Add location**: a name and a type, *Warehouse* or *Taproom*. Names are unique within the brewery. Select **Edit** on a row to open the location: its type, the brewery's timezone, and a gated **Location bins** row (bins are not built yet). Admin can rename a location or change its type with **Edit** then **Save location**; movement history stays exactly as recorded.

## Stock transfers [#transfers]

**Transfers** (Work tab; Warehouse and Admin) move stock between two of the brewery's locations without an order or an invoice. **New transfer** takes a source and destination location, the bin at each end (the first bin is preselected), and SKU lines with quantities. Materials and empty kegs move through the same command from the API. The document is numbered TRF-0001 upward and walks **Submit**, **Record pick** (quantities default to what was requested), and **Receive**. Receiving writes the paired stock movements in one step, out of the source bin and into the destination bin, so on hand changes place and nothing counts as a removal; the fleet total for kegs is unchanged. A same-location pair is refused: moving stock between two bins of one location is a bin move, which writes the paired rows without a document.

## Delivery routes [#deliveries]

<Screen name="Routes" />

**Deliveries** (Work tab; Warehouse and Admin) lists every route that has not returned, and below it every shipped order and picked transfer that is on no route yet. A route is one day, one driver, one vehicle, and its stops in order; a stop is a shipped wholesale order or a picked [stock transfer](#transfers). Each row names its next action: **Assign** on a route with no driver, **Open** on a planned route, **Resume** on a departed one, and **Add to route** on a document waiting for one. **New route** opens the builder.

<Screen name="Route" />

The builder takes the **Delivery date**, the **Driver** (any Warehouse or Admin member; staff have no display name yet, so a driver shows as the first characters of their id, as on Team), the **Vehicle**, and an optional **Route name**. Under **Stops**, tick the documents this route delivers and give each a stop number; the list holds this route's stops and everything shipped that is on no route. **Save route plan** writes the header and the stops in one step. A document can sit on only one open route: to move a stop, untick it here and tick it on the other route. A stop already delivered cannot be unticked, and a route cannot be saved without at least one stop.

**Depart route** is enabled once a driver is assigned. It stamps the departure, marks every transfer stop *in transit*, and closes the builder; after that the route's stops cannot change. Only the assigned driver or an Admin may depart, confirm, or return a route; another Warehouse member is refused, and the stop page says so instead of offering **Delivered**. A stop cannot be confirmed before its route departs, and it does not appear on Today until then.

<Screen name="Driver route" />

A departed route lists its stops with their delivered times. The lowest undelivered stop is **next** and its **Resume** opens [Confirm delivery](#orders); the same stop is the driver's **Next stop** row on Today. Confirming a customer stop stamps it and, for a shipment saved as invoice on delivery, raises the invoice. Confirming a transfer stop only stamps it: nothing is invoiced, and the stock moves when the transfer is received on its own page.

<Screen name="Return route" />

**Return route** is enabled once every stop is delivered and stamps the return; the route then leaves the Deliveries list, and the return time replaces the button. A refused delivery has no screen yet: a departed route cannot change its stops or return with one open, so a stop that cannot be delivered on the day is confirmed when it is delivered later. Before departing, untick a stop to move it to another route.

## Compliance [#compliance]

<Screen name="Compliance months" />

**Compliance** (More; Sales and Admin) lists the last three months and every month already filed. A month marked *not filed* opens for review; a filed month shows its filing date and taxpaid barrels. Below the months sit the **Compliance registry** link and, under **Lot trace**, every packaged lot. MGR reports the TTB month here; other jurisdictions and date ranges are available through the API.

<Screen name="Monthly compliance" />

A month is generated from the inventory ledger when you open it, so nothing is stored until you file. The table shows each package class (kegs, cans, bottles) in barrels: begin + in − out = end, and every class must balance. Zeros print 0.00. Beer in process is what the tanks hold now, not at the period end. Below the table, **Packaged** is production into finished goods, then each removal class (taxpaid, export, samples, losses, and so on) by the tax treatment recorded when the beer moved, so editing a sale channel later does not change a past month, and taxpaid removals by destination state. **Review auto-reconciled losses** is not available yet.

**Save filed snapshot** stores the figures as they stand, with an optional note, and marks the month filed; reopening it shows the snapshot rather than regenerating. MGR does not transmit anything: file with the TTB or the state yourself and record it here. The button stays off while a class does not balance; the month names the class, and today the only cause is a repack across package classes. A month can be filed once, and a period overlapping a filed one is refused. The report itself is a fixed shape, so a movement recorded later into a filed month appears in the next generation of a *different* month, never in the snapshot.

<Screen name="Compliance registry" />

The registry has two tabs. **Brands** lists each brand with its COLA or formula approvals and its state registrations; a brand with no COLA is flagged *COLA pending*. **Add approval** takes the brand, the approval kind, its TTB number, and optional approved and expiry dates; the same kind and number on the same brand is one record, so saving it twice is refused. **Add registration** takes the brand, the two-letter state, and an optional number and expiry; one record exists per brand and state, so saving again replaces it, and **Edit** keeps the brand and state fixed. **Licenses** lists the brewery's own state licenses by state and kind (brewery, supplier, direct to consumer); **Add license** works the same way, one record per state and kind, with state and kind fixed on **Edit**. **Edit** on any row opens the same sheet filled in. Kind is stored lower-case and trimmed. Order confirmation does not read this registry yet; the planned warning for an unregistered destination state will never block.

<Screen name="Lot trace" />

A lot is one closed packaging run. Its trace shows the brand and package, the run and packaging date, the tank and batch it was drawn from, the barrels drawn, and every ledger movement carrying the lot: the production that made it and any samples, losses, or destructions pulled from it, with what remains on hand. Shipments do not record a lot yet, so the customers who received it are not listed.

## Keg fleet [#kegs]

<Screen name="Keg fleet" />

**Kegs** (Beer tab; Warehouse and Admin) is the brewery's returnable keg fleet: pools and where their kegs are. One-way kegs are materials and do not appear here. The page shows **No keg pools yet** until you add one.

### Keg pools

A pool is one group of kegs you count together: **Owned**, **Leased**, or **Pay per fill**. **Add keg pool** takes the name, the kind, the vendor for a leased or pay-per-fill pool, the per-fill cost (required for pay per fill), and the deposit charged per keg. **Edit** on a pool row changes its name, vendor, costs, or deposit, or takes it out of service; a pool out of service accepts no more events. The kind cannot change after creation, and a per-fill cost or vendor once set can be changed but not removed.

### Record a keg event

**Record keg event** posts one line to the keg ledger. Choose the pool, the size, the reason, the location and bin the kegs are at (the first bin is preselected), and how many kegs:

- **Acquired** and **Retired** add kegs to or remove them from the fleet at that bin. No customer.
- **Shipped** and **Returned** move empty kegs to or from a customer; the customer is required. Shipped names the bin they left from, Returned the bin they came back into. The fleet total does not change.
- **Lost** removes kegs from that bin; name the customer when they were lost out at a customer, which also reduces that customer's balance. **Found** adds kegs back to a bin and never names a customer: a keg found at a customer is a shipped keg.

The quantity is always positive; the reason is the direction. Retired, shipped, and lost cannot exceed what the bin holds, so a typo is refused rather than driving a bin negative. A **Note** is optional and shows next to the event in Keg event history. Kegs moving between bins or locations are recorded through **Transfers** or a bin move, not here. Empty kegs only: beer coming back with a keg, and its credit, is [Return shipment](#invoices). A keg deposit refund is a separate credit memo.

**Record keg event** only appears once the brewery has at least one location and one pool in service; retire every pool and it disappears until you add one back or reactivate one.

Each pool row shows the kegs each bin holds per size, so 36 in the taproom and 40 in storage read as two rows; kegs out at customers have left their bin and are listed under **Customer keg balance** for each customer holding some.

<Screen name="Customer keg balance" />

**Customer keg balance** lists, for one customer, the kegs out per pool and size and the deposit invoiced for them (zero when no deposit was ever invoiced). Kegs out is shipped minus returned minus lost. It shows **No kegs currently out** once everything is back.

<Screen name="Keg event history" />

**Keg event history** is the ledger newest first. The pool and customer filters combine, and show **No matching events** when a combination finds nothing. Nothing here can be edited; a wrong entry is corrected by recording the opposite event.

## Taproom replenishment [#replenishment]

<Screen name="Pars and allocation" />

*Admin, Sales, and Warehouse can open Replenishment. Admin and Sales can create a replenishment order. **Create replenishment order** also appears for Warehouse, but submitting it returns a permission error.*

1. Open **Replenishment** and choose a taproom. The first taproom is selected by default.
2. Review each SKU's par, on-hand amount, and suggested quantity. Suggested quantity is the par shortfall, never less than zero.
3. Choose **From warehouse**. The first warehouse is selected by default when one exists.
4. Edit **Transfer qty** as needed. Only positive lines are included.
5. Select **Create replenishment order**. MGR creates it already confirmed, ready for warehouse picking.

The page says **No taprooms configured.** when none exist, or **No pars set for this taproom.** when there are no suggestions. The current interface has no controls to create locations or set par levels.

## Recipes [#recipes]

<Screen name="Recipes" />

<Screen name="Recipe" />

*Admin and Brewer.*

Open **Recipes** from the **More** navigation to see every recipe, alphabetical, with the brand each one brews. Select **New recipe** for a **Name**, an optional **Brand**, and an optional note; the brand is intent only — identity is required at packaging, not here. Select a row to open the recipe.

A recipe version is the process spec a brewer follows: the mash temperature, brewhouse efficiency and yeast attenuation, an optional boil length and target IBU, and its ingredients — each a material, a quantity per barrel, the stage it enters at (*mash*, *boil*, *whirlpool*, *fermentation*, *dry hop*, *packaging*, or *other*), and an optional timing in minutes. A version cannot be edited once it is saved; select **New version** to write the next one, so what a past batch was brewed from stays true.

Only mash-stage ingredients predict gravity. As you add ingredients, MGR previews the version's predicted OG, FG and ABV live, using the same calculation the record reads back afterward — nothing is stored, so a later edit to a material's typed extract potential can never move an old version's prediction; a new version snapshots the value it saw at the moment it was saved. Predicted OG and FG are shown in whatever unit you chose under [Gravity units](#gravity-units).

## Batches [#batches]

<Screen name="Batches" />

<Screen name="Schedule batch" />

<Screen name="Brew day" />

*Admin and Brewer.*

Open **Batches** from the **Work** navigation to see planned and brewed batches by date, newest first, each with the brand, recipe and vessel it names today. Select **New batch** for a **Recipe version** and a **Brand**, both optional, plus the **Planned barrels** and **Date** that actually reserve the slot — a batch names nothing further until it is brewed, and identity is not required until packaging.

Select a planned batch to record its brew day: choose the **Vessel** it knocked out into, the **Knockout barrels**, and the **Brewed on** date, then select **Record brew day**. This stamps the brew date and opens the vessel's occupancy in one step; it is refused if the vessel is already occupied, including a backdated day that falls inside a stretch the vessel was full but has since been emptied. A brewed batch's page shows the vessel and knockout volume it landed in instead of the brew-day form.

Vessels themselves are managed at the bottom of the Batches page: **New vessel** (or **Edit** on a row) takes a **Name**, a **Kind** (*fermenter*, *brite*, *barrel*, *kettle*, or *other*), and a **Capacity** in barrels. A vessel's contents are never entered directly — they always derive from its open occupancy.

## Cellar [#cellar]

<Screen name="Cellar transfer" />

<Screen name="Fermentation reading" />

*Admin and Brewer.*

Open **Cellar** from the **Beer** navigation to see every occupied tank: its vessel, the brand it holds, the barrels in it, and how long it has been there. Select **Transfer** for a **From** occupancy, a **To** vessel, the **Barrels moving**, and an optional **Loss (bbl)**. Moving into an empty vessel opens a new occupancy there; moving into an already-occupied vessel blends into it, keeping that vessel's existing batch identity — a blend is never a new batch. A source occupancy closes automatically once it empties.

Select **Reading** on a tank to open its reading log and record the next one: **Temperature** is required; **Gravity**, **pH**, and a note are each optional, because a quick temperature check is a legitimate reading on its own. Readings never overwrite one another — each is its own row, newest first. The gravity field is labelled and read in whatever unit you chose under [Gravity units](#gravity-units); MGR stores degrees Plato either way. If what you type there is not a gravity MGR can read, the field says so and **Save reading** stays disabled until you correct it or clear the field — a mistyped gravity is never quietly dropped from an otherwise saved reading.

## Packaging [#packaging]

<Screen name="Packaging runs" />

<Screen name="Schedule packaging run" />

<Screen name="Close packaging run" />

*Admin, Brewer, and Warehouse.*

Open **Packaging** from the **Work** navigation to see every run by planned date, newest first, with its next action named on the row. Select **Schedule run** for a **Brand**, a **Planned date**, an optional **Source tank**, and planned output lines (a SKU and a quantity each) — a run is identified by the brand it packages from the moment it is planned; the tank can be picked later.

A run without a tank shows **Pick source** on its page: choose a **Source tank** and confirm. Once it has a tank, **Start** stamps it started — both picking the tank and starting require one, so an unsourced run can never begin. A started run's page becomes the close form: **Barrels drawn**, the **Actual outputs** for each planned SKU (defaulting to the planned quantity), a **Lot code**, **Packaged on** and an optional **Best by** date, and the **Finished goods location** and bin the packaged units land in — all four of the last group are required, because a vessel has no location of its own for the goods to appear at. Select **Close packaging run** to write the lot, the finished-goods stock, and the packaging materials consumed in one step. The tank itself stays open afterward; emptying it is a separate Cellar transfer.

**Repack** (Admin and Warehouse only — a Brewer does not see this button), next to Schedule run, breaks a composed unit (a case) into the units it is made of at one location and bin — a shape change, never a production or a removal. Choose the **Location** and **Bin**, the SKU you are **Breaking** and its quantity, and the SKU you are packing it **Into** and its quantity. It is refused unless the two SKUs share a brand and their formats are joined by exactly one composition record, and unless the volumes on both sides match exactly.

## Purchasing: vendors, materials, and purchase orders [#purchasing]

<Screen name="Purchase orders" />

Purchasing is four pages. **Vendors** and **Materials** hold the definitions, **POs** (Work tab) is where an order lives from draft to received, and **Planning** shows what committed work is short of and drafts the orders to cover it.

### Vendors and contracts

**Vendors** (More tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists your suppliers. **Add vendor** and **Edit** take a name, optional email and phone, payment terms (due on receipt, net 15, net 30 default), and a **Lead time** in days. The lead time is the vendor's, not the material's: it is what Planning subtracts from a needed-by date to get a buy-by date. Once a vendor has received orders, the row also shows the observed average from sent to last receipt, with how many orders it rests on (n=3 is weak evidence) and how it was sent, because a date someone typed for an order phoned in is not the same evidence as one MGR stamped.

Below the vendors, **Contracts** lists commitments: one vendor, one material, a quantity in purchase units, an optional price, window, and **Contract number**. A contract with a number shows it as the row's label; without one, the row falls back to the material name. A contract is not an order. You order against it all year, so each row shows four numbers: **committed**, **received**, **on order** (placed but not yet arrived), and **available**, which is committed less both. Available is the one to decide against; received is the one accounting reconciles. A contract never stops you ordering: exceed it and the extra lines are ordinary spot lines.

### Materials

**Materials** (Beer tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists material definitions with on hand summed across every bin, including inactive materials, marked **inactive**. **Add material** and **Edit** take a name, a kind (malt, hop, yeast, adjunct, chemical, packaging, other), the base unit you consume in, the purchase unit you buy in and how many base units one purchase unit holds (a 44 lb hop box is purchase unit *each*, 44 base units), whether it is **lot-tracked**, an optional **default vendor**, and whether it is **Active** (available to recipes and purchase orders; on by default). Units are refused once the material has movements, because every ledger row was written in them. A lot-tracked material asks for a lot code at every receipt; an untracked one never does.

**Count** on a material row opens a cycle count for one bin: pick the location and bin, type what you counted in base units, and **Record count**. The count is always recorded, even when it matches. Only the variance posts, as a count adjustment. When the material holds several lots, a shortage comes off the earliest best-by lots first and an overage lands on the newest lot; a shortage crossing two lots writes one adjustment per lot. Count label stock as whole rolls and leave the open roll out; its remainder falls into the variance and self-corrects at the next count.

### Purchase orders

**POs** (Work tab; Admin and Warehouse) lists open orders: drafts, sent, and partially received. Each row names its next action. **New PO** takes a vendor, an optional expected date, and lines: a material, a quantity in purchase units, an optional unit cost, and on a lot-tracked material an optional **Expected lot**, the lot the vendor named when you ordered. The expected lot is advisory: it creates nothing, and receiving prefills from it. **Save draft** numbers the order PO-0001 upward.

A draft's page offers **Mark sent**. Choose how it went out, **From my mail client** or **Phoned, faxed, or vendor portal**, and confirm. MGR does not send email: it records that you sent the order today. The status then reads **Marked sent** with the date, never *Delivered*, because nothing here can know that. Marking is one-way; a sent order cannot go back to draft.

A sent order's page is the receiving form. Choose the location and bin everything lands in and the received date, then for each line still due type what you **counted**. Lines default to what is still due; type over them. Over and short are both allowed and both recorded. A lot-tracked line asks for the **lot code off the package** (prefilled from the expected lot when the order named one; if the vendor shipped another lot, type what the box says) and an optional best-by date. **Receive purchase order** writes the receipt, its lines, the lots, and receipt movements into that bin in one step; only what you counted posts to inventory. The order becomes **partially received** until every line is met, then **received**. What each line still owes is worked out from the receipts every time, so a second receipt against the same order simply closes the remainder. **Show received and cancelled** at the foot of the list reveals finished orders.

### Planning

<Screen name="Planning" />

**Planning** (More tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists each material that committed work is short of. Demand is what unbrewed batches need through their recipe and what open packaging runs need through their format's bill of materials; supply is on hand plus what is already on a sent purchase order, so a gap is never ordered twice. A draft is not supply until it is marked sent. Each row shows need, on hand, on order, the needed-by date, and the vendor the gap resolves to (a contract with commitment still available, else the material's default vendor). A resolved vendor with a typed lead time also shows the **buy by** date; one with no lead time typed says so instead, and the row still drafts, just with no date to judge it against. A row past its buy-by date is out of reach and is left out of the draft; a material with no contract and no default vendor cannot draft at all until you set one.

**Draft N purchase orders** (Admin and Warehouse) writes one draft per vendor for every buyable row, quantities rounded up to whole purchase units, priced from the contract for what its available commitment covers and left blank beyond that. The label says how many before you commit, and the page then opens the POs list so you can review and mark them sent.

## Team [#team]

<Screen name="Team" />

Admin opens **Team** from **Settings**. Each member shows as an **@handle** (the first part of their email) with the email and role; your own row is marked **you**. Open another member's row to change their **Role** and **Save role**, or **Remove** them, which ends their membership at this brewery and leaves their sign-in account alone. MGR keeps at least one admin: the only admin cannot step down or be removed, and nobody can remove themselves. **Invite staff** stays locked until invitations are made retry-safe. Sales and Warehouse are sent to **No access** if they open the address directly.

## Settings [#settings]

<Screen name="Settings" />

Admin opens **Settings** from **More** to edit the brewery's basics in one form: **Brewery name**, **Timezone** (a zone name MGR knows, such as America/New_York), **TTB registry number**, **PA license**, the **Customer-facing phone** the portal prints when online payment is unavailable, and **Reading overdue after (hours)**, which sets when a fermentation reading counts as overdue on Today. **Save brewery** writes them together. Below the form, rows open **Locations** and **Team**; Source water, Accounting, Point of sale, and Import stay locked until their areas are built, and **Chat** opens the Slack account link.

## No access [#no-access]

<Screen name="Permission denied" />

Navigation hides what your role cannot open, but a bookmarked address still lands on the page. When the page's first read is refused, MGR shows **No access** with what was refused, who you are signed in as, and the role that would satisfy it, plus **Back to Today** and **Go to Beer**. An admin can change your role in Settings, then Team.

## Sale channels [#channels]

<Screen name="Sale channels" />

<Screen name="Channel" />

*Admin only. Everyone who records or reads a movement sees the channel names; only Admin can add, rename, or remove one.*

A sale channel is a named way your brewery sells beer. Every brewery starts with four: **Wholesale**, **Taproom**, **DTC**, and **Export**. Open **Sale channels** from the **More** navigation to see each channel and its tax treatment.

1. Select **Add channel**, enter a **Channel name**, choose a **Tax treatment**, and select **Save channel**.
2. To rename a channel or change its treatment, select **Edit** on its row, change the fields, and select **Save channel**.
3. To remove a channel, select **Delete** on its row.

The tax treatment — *taxable*, *export*, *vessel supplies*, *research*, or *transfer in bond* — is the default for removals recorded on that channel. A customer may override it on their own record, and the treatment that applies is frozen onto each movement the moment it is recorded. Editing a channel later never restates a month you have already filed.

Two removals are refused, each with the reason shown beside the row:

- Every channel can be renamed: no name is load-bearing, because an order carries its own channel from the moment it is created.
- A channel a movement, a customer, an order, or a filled price cell references cannot be deleted. Rename it instead, or clear its cells and move those customers first.

## Gravity units [#gravity-units]

<Screen name="Units" />

*Everyone. Only Admin sets the brewery default; the personal preference is yours alone.*

Gravity is always stored in degrees Plato. This setting changes only how gravity is shown to you and how you type it, so switching it never moves a reading you have already recorded.

Open **Units** from the **More** navigation. **Brewery default** (Admin only) is what everyone here sees unless they choose otherwise. **Your preference** is yours: choose **Plato**, **Specific gravity**, or **Use brewery default** to go back to following the brewery. The choice applies wherever MGR shows or asks for a gravity — the fermentation reading form and log, and the predicted OG and FG on a recipe.

In specific gravity you may type either spelling: `1.050` or `1050`.

## Link your Slack account [#slack]

<Screen name="Link your Slack" />

When your brewery's Slack connection is available, open its MGR App Home and choose **Link MGR account**. Sign in to MGR if asked. A valid link confirms that Slack is linked.

Linked App Home shows a role-filtered **Today** queue with up to 20 current items. Admin sees all live items; Sales sees submitted orders to review; Warehouse sees confirmed orders whose requested ship date is due and orders with staged beer to put back. Select **Open** on an item to view its order in MGR. With no current work, App Home says **You’re caught up**.

Linked eligible staff also receive private Slack messages for newly submitted orders and picks that become due. The message contains a safe order label, requested-date detail, and **Open in MGR**. When the work resolves, that same message changes to **Resolved**. MGR never posts customer contacts, prices, or notes to Slack.

If the link is missing, expired, already used, or belongs to another account, go back to Slack and request a fresh link. Notification settings and unlink controls are not in the MGR interface yet.

## Errors and corrections [#errors-corrections]

**A form reports an error.** Read the message, fix the visible fields, and submit again. Your entries stay until the form succeeds or you close it. Permission errors need an administrator.

**A page fails to load.** Select **Try again** once. If it still fails, give your administrator the brewery or customer name, page, approximate time, and the action you tried.

**A record seems missing.** Confirm the brewery shown in the navigation, clear any order-status filter, and check the exact brand, SKU, customer, or location name.

**A page says "Not found".** Opening an order, customer, or invoice link that matches nothing in your brewery shows a Not found page with a link back to Today, not an error. The record was removed, the link is wrong, or it belongs to a brewery you are not signed in to. Switch brewery, or ask the person who sent the link.

**Inventory is wrong.** Do a physical count and figure out what happened. Record an adjustment only for a verified count difference. Never disguise a sale, sample, loss, destruction, festival removal, return, or transfer as another type. Put a note on the correction.

**A shipped order or invoice is wrong.** Do not add an unrelated inventory movement. Use **Return** on the invoice so the credit and the returned stock stay linked to the shipment history.

## Actions not yet available [#unavailable]

Do not hunt for hidden controls for these:

- public account sign-up
- staff or customer-portal invitations
- CSV import
- adding, renaming or removing bins, setting taproom pars, or choosing the warehouse that fulfills wholesale-customer portal orders, from the interface
- cancelling a purchase order, editing one after it is saved, or sending one by email from MGR
- editing brands, formats, or SKUs after creation
- deleting customers, ship-tos, inventory history, orders, invoices, or credit memos
- recording payments
- reviewing or reattributing auto-reconciled losses before a filing, a printed lot trace packet, and the customers who received a lot
- an order-confirmation warning from the compliance registry, or deleting an approval, registration, or license
- filing a state or other non-TTB report, or a period other than a calendar month, from the interface (the API accepts any jurisdiction and range)
- Slack installation, notification settings, or unlinking from a visible MGR settings page

Ask the person who configures your brewery when one of these is required.

This manual describes controls that exist now. If the application and this guide disagree, report the screen, action, and exact message to your MGR administrator.
