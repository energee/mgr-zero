Join through an invitation, then sign in with your email and the password you chose. There is no public account sign-up.

Number fields use shared **− / +** controls; you can also type an exact value, including decimals where the field permits them. Required fields and minimum or maximum amounts still apply. Optional amounts can remain blank.

## Sign in and sign out [#sign-in]

<Screen name="Sign in" />

1. Enter your **Email** and **Password**.
2. Select **Sign in**.
3. MGR opens the page you were trying to open, or **Today** for your brewery when you came straight to Sign in.

Browser tabs identify **Sign in**, **Reset password**, and **Invitations**, followed by **· MGR**.

A customer who opened the staff sign-in by mistake can select **Customer? Sign in to the portal**; the portal sign-in links back with **Brewery staff? Sign in to MGR**. Wrong credentials show **Incorrect email or password.** For passwordless entry, enter a valid **Email**, leave **Password** blank, and select **Email me a link**. An empty or invalid email must be corrected before requesting a link. This does not create a new account. **Sign in** still requires both email and password. Signing in, by password or emailed link, opens **Invitations** first when a pending invitation is waiting for you; see [Accept an invitation](#getting-started). Signed in but not on a brewery? MGR shows **No brewery yet** with **Sign out**; ask an admin for access. On a hosted installation, **Create brewery** is also available to signed-in accounts. Entry cards sit near the bottom of a phone screen and center on wider screens. If your session ends while you work, **Session expired** confirms that eligible queued readings remain on this device; select **Sign in to retry** as the original user and brewery. Without JavaScript, that URL shows the sign-in card directly. Open **Me** in the header when you want **Sign out**.

While password sign-in is being checked, the button says **Signing in…** and cannot be submitted again. A failed attempt keeps your email and requested page in the form on the same sign-in page and leaves the password blank. The email is not added to the page address or browser history. Password and email-link sign-in both return you to your requested page. If you are already signed in, opening the staff sign-in page with a return target also takes you there. Without a return target, either sign-in page sends you to Today (staff), Shop (customer-only accounts), or **No brewery yet** when you have no membership.
Pending invitations take priority: either sign-in page opens **Invitations** first when consent is waiting.

You do not need to change the password again.

If you followed a recovery link or opened the address directly, it returns to Today. An unknown page offers **Back to Today**, so a mistyped address is not a dead end.

## Accept an invitation and create a brewery [#getting-started]

<Screen name="Accept invite" />

Open the invitation email, enter **Your name** and **Choose a password** (at least eight characters), then select **Join**. The shared entry card shows the verified brewery and your assigned role. Staff open Today; buyers open their customer portal. The link is single use. A used, expired, or wrong-audience link opens **Invite expired**, with **Reset password** and **Back to sign in**; contact the brewery if access is still missing. Validation keeps your entered name for correction without filling the password back in. An existing account receives a pending invitation instead of immediate access; their password stays unchanged.

<Screen name="Account invitations" />

If you already have an MGR sign-in and someone invites that email to staff a brewery, signing in sends you to **Invitations** first; you can also open it yourself at `/invitations`. The page reminds you that joining grants access to the brewery shown and does not change your password. Each pending invitation names the brewery and the role offered; select **Accept invitation** to join. Accepting switches you to that brewery and opens Today with your new role active. Consent expires seven days after the invitation is sent; an expired or revoked invitation needs a new one from the brewery. Replaying an old accepted invitation never restores removed access. With nothing pending, the page says **No pending invitations. Ask the brewery for a new invitation if yours expired.** If acceptance fails, MGR asks you to check you're signed in with the invited email and ask the brewery whether it expired or was revoked.

<Screen name="Create brewery" />

On hosted MGR, a signed-in account can open **Create brewery** from **No brewery yet**. Enter **Brewery name**, a valid **Timezone** such as America/New_York, and an optional **TTB registry number**. The timezone field suggests common US zones and still accepts another valid IANA zone. **Create brewery** creates the brewery and makes you its first admin, then opens Today. Validation errors keep your entries for correction. Dedicated installations hide this entry and refuse brewery creation from the page and the API alike.

<Screen name="First-run checklist" />

An admin sees **Set up** until the brewery has both a location and a brand. Once both exist, Today returns to its usual work list. Add locations, optionally open **Import CSV**, add a brand, optionally **Invite staff**, and record opening inventory. Importing and inviting staff are optional; they do not hold the checklist open.

## Search and pick records [#search]

<Screen name="Search" />

**Search** in the header finds app pages and records. Before you type, it lists the last five records you opened from it as **Recent**; until you have opened one, it shows a hint to type a page name, record name, or document number. Recent records last until you reload the page, switch brewery, or sign out. Page names such as `invoice` and `catalog` show matching destinations in a separate **Pages** group, using the same role-visible navigation as the sidebar. Each page shows one line saying what you do there; a page listed on **More** shows the same line on its card. Pages remain available while record searches load or fail. Record search covers SKUs, orders, invoices, lots, ingredient lots, customers, purchase orders, batches, packaging runs, transfers, routes, recipes, vessels, vendors, ship-tos, materials, keg pools, bins, state licenses, and water profiles. Type a name to match on its first letters (a license matches on its license number), or a document number (ORD-0231, INV-1042, PO-0142, B-0012, RUN-0007, TRF-0003, a lot code) to match it exactly; an exact number sorts first. A number without its prefix, such as `0199`, matches every kind of numbered document and lists each one it finds. A vendor opens its own page. Materials, keg pools, state licenses, and water profiles open their list page, a ship-to opens its customer, and a bin opens its location's bins. Results group by kind; use the Up and Down arrow keys to move between matches and press Enter or click to open one. You only ever see records your role may read and open: Brewer searches batches, packaging runs, recipes, vessels, vendors, materials, bins, and water profiles; Warehouse does not see lots, batches, recipes, vessels, licenses, or water profiles; and Sales does not see purchase orders, batches, packaging runs, recipes, vessels, vendors, materials, keg pools, or water profiles. The sheet distinguishes loading, failed, offline cached, and valid empty results.

SKU fields in **New order** open their own picker. For a wholesale order with a customer chosen, it lists the active SKUs priced for that customer; otherwise it lists the brewery's SKUs. Typing filters that list by name. It always starts from that full list; it does not move SKUs you picked before to the top or save past searches.

Screen readers announce a searchable picker's field name and its current choice together, including SKU and customer filters. Before you choose, they announce the field name and its selection prompt.

When no active SKUs are available, **Select SKU** says **No active SKUs yet.** and offers **Add a SKU** in **Catalog**. **No matching SKUs** instead means that existing choices do not match the search; clear or change the search.

Search accepts up to 80 characters; longer pasted text is shortened to fit.

Use the arrow keys to move through an open search, SKU picker, or drop-down list, then press Enter to choose the highlighted option. A tinted fill and a solid outline mark the active option in both light and dark themes.

Shared forms use the same text fields and pickers throughout MGR. Number-field minus/plus buttons change the value by one, keeping its decimal part; you can also type decimals where the field allows them. Reading cadence uses whole hours. Optional numbers can stay blank. Quantities with a unit beside them keep that unit visible.

If a customer, ship-to, vendor, brewery phone, or invitation email is refused
for its format, the affected field is highlighted and a correction appears
under it. Your entries stay in the form. US states are chosen from the list,
such as PA, US postal codes use five digits or ZIP+4, and phone numbers can include
spaces, parentheses, dashes, or a leading +.

Forms tell you what they need. On New order, Ship order, Complete transfer, New batch, New PO, New transfer, New vessel, the route builder, Repack, the Replenishment forms, Record payment, Void payment, Write off, Beer return, Receive purchase order, Correct receipt, and the vendor form, an unavailable save button has a line beside it naming what is still missing, such as *Still needed: a vendor and a quantity above zero on each line.* The line changes as you fill the form and disappears once the button is available. A picker with nothing to choose says so under it and links to the page where that record is made, such as **No vendors yet.** with **Add a vendor**. When your role cannot make that record, the note names the role that can instead of linking. A picker whose choice depends on another field says so too: a chosen location with no bins says **This location has no bins.** A typed value MGR cannot accept is marked at its own field once you leave the field, with what to enter instead underneath, such as *Enter a quantity above zero.* On a line, the message names the line and marks the field that is wrong: the SKU, the quantity, or both.

If stock cannot load after you choose a location in **Record movement** or **Repack**, the form shows the failure message without a technical error prefix. If no message is available, it says **The request did not finish. Try again.**

Quantity corrections sit directly below the quantity control, not under a neighbouring SKU. Order and transfer quantities must be above zero; fractional quantities are allowed and stored to two decimal places. **Planned barrels** accepts fractional barrels and stores three decimal places. A value below 0.0005 would store as zero, so it is marked at the field and keeps **Schedule batch** unavailable. **Set taproom par** and **Set standing allocation** allow zero or a positive fractional quantity; **Packages to repack** must be above zero. These quantities store two decimal places. PO lines keep visible **Quantity** and **Unit cost ($)** labels, with each correction under its own control; material quantities may be fractional and costs cannot be negative.

Other fields give their own corrections after you leave them. Brand **ABV %** accepts 0–99.99, including 5.55, and stores two decimal places. Price-group **Position** uses whole numbers from 1 to 2,147,483,647; **Cost ceiling** cannot be negative and dollar amounts round to cents. A vendor's optional **Email** and an invited staff member's or portal user's required **Email** must be valid email addresses, with at most 64 characters before the @ and 254 in total. Vendor **Lead time** uses whole days, from 0 to 2,147,483,647. Water-profile **pH** must be between 0 and 14. Correct invalid entries before saving.

For planned barrels, purchase-order and contract quantities and unit costs, par and reserved quantities, repack quantity, ABV, price-group position and cost, vendor lead time, and water pH, type ordinary numbers rather than exponent notation: enter **1000**, not **1e3**. Fractional values remain valid wherever that field's domain allows them.

## Know what your roles can do [#roles]

A member can hold more than one staff role. You get everything each role allows: Sales + Brewer can manage customers and brew batches. Adding Taproom never takes away another role's access. Admin already includes every staff permission, whether held alone or alongside other roles. Staff roles do not grant access to a customer's portal account; portal membership stays separate.

*MGR checks your roles when you submit an action. A button can still be on the page even when your roles cannot finish it. The same limits apply everywhere, including the API: customers, orders, invoices, prices, and compliance filings are readable only by the roles listed for them below.*

| Role | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| **Admin** | Every staff screen and action that exists. |
| **Sales** | Catalog; customers and pricing; create, submit, confirm, adjust, and cancel orders, plus credits; view inventory; invoices, including recording and voiding invoice payments; replenishment suggestions and creating the replenishment order; brand approvals and state registrations, the brewery’s licenses, the monthly report and its filed snapshot, and lot trace. |
| **Warehouse** | View inventory and record movements; keg pools and keg events on **Keg fleet**; view orders, pick, and ship; build delivery routes, and depart, confirm each stop, and return a route you are the assigned driver of; view replenishment suggestions (write controls are available only to Admin and Sales); schedule, pick a tank for, start, and close packaging runs, and repack; vendors, materials and contracts; purchase orders (draft, mark sent, receive), material counts, and drafting orders from Planning. Catalog, Customers, and the Invoices list open if you type their address, but they are not in Warehouse's navigation. Locations opens the same way; there Warehouse can add, rename, or remove a location's bins, though only Admin can add a location or edit its name and uses. |
| **Taproom** | Today links the tap board, weekly count, and four-week variance with their last observed facts. Beer shows stock at this brewery’s taproom locations and opens the same taproom tools. You have no More tab: **Me** in the header holds your own Chat and gravity unit settings. You can link or unlink your Slack account, mute personal reminder reasons, and select an existing verified personal delivery destination. Warehouse stock, lot history, Work, Search, business records, shared channels, brewery quiet-hour changes, and direct inventory writes are unavailable. |
| **Brewer** | Today; linking your Slack account from App Home; Recipes and versions; Batches, vessels, the Prep pull list, and recording brew day; Cellar transfers and fermentation readings; packaging runs (schedule, pick a tank, start, and close), but not **Repack**, which is Admin and Warehouse only; vendors, materials, contracts and material counts, and reading Planning (drafting the orders is Admin and Warehouse). See [Navigate MGR](#navigation). |

## Taproom variance [#taproom-variance]

<Screen name="Variance by brand" />

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Variance by brand** from Weekly count, Beer, or Taproom Today. Choose an owned taproom location and 4 or 12 weeks. The report compares whole completed count periods whose ending dates fall in that brewery-local window, with exact exclusive starting and inclusive ending times shown. Sold depletion is the actual used in the comparison; destroyed volume is displayed separately. The first count has no starting observation, so its actual depletion is shown without a comparison. A period may begin before the selected window.

Actual is the saved count's depletion. Expected is reconciled POS quantity times its frozen serving size; editing today's pour or package size never changes an existing expectation. A later reconciled sale can update a closed period's expected amount and variance, but cannot change counts or stock. Variance is expected minus actual.

The shared **Variance by brand** screen puts the location and the **4 weeks** / **12 weeks** tabs under its title, then the brand totals, followed by excluded and unattributed volumes and the **Count periods** list with each period's coverage. When no count period ends in the window, the page shows one message, **No completed count periods in this window**, and no empty period list. A window whose only count is a first count shows the same message and lists that first count under **Count periods**. The report totals each brand over the whole window, not week by week, so it shows no recurring brand trend and MGR does not infer a repeated shortage from those totals. A period without a comparison says why in plain words: a first count has nothing to compare against, or no usable POS sales data covers it. Other periods say **Comparable**, with their coverage.

Without usable POS observations, comparison rows are empty and expected stays blank. A dash means a value is unavailable; read zero alongside coverage and excluded consumption. Mapped lines still contribute when other items need mapping, with coverage and mapping gaps shown. Overlapping taps split each sale equally as an estimate; shares assigned to kegs outside inventory are excluded while count-derived actual stays intact. Sales without a matching open interval remain explicitly unattributed. Guest names do not identify POS sales, so guest yield is unavailable. Square connection and sales sync live under [Point of sale](#settings); they supply expected amounts only and never post stock.

## Taproom physical counts [#taproom-counts]

<Screen name="Weekly count" />

**Weekly count** uses the shared explorer layout. The count worksheet shows expected consumption first, then the checked print action and one remaining-package stepper per stock bucket. When expected consumption is unavailable, that card moves below the count, after **Record count**, so the first bucket is in view; a changed baseline warning still shows first. Buckets are listed by SKU, then bin. Each row shows its bin and recorded stock; a tracked lot also shows its worksheet row, which matches its printed label. Type a whole quantity or use minus/plus; blank remains uncounted, and zero must be explicit. When you counted exactly the recorded stock, select **Same as recorded** on that row to fill it in. A brand with no expected amount has no comparison row. **Record count** stays unavailable until every bucket has a whole quantity from zero through its recorded stock; invalid quantities have an explanation beside the field. A saved count shows its authoritative receipt, not an unfinished draft comparison. **Count worksheet** returns to the current worksheet. **Correct count** opens the same correction sheet from an eligible Admin receipt; its reason and quantities lock during an uncertain retry.

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Weekly count** under Beer to prepare, record, and read physical counts. Choose a taproom location, then enter remaining whole packaged units for every displayed bin, SKU, and lot identity, including zero quantities. A partially full keg counts as one until it is gone; estimated fill levels never change the inventory ledger.

The page uses today's date in the brewery's timezone and holds the revision and prior-count identity from the prepared count. The expected card links the last saved count by date; a first count shows no link. A saved receipt that follows another count links it as **Open the previous count**. A first count's saved receipt says there is nothing to compare against. Every displayed bucket needs its own observation. A tracked lot and **untracked** stock are separate buckets; there is no automatic lot allocation. Admin and Warehouse see existing stock labels when one is available. Taproom sees the full worksheet row number on every bucket instead of raw lot identifiers on screen.

**Print current stock labels** uses the same captured count revision for Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom. The printed worksheet includes only current positive Taproom stock, keeps each row's position from the full count worksheet, and names a tracked lot by its current code or says **Untracked**. MGR rechecks the revision immediately before opening the browser's native print flow. If stock or the prior count changed, printing is refused and the page tells you to reload and review; entries and an uncertain count retry remain in place. When there is no positive stock, the print button is disabled with the inline reason **No current positive stock to print.** Full lot history remains outside this projection as Admin and Sales compliance data.

A matching count still saves its server date and time, author, and all observations. Its receipt says **matched · no movement** per unchanged bucket. For each shortage, choose **Sold** (the default) or **Destroyed**. Sold includes comps, staff pours, and unexplained shortages and uses the Taproom sale channel. Destroyed posts only the missing units as destruction without channel, tax, or destination-state facts. One choice covers each bucket; allocations and samples are not supported. Matching Sold does nothing; matching Destroyed is refused. The choice freezes during an uncertain retry; the receipt shows whether stock was depleted or destroyed, its lot code when tracked, and frozen depleted BBL. POS setup and sales do not affect this posting. The page lists the newest 50 saved count headers for the selected taproom and can reopen each complete durable receipt, including counts larger than catalog list limits. Stock changes or a draft held past the brewery's local midnight require **Start fresh recount**, which clears entries and loads a new revision and server-owned date. Counts above recorded stock, incomplete counts, and a second count on the same date are refused. A taproom with no recorded stock shows no buckets. **Record count** still saves a count of zero for today. Your next count compares against it.

The page also shows expected consumption while the next count is still a draft. It uses the latest saved count as the starting observation and the server's current time as the end, and identifies that saved count and both timestamps. Expected amounts are grouped by brand because several package SKUs may belong to one beer; your physical entries remain separate by bin, SKU, and lot. As you complete every bucket for a brand, the comparison shows draft actual depletion from the snapshot's package volumes and expected minus actual. A missing bucket keeps draft actual and difference blank. This is a draft estimate; the saved count receipt remains authoritative. When expected is blank, the card says why in plain words: this is the first count, no usable POS sales data covers the period, the data covers only part of it, or some POS items still need mapping. A complete empty observation may show zero. Coverage bounds, partial coverage, unmapped or ignored lines, shares excluded because a tap was outside inventory, and poured volume without an active matching tap remain visible. **Refresh expected** updates only this comparison; it never posts stock, allocates lots, or replaces the count form's stock revision. If that refresh finds a newer saved-count baseline, the comparison becomes unavailable and the form stays locked until **Start fresh recount** loads the new snapshot and clears entries. Late POS reconciliation may change the aligned comparison until the count is saved.

If the page cannot tell whether a submission reached the server, including a timeout or `5xx` response, it freezes every quantity and the request ID. Select **Retry unchanged count** to recover the original result without posting twice. A later refusal cannot unlock an already uncertain request. A definitive first validation error leaves the fields editable for a corrected new attempt.

Admin can select **Correct count** on the latest saved count, once, when sold quantities were counted too low or too high. Enter the corrected whole units from zero through the stock recorded before the count, and a reason. A count that originally matched recorded stock can also be corrected downward while that stock remains; once it has all been used, **Correct count** no longer appears for it. Destroyed shortage lines cannot be corrected. In a mixed count, the form sends only changed sold lines and preserves every original bucket, including destruction, in the effective receipt. A lower correction is refused if stock used since the count cannot cover the extra depletion; ask Warehouse to review it. It cannot exceed the recorded-before quantity, correct an older count, or correct an earlier correction. Warehouse and Taproom can read the corrected receipt and its author, time, and reason, but only Admin sees the action.

The original header, observations, and ledger rows remain unchanged. MGR appends an exact compensation for each changed depletion and, when the corrected amount still falls below recorded stock, a replacement depletion using the original package volume, channel, tax treatment, state, bin, SKU, and lot. The replacement can be smaller or larger. A formerly matching line has no original depletion to reverse; its first depletion uses the package volume and Taproom channel facts at correction time. The corrected count keeps the original observation time, appears as one logical occurrence in history, becomes the next count's baseline, and updates variance without counting unchanged lines twice. Its signed ledger entries belong to the time when Admin made the correction; an earlier filed compliance snapshot stays byte-for-byte unchanged. If the response is uncertain, **Retry unchanged correction** reuses the frozen reason, quantities, payload, and request ID.

## Tap intervals [#tap-intervals]

<Screen name="Tap board" />
<Screen name="Kick keg" />
<Screen name="Swap keg" />

**Tap board** uses the same vessel-style tiles for own and guest kegs, with **Swap** and **Kick** on each. Each tile shows its nominal size, opening day and date, actor, and opening fill. Guest tiles also say **no guest yield**. After a tap, kick, or swap saves, the sheet closes immediately and the board says **Updating the board…** until its refreshed tiles arrive. **Tap keg** opens the same entry sheet in the explorer and application. Closing fill is **Empty**, **About 25% left**, or **About 50% left**; opening fill remains a separate coarse observation. A tile says **opened full** for a full keg, and a history row prints the closing fill chip it was closed with. **Swap keg** keeps the outgoing captured SKU available for **Same own SKU**, even if it is no longer in the active SKU chooser. The recent-activity rows show the two most recently opened current intervals; closed intervals remain in recent history. These actions never post finished-goods depletion.

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Tap board** from Beer or Taproom Today. Choose an owned taproom location. **Tap keg** opens a keg with its packaged keg SKU, or with an explicit guest label and positive nominal size in bbl. Own keg size comes from its format and is frozen by the server for that interval. **Tap keg** and **Swap · one record** stay unavailable until the sheet is complete. A line next to the button names what is still needed, such as a packaged keg SKU or a guest keg label. A guest or tap-number field you leave invalid is marked with the same need. **Kick keg** is always ready, because its reason starts at **Kicked empty**. A new tap starts on the first keg SKU in the picker. The picker lists active keg SKUs whose keg format has a volume. When there are none, the picker says so and explains how to choose **Guest keg** under **Identity**. A member who may create SKUs (Admin or Sales) gets an **Add a keg SKU** link to Catalog. Anyone else is told that an admin or sales member adds keg SKUs. Selecting **Guest keg** removes the packaged SKU requirement. The button then names only the missing guest label or positive nominal bbl, and becomes available once both are entered. A guest always shows as not in inventory; an own keg also receives that flag when its taproom has no positive stock. Tapping never changes inventory.

Closed tap entries cannot be edited or deleted. **Recent history** lists only closed kegs; kegs still on tap appear only on the board above. Each entry keeps who opened and closed the keg and when, and those intervals support historical variance calculations. **Kick** closes an open keg; it does not erase its history. The kick sheet is headed **Kick tap 4** for a numbered keg and **Kick keg** for one with no number. A history row names the tap number when there is one, then the beer, the reason and the remaining fill. A keg kicked empty reads **Kicked empty** alone, without a second **Empty**.

Opening fill is **25%**, **50%**, **60%**, or **Full**. The **Tap number** is optional and can repeat. Enter digits for a positive whole number. Leave it blank or enter only spaces to keep the keg unnumbered. Letters, signs, decimals, exponents, zero, and very large numbers are refused. Two kegs can share a number, and a keg with no number sorts last. The sorting hint appears only when an open keg has no number. Existing historical tap labels remain unchanged. Kick closes an interval with a reason and estimated remaining fill: empty, about 25%, or about 50%. Swap closes and replaces it in one action; if either change cannot be saved, neither is saved. Omit the incoming keg to reuse the outgoing own SKU; a guest replacement always requires an explicit name and size. An omitted replacement number leaves the new interval unnumbered.

Retry an unchanged action with the same request ID to recover its original result, even after another action. A new action against an already closed interval is refused and reports the closer's saved name, or **staff** when no name is available, plus the closing time. Open taps includes every interval, with unnumbered entries last; recent history returns the latest 50 closed intervals with saved actor names and times. These fill estimates do not change counts or inventory. No POS yield or remaining-volume bar is supplied.

The board refreshes its open rows and recent history every 30 seconds and when you return to the tab. A sheet with unsaved edits stays open and unchanged. If MGR cannot tell whether a tap, kick, or swap reached the server, its fields and request ID freeze for **Retry unchanged**. A later refusal cannot unlock an uncertain request. An already-closed conflict reloads recent history and shows the closer's saved name and time when available.

Guest labels are never matched to POS items, so a guest row never shows numeric yield. When there is no usable POS numerator, the board shows what is open, when, and by whom without inventing a bar. Tap, kick, and swap create no finished-goods movement; the weekly physical count owns depletion.

## Navigate MGR [#navigation]

Orders and New order reuse data already loaded in this tab. While visible, Orders checks for changes every five seconds; New order checks its customer, location and SKU options every 30 seconds. Checks pause in hidden tabs and resume immediately when you return or reconnect. On Orders, **Last checked** shows the most recent successful check. New order has no **Last checked** timestamp because the order has not been saved. **Updating** keeps existing data and your typed draft visible. A connection pause or failed refresh identifies **last-known data** rather than claiming it is current; failed reads offer **Try again**. Slow or interrupted connections can delay updates beyond the normal interval. Saving changes marks cached data for refresh. Switching brewery or signing out discards that cache. The first visit fetches data on demand with a short loading message, not placeholder rows. Ask MGR loads its conversation and history when you first select **Ask MGR** in the header, rather than when you enter the site.

**Last checked** uses the active brewery's timezone, not your device's timezone. Planned and requested dates remain calendar dates.

<Screen name="Today" />

The brewery name in the header is the brewery whose records you are looking at. Navigation has up to four sections: **Today**, **Beer**, **Work**, and **More**. ## Catalog: brands, formats, and SKUs [#catalog]

<Screen name="Catalog" />

*A brand is the beer, such as Hazy IPA. A format is the physical shape it ships or pours in, such as a half-barrel keg or a case of 16 oz cans. A SKU is one brand in one packaged format. SKUs hold inventory and appear on wholesale orders. A pour is a serving from the tap, not a SKU: it lives on the beer's price group, and a sale draws from keg stock. Admin and Sales can create brands, formats, and SKUs. Warehouse opens **Menu** under More and reads Catalog at its address without catalog mutation controls. Catalog is not reachable by Brewer.*

**Start an empty catalog.** With no brands yet, Catalog hides the search, sort, and category controls and offers **New brand** as the primary action. Add a brand, save it, then turn on its packaged formats under **Sells as** to create its SKUs: the sizes and packages you sell. Filters appear once brands exist, and stay available when a search or category has no matches so you can clear it.

**Read the brand list.** Each brand shows its **Brand**, **Style**, **ABV**, **SKUs**, and **COLA**. A staff-only alias appears under the name. **Not set** means no style or ABV is saved; a saved ABV of zero reads **0%**. **SKUs** counts saved packaged SKUs, active or inactive, not eligible formats or pours. It is not a stock or orderable count. Admin and Sales select a brand record to open it. For a role that cannot open the brand page, such as Warehouse alone, the name is plain text. On a wide screen the brands are a table; on a phone each brand lists the same facts under its name.

**Read a brand's COLA.** The **COLA** column shows the COLA approvals saved on the brand; every staff role that reads Catalog sees it. **None on file** means no COLA is recorded for the brand. It does not mean an application is pending or that approval is required. One saved COLA shows its TTB ID and its approval date, or *approval date unknown* when none was entered. Two or more show how many are on file, such as **2 on file**. Formula approvals and state registrations are not counted. A selected label image appears as a small thumbnail before the brand's name; when several approvals have images, Catalog shows the most recently approved one. A brand without a selected image leaves that space blank. The first time a saved label is shown, MGR retrieves it from TTB, checks that the COLA is approved and belongs to the brewery's permit, and keeps a copy; later views show that copy. A failed thumbnail shows **Retry**, not an unrelated label. Admin and Sales open the brand to see each approval under **Compliance**.

Stored label images do not count against your request limit. If MGR still needs to retrieve a label from TTB, that retrieval uses the normal limit; wait before selecting **Retry** after a “too many requests” error.

**Bulk process COLAs.** Admin and Sales select **Bulk process COLAs** to review labels for the brands currently shown in Catalog. Narrow the catalog search first if you want a smaller group.

1. Brands showing **None on file** start selected, so **Review … brands** processes every missing COLA at once. **Select brands with no COLA on file** returns to that selection; **Select all shown brands** and each brand's switch change it. MGR searches TTB once using the brewery's **TTB registry number** from Settings.
2. For each brand, choose a COLA from the suggested matches. Search the COLA list by name or TTB ID. When exactly one COLA matches, it is already chosen. Suggestions use its saved TTB IDs, name, and alias; nothing is saved until you confirm. Turn on **Show all COLAs under this permit** if the label has a different name.
3. Choosing a COLA loads its approved label images. Only an **APPROVED** COLA under the brewery's permit can supply images. The brand's saved image, or the only image TTB publishes, is already selected; otherwise choose the one Catalog should show. **Open COLA on TTB** opens the original record.
4. Select **Save and next**, or **Skip** to leave that brand unchanged. Saving an already recorded COLA updates it rather than adding a duplicate. If TTB publishes no image, you can save the approval without one. Other approvals stay on file.
5. The completion message counts saved and skipped brands. Each save applies immediately; closing the review keeps saved choices and discards only unsubmitted choices.

Changing a recorded approval's TTB ID, changing it to a formula, or clearing its approval date removes its selected image. Run the review again to choose a label for the corrected approval.

**Find a brand.** Above the brand list, **Search brands or aliases** finds brands whose name or alias contains what you type. The list narrows a moment after you stop typing; Enter searches at once. **Sort** orders the list by **Name** (A–Z), **ABV** (highest first; brands with no ABV come last), or **Newest** (most recently added first). **Category** shows one category's brands, or **All categories**; it appears once the brewery has a category. Sort and Category are drop-down lists aligned beside the search on a wide screen and under it on a phone. Choosing a sort or category reloads the list at once and keeps the search and the other setting. The search, sort and category stay in the page address, so a link or Back returns to the same list. While a search is on, **Clear search** below the controls removes it and keeps the sort and category. When nothing matches, Catalog reads **No brands match**; select **Clear search** or choose **All categories** to see every brand.

**Below the brands.** **Price groups** shows how many price groups the brewery has and opens them for Admin and Sales. With none yet, it reads **0 groups · Create one to price your brands**; open the row to create the first group. **Water profiles**, captioned **Manage water profiles**, opens the water profiles for Admin and Brewer; Brewer reaches Catalog only alongside another role. A row you cannot open shows no link and no **Open** arrow. With several roles you get every link any one of them opens.

### Create a brand

<Screen name="Brand" />

**Manage categories**, beside the **Category** field, lets Admin and Sales add a category, edit its name with **Save name**, or choose **Delete → Confirm delete**. Renaming immediately updates every brand using that category. Deletion is allowed only when no brands use it; first reassign those brands or save them as **Uncategorized**. Category changes save independently of **Save brand**. A success notification confirms each addition, update, or deletion. **Save brand** also shows **Brand saved** after a successful save. Adding a category does not change the brand's **Category**; pick it there and save the brand to assign it. Renaming the brand's current category updates the field to the new name. Blank category names are refused, and so is a name another category already has in any letter case: with **Core** on the list, typing `core` reads *A category named Core already exists* and **Add category** stays unavailable. A brand saved or imported with `core` is filed under **Core**. Existing category values are preserved, including values supplied by imports. New breweries can add their first category here.

1. Open **Catalog** and select **New brand**.
2. Enter **Brand name** (required), **Style** (optional; a new style is added to the brewery's list), and **ABV** (optional percentage from 0 to 99.99; type `6.5` for 6.5% or step it with **−**/**+**; a value outside that range is refused with *ABV must be a percent from 0 to 99.99*). **Description** and **Hops** are optional notes. **Category** uses the brewery’s shared list; choose **Uncategorized** to clear the assignment. **Price group** is the row of the [price grid](#pricing) this beer sits on; leave it **No price group** and none of its SKUs can be ordered until you choose one. **Manage price groups** opens **Price groups**, where groups are created. With no groups, the field says **No price groups yet.** When the brand has a recipe, a line under the field reads its cost per barrel (from the most recently brewed version, or the newest if none has been brewed, using each ingredient’s last receipt cost) against the groups’ ceilings: *Suggested group 2 · Recipe cost $48.10/bbl* with **Use**, which only fills the field; **Save brand** still commits. *Above every ceiling* means no band covers it, and *Recipe cost unknown* counts the ingredients that have never been received and names up to three, so no number is shown.
3. Optionally enter an **Alias**: an informal name staff also use, such as a series number. A beer named "Appaloosa" might carry the alias "Animal #14". Only staff see it: it shows under the name in Catalog and beside it in brand pickers ("Appaloosa · Animal #14"), and search finds the brand's SKUs by it, but buyers, the portal, Square and SKU names only ever show the brand name. A note under the field says so. Clear the field to remove it.
4. Select **Save brand**. Brand names must be unique within the brewery. The page moves to the new brand's own address, where **Sells as** and **Compliance** appear; until the first save they read *Save the brand to add SKUs and compliance.* In **Catalog** the brand lands alphabetically and its **SKUs** column reads **0**. Every switch under **Sells as** starts off.

With an empty or whitespace-only **Brand name**, a line beside **Save brand** says *Still needed: a brand name.* Enter the name to clear the hint and make saving available.

**New brand** opens the brand's own page rather than a dialog, and so does selecting a brand's name in **Catalog**. Catalog lists the brands, not their packages. The brand page starts with all saved values; changing one field keeps the others. The title shows the saved name and alias, and changes only when you save. **Save brand** keeps you on the page; use the **Catalog** link above the title to go back. On a wide screen the form is on the left (the name and style fields, then **Selling** with **Price group**, then **Sell sheet**, ending in **Save brand**), and **Sells as**, **Pours**, and **Compliance** are on the right; on a phone they follow the form. **Save brand** saves only the form: SKUs, compliance records and categories save on their own. Badges under the title show the brand's state at a glance: **COLA · None on file** (select it to jump to **Compliance**), the price group or **Unpriced**, the category, and the active SKU count. The brand page is for Admin and Sales; other roles see the catalog without brand links.

Brands cannot currently be deleted or archived. Edit a mistaken brand instead. Switching off a packaged format under **Sells as** deactivates that SKU; its inventory and history remain. This does not archive the brand itself.

**Compliance** lists the brand's COLA or formula approvals and state registrations, each with **Edit**. Each approved COLA with a saved label image shows that image directly beneath its approval, without opening the edit form. If several COLAs have images, the detail page shows each one alongside its own TTB ID and date. A brand with no recorded COLA shows *COLA · None on file*, matching Catalog without inferring an application status. **Add approval** opens the same form for a new record. The brand is stated, never chosen, and must be saved before compliance can be recorded.

**Where label images live.** MGR saves the selected TTB attachment reference and, the first time the label is shown, a copy of the image. Later views use that copy, so they no longer depend on TTB and do not recheck the COLA's status there; open the COLA on TTB to confirm it is still approved. A label you preview but do not save is not kept, and is retrieved from TTB each time. A failed retrieval shows an error and **Retry image**. Choosing a different image, or removing it, stops MGR showing the old copy. If another staff member changes or removes the selected image while your page is open, refresh the page to see the current selection.

**Load a known TTB ID.** Choose **COLA**, enter the 14-digit **TTB ID**, and select **Load COLA by TTB ID**. This does not depend on the label matching the brand's name. The field suggests IDs already saved on this brand. Loading retrieves the approval date and label images from TTB. Only an approved, dated COLA under the brewery's **TTB registry number** from Settings can supply images. A missing permit, unavailable registry, or unapproved COLA produces an error instead of an empty answer.

**Find a COLA by name.** **Find COLA on TTB** searches the brewery's permit for COLAs whose fanciful or brand name contains this brand's name, newest first. Each result shows its TTB ID, applicant serial, class, and completed date. **Use** fills the identifiers and loads its approved label choices automatically; the saved image, or the only published image, starts selected. If no name matches, enter the known ID directly. The name search covers the last 15 years.

**Choose and save the label.** Review the images and choose the one Catalog should show, then select **Save approval**. The fields and image save together; loading or choosing an image alone does not save anything. If TTB publishes no images, the approval can be saved without one. Entering an ID already on this brand updates its saved approval rather than inserting a duplicate, including when entered through **Add approval**. **Edit** keeps the identity of the record being edited. Changing the TTB ID or approval kind clears the draft image choice. Until the TTB ID has 14 digits, **Save approval** stays unavailable and says why, and leaving the field names the problem under it. **Cancel** closes the form without saving.

**Manual approval fields.** The **TTB ID** is TTB's identifier, such as `25318001000034`. The optional **Serial number** is the applicant's own serial, such as `260135`. Leading zeros are preserved. A formula asks only for its **Formula number**. **Date approved** is optional for manual entry; choosing an image requires the approved date returned by TTB. **Open COLA on TTB’s public registry** opens the original record by TTB ID, but the link alone does not verify approval. A COLA does not expire, so no expiry is asked for.

**State registrations.** **Add registration** takes a state from the same list as **Add license**, plus an optional number and expiry. **Save registration** waits for a state and explains what is still needed. **Cancel** closes the form without saving. One record exists per brand and state, so saving again updates it; **Edit** keeps the state fixed. The brewery's own state licenses live under [Compliance → Licenses](#compliance), not on the brand.

A COLA’s **TTB ID** must contain exactly 14 digits, including any leading zeros, as described by [TTB’s public-registry instructions](https://www.ttb.gov/system/files/images/pdfs/labeling_colas-docs/display-cola-detail-through-public-cola-registry.pdf). MGR explains this format beside the field and withholds **Save approval** and the public-registry link for a malformed ID. Formula numbers and applicant serial numbers are different identifiers and do not use this 14-digit rule. **Find COLA on TTB** searches by the brand and brewery permit, so you can use it to find the correct ID without already knowing it.

### Create a format

<Screen name="Formats" />

Each format's name opens it. On desktop, the format table keeps names, basis, and package contents left-aligned, with volume right-aligned for comparison. On a phone, each format lists the same **Basis**, **Volume**, and **From** under its name. Price groups remain a comparison grid with formats across the top; scroll that grid horizontally on a phone. Other wide tables also scroll within their own region, with quantities and amounts right-aligned and descriptive text left-aligned.

<Screen name="Format" />

1. In **Catalog**, scroll below the brand list and pricing links to **Formats**, then select **New format**. The Catalog screen preview shows the same section. Formats are shared across brands; each packaged SKU selects one.
   Selecting **Can** or **Bottle** requires a positive **Size of one container** and a whole **Containers per package** count. The form explains any missing size, count, or required name while creation is disabled.
2. Choose a **Container**. For a standard case, choose **Can**, enter **16 oz** for the size and **24** containers per package.
3. For kegs, choose **Keg size**. Standard sizes calculate beer volume automatically. **Custom size** accepts a volume. For cans and bottles, enter **Size of one container** and **Containers per package**; MGR calculates the total. A single can uses a count of 1.
4. Review **Beer per package** and the suggested name. Expand **Rename · optional** to use your own name. Select **Create format** to save and open its detail page. Packaged format names must be unique within the brewery: a name another packaged format already uses shows *A format named ½ bbl keg already exists* under **Format name**, and **Create format** stays unavailable until you rename it.

Catalog shows one entry per brand. Open the brand and switch a packaged format on under **Sells as** (see *Choose what a beer sells as* below). Glass sizes are not SKUs: add them on the beer’s [price group](#pricing) with **Add pour**. A pour’s serving size is fixed once a POS mapping, menu, or sale uses it; a rename still saves. Pours are not individually stocked and cannot enter wholesale orders or packaging materials. The taproom menu offers the group’s pours for each beer on that group.

Select the format's name, then **Edit format**. The same sizing fields are prefilled; existing names and custom volumes are preserved. Adjusting a legacy keg's custom volume keeps its existing fleet-size classification. Once a SKU, another package, packaging materials, or a POS mapping, menu, or sale uses a format, its volume, units per case, and serving ounces are fixed and a change is refused with **format is in use**; renaming still saves. Create a different format when another package needs a different definition. Recorded movement volumes, closed packaging yield, and consumed material quantities stay as recorded.

**Packaging materials · optional** is in the format editor. After the format exists, expand it to add trays, labels, or other materials. If none exist, follow **Add a material** and return. Choose distinct materials, enter positive quantities per complete package in each material’s base unit, and select **Used up** or **Return to stock** under **When unpacked**. **Save materials** saves the complete material list separately from **Save format**. Removing every row requires the explicit clear confirmation. Materials are optional and do not block creating formats or preparing imports. Warehouse can view definitions; Admin and Sales can edit them.

For packages built from smaller packages, expand **Build from other packages** and enable calculated volume. The **Package contents** picker appears immediately: choose smaller formats and their quantities, and enter the required format name. **Create format** saves the definition and contents together, then opens the finished format. If creation fails, neither is saved and your entries remain for correction or retry. The parent stores no volume: its total is always derived from the saved child quantities. Only packages with their own volume are eligible; already-composed packages cannot be nested. Existing unfinished formats can be completed through **Edit format → Package contents → Save contents**. A format with a typed volume, a poured format, or a format already used as a child cannot have its components replaced here. Clearing contents leaves no volume, so the format cannot hold stock until contents are added again.

Admins can open a packaged format, select **Edit format → Delete format**, and confirm its removal. **Delete format** sits beside **Save format**, with destructive styling. Deletion removes that format's contents and packaging-material assignments, leaving the smaller formats and materials themselves intact. Formats referenced by SKUs, other packages, prices or history cannot be deleted; the confirmation shows the refusal. Cancel leaves the format unchanged.

### Water profiles

<Screen name="Water profiles" />
<Screen name="Water profile" />

Brewer and Admin use **Water profiles**. Admin opens it from Catalog or Settings. Admin and Brewer also open it from the **Water profiles** row under the Recipes list. The **Water profiles** row on a recipe’s Water sheet opens it in a new tab, so the unsaved version stays put. When Admin arrives from **Settings → Source water**, the back arrow returns to Settings. Arriving from the Recipes list, it returns to Recipes. Other entries return to Catalog for Admin and to Recipes for Brewer. **Water profiles** keeps the water you start from and the waters you aim at: a name and six ions in parts per million (ppm, the same as mg/L): calcium, magnesium, sodium, sulfate, chloride and bicarbonate. **Add water profile** and **Edit** open the same sheet, and **Save water profile** saves it. When Save is disabled, **Still needed** beside it names the missing or out-of-range values. Every ion is required and cannot be negative, and two profiles cannot share a name. Three fields below the ions are optional, as the line above them says. **Alkalinity as CaCO3 (ppm)** is the total alkalinity from a water report; it cannot be negative, and bicarbonate does not stand in for it. **Water pH** is the water’s own pH, from 0 to 14. It is not mash pH: a recipe’s target mash pH is set on the recipe’s Water sheet. **Notes** holds up to 2000 characters, such as where a report came from. A row on the list shows alkalinity and water pH after the ions when the profile has them. To remove an optional value, clear it and save. A profile is not classified as a source or a target here, as the empty list explains. A recipe version’s Water sheet can pick its source and its target from this list, so one profile can serve either role. Nothing is calculated here: the page records a water report or a target. You enter the salt additions yourself.

Ion amounts and alkalinity accept 0 to 999,999.9 ppm and are stored rounded to one decimal place: for example, 999999.129 becomes 999999.1. Enter ordinary decimal numbers, not exponent notation. Invalid ion amounts, alkalinity or water pH keep Save unavailable; leaving the field shows its correction beside the measurement. Water pH is stored rounded to two decimal places. These are storage limits, not recommended water targets. Notes show a character counter when you approach their existing 2000-character limit.

Water profiles cannot currently be deleted or archived. Use **Edit** to correct a mistaken profile.

### Choose what a beer sells as

<Screen name="SKU" />

A saved brand's page has **Sells as** at the top of its right column. It lists every packaged format with a switch. On means the beer sells in that format. Each row also shows the format's price on each sale channel for the beer's price group, such as *DTC $22.00 · Wholesale $18.00*. A row reads **Unpriced** when no channel has a price for it, and every row does when the brand has no price group.

If no eligible packaged formats exist, **Sells as** explains the next step. Select **Manage formats** to open Catalog at its **Formats** section, create a packaged format with **New format**, then return to the brand and switch it on. Creating a format alone does not create any SKU or change the Catalog SKU count. Inactive saved SKUs still count. The addresses `/catalog/skus` and `/catalog/skus/new` redirect to Catalog, where you choose the brand; there is no separate Add SKU form.

1. Open the brand from **Catalog**.
2. Under **Sells as**, turn on the switch for a format, such as **Case**.
3. The switch flips at once. The first time, MGR creates the SKU, named *Brand · Format*, with no UPC and no keg pool. After that, the SKU is available in inventory, pricing, and order forms.

A brand has at most one SKU per format. Admin and Sales can flip the switches. All switches are disabled for a moment while one saves. If the save fails, the switch flips back and the error shows under the switches.

**Switching off** makes the SKU inactive. It is never deleted: stock, orders, and history keep it. Switch it back on to sell it again. That reuses the same SKU with its UPC, keg pool, and batch share.

**Package contents.** A format that is inside another format, such as a Four Pack inside a Case through **Package contents**, has no switch of its own. While its parent is on, it shows as a read-only line under the switches, such as **Four Pack · 6 per Case**, with its prices. If the beer already has a SKU in that smaller format, that format keeps its switch.

**Pours** lists the price group's pours, each with its serving size and prices. They're read-only here. A pour is a serving from the tap, not a SKU. An empty section explains where to add one. **Edit on price group** opens [Price groups](#pricing), where you add and edit pours and set prices. A brand with no saved price group instead asks you to choose a group and save the brand; it offers no edit link until then. Format setup and pricing actions appear only for staff allowed to use them.

**One-way kegs** (disposable shells you buy and never get back) are a keg format of their own, not a keg pool. Create a format for the shell's size, add the shell as a packaging material on it (category Packaging, counted in each, with its supplier as default vendor), and choose **None** for the keg pool on each SKU. Each keg packaged then draws one shell from stock, Planning orders shells from that supplier ahead of packaging runs, and no deposit is charged. Keep returnable kegs on their own format so they never draw a shell. Instead of a deposit, each one-way keg shipped carries the brewery's **One-way keg fee** (set in [Settings](#settings)) as its own invoice line. The fee is never refunded. The fee in force when the order is submitted is frozen on the line, including a zero fee, so a fee changed later does not reprice it when staff adjust the order or when the invoice is written at delivery. A submitted order's page lists these fees and the keg deposits under **Keg charges**. A fee of zero adds no line.

**Edit SKU** sits beside the switch on any **Sells as** row that has a SKU, on or off. It changes **Active**, **UPC**, and the **Keg pool** for keg formats. A format switched on for the first time has no keg pool, so open **Edit SKU** to choose one, or leave it at **None** for no deposit. A pool must be in service and the format must have a keg size. Its kind sets the container source automatically. A retired pool already linked to a SKU stays linked; it cannot be newly selected. Clearing or changing the pool affects future charges, not existing invoice deposits or refunds. Orders already submitted retain their deposit rows. Adjusting a confirmed or picked order can add a missing deposit to retained keg lines. Unticking **Active** does the same as switching the format off. Deactivating it removes it from New order choices and the portal catalog, and prevents adding it to new orders; recorded inventory and order history remain. Reactivate it to sell it again at its current grid price. UPC is 8, 12, 13 or 14 digits and unique within the brewery; clear the field to remove it. Any other UPC is named under the field once you leave it, and **Save SKU** stays unavailable with a *Still needed* line until it is fixed or cleared. A SKU's own UPC overrides its [price group's](#pricing) code for that format. A SKU with no UPC of its own scans as its group's code, and has no barcode when the group has none for that format. The brand and format stay fixed, and provider mappings are preserved.

**Batch package share (%)** on **Edit SKU** is the share of a batch's beer volume normally packaged into this SKU. Create the SKU first, then edit it to set a percentage above 0 and at most 100. A share outside that range is named under the field once you leave it, and **Save SKU** stays unavailable with a *Still needed* line. All package shares for one brand, including inactive SKUs, must total no more than 100%; they need not use the whole batch. Leave the field blank to remove its projection. Only active SKUs with a share create projected demand on [Planning](#purchasing).

**BBL per unit** is the barrel volume of one inventory unit and lives on the format, so every SKU in that format shares it. One US beer barrel is 31 gallons (3,968 fl oz). Half-barrel keg = `0.5`. Sixth-barrel keg is about `0.16666667`. A 24 × 16 oz case is `384 ÷ 3968`, about `0.09677419`. Set it on the format before creating a SKU for it: once a SKU, another package, packaging materials, or a POS mapping, menu, or sale uses the format, its BBL per unit is fixed.

Catalog shows this as **Volume** in brewery units: common keg fractions stay in bbl, smaller exact totals use whole gallons, and anything under a gallon uses fluid ounces. So `0.5` reads as **½ bbl**, a 24 × 16 oz case as **3 gal**, and a can as **16 oz**. Composed formats show the calculated total from their saved contents (four 16 oz cans show **64 oz**), with those contents under **From**. The format detail and each brand’s SKU list show the same derived total. Formats without a volume or saved contents show **—** in Catalog. A volume that converts to no whole unit stays in bbl. MGR still stores and calculates all beer volume in bbl.

An empty Catalog says **No brands yet** and an empty Formats section **No formats yet**.

Wholesale Shop shows the packages the brewery has designated for wholesale (½ keg, ⅙ keg, case, bottle), grouped under the beer name. Buyers do not see warehouse counts. Staff make that designation on the packaging plan. Prices come from the buyer's [sale channel](#pricing) and the beer's price group; a package with no price for that buyer does not appear on Shop.

A negative **Available** is red and has **Shortfall** under it. Select **Shortfall** to see what competes for that SKU in [Taproom replenishment](#replenishment). Select a SKU’s name to open its SKU detail.

**Bins.** A bin is a named place inside a location: a walk-in, a cold room, a dry rack. Every location starts with three, **Cold**, **Dry**, and **Walk-in**, and always keeps at least one; add, rename, or remove them from [Locations](#locations). A bin that has ever recorded stock cannot be removed, even at a zero balance, because the movement history still names it; rename it instead. A bin a POS menu uses cannot be removed either, so the menu, its prices, and its public menu address stay intact; configure that location's menu against another bin first. When shipping, choose the actual source bin and lot for each quantity; tracked lots stay identified through shipment and returns.

<Screen name="SKU detail" />

A SKU’s name on Inventory opens its SKU detail, separate from **Edit SKU** in Catalog. Admin and Warehouse get **Record movement** in its header, which opens the movement form with that SKU already chosen. It shows on-hand by location, global **Available** and allocations, and immutable paginated movement history, including inactive and zero-stock SKUs. Locations and bins retain their recorded identities; untracked lots are labeled **Untracked**. Each movement's volume reads in barrels to two decimal places.

**Reverse movement** is available to Admin and Warehouse only for a standalone `adjustment` or `loss` that has not already been reversed. MGR appends the exact opposite quantity and frozen barrel volume in the original bin and lot, preserving package and reporting classification. The original remains; **Reversal recorded** and **Original movement** link the two entries. Removing a prior positive adjustment is refused if that exact bin/lot no longer holds enough stock. Retry an unchanged failed form to recover the same result.

Sales sees history without correction controls. Shipments retain **Return shipment** through their order workflow. ### Create or edit a customer

<Screen name="Customer detail" />

### Create or edit a ship-to

<Screen name="Ship-to form" />

Select the customer name, then **New ship-to** or **Edit**. Choose the **Country**: a new ship-to starts in the customer's country, and an edited one keeps its own. For a US address, **Label** and **State** (chosen from the list of US states) are required. **Address**, **City**, and **Postal code** are optional until you know them; a postal code you enter must be a 5-digit ZIP code or ZIP+4, such as 97201-1234, and an invalid one is marked under the field. If someone else saved that label first, **Save ship-to** is refused with the same message under **Label**. **Save ship-to** stays disabled until the form is complete, and the line beside it names what is still needed, such as *Still needed: a state.* Outside the US only the **Label** is required, and there is no **State**: add the street, city, and postal code when you know them. **Address 2 (optional)** holds a suite or second address line. **Default ship-to** selects this address for new staff and portal orders. Select **Save ship-to** to save. Setting it replaces the previous default for this customer; clearing it leaves no designated default, so new orders start with the first address. Existing orders keep their chosen destination. Ship-to state is the destination used when a wholesale shipment leaves inventory.

Admin and Sales can select **Delete** beside a ship-to that is not the customer's default, then confirm. A ship-to that any order names cannot be deleted: MGR says *This ship-to is on an order and cannot be deleted. Orders keep their destination.* The default ship-to offers no **Delete**: make another ship-to the default first, then delete this one.

Empty lists say **No customers yet** or **No ship-to addresses yet**. Only an unused customer can be deleted; see [Delete an unused customer](#customers). Admin and Sales can select **Invite portal user**, enter an email, and **Send invite**. **Send invite** stays unavailable, with *Still needed: a valid email address*, until the email is complete and within 64 characters before the @ and 254 in total. This creates buyer access for this customer only; there is no Account admin choice. The recipient chooses their name and password from the email. After an uncertain response, use **Retry saved request** to recover the original invitation. Changing the email waits until that request is recovered; reloading the same browser tab preserves it. A definitive first rejection allows a corrected new invitation. An invitation interrupted before its email was sent stops blocking after 15 minutes. Existing sign-in accounts get no email. They see the invitation in [Invitations](/invitations) the next time they sign in, and access is not granted until they accept there, within seven days.

**Portal users** lists each buyer who can sign in for this customer, by email, and says **No portal users yet** when there are none. Admin and Sales can select **Remove access** beside a buyer and confirm. The buyer loses this customer's portal at once, even if they are signed in: their next page or action is refused. Their orders, invoices, and sign-in account stay. To restore access, send a new invitation and have the buyer accept it. Replaying an old accepted invitation never restores removed access.

## Price groups [#pricing]

<Screen name="Price groups" />
<Screen name="Price group" />

*Admin and Sales manage prices. Order lines snapshot their price when the order is created.*

Price groups are the rows of the price grid, with formats across the top. Each [sale channel](#channels) has its own tab above the grid, and the grid shows the selected channel; the first channel shows when you open the page, and the address remembers the tab you choose. Put each brand on a group (**Catalog** → **Brand** → **Price group**) and fill the cells; every SKU of that brand then sells at its format's cell on the customer's channel. An empty cell means unpriced, and an order for that SKU is refused. Clear a cell to unprice it. A group a brand sits on, a pour belongs to, or a cell is filled for, cannot be deleted.

On a phone or at high browser zoom, scroll the page down to reach every group above the bottom navigation. Scroll the grid horizontally to reach the remaining formats; Tab also brings each price field into view.

1. Open **Price groups** and select **New price group**. The sheet is the same one a group's name opens. Enter a required **Group name**. **Position** is required: it is the group's row in the price grid, a whole number from 1 to 2,147,483,647 where lower numbers sit higher and the lowest is the top row, pre-filled after the last row. **Cost ceiling ($/bbl) · optional** comes next, with the previous group's ceiling below it for reference. Then select **Save price group**. It stays disabled until the group has a name and valid field values, with the missing correction beside it. Name and position must each be unique within the brewery. If either is already used, the message names the duplicate: **Another price group already uses that name.** or **Another price group already uses position 2.** Change that field and save again. Editing the name clears the message.
2. On that group, **Add pour** and enter **Serving size · oz** (for example 14). It takes a decimal number above 0 and under 1000, kept to four decimal places, so the smallest is 0.0001; exponent notation such as 1e1 is refused, and so is a size that rounds to 0 (like 0.00004) or to 1000 (like 999.99995). Once you leave the field, the reason shows under it, and **Save pour** says what is missing until the size is valid. Expand **Rename · optional** to call it something other than the default *14 oz pour*. Pours belong to the price group, not the beer. Every beer on the group uses them. A pour stays on the group it was added to; it cannot move to another group. The grid adds a column by that name. To price the same pour on every group, give it the same name on each (for example **Half**, with each group's own serving size). A group with no pour of that column's name shows **+ add** there instead of a price; it opens **Add pour** for that group with the name filled in. **Remove** on a pour asks you to confirm, then deletes it. Removal is refused while its price cells are filled, or while a Square mapping, menu line or recorded sale uses it; clear the cells and mappings first. **Edit pour** beside **Remove** changes the serving size or name later; the serving size is fixed once a POS mapping, menu, or sale uses it, but a rename still saves. A beer's page lists its group's pours under **Pours** read-only, with **Edit on price group** linking here.
3. Choose a channel's tab, then type a non-negative price in dollars with at most two decimal places into a cell. Zero is a valid price; negative amounts, exponent notation (such as `1e3`), and extra decimal places are refused with an inline explanation, not rounded. Press Enter, press Tab once, or select elsewhere to save it as whole cents. The cell reads **Saving…**, then **Saved** after confirmation. A failed save shows its error under the cell and keeps your entered amount. Every SKU of every brand on that group sells at that price, in that format, on that channel. An unpriced cell is empty and shows **—**.
4. To unprice a filled cell, empty it and leave the input (press Enter, press Tab, or select elsewhere). The price is removed and those SKUs stay unpriced on that channel until you save a price. There is no separate Clear button or confirmation. To undo a removal, type the price again.
5. Under **Barcodes**, each packaged format has a **UPC** row: select **Add** (or **Edit**), enter the code, and **Save UPC**. Every SKU of every brand on that group scans as that code for that format, unless the SKU has its own UPC. One code may cover several formats, for example the can label's code on both the Four Pack and the Case. A format with no code reads **none**; that is normal for kegs and raises no warning. Save it blank to clear it. A code is 8, 12, 13 or 14 digits; any other code is named under the field once you leave it, and **Save UPC** stays unavailable with a *Still needed* line.
6. To delete a group, open it by name, select **Delete**, and confirm. Its barcodes go with it.

Pour columns are headed **Pour ·** and the name, for example **Pour · Half**.

Opening an existing group also shows a **Prices** line summarizing where it's priced already, for example `$132.00 on Wholesale · ½ bbl keg, and 3 more cells`, or **none** if nothing prices it yet.

A cost ceiling is dollars per barrel of recipe cost. Position sorts the rows, and the ceilings are read in that order. A brand's page suggests the first group, from the top row down, whose ceiling is at or above the brand's recipe cost; groups with no ceiling are skipped (see [Catalog](#catalog)). Saving a ceiling never moves a brand or changes a price. A brewery with no groups yet says **Add a price group, then put each brand on one from Catalog.**

## Staff orders [#orders]

Orders show up to 50 records per page, newest first. **More** opens older orders only when another one exists; **Newest** returns to the latest page. Status and customer filters remain applied while paging. Changing a filter starts at its newest orders.

**Status** under the Work tabs picks **All states** or one state: **Draft**, **Submitted**, **Confirmed**, **Picked**, **Shipped**, **Delivered**, or **Cancelled**. **Delivered** lists shipped orders whose delivery stop was closed as delivered or partly delivered; **Shipped** still lists every shipped order. Once its stop closes, a shipped order reads **Delivered**, **Partly delivered**, or **Refused** in the list and on the order page, with no next step. A shipped pickup order reads **Shipped** with no next step. The choice is kept in the page address, so a link, a refresh, or Back keeps it. Opening Orders from a customer adds a **Customer:** line naming that customer, even when it has no orders; if the customer cannot be loaded, it says **Customer unavailable**. While that line shows, the list drops its **Customer** column, since every row is that customer's. Changing **Status** keeps the customer. **Clear** removes only the customer and keeps the status. A filter that finds nothing says **No orders for** that customer (or **No shipped orders for** it, with a status chosen).

An unrecognized status in an Orders link is ignored and shows **All states**.

On a wide screen the list is a table with **Order**, **Customer**, **Requested ship date**, and **Status** columns, followed by the next action. On a phone each order shows the same facts, labeled, under its order number. The order number opens the order; an order not yet numbered is titled **Order**. An order without a customer, such as a transfer, says **transfer**. **Requested ship date** is the day the customer asked for, not a deadline or a shipped date; an order without one says **Not scheduled**. **Status** shows the order's state. An order with staged beer also shows **Restock staged**. On a confirmed or picked order, put the beer back before picking again. A cancelled order that had been picked, or a shipped order that left picked beer behind, also shows **Restock staged** beside its status but has no list button; Warehouse and Admin put that beer back from the **Put back** row on Today.

*Admin and Sales create and manage orders. Admin and Warehouse record picks and shipments. Admin, Sales, and Warehouse can view the order list and detail.*

`draft` → `submitted` → `confirmed` → `picked` → `shipped`

An order can become `cancelled` any time before shipping. The Orders screen lists newest first and can filter by any status.

Orders list actions follow your role. Sales and Admin see **Finish** on drafts and **Confirm** on submitted orders. Warehouse and Admin see **Pick**, **Ship** (**Complete transfer** for a taproom transfer), and **Put back**. ### Create an order

<Screen name="New order" />

1. Open **Orders** and select **New order** to open the order-entry page. The **Orders** back link leaves without saving. **New order** under **Customer activity** on a customer's page, or on Orders filtered to one customer, opens the form with that customer and its default ship-to already chosen. A brewery with only one location starts with it as **Source location**. With several, Source starts on the [Portal fulfillment warehouse](#portal-fulfillment-warehouse) chosen in **Settings**; with none chosen there, you choose. **Requested ship** starts blank.
2. Choose **Kind**: **Wholesale** (default) or **Taproom transfer**.
3. For wholesale, choose the **Customer**; its default **Ship-to** is selected automatically (the first address if no default is set), and you can choose another. For a transfer, choose the destination in **To location**; it must differ from the source, or saving fails with "A transfer needs a different source and destination location."
4. Choose the source in **Source location**. Both location selects list every location of the brewery, not just taprooms or warehouses, so you pick a warehouse source and a taproom destination yourself.
5. Optionally choose **Requested ship** from the calendar and enter **Customer PO**. A date before today, in the brewery's time zone, shows a warning under the dates; the order still saves with it. Turn on **Pickup** for a wholesale order the customer collects at the brewery. A pickup order is never put on a delivery route. It still names a ship-to, which stays its destination for tax reporting. The order page shows **Delivery** as **Pickup**.
6. Add one or more lines. Select or search the available SKUs, then type a quantity greater than zero or use **−** and **+**. SKU labels show the brand once, followed by the format. Once a wholesale customer is chosen, the SKU list offers only active SKUs priced on that customer's sale channel; a transfer offers every active SKU. If a line's SKU has no price for the customer, saving names that SKU and says it is not active and priced for this customer. **Add line** adds a row. **Remove** takes one away, but at least one row stays. Availability is shown only when supplied; a missing availability figure does not mean zero stock.
   The line list and **Select SKU** picker show **Loading customer-priced SKUs** while the first pricing read runs. **Waiting for connection** means the read will resume when you reconnect. A failed read shows its error and **Try again**. Previously loaded choices stay visible after a failed refresh and are marked **Showing last-known data**. A successful empty list says **No active SKUs are priced for this customer**; check **Price groups** or choose another customer. **No matching SKUs** means your search does not match a loaded choice.

   Changing customers keeps entered quantities and SKU selections. A selection absent from the new customer's options remains visible with an **unavailable** warning. Choose an available SKU or remove that line before saving; no entered unavailable selection is silently dropped. Compatible selections stay valid. **Save draft** waits for the first pricing read and remains disabled while any selected SKU is unavailable. The server checks eligibility again when saving.

7. Select **Save draft**. Saving the draft assigns the order number. The button stays disabled until the required fields are chosen: for wholesale, a customer, a ship-to, a source location, and at least one line with an available SKU and a quantity above zero; for a transfer, a destination instead of the customer and ship-to. The first pricing read must finish, and every selected SKU must be available. Wholly blank spare rows are ignored. Every row you start must have both a SKU and a finite quantity greater than zero. Otherwise, once you leave the line's quantity, the missing SKU or the bad quantity is marked, with numbered feedback under the line asking you to complete or remove it, and saving stays disabled. The line beside **Save draft** names what is still needed, such as *Still needed: a ship-to.* No entered product is silently omitted. Saving opens the draft order, where you can submit it. Wholesale line prices are captured from the cell where the customer's sale channel meets each beer's price group. Later price changes do not rewrite those lines.

Changing **Kind** does not mark untouched lines as errors. Line corrections appear only after you start a line and leave its quantity field.

It links only the pages that add what is missing: **Customers** for a customer and **Catalog** for a SKU. Only an admin can add a location, so a missing location is named but not linked; ask an admin to add one. A transfer never needs a customer, so it never asks for one or links **Customers**. **Save draft** stays disabled until those exist. The **Customer** picker itself says **No customers yet.** with **Add a customer**.

### Move an order through fulfillment

<Screen name="Confirm order" />

Every confirmation goes through this review. A confirmation also waits for a line raised or a standing allocation set on that SKU at the same moment. Stock removed or counted at that same moment can still be missed by the warning. Shipping still refuses stock that is not on hand. Each line's quantity is a number field: change one and the stock and availability notes follow it, a required **Reason** appears, and **Save lines** saves the new quantities (or **Discard changes** puts them back). If cancelling fails, the error shows inside that dialog; the button reads **Cancelling…** while it runs, and closing the dialog clears the reason and error.

Emails describe the order as confirmed; later order edits do not rewrite them. Sending requires the brewery's hosted email setup. Delivery failures do not undo the confirmed order. MGR retries uncertain sends with the same saved message for up to 23 hours, then stops for investigation to avoid sending duplicates. Provider acceptance does not prove the email reached an inbox. Removed buyers and changed email addresses are checked before a pending message is sent; an email already accepted by the provider cannot be recalled.

Admin and Sales can check **Confirmation email** under **Order details**. It counts the saved outcomes for all buyers: **Queued**, **Sending**, **Retry pending**, **Accepted by provider**, **Blocked**, or **Suppressed**. A blocked email explains whether there was no linked portal buyer, the provider rejected it, or the retry window expired. **No confirmation email recorded** is not proof that an email was sent. For **Retry pending** or **Blocked**, ask the person who manages your email setup to investigate; do not resend an uncertain email as a new message. Refresh the order to read the latest status. Warehouse does not see these diagnostics.

It stays available after the route returns. The **Return location** starts at the location the beer shipped from. A line with one shipped source starts with its outstanding refused quantity, in the location's only bin when it has just one; changing the location resets these starting values. Then for each shipped source (lot and bin) enter the **Received quantity** (no more than what is outstanding), a **Destination bin** at that location, and the **Damaged** switch. A quantity above the outstanding refusal is refused. If the shipment was invoiced at ship, this page instead says to use **Return and credit** (Admin or Sales), and its button opens that form for the refused quantities. Put back appears only when picked beer exceeds what the order now takes; raising a line never needs it. The page lists each line's staged quantity: everything picked on a cancelled order, what was picked but not shipped on a shipped order, or the picked excess on an order adjusted down. **Put back N** clears the flag, lowers each line's picked amount to what the order still takes, and records a *restocked* event with the amounts put back, so a later ship does not flag the same beer again; nothing moves in the ledger, because the beer never left the warehouse. An order whose flag is already clear says so instead of offering the button. After **Put back N**, MGR returns to the order. |

For a refused delivery, Admin or Sales can open the shipped order and choose **Reorder refused beer**. MGR creates an editable draft containing only the refused quantities, with the same customer, ship-to, fulfillment source, pickup choice, customer PO and note. It uses current prices and leaves the new ship date blank for scheduling. Review or change the draft before submitting it. Each shipped order is reordered once: afterwards its history shows **Reorder created** with the draft's number, the button is gone, and another attempt is refused. This does not check in the refused beer or move stock; use **Check in refused beer** separately when the truck brings it back.

An order waiting for **Put back** cannot record another pick or short pick. Confirm **Put back** first so the timeline retains the physical put-back quantities; recording a pick never clears that obligation.

The Ship preview and the shipment confirmation describe each stock change as **removed for sale**. When the ship-to has a state, the row adds **ships to PA** (using that state's abbreviation); with no state supplied, that clause is absent.

The order page reads as one document. The header names the order number, the customer (or **Taproom transfer**), the status, and the next step. The verbs your role can use for that status sit beside it on a wider screen and below it on a phone. The main verb comes first, in reading and tab order: a confirmed order shows **Record pick**, then **Add or remove lines**; a picked order shows **Ship** (or **Complete transfer**), then **Record pick** and **Add or remove lines**. While picked beer is staged for restocking, **Put back** comes first instead and the fulfillment verbs turn secondary, because the beer must go back before anything ships. Admin and Sales see **Cancel order**, a quiet red action at the bottom of the page, after the history; it still asks for a reason and confirmation.

The pending restock note (naming what to put back) and the **changed after pick** note sit directly under the header. **Order details** lists the fulfillment source, the ship-to (or **Pickup**), the requested ship date, the customer PO, the **Invoice** once the shipment is invoiced (select its number to open it), and the note when one was entered. A fact with no value reads **Not specified**, including an empty or whitespace-only customer PO. History shows a reason only when one was recorded; older blank cancellation reasons have no trailing separator. A taproom transfer has no ship-to or PO, and the page does not display its destination.

**Price summary** sits beside the details on a wider screen and before the lines on a phone. It is in US dollars and never includes tax:

- A submitted, confirmed or picked wholesale order shows **Ordered items**, **Returnable keg deposits** and **One-way keg fees**, then **Order amount · before tax**. This is the ordered quantities at the prices and keg charges captured on the order. It is not an invoice or a balance due.
- A shipped order shows the same ordered amount and says it is not the final invoiced amount. The invoice bills what actually shipped.
- A cancelled order shows **Cancelled order amount · before tax**, as history rather than an amount to collect. A draft cancelled before it was ever submitted shows an **Items estimate** instead, because its keg charges were never set.
- A draft shows an **Items estimate** only. Keg deposits and fees read **Not finalized until submission**, because they are set when the order is submitted.
- A taproom transfer says it is not a customer charge and shows no amount.
- As a safeguard, if keg charge data is ever missing from the order, the page shows the ordered items only and says **Order amount unavailable** rather than a total that leaves charges out.

Each line amount is the ordered quantity times the captured unit price, rounded to the cent. The ordered-items subtotal is the sum of those rounded line amounts. A one-way keg fee is charged per keg, so it is multiplied by the line's quantity. A deposit uses the amount saved on the order.

The one-way fee is captured when a line is submitted, including a zero fee. Raising the brewery's fee later does not add a fee to that existing line when its quantity is adjusted. Newly added lines capture the fee in effect when they are added.

**Ordered items** is a table on a wider screen and a list of labeled lines on a phone. Each line shows its ordered quantity, the captured unit price, and the line amount. **Picked** and **Shipped** appear once any line has that count; a line not yet counted then reads **Not recorded**. **Available (all locations)** appears only while the order is submitted or confirmed. It counts every location, not only the fulfillment source, and missing availability reads **Not supplied**, never zero. A short reason shows under its line as **Short:**. A line picked above its ordered amount carries a warning in words, and so does a line whose available quantity is below zero while the order is submitted or confirmed. **Keg charges** lists each deposit and one-way fee with the line it belongs to. **History** lists each event in the brewery's time zone and who made it: staff by their saved name (or full email when no name is saved), a portal buyer by email (Admin and Sales; others read **a user not on the team** for a buyer, as everyone does for a removed staff member); an empty history says **No events yet**. An empty order list says **No orders yet**, or names the status or customer filter that found nothing. A shipped or cancelled order has no further action controls.

An empty **Draft** filter says **Drafts you save appear here.** Drafts do not need to be placed to appear in this list.

Admin and Sales also see a separate **Cost and margin** section on a shipped order, below the keg charges and apart from the price summary: what the beer it shipped cost, and the margin on it. Cost is the per-package cost frozen when each shipped lot was packaged (see [Inventory](#inventory)); beer that came back through a credit memo or a checked-in refusal is taken out of both cost and revenue. Revenue is the shipped quantity at the order line prices, so an order whose invoice is deferred to delivery already shows its margin. Margin is a percent of that revenue. A shipped unit with no cost (opening-balance stock, historical runs, or a lot with no receipt cost) is counted but not priced: the line then reads *N units without cost, margin withheld*, and the cost shown is only the costed part. Warehouse does not see the line.

Shipping asks for each line's actual **source bin and lot**. When exactly one recorded bin and lot holds a line's whole picked quantity, the page starts with that source filled in; a transfer with only one destination bin also starts with that bin chosen. Check it, and change it if the beer came from somewhere else. Otherwise select **Add source** for every bin/lot used. Each new source quantity starts at what the line still needs: the edited shipped quantity minus its other sources. Choose the actual bin and lot, then adjust the source quantity if needed. The sources must sum to the shipped quantity, with each bin and lot chosen only once; zero lines have no sources. **Ship order** stays disabled until each source has a bin and lot and a positive quantity. The reason beside the button names what to complete, or shows both the current source total and the shipped quantity to match. A line with no recorded stock in any bin at the ship-from location says so, instead of asking for a source it cannot offer. Untracked legacy stock is labeled explicitly. A selected source must have enough stock when the shipment commits. An internal transfer also requires its destination bin before **Complete transfer** becomes available and preserves the same lot on both legs. Shipping keeps one invoice line per order line even when several sources supplied it. On-delivery shipping moves the beer once; delivery confirmation creates only the invoice.

After shipping, the confirmation shows the recorded quantities and the invoice link when one was created. To correct the shipment, select **Return shipment** there; it opens the invoice's Beer return page (see [Return beer and correct the invoice](#invoices)). **On delivery** shipments say the invoice is deferred: the return starts on the invoice that delivery raises. Refreshing this page keeps the recorded confirmation.

## Invoices and credit memos [#invoices]

Invoices show up to 50 records per page, newest first. **More** opens older invoices only when another one exists; **Newest** returns to the latest page. **Show** picks **All invoices** or **Unpaid**. Unpaid keeps only invoices that still owe money: not written off, voided or deleted in QuickBooks, and either holding a positive QuickBooks balance or, with no QuickBooks balance, not marked paid. With none left it says **No unpaid invoices**. **Open balance** on a customer's page opens the list filtered to that customer: a **Customer** line names them, **Show** keeps them, and **Clear** returns to every customer. The list comes before the QuickBooks status and **Sync QuickBooks**; when QuickBooks needs connecting, that row stays at the top.

With no invoices, the list explains that invoices appear after you ship an order. You can then push them to QuickBooks or record payments in MGR; a QuickBooks push is not required for an invoice to appear. **Email delivery status** is unavailable because MGR cannot tell whether QuickBooks emailed an invoice. That explanation stays below the locked row at normal text contrast.

**Paid** is a payment status, not a settlement date. For an invoice in QuickBooks, MGR does not supply an actual payment date for one or multiple payments. An invoice paid in full by payments recorded in MGR is marked paid on the day its last payment was received, and **Payments** on the invoice lists each payment's received day. A partial cash payment remains due until the balance reaches zero; credit-only closure is **Settled**, not **Paid**. In the register, a balance closed with a mix of partial cash and credits shows **Settled** with the cash amount, not **Paid**. MGR does not display a settlement date for an invoice in QuickBooks.

An invoice reads as a document. Its number and state in words (such as **Unpaid**, **Partially paid**, **Settled**, **Paid**, **No payment due**, **Voided**, **Written off**, or **Credit memo**) head the page, with the customer under them. **Issued** and **Due** are separate facts; an invoice without a due date says **Not set** for Due. An invoice also names its **Order**; select the order number to open it. A credit memo has no Order fact. Beside them on a wide screen, and before the lines on a phone, the **Price summary** is the one place that shows the invoice's total. It adds the stored line amounts by line kind: **Items**, **Keg deposits** (refunds included), **One-way keg fees**, and **Adjustments** for anything else, so the rows always add up. **Total** is the QuickBooks total once QuickBooks has one, even zero, and otherwise the sum of the lines. When a QuickBooks sync reports that accounting edited the invoice there, the summary labels **Current invoice total** and shows **Frozen MGR line subtotal** beside it; the original lines do not change, and the difference is reviewed in QuickBooks. An invoice QuickBooks taxed but nobody edited instead shows a **Tax (QuickBooks)** row, so the rows add up to **Total**. A voided invoice reads **Total** $0.00 with no balance. A credit memo shows a negative **Credit memo total**, which is not money the customer owes. Under the total, an invoice that is still owed shows the **Outstanding balance** from QuickBooks and any **Cash received in QuickBooks**. For an invoice QuickBooks never received, the outstanding balance is the total minus payments recorded in MGR, and a **Paid in MGR** row shows their sum. A balance QuickBooks has not supplied reads **Unknown**, never $0.00. A paid invoice shows its balance only when it is known: from QuickBooks, or $0.00 with a **Paid in MGR** row for an invoice paid by payments recorded in MGR. Credit memos and voided, deleted, or written-off invoices show no balance due. **Line items** lists each line's description, quantity, unit price, and amount: a table on a wide screen, labeled records on a phone. The amount is the stored line amount, not recalculated from quantity and price. **Payments** lists each payment recorded in MGR, newest received first, with its amount, received day, method and reference; a voided payment shows **Voided** and its reason. Admin and Sales see **Record payment** there only on an invoice that can take one (see [Record a payment in MGR](#record-payment)), so it is hidden for a QuickBooks invoice, a pending push, a push to a previous QuickBooks company, and a paid, written-off, or $0.00 invoice. **QuickBooks and accounting** states the invoice's accounting status once, then **Open in QuickBooks** and any permitted push, retry, re-push, or write-off action. QuickBooks reconciliation is manual. Admin and Sales choose **Sync QuickBooks** from **Invoices**; Admin can also start it from **Settings → Accounting**. **Invoices** shows the sync only while QuickBooks is connected. Otherwise its QuickBooks row says **Not connected** or **Reconnect required**. Only a previously connected brewery sees the warning that payment status may be stale until QuickBooks is reconnected and synced; a brewery that never connected does not see that warning or sync history in Accounting. When QuickBooks is turned on in MGR, Admin chooses **Connect** on the invoice row to open the connection page, while Sales sees **Admin must connect**. When it is not turned on, Connect is disabled before navigation and both Invoices and Accounting say **QuickBooks isn’t turned on in MGR yet.** A saved batch waits until QuickBooks is reconnected; **Retry saved sync** then appears on **Invoices** again. While the sync is shown, it lists the last successful sync and who ran it beside the button. The latest attempt appears only when it did not finish or was superseded. The latest recorded failure stays visible beside the sync. The operator appears by full name, or by email when no name is set. Until a whole batch succeeds, displayed payment status may be stale. A provider failure never advances the last-success time.

A sync re-reads every open and part-paid invoice. It also re-reads an invoice for 90 days after a sync first sees it paid in full, voided, or deleted. After those 90 days, MGR stops reading that invoice. A later QuickBooks edit, reversed payment, or reopening of it does not reach MGR. An invoice closed by credit alone (**Settled**) is re-read on every sync.

**Retry saved sync** resumes your own unfinished batch, even after a reload. It preserves the request identity and invoice set. Another operator cannot retry your request. If a newer sync has superseded that batch, the retry records that terminal outcome without changing payment status. Then choose **Sync QuickBooks** to start a new batch. A superseded batch never advances the last-success time. Reconnect QuickBooks first when its authorization requires recovery. A historical failure remains visible separately after a successful retry. Successful sync history begins when this tracking feature is installed; MGR does not invent timestamps for older syncs.

Admin and Sales can run the QuickBooks invoice sync. It freezes the eligible invoices for that request, reads each one in the same connected company, then updates the whole batch together. If one read or update fails, none of that batch is applied; retrying the same request reads the same frozen invoice set, and a completed retry returns its recorded result. If two syncs overlap, a result from the older read cannot replace the newer invoice state. The sync updates current balance, total, tax, payment state, and edit marker. Staff and portal screens show that current QuickBooks total, while the original local lines and pushed snapshot remain unchanged as audit history, so an edited total can differ from the visible line sum. A partial payment stays due; a payment that is later reversed makes the invoice due again. MGR reads linked Payment details once per batch and records only positive cash that QuickBooks assigns to that invoice. A zero-dollar Payment created by applying credit records no cash and shows the invoice as settled rather than paid; a single-invoice mixed cash-and-credit settlement records only its cash portion. If one Payment covers multiple invoices and its cash is less than their combined applications, MGR records no cash from that ambiguous Payment instead of counting the same cash more than once. Voided and deleted invoices keep any earlier payment-confirmation marker as history but no longer appear paid. MGR compares only fields it originally sent, so a QuickBooks default such as a derived due date is not reported as an accountant edit. MGR reports a real accountant edit and stops; it never overwrites or pushes the invoice automatically.

Admin opens **Settings → Accounting** to connect, reconnect, inspect safe connection health, manage mappings and choose whether future QuickBooks invoices allow bank transfer, card, or both. Admin and Sales can open **Fix mapping** from an invoice and enter an exact customer or item ID they verified in the connected QuickBooks company; MGR never selects a record from a matching name. After a mapping saves, reopening it shows the ID the server retained. The payment choices are copied when the next invoice is pushed and do not change invoices already there. Every push, exact retry, corrected push, or re-push first opens a confirmation that names the invoice and the operation. Closing it sends nothing. Selecting its **Confirm** action sends it; an exact retry still reuses the previously saved request. QuickBooks payment, edit, void, and deletion status remains visible after disconnect. After sync confirms an invoice was voided or deleted in its original QuickBooks company, Admin may mark it **Written off** with a reason even while QuickBooks is disconnected. Admin can also write off an unpaid invoice QuickBooks never received (see [Record a payment in MGR](#record-payment)). Credit memos never offer write-off. That status records who acted and when inside MGR; it does not change QuickBooks, record cash, or create a credit.

Accounting shares the explorer's connection, online-payment status, and **Bank transfer (ACH)** / **Card** switches. The QuickBooks status reads **Connected**, **Not connected**, or **Reconnect required**. While QuickBooks is connected, **Online payments** says customers pay through each invoice's QuickBooks payment link: the portal's Pay button opens it, and when QuickBooks has none the customer is asked to contact you. While both **Bank transfer (ACH)** and **Card** are off, it reads **Off**: new invoices get no online payment option, and invoices pushed earlier keep theirs. While QuickBooks is not connected, it reads **Not available** and says it needs a QuickBooks connection. If QuickBooks is not turned on in MGR, **Connect QuickBooks** is unavailable and the page says so. The payment switches sit under **Push defaults**; before QuickBooks is connected they show as one locked row. While connected, a switch saves as soon as you tap it and MGR confirms **Push defaults saved**. If the save fails, the switch returns to its saved position and the error shows under it. Saved settings remain read-only during recovery. If a refresh credential has expired or QuickBooks rejects it as revoked, MGR shows **Reconnect required** and the authorization error. Admin can reconnect or disconnect. Rarely, if MGR is interrupted while renewing its QuickBooks access, the next QuickBooks action shows **Reconnect required**, and an Admin reconnects. A temporary provider failure leaves the connection state unchanged. **Customers missing a portal login email** says how many customers have no portal login email and offers **Review** only when some are missing or the count is unavailable. If none are missing, it says **All customers have a portal login email** without a Review action. **Connect QuickBooks** sits on Accounting itself while QuickBooks is not connected. **Disconnect** on the connection row opens the **Disconnect QuickBooks** dialog over Accounting, including while the status reads **Reconnect required**. Disconnecting deletes MGR's stored credential first, then asks QuickBooks to revoke it. The dialog then closes, and Accounting offers **Connect QuickBooks** again. If QuickBooks did not confirm, Accounting says the credential is deleted but revocation was not confirmed, so remove MGR's access in QuickBooks or reconnect. Closing the dialog changes nothing.

When QuickBooks is disconnected or needs authorization again, **Sync QuickBooks** says *Connect or reconnect QuickBooks before syncing payments.* Saved **Push defaults** remain locked during recovery, and the switches carry the same connection prerequisite: *Connect or reconnect QuickBooks before saving payment options.* A running action instead reads **Syncing…**, **Saving…**, or **Opening QuickBooks…**; these are pending actions, not missing form inputs. These hints do not change which roles can access Accounting.

The **QuickBooks mappings** list separates customers, SKUs, vendors, the purchase account, the deposit/refund item, and the one-way keg fee item. **Show** offers **All** and **Unmapped (N)**. N counts the records with no ID in the connected QuickBooks company, including one saved in another company. **Unmapped** lists only those records, and the choice stays in the page's link. **Map** or **Change** opens **Map to QuickBooks** from Accounting, or **Fix mapping** from an invoice's mapping list. Each sheet identifies the local record. For a customer, an item, the deposit/refund item or the one-way keg fee item, opening the sheet reads the connected company's active customers or items from QuickBooks, and you pick one by name; each choice also shows its QuickBooks ID. Nothing is picked for you, and MGR never chooses from a matching name. While the list loads, the picker says so. If QuickBooks cannot return the list, the sheet says why and takes the exact ID typed instead, without changing leading zeroes. **Save mapping** is unavailable while nothing is chosen or a save is pending. Closing discards unsaved edits. A company claimed by another brewery must be disconnected there first; a mapping cannot override that claim. A QuickBooks customer maps to one MGR customer per company: saving an ID another customer already uses in the same company is refused with "That already exists. Use a different name or value." Remap the other customer first.

For received materials, Admin also maps each **Vendor** by its exact QuickBooks vendor ID and sets one **Purchase account** for the entire brewery. Use the expense or inventory asset account your accountant chose; there are no per-material account mappings. Both mappings belong to the connected company, and connecting a different company clears them. A missing vendor or purchase account mapping blocks a receipt Bill and links back to this list.

While connected, invoice detail lists only the mappings that need action for the current QuickBooks company: a missing customer, SKU, keg-deposit, or one-way keg fee mapping. A mapped customer or item is not listed. An invoice with a payment recorded in MGR that is not voided lists none, and neither does an invoice from before the books opened, which is never pushed. The rows have no link of their own: the invoice's one **Fix mapping** action opens the mapping list. A missing deposit or fee mapping requires Admin in Accounting. An old company's ID is not a match. Missing local records show **Mapping unavailable**. The QuickBooks status, buyer questions, and permitted confirmation actions remain visible alongside these rows. The balance is in the price summary.

After fixing a rejected mapping, **Push corrected invoice** starts a corrected attempt. **Re-push deleted invoice** creates it again only after sync confirmed deletion. Each action opens a confirmation explaining that operation before anything is sent; a failed request stays in that dialog with its error.

### Record a payment in MGR [#record-payment]

<Screen name="Record payment" />
<Screen name="Void payment" />

Admin and Sales can record a payment received outside QuickBooks, such as cash or a check, on an invoice QuickBooks never received. That includes an invoice never pushed, one QuickBooks rejected, and every invoice at a brewery that never connected QuickBooks. MGR's payments then own that invoice's balance, paid state and overdue mark.

On the invoice, select **Record payment** under **Payments**. Enter **Amount ($)**, **Received on**, **Method** (**Cash**, **Check**, **ACH**, or **Other**), and an optional **Reference** such as a check number. The amount must be more than $0.00 and no more than the balance due, in dollars and cents. A payment can be partial. An amount outside that range is marked under **Amount ($)** once you leave the field, such as *Enter an amount from $0.01 to $450.00, in dollars and cents.* **Still needed** beside **Record payment** names each missing value. The received day cannot be after today in the brewery's time zone. A day before the invoice was issued is accepted, for a payment taken at order time. When payments reach the total, the invoice reads **Paid**, marked on the day the last payment was received. The customer portal then shows it as paid too.

**Amount ($)** starts at the balance due; type over it for a partial payment. On the invoice, **Method** starts on the method of the customer's last payment recorded in MGR, on any of its invoices; a voided payment does not count. A customer with no such payment starts with no method. The **Invoices** list also offers **Record payment** under **Payment** on each row that takes one, so you can record it without opening the invoice; there, **Method** starts empty.

A payment is never edited or deleted. To correct one, select **Void** beside it, enter a reason of up to 500 characters, and select **Confirm void**. The payment stays on the invoice, marked **Voided** with its reason, and no longer counts toward the balance. If the invoice drops below its total, it is no longer paid. Record the correct payment again if one was received.

At a brewery connected to QuickBooks, **Record payment** warns that the invoice will not be sent to QuickBooks while it has payments recorded in MGR. While it has a payment that is not voided, the invoice offers no push and lists no mappings. A push of it, from MGR or the API, is refused with the reason "payments for this invoice are recorded in MGR, so it is not sent to QuickBooks". Once every payment on it is voided, it can be pushed again.

Some invoices with no QuickBooks number still cannot take a payment, and keep an unknown balance:

- An invoice whose QuickBooks push never finished (**push result unknown**). Retry the push first. If the QuickBooks company or connection changed while that push was pending, the push can never finish, so the invoice can be neither pushed nor paid in MGR.
- An invoice pushed to a previous QuickBooks company. Push it to the current company, and record its payment in QuickBooks.

Paid, written-off and historical invoices take no payment, nor does a $0.00 invoice, which reads **No payment due**. Credit memos take no payment, and a credit memo does not lower the balance of the invoice it credits. An invoice that will never be paid, including one a credit memo credits in full, stays **Overdue** past its due day until payments cover its total or Admin writes it off. On an invoice that can take a payment, Admin sees **Write off** under **QuickBooks and accounting**; enter a reason of up to 500 characters and select **Confirm write-off**. The invoice then reads **Written off** and is no longer overdue. A partly paid invoice can be written off for the rest of its balance: its payments stay as they are and can still be voided, but it takes no new payment. A write-off records who acted and when inside MGR; it records no cash and creates no credit. MGR has no void for an invoice QuickBooks never received.

The **Write off invoice** dialog keeps **Confirm write-off** unavailable until a nonblank reason is entered. It shows a sending state while saving and keeps the reason visible if saving fails. This is a local status change only: it does not change QuickBooks, record cash, or create a credit.

### Return beer and correct the invoice

<Screen name="Return and credit" />

1. Open the invoice and select **Return**, under **Correct a shipment** at the bottom, to open the Beer return page.
2. Enter the quantity coming back for each applicable line. Leave unrelated lines at zero. A return cannot exceed the remaining uncredited quantity. Beer accepts fractions to two decimal places. A negative quantity, or one above what can be returned, is marked under that line once you leave it, and **Still needed** beside **Return shipment** names the line, so a bad line is never left out of the return. The invoice's keg deposit lines are listed too: enter the number of kegs whose deposit you are refunding, in whole kegs.
3. For beer lines, split the returned quantity under **Original shipped source**. The source amounts must sum to the line quantity; a source cannot be returned more than was shipped. Choose **Return to**, the destination **Return to bin**, and a reason: **Unsold** and **Wrong item** put the beer back as sellable stock at that location; **Damaged** puts it back and writes the same quantity to loss in the same step, so broken beer never lands on a pick list.
4. Select **Return shipment**.

For older invoices that have no shipment, choose the return location; MGR records untracked stock in its first bin. Shipment-backed invoices always require the original source selection.

The preview uses the invoice's captured beer and deposit prices. A deposit-only refund needs no reason, source, location or bin. If the empty kegs are arriving with that refund, turn on **Check in empty kegs with this refund** and choose the receiving location and bin. Saving posts both the refund and **Returned** shell events in one step; it refuses the whole action if the customer holds fewer kegs than you entered. Leave the switch off when the empties were already recorded or have not arrived. Full kegs returned with beer post **Returned** automatically and do not offer this switch. After saving, MGR returns to the original invoice.

MGR creates a separate credit memo at the prices frozen on the invoice lines, never today's price, with a keg deposit refund line for each refunded deposit (which lowers that customer's deposit held on **Customer keg balance** and the portal Account page), records the return-in (and, for damaged, the loss) against that credit memo, and notes the correction on the originating order's timeline. A paid invoice can still be returned; the credit sits as available credit. Credit memos cannot themselves be credited. An invoice that is **Written off**, or voided or deleted in QuickBooks, is no longer owed: it shows no **Return**, and MGR refuses a return or credit memo against it. MGR also refuses a return or credit memo against an order recorded before the books opened, because no stock left with it.

## Locations [#locations]

<Screen name="Locations" />
<Screen name="Location detail" />

**Locations** (open `/locations`; Settings links here) lists every location with what it is used for. Admin sees **Add location**: a name and its **Uses**: any of *Warehouse*, *Taproom* and *Storage*. No use starts selected. **Uses** takes more than one answer; choose everything the location does. The help below the choices separates the multi-select instruction, Warehouse behavior and Taproom behavior into readable lines. A building that pours pints, holds pallets and ships orders can use all three. A name and at least one use are required. **Add location** and **Save location** show **Still needed** beside the button until both are supplied; the reason updates as you type and choose uses.

A note in the form explains bins: they are the rooms and racks inside a location where stock sits. Every new location starts with **Cold**, **Dry** and **Walk-in**. Names are unique within the brewery. Admin returns to **Settings**; other staff return to **Beer**. **See finished goods across all locations** opens the global inventory, not a location filter. Select **Edit** (Admin) or **Review** (read-only staff) on a row to open the location: its uses, the brewery's timezone, the same note about bins, and then its **Bins** on the same page. Admin and Warehouse add, rename or remove bins there. **Timezone** reads **Brewery default** and then the zone by name, for example *Brewery default · Eastern Time (America/New_York)*. Settings and Chat preferences use the same readable zone names and exact IANA identifiers. A location has no timezone of its own; the zone is the one set under **Settings** → **Timezone**. Admin can rename a location or change its uses with **Edit** then **Save location**; movement history stays exactly as recorded. The tap board, the weekly count, variance and **Replenishment** list only locations with *Taproom* among their uses. Taproom staff see stock only at those locations. **From warehouse** on **Replenishment** and the **Portal fulfillment warehouse** in **Settings** list only locations with *Warehouse*. Every other location picker lists every location, whatever its uses.

Locations cannot currently be deleted or archived. An Admin can rename a mistaken location or change its uses; neither action removes its recorded history.

### Add or rename a bin

<Screen name="Bin" />

A location's page lists its **Bins** below the location's facts. For Admin and Warehouse, each bin shows how many kinds of stock it holds (for example *3 kinds of stock*), or **Empty**. Each SKU or material lot, untracked stock, and keg size counts once, whatever its quantity. Other staff see the bins without counts. When nothing is stored in any bin, the section says *Nothing is stored in these bins yet*, and **Record movement** opens Inventory's movement form with this location chosen.

Admin and Warehouse can select **Add bin**, or **Edit** on a bin, to enter a **Bin name** and **Save bin**. A name already used at this location shows an inline message such as *A bin named Dry already exists here* and cannot be saved. The sheet is titled with the bin's name, for example *Edit bin · Cold*. **Remove** sits in its own **Remove bin** section below **Save bin** and asks you to confirm first. A location always keeps at least one bin, and a bin that has ever recorded stock keeps its name in the movement history, so both are renamed rather than removed. A bin a POS menu uses is refused too until the menu points at another bin. Taps are not bins: the sheet's note links the [Tap board](#tap-intervals), where taps are managed. See [Bins](#inventory) for the three bins every location starts with.

Bin names are compared without surrounding spaces and ignoring capitalization: **dry** cannot be added beside **Dry**, even when two people save at once. You can change a bin's capitalization when no other bin has that name. Older bins that already share a name keep their identities and stock; saving their exact unchanged names is allowed. Long names wrap in the list and edit title without a new length limit.

An empty bin name keeps **Add bin** or **Save bin** unavailable and says **Still needed: a bin name.** beside the action.

## Stock transfers [#transfers]

<Screen name="Transfers" />

**Transfers** (Work tab; Warehouse and Admin) move stock between two of the brewery's locations without an order or an invoice. **New transfer** takes a source and destination location, the bin at each end (the first bin is preselected), and SKU lines with quantities. When the brewery has only one other location, choosing the source also chooses it as the destination; **Create transfer** creates it ready to pick and opens the new transfer. A wholly blank spare line is ignored. Every other line needs a SKU and a quantity above zero. Until it has both, **Create transfer** stays unavailable with the missing pieces named beside it, and once you leave the line's quantity, the missing SKU or the bad quantity is marked with what to fix under the line. An entered line is never silently left out. With no SKUs, the SKU picker links Admin to **Catalog**; for Warehouse it says an admin or sales member adds them in Catalog. A chosen location with no bins says so under its bin picker and links to **Locations**, and **Create transfer** asks for a location that has a bin. There is no draft step. Materials and empty kegs can also move through the API. The document is numbered TRF-0001 upward. A new transfer goes straight to **Receive**: there is no separate pick step, because the pick is what was requested. **Receive** on a new transfer records that pick first, then receives. **Receive** asks, under each line, how much to take from each lot and from untracked stock in the source bin, totaling the picked quantity. A line whose stock sits in one lot (or is all untracked) starts with the whole picked quantity there. The pick cannot exceed what the source bin holds: lines drawing the same stock from the same bin count together, across every lot of it (the lots are chosen at **Receive**), and picking more is refused with **Not enough stock in the selected bin and lot**, followed by what is on hand and a request to reduce the quantity or choose another source; nothing is recorded and the transfer keeps its status. Receiving writes the paired stock movements in one step, out of the source bin and into the destination bin, so on hand changes place and nothing counts as a removal; the fleet total for kegs is unchanged. A same-location pair is refused: open the location from **Locations** and use **Move stock** under its bins instead. **Move stock** needs some stock in the location's bins and a second bin. With nothing stored, the page shows its empty state instead. With stock in a lone bin, **Move stock** shows as unavailable and says to add a bin first. Admin and Warehouse choose stock and its source bin, a different destination bin, and a positive quantity no larger than the source bin holds for that stock. Materials use base units; empty kegs require whole units. Each finished-goods or material lot is listed separately from **Untracked stock**; choose the identity actually being moved. The selected lot is preserved on both entries. A bin move writes paired rows without a document. Neither a bin move nor **Receive** takes out more than the source bin holds, whether finished goods (lot or untracked), materials, or empty kegs: the whole move or receipt is refused with **Not enough stock in the selected bin and lot**, followed by what is on hand and a request to reduce the quantity or choose another source, and nothing is written. Untracked stock is checked on its own: when it falls short and the bin holds lot stock, MGR asks you to **Choose the recorded lot for this removal.** Receiving checks the source bin as it stands at receipt, so stock that left the bin after the pick blocks it. A pick does not hold the stock for its transfer: two transfers can pick the same stock, and the one received second is refused. Cancel it and create a new transfer with what the bin holds. Until a transfer is received, **Cancel transfer** closes it out with a required **Reason**: a pick never takes stock out of the source bin, so nothing moves, the picked quantities are cleared, and the transfer shows *cancelled* with the reason. A received or already-cancelled transfer cannot be cancelled. In the list, select anywhere on a row to open its transfer. An open row also names its next step as a shortcut; a received or cancelled transfer has no verb.

The **From bin** and **To bin** fields say **Choose a location first** and stay unavailable until you choose the corresponding location.

The transfer detail shows separate **From** and **To** locations and one **Status**, such as **Submitted** or **Cancelled**. Each line includes its quantity and unit: **units** for SKUs, **kegs** for keg pools, or the material's base unit. After picking, it reads *picked of requested*, with that same unit. A cancelled transfer retains its **Cancel reason** beneath the status.

Before **Receive**, enter the quantity taken from each recorded lot in the source bin. Each source shows its available quantity; a line with only one source starts with the full line quantity. A line with several sources starts blank so you can split it across the actual lots. Historical untracked stock is labelled **Untracked / legacy stock**. **Receive** records both sides of the move. **Cancel transfer** opens a dialog with a required **Reason** (up to 500 characters); closing without saving clears that reason. Cancellation moves no stock.

Open `/transfers/new` to go straight to **New transfer**. It redirects to `/transfers?newTransfer=1`; opening the dialog also puts that address in the browser. Closing it clears the shortcut so reloading does not reopen it. Sales can read the Transfers list but has no **New transfer** button, and opening this address shows the list without the dialog.

## Delivery routes [#deliveries]

<Screen name="Routes" />

**Deliveries** (Work tab; Warehouse and Admin) lists every route that has not returned, and below it every shipped order and picked transfer that is on no route yet. The **Status** pick switches the list: **Open** (the default) shows those routes, and **Returned** shows finished routes, newest first, so a past run stays one click away. When every route has returned, the Open list says *No open routes* and points to **Returned**. A route is one day, one driver, one vehicle, and its stops in order; a stop is a shipped wholesale order or a picked [stock transfer](#transfers). Select anywhere on a route row to open it. Each row names its next action as a shortcut: **Assign** on a route with no driver, **Resume** on a departed one with a stop still open, **Return** on a departed one whose every stop has an outcome, and **Add to route** on a document waiting for one, which opens **New route** with that document already ticked as stop 1. A planned route with a driver has nothing to do yet, so it has no verb. **Return** opens the route, where the driver taps **Return route** once the truck is back: the return time is when the driver records it, not when the last stop closed. A returned route has no verb; select it to read its stops and return time. **New route** opens the builder. With nothing shipped and off a route, it shows only **No stops ready for a route** and **Open Orders**, not the empty form.

<Screen name="Route" />

The builder takes the **Delivery date**, the **Driver** (any Warehouse or Admin member, shown by their email with their roles after it), the **Vehicle**, and an optional **Route name**. On the route list, a route that has not departed names its driver by the same email; the route page does too. A route whose saved driver has since left the team shows **driver not on the team**, and a saved driver who no longer has Warehouse or Admin shows as **can no longer drive** in the picker. Choose a current Warehouse or Admin member before saving, since saving refuses any other driver. Under **Stops**, tick the documents this route delivers and give each a stop number; the list holds this route's stops and everything shipped that is on no route. **Save route plan** writes the header and the stops in one step; until there is a delivery date and at least one stop, it is unavailable and says which is missing. Saving a new route opens it, so **Depart route** is right there. A document can sit on only one open route: to move a stop, untick it here and tick it on the other route. A pickup order never appears in this list, and saving a route with its shipment is refused with "a pickup order is not delivered on a route." A stop already delivered cannot be unticked, and a route cannot be saved without at least one stop.

When **Stops** is empty, **Open Orders** takes you to Orders. Confirm an order, pick its stock, then ship it: shipped delivery orders appear as stops until assigned to a route. Pickup orders do not appear. After shipping a delivery order, **Add to route** on the Shipped page opens **New route** with that order already ticked as stop 1. Picked stock transfers also appear as stops.

The route list keeps **Resume**, **Return**, **Assign**, and **Add to route** beside their rows, with Work tabs limited to your role. In the builder, selecting a stop assigns the next number after the highest selected stop; you can edit that number before saving. Delivered stops and their numbers stay locked. Selecting a driver is not enough to depart: save the plan first. Departure uses the saved route, not unsaved edits.

**Depart route** is enabled once a driver is assigned. It stamps the departure, marks every transfer stop *in transit*, and closes the builder; after that the route's stops cannot change. Only the assigned driver or an Admin may depart, confirm, or return a route; another Warehouse member is refused, and the stop page says so instead of offering **Delivered**. A stop cannot be confirmed before its route departs, and it does not appear on Today until then.

<Screen name="Driver route" />

A departed route lists its stops with their delivered times. The lowest undelivered stop is **Next** and its **Deliver** opens [Confirm delivery](#orders). On Today, that stop appears with its route name, stop number, and **next stop** detail; **Resume** opens the same confirmation. Today shows the next stop, not the full route or a return status. Open the route from Deliveries to see all stops and Return. Confirming a customer stop stamps it and, for a shipment saved as invoice on delivery, raises the invoice for the accepted quantities. Confirming a transfer stop only stamps it: nothing is invoiced, and the stock moves when the transfer is received on its own page. After **Delivered** (or a refusal), the page returns to the route, where the next open stop is **Next**.

The customer stop's **Ship to** shows the recorded destination label, both street lines, city, state or region, and postal code; destinations outside the US also show the country. Missing street or postal details are called out as **not recorded**, not guessed from the customer name or label. A missing postal code does not mean every country requires one. Confirm any missing directions with the route planner before travelling. The stop reads the linked ship-to's current details; it is not a saved historical address. Internal transfer stops keep their actual destination location instead of a customer address.

Enter each **refused** count as a whole number from **0** to that line's shipped amount. Negative counts, larger counts, decimals, exponent notation, and a cleared count show an inline correction instead of an **Accepted** total. Your entry stays available to correct; it is not rounded or clamped. **Delivered** (or the refusal action) stays disabled until all counts are valid. Keep **0** for no refusal. Accepted beer still requires **Received by**; a full refusal does not. **Received by** starts with the name that signed the last delivery to this ship-to, and suggests the others who signed there before; change it if someone else receives. Any refusal requires a reason, and **Other** also requires a note.

Until then the page says how many stops are still open, and the return is refused. An unattempted stop uses **Not delivered · leave for a later route** on its page and becomes unassigned for another route. A customer rejection is recorded as a refusal, and any beer still waiting to come back shows *awaiting check-in* with a **Check in** button on the route, even after the route has returned. ## Compliance [#compliance]

<Screen name="Compliance months" />

**Compliance** (More; Sales and Admin) reviews TTB periods monthly, quarterly, or annually and records each filing you make with TTB: pick **Monthly**, **Quarterly**, or **Annual** at the top. Monthly lists the last three months, Quarterly the last four calendar quarters (January–March, April–June, July–September, October–December), and Annual the last two calendar years, each plus every period of that length already filed. If the brewery has a **Books opened on** date in [Settings](#settings), periods that start before it are left out of the list. When that leaves a tab with no period, the tab says so, names the books-opened date, and names the first period MGR can report, which appears once it starts. Until that date arrives, the tab says the books **open** on it rather than **opened**. Activity dated before the books-opened date, such as a lot packaged the week before, falls in no period, because MGR does not report it. The empty tab and the **Before the books opened** page both say so. Opening a period that starts before the books opened shows **Before the books opened** with the date instead of a report; Admin gets an **Open Settings** button, and Sales, who cannot open Settings, is asked to have an admin change the date; a period already filed still shows its snapshot. A period marked **Not filed · Ready to review** opens for review. An ended period with nothing for the report (no finished-goods movement, cellar removal, or invoice dated in it) is marked **Not filed · No activity** instead; it still opens, and you still record its filing. The current period is marked **In progress · File after the month ends** (or quarter, or year) and can be reviewed but not recorded as filed until its last day has passed. A filed period shows **Filed** with its filing date and taxpaid barrels, or **Needs amendment** after a basis correction. Below the periods sit the **Licenses** link and **Lot trace**, a search by lot code. No lot is listed until you search; then the 50 most recently packaged lots whose code contains your text are listed, each opening its trace. **No lots match** means no lot code contains it. MGR reports TTB periods here; other jurisdictions and date ranges are available through the API. A TTB filing always covers one whole calendar month, quarter, or year; any other TTB range, such as February 1 through April 30 or a partial month, can be reviewed but not recorded as filed.

The period badge says **Past**, **Current**, or **Upcoming** using the brewery's calendar day. **Current** means the period contains today, not that the figures have been saved. An **Upcoming** period names its start date and cannot be filed until it ends. **Live figures** are generated from the ledger now, even for a past period; a **Filed** snapshot keeps its original figures. Historical periods remain available from the books-opened date, or for all history if that date is not set.

Destination-state totals show **Removed (bbl)**, **Returned (bbl)**, signed
**Adjustments (bbl)**, and **Net (bbl)** alongside invoiced beer **Sales**,
**Credits**, and **Net sales**. **Net (bbl)** is **Removed** less
**Returned**, plus signed **Adjustments**; **Net sales** is **Sales** less
**Credits**. Deposits are excluded from beer sales. Movement dates and invoice dates remain separate.
**Export state transactions** downloads the exact transactions, source IDs,
bbl, and cents behind those totals. Returns retain the original shipment state;
later address edits do not reassign sales. Transactions without a recorded destination (shipments, returns,
adjustments, invoices, or credits) appear under **Unassigned** for review. MGR does not infer their state. Export
sales, and returns or credits against them, are not flagged Unassigned just
because they have no US destination.
Saved snapshots retain their own export. Older snapshots saved without these
transactions say so. State tax calculations and filing forms remain external.
When there are no state transactions, the page says **No transactions in this period. Nothing to export.** and disables **Export state transactions**. It does not download an empty CSV.

<Screen name="Monthly compliance" />

A period is generated from the inventory ledger when you open it, so nothing is stored until you record the filing. Each finished-goods movement counts on its effective date: **Packaged on** for a packaging close, otherwise the day it was recorded. Historical packaging closes keep their original recorded day, so previously reported periods do not change. Cellar losses count on the day they were recorded. The table shows each package class (**Kegs**, **Cans**, **Bottles**) in barrels: **Begin** + **In** − **Out** = **End**, and every class must balance. Figures print to two decimals; the printed cells always add up and a period's printed end is always the next period's printed begin, so an **In** or **Out** cell can differ by 0.01 from its movements rounded on their own. Zeros print 0.00. For a **Past** period, **Beer in process** reconstructs the beer remaining in tanks at the end of the period in the brewery's time zone. Brew turns count on their brewed date, transfers and tank adjustments on their recorded event date, and packaging draws on **Packaged on**. A later brew, transfer, loss or tank closure does not become an earlier period's tank balance. Its hint names the period-end date. For **Current** and **Upcoming** periods, this line instead shows the live tank balance to date, not a forecast of their period end. Older saved snapshots and correction records keep their original filing-time or correction-time tank quantities and say so; they are not rewritten. Below the table, **Packaged** shows production added to finished goods, then each removal class (taxpaid, export, samples, losses, and so on) by the tax treatment recorded when the beer moved, so editing a sale channel or format later does not change historical classification, and taxpaid removals by destination state. The removal classes are printed so they add up to the printed **Out** plus any cellar removals, so one class can differ by 0.01 from its own movements rounded alone.

**Review cellar losses** lists each generic loss posted in the period: a batch's completion loss and each cellar transfer loss, labelled **completion loss** or **transfer loss** after the batch number. A batch can have several. Each row shows the barrels recorded, any earlier reattributions, and the exact barrels left to reattribute, with trailing zeros trimmed. ## Keg fleet [#kegs]

<Screen name="Keg fleet" />

**Keg fleet** (Beer tab; Warehouse and Admin) is the brewery's returnable keg fleet: pools and where their kegs are. One-way kegs are materials and do not appear here. **Record keg event**, the everyday action, sits at the top of the page. **Add keg pool** sits below the pool list. The page shows **No keg pools yet** until you add one.

### Keg pools

A pool is one group of kegs you count together: **Owned**, **Leased**, or **Pay per fill**. **Add keg pool** takes the name, the kind, the vendor for a leased or pay-per-fill pool, the per-fill cost, and the deposit charged per keg. **Per-fill cost** is labeled **required** for a **Pay per fill** pool and **optional** for an **Owned** or **Leased** pool. An optional blank cost shows **Not set**: it is missing, not $0.00. Enter 0.00 only when the cost is known to be zero. **Deposit per keg** is charged on wholesale orders for each keg from the pool; it starts at 0.00, which means no deposit. Both money fields take dollars and cents up to $21,474,836.47; a larger amount is marked at its field and keeps the pool from saving. **Edit** on a pool row changes its name, vendor, costs, or deposit, or turns **In service** off to take it out of service; a pool out of service accepts no more events. An owned pool has no vendor, so its Vendor row reads **None · the brewery owns these kegs**. The kind cannot change after creation, and a per-fill cost or vendor once set can be changed but not removed; on **Edit**, the per-fill help says so.

Deposit and per-fill cost take zero or more, in dollars and cents with at most two decimal places; exponent notation is not accepted.

### Record a keg event

**Kegs** must be a whole number, at least 1, without exponent notation; the button says what is still needed before you can record the event.

**Record keg event** posts one line to the keg ledger. Its **Record keg event** button stays at the bottom of the form while the fields scroll. Choose the pool, the size, the reason (**Acquired**, **Retired**, **Shipped**, **Returned**, **Lost**, or **Found**), the location and bin the kegs are at (the first bin is preselected), and how many kegs. Until the form is complete, the line beside the button names what is still needed, such as *Still needed: a location and a bin.* The **Reason** picker says under it what the chosen reason does to the kegs:

- **Acquired** and **Retired** add kegs to or remove them from the fleet at that bin. No customer.
- **Shipped** and **Returned** move empty kegs to or from a customer; the customer is required. Shipped names the bin they left from, Returned the bin they came back into. The fleet total does not change.
- **Lost** removes kegs from the fleet. Without a customer they come out of that bin; name the customer when they were lost out at a customer, and they come off that customer's balance instead (they already left the bin when shipped). **Found** adds kegs back to a bin and never names a customer: a keg found at a customer is a shipped keg.

Shipping returnable keg beer posts **Shipped** automatically; full kegs returned with their beer post **Returned** automatically. The physical pool, size, and quantity are saved when shipped, including zero-deposit kegs. Later catalog edits do not change the returned shell’s pool or rewrite the deposit charge. An invoice's **Return** can check in empty kegs with a deposit-only refund in one step. Empty pickups without a refund still use **Record keg event**. If recorded shells are short, shipping still succeeds and Shipment done names the pool, size, and location that is short, for example *Owned ½ bbl · Warehouse: 2 kegs short*. Keg fleet shows the same warning for each pool, size, and location below zero; record Acquired kegs to reconcile.

For manual keg events, the quantity is always positive; the reason is the direction. Retired, shipped, and lost at a bin cannot exceed what the bin holds; returned and lost at a customer cannot exceed what that customer holds of that pool and size. A typo is refused rather than driving a bin or a customer negative. Before you submit, a retired, shipped, or lost-at-a-bin count above what the chosen bin holds of that pool and size is marked at **Kegs**, for example *Enter at most 8 kegs in Cold.*, and **Record keg event** stays unavailable. A returned or lost-at-a-customer count above what the chosen customer holds of that pool and size is marked the same way, for example *Enter at most 4 kegs at Ridgeline.* The message names the bin or customer balance and asks you to check the quantity and selected bin or customer. A **Note** is optional and shows next to the event in Keg event history. Kegs moving between bins or locations are recorded through **Transfers** or a bin move, not here; both refuse to take out more kegs than the source bin holds. Empty kegs only: beer coming back with a keg, and its credit, is [Return shipment](#invoices). A keg deposit refund is a separate credit memo: open the invoice that charged the deposit, select **Return**, and enter the kegs on its deposit line.

**Record keg event** needs at least one location and one pool in service. Until then the top of the page shows **Record keg event** as unavailable and says what is missing, such as *Recording a keg event needs a keg pool in service.* Take every pool out of service and the same message returns until you add one or turn one back on.

Each pool row shows the kegs each bin holds per size, so 36 in the taproom and 40 in storage read as two rows; kegs out at customers have left their bin and are listed under **Customer keg balance** for each customer holding some. The fleet includes every bin balance and sums each customer's holdings across all pools and sizes, even for a large fleet. The page hides bin balances of zero and customers with nothing out. If holdings change while loading, reload rather than relying on partial totals.

### Keg report

<Screen name="Keg report" />

**Keg report** (from Keg fleet; Warehouse and Admin) shows whether the fleet is working or sitting and who to chase. The headline is kegs out at customers over the fleet total (acquired or found, minus retired or lost), then the same split per pool and size. The age table groups unreturned kegs into 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, and over 90 days with the deposit at the pool's rate for each bucket. The ledger counts kegs rather than serials, so a return or a loss closes the oldest open shipment first; a keg's age is the shipment it is still attributed to. Each customer holding kegs is listed with how many are over 90 days and their oldest open shipment; select the row to open that customer's keg balance. Under **Deposit mismatches**, each customer, pool, and size where a deposit was refunded and the kegs on deposit differ from the kegs out plus the kegs lost at that customer is listed and flagged; select the row to open that customer's balance. With no keg pool in service the page says **No keg pools** and offers **Open Keg fleet**, where you add a pool. With a pool but no kegs recorded as acquired yet it says **No kegs recorded yet** and offers **Record keg event**, which opens that form on Keg fleet.

<Screen name="Customer keg balance" />

**Customer keg balance** lists, for one customer, the kegs out per pool and size and the deposit held for them: deposits invoiced less deposits refunded on credit memos (zero when no deposit was ever invoiced). An invoice or credit memo that is **Written off**, or voided or deleted in QuickBooks, no longer counts, and neither does a refund of a deposit whose invoice no longer counts. Kegs out is shipped minus returned minus lost. Refunding a deposit can check in empty kegs in the same step. A refund without check-in and a separate physical return can still disagree. Once a deposit on a row has been refunded, the row is flagged when the kegs on deposit differ from the kegs out plus the kegs lost at that customer, and reads, for example, **4 out · 3 on deposit**. A lost keg keeps its deposit, so it is not a mismatch. Shipping charges the deposit and posts its **Shipped** event. A row with no refund is never flagged as a deposit mismatch. A deposit still held with no keg out is listed too. Record the missing keg event in Keg fleet, or refund the deposit with **Return** on the invoice that charged it, and the flag clears. It shows **No kegs currently out** once everything is back. Each row with kegs out has **Record return**, which opens **Record keg event** with that pool, size, **Returned** and the customer already chosen; choose the location and bin the empties came back to and how many.

<Screen name="Keg event history" />

**Keg event history** is the ledger newest first. Narrow it with the bordered **Keg pool** and **Customer** pickers; both show a down arrow. **Customer** opens a search and lists only customers with a keg event. Each has an **All** choice, and the two combine. A combination that finds nothing shows **No matching events**. With no filter set, an empty ledger shows **No keg events yet** and a **Record keg event** button, which opens that form on Keg fleet. The choice stays in the page address, so a link, a refresh, or Back keeps it. Nothing here can be edited; a wrong entry is corrected by recording the opposite event.

## Taproom replenishment [#replenishment]

The heading is **Replenishment**, matching the Work sidebar. Its back link returns to **Work**, even when you arrived from a Finished goods shortfall. The same Work navigation strip appears here with **Replenishment** selected.

With no other warehouse, the picker says no other location has the *Warehouse* use and links to **Locations**, where an admin sets a location's **Uses**.

Tables initially sort by SKU name; your browser remembers a different sort you choose. Sorting keeps each entered quantity with its SKU and does not submit the form. Clearing a quantity leaves it blank rather than restoring the suggestion. Quantities are SKU units, so a case and a keg are not a volume comparison.

Use **Set taproom par** to choose any SKU and set its target stock at the selected taproom, including a SKU with no existing par. With no SKUs yet, the sheet's **SKU** picker links to **Catalog**, and saving says it needs a SKU. A zero par removes its replenishment target. Suggestions refresh after saving.

Use **Set standing allocation** to reserve a named quantity for this taproom without creating an order. This leaves less stock to promise to other orders. Selecting an existing SKU loads its current reserved quantity. Zero releases the standing reservation. If saving leaves the SKU's available quantity below zero, the reservation still saves: the sheet says **Allocation saved.** and shows **Available is below zero for …** until you select **Done**. Releasing never warns. Under **Standing allocations**, **Release** opens a confirmation showing the SKU and quantity; confirming makes that stock available to other orders without moving stock or changing its par. Failures appear in the form and keep your inputs available to correct or retry.

The page says **No taprooms yet: add one under Locations** when none exist, or **No pars set for this taproom.** when there are no suggestions. Admin and Sales can follow **Add SKU in Catalog** from Inventory to Catalog before setting quantities.

**Shortfalls across all locations** shows each SKU's global **On hand**, **Reserved**, and **Available** quantities in one sortable table. Open a SKU's reservation details to see each named reservation's quantity and its unit and volume context. **Review** opens a competing order, or the correct taproom's **Standing allocations** section for a standing reservation.

Selecting **Shortfall** in Finished goods opens this section filtered to that SKU, with its reservation details open. A filtered SKU with no current shortage says so. Changing **Location** keeps that SKU filter. **All shortfalls** clears only the SKU filter and keeps an explicitly selected location. The filter never narrows the taproom's replenishment rows.

newest is marked **(current)**; the version you are viewing is emphasized.
Choose an older number to see its own ingredients, process, schedules, water,

The process numbers sit inline: **Pre-boil volume**, **Boil time**, **Whirlpool** minutes, temperature and rest, **Knockout temp**, **Brewhouse efficiency** and **Yeast attenuation** (both as percents). Boil and whirlpool minutes are whole numbers, a pre-boil volume, when entered, is above zero, and whirlpool, knockout, mash step and fermentation stage temperatures run from 25 to 212 °F. Exponent notation such as `1e1` is refused everywhere. The form names everything still wrong before saving, because a saved version cannot be edited. Ingredients are listed on the draft itself: the **Add ingredient** row opens a sheet to add one, and each ingredient's **Edit** opens it to change or delete. With no materials yet, the sheet's **Material** picker says **No materials yet.** with **Add a material**, which opens **Materials**. In that sheet, **Material** is a search: type part of a name, and each choice reads its name and category. The quantity label names the material's unit, as in *Quantity per bbl (lb)*. Quantity and optional timing both have minus/plus controls and accept typed decimals. Three rows each open a sheet: **Mash schedule** (ordered rests, each a type, a name, a temperature and minutes; the longest rest between 144 and 162 °F is recorded as the version's mash temperature; the sheet names it, as in *The 152 °F rest is recorded as the mash temperature.*), **Fermentation schedule** (stages with a temperature and days), and **Water** (the source profile, a target profile from Water profiles, mash and sparge volumes, target mash pH, and salts or acids each with an amount, a unit and one stage: *Mash*, *Sparge* or *Kettle*). An empty fermentation schedule says **No stages yet** and prompts you to add a stage to plan temperatures and timing. Water with no source picked reads **No source**. You enter every addition and its amount yourself. The sheet does not suggest salt additions or show an ion read-out yet. Nothing is written until **Create recipe version**. While the button is disabled, a line above it names what the draft still lacks, such as *Still needed: a mash step and yeast attenuation from 0 to 100%.*

**Save ingredient** names any missing material or quantity per barrel above zero beside the button. A recipe row shows a day-timed line as *Dry hop · day 3*. The hint updates as you edit and disappears when the ingredient is ready; saving the ingredient only changes the recipe draft.

With no materials yet, **Add ingredient** offers **Add a material (opens in a new tab)**. Add the material from **Materials on hand** in that tab, then return to the original tab. Your typed recipe fields and unfinished ingredient stay open; the recipe is not saved by opening Materials.

In **Water → Add addition**, the **Material** picker also says **No materials yet.** and offers **Add a material** in **Materials** when nothing is available. The link opens a new tab so your unsaved recipe stays open. Add the salt or acid there, then return to the recipe.

A draft you leave unsaved is kept in that browser tab. Coming back to it shows *Restored your unsaved draft* with **Start over**, which returns to a fresh copy of the current version. If someone saved a newer version meanwhile, the notice says your draft is based on an earlier one. Saving clears the kept draft. A new recipe's first draft is not kept.

*Admin and Brewer.*

Open **Recipes** from the **More** navigation to see every recipe, alphabetical, titled with its current version (*Hazy IPA v4*) and the brand it brews. A recipe with no version yet says **No version yet** instead. On a recipe, **Brand · optional** sets or changes the brand it brews. Saves when you pick, and *Brand saved* confirms it; choose **Not decided** to clear it. Batches already scheduled keep the brand they were given. Select **New recipe** to open a recipe with no version yet: a **Name**, an optional **Brand**, and an optional note sit above the first version's rows and fields. The form says *Pick the brand this recipe brews, or leave it undecided. Packaging always asks for the brand.* A recipe takes its style from the brand. One **Create recipe** saves the recipe and its first version and opens it. Tap anywhere on a row to open the recipe.

A recipe version is the process spec a brewer follows: its mash schedule, brewhouse efficiency and yeast attenuation, an optional boil length, and its ingredients. Each one is a material, a quantity per barrel, the stage it enters at (*Mash*, *Boil*, *Whirlpool*, *Fermentation*, *Dry hop*, *Packaging*, or *Other*), and an optional timing in minutes. A version cannot be edited once it is saved; select **New recipe version** to write the next one from a copy of it, so what a past batch was brewed from stays true.

Only mash-stage ingredients predict gravity, and only when every one of them has an **Extract potential** on its material (see [Purchasing](#purchasing)); until then no prediction is shown, rather than a misleading 0.0. In its place the page names the first ingredient that blocks it and how to fix it: set the material's **Extract potential** under Materials, then create the next version (a draft picks the new value up at once). Give a mash ingredient that adds no extract, such as rice hulls, a potential of 1.000. Quantities are converted from the material's unit (lb, kg, oz or g) to pounds per barrel; a mash ingredient kept by volume or count, such as a syrup in gallons, has no weight to convert, so no prediction is shown, and the page says which ingredient and unit. As you add ingredients, MGR previews the version's predicted OG, FG and ABV live, using the same calculation the record reads back afterward. Nothing is stored, so a later edit to a material's typed extract potential can never move an old version's prediction; a new version snapshots the value it saw at the moment it was saved. Predicted OG and FG are shown in whatever unit you chose under [Gravity unit](#gravity-units).

### Batch totals in packs

**Show totals at (bbl)** sits under **Ingredients** when the version has a mash, boil or whirlpool ingredient. Type a batch size and each of those ingredients shows its batch total. When its material has a pack size, the total also reads in packs, such as *22 bbl → 220 lb = 4 × 50 lb + 20 lb*. The pack size is the material's **Base units per purchase unit**, set under [Materials](#purchasing) (for a 55 lb sack: Purchase unit *each*, Base units per purchase unit *55*). A material with no pack size, or a total under one pack, shows just the amount. The batch size is for this page only: it isn't saved and doesn't change the recipe. Until you type one, the page says *Enter a batch size to see totals in packs.* A size in exponent notation, such as `1e1`, is marked at the field and shows no totals.

A total that already comes out in whole packs shows **Whole packs**. In a draft, any other total offers **Round to N × size** down and up, such as **Round to 4 × 50 lb** and **Round to 5 × 50 lb**. A total under one pack offers only **Round to 1 × 50 lb**. Choosing one rewrites that ingredient's per-barrel amount so the total at that batch size is exactly that many packs. It's a draft edit like any other: nothing is saved until **Create recipe version**. A saved version shows the totals but offers no rounding.

## Batches [#batches]

Open an unstarted batch to **Reschedule** its planned date or **Cancel plan** and confirm. **Reschedule** waits for a date other than the planned one and says *Still needed: a different planned date.* until then. Only Admin and Brewer can change batch plans. A cancelled batch stays in the **Cancelled** list and no longer contributes material requirements, planned beer supply, or the buyer schedule. These actions move no stock. Once brew day or other physical work is recorded, cancellation and rescheduling are refused. On a planned batch, both sit under **Reschedule or cancel** at the bottom of the **Prep** and **Brew day** tabs.

A planned batch dated before today shows **Overdue** beside its planned date in the **Planned** list. Until it is brewed, rescheduled or cancelled, its recipe's materials still count as needed, and **Planning** marks any shortage they cause as overdue. Tap the overdue row to open the batch at **Reschedule or cancel**, already unfolded. Then **Cancel plan** clears it, or **Reschedule** moves it to a new date.

*Admin and Brewer.*

Open **Batches** from the **Work** navigation to see the **Planned**, **Active**, **Completed**, and **Cancelled** groups, newest planned date first within each group. On a wide screen each group is a table; on a phone each batch is a short list of labeled facts. Each batch shows its **Batch** number, **Brand**, **Recipe**, **Planned on** date, and **Planned barrels**. A plan with no brand says **No brand yet**, and one with no recipe says **No recipe**. **Planned barrels** is the plan, not the beer in a tank. Select the batch number to open the batch; a planned batch opens on its [Prep or Brew day tab](#prep). Each active batch lists its open tanks under **Vessel and latest reading**, one line per tank, with the latest reading in your [gravity unit](#gravity-units) and when it was taken. A batch split across tanks by a partial transfer lists every tank it is in. A tank with no reading yet says **No readings yet**. **Reading** beside a tank opens that tank's [reading sheet](#cellar) directly, without opening the batch first. An active batch with no open tank shows **No open occupancy** instead and has no reading shortcut. In the other groups, a batch that is in a tank also shows its **Vessel**. Work tabs show only areas your role may open. Select **New batch** for a **Recipe version** and a **Brand**, both optional, plus the **Planned barrels** and **Date** that actually reserve the slot. **Schedule batch** stays unavailable until both are set, with planned barrels above zero, and says which is missing. A batch names nothing further until it is brewed, and identity is not required until packaging.

**Cancelled** is folded by default, with the number of cancelled plans in its heading. Select it to show or hide that history without mixing it into current work. Opening a cancelled batch labels its header **Cancelled**; the barrels and date remain the original planned facts, not a recorded brew or the cancellation date. Missing brand and recipe values stay explicit.

With no recipe versions available, **New batch** links to **Recipes** and explains that a recipe needs a saved version before it can be selected. With no brands, Admin or Sales can follow **Add a brand** to **Catalog**; Brewer is told which role can add one instead. Both fields can stay **Not decided**. Neither a missing recipe nor a missing brand prevents scheduling. Choosing a recipe fills in the brand it brews when no brand is chosen yet, and the date starts on the brewery's today.

Select a planned batch to pull its ingredients on [Prep](#prep) and record it on [Brew day](#brew-day). A brewed batch's page shows its record, turns and corrections ([After the brew](#after-the-brew)).

**New batch** warns when the planned date is before the brewery’s current calendar day. You may still save a historical plan. Scheduling a plan does not record a brew day or change **Brewed on**.

Batches brought over from another system appear under **Completed** with their brew date, close date and knockout barrels, but no vessel. Only history closed before the brewery's **Books opened on** date (see [Settings](#settings)) can be recorded this way, so these batches never appear on the Cellar map, on Today, or in a report. They carry their knockout, no loss written off at completion, and their packaging as closed runs and lots with the quantities filled. That packaging put no stock on hand: these lots appear in **Packaging** history and in a lot trace, which names the batch, but never in inventory, on an order, or in a report. They cannot be corrected with **Correct turn**; fix them by importing again.

A vessel's contents are never entered directly: they always derive from its open occupancy.

The Vessels section is setup for brewing and cellar work, separate from the batch lists above it. If there are no vessels, **No vessels yet** appears as a short note beneath the setup action.

Its name is free again, so a replacement tank can take the same name. A vessel that never held beer is deleted. **Delete vessel** sits in its own section below **Save vessel**, apart from the save action.

### Prep: pull the ingredients [#prep]

<Screen name="Brew prep" />

A planned batch has two tabs: **Prep** and **Brew day**. Prep is the pull list you work through before the brew. Brew day is where you log the brew and record it. The batch opens on **Brew day** once Prep has saved pulls, and on **Prep** until then. Select a tab to switch.

Both tabs sit under the batch title, its number and brand such as **B-0003 · Hazy IPA** (or *no brand*), followed by one **Planned** line: the planned barrels, date and note. **Reschedule or cancel** at the bottom of either tab opens **Reschedule** and **Cancel plan**.

Prep works like the order [pick sheet](#pick-sheet). Lines are grouped under **Mash**, **Boil** and **Whirlpool**, the stages that use them. Each line starts at the recipe plan for the batch's planned barrels. The amount reads as whole packs plus loose pounds, such as `4 × 50 lb + 20 lb`. A pack is the material's purchase unit, and its size is the material's **Base units per purchase unit**, set under [Materials](#purchasing). A material with no pack size is counted in its base unit alone.

Touch only the exceptions. Each line has a packs field and, for a material with a pack size, a loose field beside it. A line planned at less than one pack shows only its loose field. Type what you actually pulled. Packs take decimals, so `4.4` sacks of 50 lb is 220 lb. A partly used bag is just loose pounds: MGR doesn't track open bags. A blank field counts as zero. Under each line's name you see its plan, its lot and its bin, such as *plan 220 lb = 4 × 50 lb + 20 lb · C-2 · Malt room / A2*. An untracked material reads *untracked* in place of a lot.

A line is amber while it's short (pulled less than plan) or has no source chosen.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Short** | Shows on a line pulled below plan. It jumps to that line's first amount field so you can enter what you really pulled. |
| **Choose lot** | Shows on a line with no source. It opens **Source**, this material's stock by lot, location and bin with the amount available, such as *C-2 · Malt room / A2 · 1000 lb available*. Pick the exact lot and bin you're pulling from. A material with no stock anywhere has no **Choose lot**. Instead, one note above the list names every such material, such as *No stock to choose a source from for Chit Malt.* |
| **Vessel** | The tank you plan to knock out into. Optional. Brew day's Knockout starts from it. Retired tanks aren't offered, and a tank retired after you saved Prep shows as not chosen. |
| **Later, in cellar** | Counts the recipe's additions you don't pull for brew day, such as *Fermentation 1 · Dry hop 6*, or says *None*. Add those in [Cellar](#cellar) when the time comes. |
| **Print pull list** | Prints the page so you can pull on paper. |
| **Done pulling** | Saves the list and opens the Brew day tab. On a phone it stays at the bottom of the screen, beside **Print pull list**. |

A material held in exactly one bin starts with that source already chosen. A line that already has a source has no **Choose lot** on Prep. To pull it from a different lot or bin, use **Edit** on the Brew day tab.

**Done pulling** saves each line's lot, bin and pulled amount, plus the vessel. It moves no stock and reserves nothing, so you can pull the day before. Saving again replaces the whole list. A line pulled to zero with no source is left off it, and the Brew day tab starts that line at zero. **Done pulling** stays disabled while a line is pulled above zero with no source, or while a packs or loose amount isn't a number at zero or above. The line beside it names the ingredient, such as *Still needed: a source for Chit Malt.*, or counts several, such as *Still needed: a source for 7 items.* Tap that line to scroll to the first ingredient still without a source.

To fix a saved pull's amount, change it and select **Done pulling** again. To change a saved lot or bin, use **Edit** on the Brew day tab. Nothing on Prep touches inventory, so there's nothing to reverse.

If saving is refused, the message shows above **Done pulling** and your entries stay. MGR refuses Prep for a batch that's already brewed (*batch 202 was already brewed on 2026-10-22*) or cancelled (*batch is cancelled*); there's nothing left to prepare, so go back to **Batches**. It refuses a retired tank: *vessel FV-3 is retired; choose another tank*. It also refuses a lot on a material that isn't lot-tracked, or no lot on one that is: *choose a lot exactly when the material is lot-tracked*.

### Brew day: log and record the brew [#brew-day]

<Screen name="Brew day timeline" />

Brew day runs in process order: **Mash**, **Lauter**, **Boil**, **Whirlpool**, **Knockout**. Each stage heading shows how many of its ingredients you've confirmed, such as *2 of 4 confirmed*, and the recipe's target for it, such as *152 °F · 60 min · pH 5.3* for Mash. A stage the recipe sets nothing for shows no target. On a phone one stage is open at a time: the first one with an ingredient you haven't confirmed. Tap another stage's heading to open it. On a wider screen every stage shows.

Each ingredient sits in the stage the recipe gives it, so there's no stage to choose. Lines start from the saved Prep pulls, already confirmed, so you don't confirm a Prep pull twice. A line Prep left off starts at zero and unconfirmed. Before Prep is saved, every line starts at plan. Either way, its source is already chosen when the material sits in only one bin. A short line reads *pulled …* before its plan and is amber until you confirm it.

- **Confirm all**, at the top, confirms every line at the amount and source it shows. Use it when the pull went to plan, then **Edit** any exception.
- **Confirm** marks the ingredient as in the brew. It turns to **Pulled** and the line goes green. A confirmed line stays amber while it's pulled above zero with no source, or pulls more than its lot and bin hold. Tap **Pulled** to undo it.
- **Edit** opens the same packs and loose fields as Prep, plus **Source** when the material has stock to pick from. Change the amount or the exact lot and bin there.

Process readings sit in their stage. All are optional, and a blank one stays unrecorded. Minutes are whole numbers of zero or more, **Mash pH** runs from 4 to 7, whirlpool and knockout temperatures from 25 to 212 °F, and water and pre-boil amounts must be above zero (sparge water may be zero). A reading outside its range, or typed in exponent notation such as `1e1`, shows its rule under the field, and **Record brew day** stays disabled with *Still needed: process readings in range.* Knockout barrels cannot exceed the vessel's capacity; the reason names the limit, such as *at most 25.5 bbl into FV1*.

| Stage | Readings |
| --- | --- |
| Mash | **Mash water gallons**, **Mash pH** |
| Lauter | **Sparge water gallons**, **Pre-boil barrels** |
| Boil | **Boil minutes** |
| Whirlpool | **Whirlpool °F**, **Whirlpool minutes**, **Whirlpool rest minutes** |
| Knockout | **Knockout °F** |

Knockout holds the facts you only know at the end of the brew. **Vessel** is the tank it knocked out into, and starts at the tank chosen on Prep. **Knockout barrels** must be above zero, and starts at the planned barrels. **Brewed on** starts at today. Once all three are set, Knockout shows the loss baseline, such as *Knockout 22 bbl → FV-3*. Until then it reads *Choose a vessel, positive barrels and a brew date*.

When nothing is pulled above zero, **Confirm no ingredients used** appears below the stages. Switch it on to record a brew with no ingredients.

**Record brew day** stays disabled until:

1. every ingredient is confirmed;
2. every amount is a number at zero or above, and every line pulled above zero has a source;
3. the lines pulled from each lot and bin together fit what it holds;
4. something is pulled, or **Confirm no ingredients used** is on;
5. Knockout has a vessel, barrels above zero and a brew date.

Until then, the line beside **Record brew day** names what is still needed, such as *Still needed: a source for Chit Malt.* **Confirm all** does not choose a source: set a row's lot and bin with **Edit**, or on Prep.

On a phone, **Edit** and **Confirm** sit on their own line under each ingredient, and **Record brew day** stays at the bottom of the screen.

**Record brew day** is the step that uses stock. In one step it freezes the plan and actuals, takes the confirmed amounts out of their lots and bins, stamps the brew date and opens the vessel's occupancy. Insufficient stock refuses the whole action. It is refused if the vessel is already occupied, including a backdated day that falls inside a stretch the vessel was full but has since been emptied. A vessel emptied earlier the same day (in the brewery's time zone) can be brewed into with that day's date: the new occupancy starts when the vessel was emptied, not at midnight. It is also refused when the knockout barrels exceed the vessel's **Capacity (bbl)**; the message names the vessel and what it holds.

Confirmations and edits on Brew day aren't saved until you record. Leave the page and they start over from the saved Prep pulls. Changing an amount here doesn't change the Prep list.

The recipe's planned water additions are rows too, confirmed and recorded with the brew like any ingredient. Prep doesn't list them. Each amount is converted to the unit its material is stocked in, so 40 g of gypsum stocked in pounds reads *plan 0.0882 lb*. Mash and sparge additions sit under **Mash**, and kettle additions under **Boil**. An addition whose unit can't be converted, such as millilitres of an acid stocked by weight, is listed under **Mash** as a reminder, such as *5 mL planned · add with Correct turn after recording*. Record the brew, then open **Correct turn** (below) on that turn and use its **Add ingredient** to add it.

Brew day has no **Add ingredient**, and you can't swap in a different material.

### After the brew [#after-the-brew]

<Screen name="Brew day" />

A brewed batch's page is titled with its batch number and brand, such as **B-0003 · Hazy IPA**. It shows its current open vessel and total confirmed knockout. Its **Planned** line shows only when the batch was brewed on a different day or knocked out a different volume than planned. Each frozen brew record names its **Recipe** with the version it was brewed from, such as **Hazy IPA v4**, under **Frozen recipe plan**, which starts folded: tap it to see the plan. A record shows its own **Confirmed knockout** only when the batch has several turns or corrections, or that figure differs from the knockout above. A completed batch also shows **Completed** with the day it completed, **Packaged** with the barrels its closed packaging runs packaged, and **Written off as loss** with the barrels its completion wrote off, or **None** when the residual was below the threshold. **Add brew turn** records another brew of the same batch into that tank, with its own actual ingredients, process and date. The first batch brew date stays unchanged. The remaining recipe plan prefills the next turn; confirm the actual quantities. Another turn is refused after a transfer, started packaging, closure, a dependent filed report, a date before the first turn, or a capacity excess. Normal later additions do not prevent another turn. The frozen record retains its original knockout, sources and process observations even after the occupancy closes or master data changes. Older batches without a frozen record explicitly say it is unavailable. **Correct turn** selects the current revision of that turn, requires a reason and appends a revision, exact material reversals and any knockout measurement delta. It never changes the original record. Ingredient and process corrections remain available after later additions, transfers, packaging and closure. They leave the beer volume and downstream records unchanged. A knockout volume change requires an open batch and the original occupancy, enough remaining beer to cover any reduction, room for any increase, and no dependent filed report.

**Add brew turn** and **Correct turn** use the older one-page brew form, with a row per ingredient rather than the timeline. Confirm each **Material source**, **Stage**, and **Actual quantity** before recording. Choose the exact lot and bin, and enter stock units for water additions rather than assuming a conversion. Use **Add ingredient** for substitutions or other actual use, and **Remove ingredient** to drop a line. If none were used, remove the rows and select **Confirm no ingredients used**. Optional process observations record measured values; blanks stay absent. A new turn goes into the same tank, so its **Vessel** is fixed. A correction also fixes **Brewed on** and asks for a **Correction reason**.

The last occupied tank offers only **Save reading**, which returns you to where the round began. If the connection or response is lost, **Retry exact reading** keeps the original time and request ID; it never substitutes the reconnect time. When the shell sends that saved reading after reconnection, the open sheet unlocks without another Retry or Fix; open Vessel detail to see it added to **Reading history**. If the tank closed before the first send, choose **Fix as new reading** only after reviewing a fresh observation. A reading that the server already committed still replays its one original result after the tank closes. Readings never overwrite one another. Each is its own row on Vessel detail, newest first. The gravity field is labelled and read in whatever unit you chose under [Gravity unit](#gravity-units). Changing the unit never changes a saved reading. If what you type there is not a gravity MGR can read, the field says so and **Save reading** stays disabled until you correct it or clear the field. A mistyped gravity is never quietly dropped from an otherwise saved reading.

The reading sheet opens over the vessel's existing details and history, including from a direct link. **Observed at** names the brewery's time zone and defaults to the current time there, even if your device uses another zone. It stays folded as **Observed** with that time and **Change**; select **Change** to edit it. Saved history uses that same brewery clock. An edited time skipped by a daylight-saving clock change, or one that occurs twice, is refused; choose an unambiguous time instead. An unchanged default or recovered observation keeps its exact original instant, including during a repeated hour. **Retry exact reading** resends the stored observation time unchanged; it never converts an old saved timestamp to a new instant.

Unavailable **Save reading** actions name the missing or invalid measurements beside the button. Temperature and pH accept ordinary decimal notation, not exponents. Negative specific gravity is invalid, but negative °Plato and specific gravity below 1.000 can be valid readings for fermented beer; MGR preserves those unit conversions. Correct invalid readings or leave optional measurements blank before saving a fresh request. Retrying an already-saved reading still sends its exact original request.

## Packaging [#packaging]

Closing a run freezes package cost from its batch ingredient receipts divided by knockout volume (as corrected on the brew record), charged by barrels drawn, plus its standard packaging BOM at the used source receipt costs. Draw loss is absorbed; packaging material loss is not. Later brew, packaging-material, or receipt corrections do not change that cost. Later ingredient additions affect only later closes. A blend receiver currently includes only its own ingredients and knockout; incoming beer cost is not apportioned. Historical runs are not backfilled, and repack children without original output costs remain uncosted.

Open an unstarted run to **Reschedule** its planned date or **Cancel plan** and confirm. As on a batch, **Reschedule** waits for a different planned date. Admin, Brewer and Warehouse can change these plans. Cancellation retains the run and planned outputs in history, removes their material and beer demand, and stops the run from blocking batch completion. It moves no beer or stock. Started or closed runs cannot be cancelled or rescheduled. An active old plan continues to count as demand until it is cancelled.

Shortages do not prevent saving a plan; closing requires enough stock for confirmed actual use and loss.

**Save run plan** names what is **Still needed**: a brand, a planned date, and at least one positive planned output quantity. An empty **Brand** picker says **No brands yet**. Admins and Sales can follow **Add a brand in Catalog**; Brewers and Warehouse are told that an admin or sales member can add one. **Source tank · optional** picks an existing occupied tank, not a typed name. With none available, it explains that you can leave the source blank and pick it before starting the run.

Planned **keg** quantities must be whole numbers. Other packages may use fractional quantities. Invalid entered quantities must be corrected before saving, even when another output is valid; use ordinary decimal notation rather than exponents. If a selected tank holds less beer than the plan needs, MGR warns before saving but still allows the plan. No tank is required, and saving does not reserve beer or move inventory. A date before today in the brewery’s timezone also warns without blocking the plan.

When the selected brand has no packages, **Add a package in Catalog** opens Catalog for Admin and Sales. Brewer and Warehouse instead see who can add the package.

Packaging separates **Upcoming** and **Recent** runs into sortable tables on desktop and labeled lists on phones. Upcoming shows the run number, brand, planned date, source, planned quantity, and next action. Recent shows status and the **Closed or cancelled** date instead of a next action. Planned quantity is the total package count from the plan, not the actual output; when a package unit is not supplied, no unit is guessed. The detail's planned outputs table labels SKU counts **Planned units** and **Actual units**. An unsourced plan says **No source yet**; a cancelled plan labels its date **Cancelled** and retains the cancellation note. Lot, output, yield, and loss columns appear only when those facts are available. Select a run number or its row to open the record. Closed and cancelled dates follow the brewery’s timezone.

An active, unstarted run whose planned date is before today in the brewery’s timezone is marked **Overdue** beside its planned date. Today’s plans are not overdue, and cancelled or closed runs never carry that cue.

The scheduling material preview separates **Material**, **Planned**, **On hand**, and **Short**. Planned amounts include each material's base unit. A dash means unavailable, while zero means a known zero. Plan and close requirements also show **On order**; incoming stock is not on-hand stock.

A run without a tank shows **Source tank** and **Start run** on its page: choose the tank (with only one tank holding beer, it is already chosen) and select **Start run** to pick it and start the run in one step. Until a tank is chosen, it says *Still needed: a source tank.* A run whose tank was picked when it was scheduled shows only **Start run**. Starting requires a tank, so an unsourced run can never begin. A started run's page becomes the close form: **Barrels drawn** (starting from the planned outputs' volume when every planned package has one), the **Actual outputs** for each planned SKU (defaulting to the planned quantity), a **Lot code**, **Packaged on** and an optional **Best by** date, and the **Finished goods location** and bin the packaged units land in. With only one location it is already chosen, and choosing a location that has only one bin fills that bin. All four of the last group are required, because a vessel has no location of its own for the goods to appear at. Each actual output must be a plain number of zero or more, and a keg output a whole number of kegs; otherwise **Close packaging run** stays disabled and says *Still needed: valid actual counts (whole numbers for kegs).* MGR refuses a fractional keg count on its own too, so a close can never stock half a keg. **Close packaging run** stays disabled until **Barrels drawn** is above 0, every actual output is filled in (an explicit 0 is allowed), **Lot code**, **Packaged on**, the finished goods location and bin are set, and every planned material has complete confirmed actuals. The line beside it names whatever is still missing, such as *Still needed: a lot code, a finished goods location, and a finished goods bin.* Select **Close packaging run** to write the lot, the finished-goods stock, and the packaging materials consumed in one step. The finished stock is dated **Packaged on**, so it counts in that month's compliance report. A date after today, or one inside a filed report period, is refused and the run stays open. Review **Planned materials** beside **Confirmed material actuals**. Each row names the exact material, source location, bin and tracked lot. Used quantities start from the plan with first-expiring sources suggested; confirm or change them, and use **Add material source** to split quantities or name an extra material. Counted materials require whole units; measured materials allow four decimal places in their stored unit. Enter **Used**, **Damaged / lost**, and **Unused returned** for each source, including explicit zero for a planned material not used. An entirely unused tracked material can leave its lot blank when all three quantities are zero. Stock decreases by used plus lost; unused returned is an observation and never adds stock. MGR refuses insufficient exact-source stock and never silently substitutes another lot. **Close packaging run** first re-reads the material plan. If the BOM changed since the page opened, it shows the current plan instead of closing, keeps your entered actuals, and says *The material plan changed. Check the material actuals against it, then close again.* Check the actuals and select **Close packaging run** again. The close freezes the reviewed BOM, material names and units, source identities and actual quantities with the output lot in one transaction. MGR records **Barrels drawn** rounded to three decimal places. A close whose packages exceed that recorded draw by more than 0.0005 bbl is refused; check the drawn amount and package counts. Equality and differences up to that rounding tolerance are allowed. A refusal leaves the run open and records no lot, finished stock or material consumption. A close whose packages add up to more beer than the tank held is also refused, and so is one that brings the batch's packaged total, across all its runs, past what the batch had after recorded losses; the message gives both figures, so check the counts. A closed run cannot be reopened. The tank itself stays open afterward; emptying it is a separate Cellar transfer, which MGR accepts only once no started run still draws from that tank.

A closed run shows its **Brand**, **Source** tank, **Planned** date and the day it **Closed**, then its committed **Lot**, **Packaged on**, **Barrels drawn**, **Actual outputs**, and **Material record history**. Actual quantities come from the saved close or latest output correction, not the plan; a missing recorded value says **Not recorded**. A run closed with no packaging materials shows **No packaging materials.** in its history and offers no material correction. **Correct material actuals** requires a reason and appends a replacement record with exact reversals of the previous usage and loss. It preserves the original record, finished output quantities and tank draw. Correction is refused after finished goods are sold, transferred, repacked or adjusted, after batch completion, or when a filed report depends on the run.

For a mistaken finished count or **Barrels drawn**, Admin, Brewer and Warehouse can use [Correct packaging run outputs](/docs/api/packaging#correct_packaging_run_outputs) through the command API. This does not reopen the run. Supply the reason, corrected draw, and corrected quantities for its planned SKUs; omitted SKUs become zero. Keep the original finished-goods location and bin if the run has produced stock. If it has never produced stock, choose a valid finished-goods location and bin. Read [output correction history](/docs/api/packaging#get_packaging_output_corrections) first and supply its latest correction ID, or null when there is no earlier correction. A stale revision is refused. The original count stays in history, its stock is exactly reversed, and replacement stock is dated to the original **Packaged on** day on the same lot. Correction history records every planned SKU at the quantity actually stored, including zeros. The lot balance, finished stock, batch packaged volume, compliance production and tank draw all reflect the correction. Packaging material actuals stay unchanged; correct those separately if needed.

Output correction has the same sold, transferred, repacked, adjusted, completed-batch and filed-report restrictions as material correction. The corrected packages must fit within the corrected draw and the available tank and batch volume. The corrected draw is stored to three decimal places and cannot round up beyond the available beer. Once the source tank has been emptied, its recorded draw cannot be reduced, because that would restore beer to a tank stay that has ended. Output counts can still be corrected without reducing that draw. Keg counts must be whole. A zero-output correction is allowed. Historical runs recorded without stock cannot use this correction. If the response is uncertain, retry the exact command input with its original request ID; do not start a changed request.

If a close or material correction response is uncertain, its exact input and request ID stay saved in this browser tab across navigation and reload. **Unresolved requests → Saved request** shows the original input. Use **Retry saved request** to recover it, even if the run now shows closed; retry never creates a second close or correction. The open form also offers an unchanged retry. Both close and material correction stay frozen for that run until recovery or deliberate discard; other runs remain available. A changed material plan definitively refused on the first send permits review and a corrected new close, but a later refusal cannot prove an earlier uncertain send did nothing.

Before **Discard saved request → Confirm discard**, check the closed run's lot, actual outputs and material history. Discard only removes this tab's retry; it sends nothing and does not undo saved work. Recovery belongs to the original signed-in person and brewery, and sends nothing automatically. Closing the browser tab clears its recovery storage. These packaging actions are not queued for offline use.

**Repack** also opens directly from `/packaging/repack` (which redirects to `/packaging?repack=1`). When no SKU can be broken, it shows **No packages to repack** and explains that a SKU breaks when its format is made of another format, without offering stock fields or a confirmation. For Admin it links to **Catalog**, where a format's contents are set; for Warehouse it says an admin or sales member sets them there. Otherwise choose **Package to break down** first; its location, bin, lot, quantity and preview appear after the choice. **Confirm repack** stays disabled until its required selections and quantity are filled, and names what is missing beside it. A chosen location with no bins says so under **Bin** and links to **Locations**.

**Repack** (Admin and Warehouse only; a Brewer does not see this button), next to Schedule run, breaks a composed unit (a case) into the units it is made of at one location and bin. It is a shape change, never a production or a removal. Choose the **Package to break down** (only composed SKUs are offered), the **Location** and **Bin**, the **Lot** the cases come out of, and the **Packages to repack**. **Lot** lists that SKU's lots in the bin with how many each holds; **Untracked stock** is stock recorded without a lot, and the note under **Lot** says so. It is not a new lot, and it cannot stand in for lotted stock. When the bin holds only one lot of that SKU and no untracked units, that lot is already chosen. The broken units leave that lot, and the units they break into keep it, so the lot's trace still reaches them. A bin holding lotted stock refuses a repack with no lot chosen (**Choose the recorded lot for this removal.**). A repack of more than the lot holds says **Not enough stock in the selected bin and lot**, followed by what is on hand and a request to reduce the quantity or choose another source. The "into" side is not typed: the component SKU and its count are derived from the case format's composition, and the tape shows both legs and the barrels they conserve before you **Confirm repack**. Repack doesn't record damage, and the sheet says so: the count it adds always comes from the composition. The case format's packaging comes off or goes back on the same bin in whole units for a counted material: what is used up rounds up and what returns to stock rounds down. A repack that uses up more packaging than the bin holds is refused with **Not enough stock in the selected bin and lot**, followed by the material quantity on hand and a request to reduce the quantity or choose another source, and nothing is recorded. A composed SKU whose format has no single component record cannot be broken and says so in place of the button.

Purchasing is four pages. **Vendors** and **Materials** hold the definitions, **Purchase orders** (Work tab) is where an order lives from draft to received, and **Planning** shows what committed work is short of and drafts the orders to cover it.

### Vendors and contracts

<Screen name="Vendors" />
<Screen name="Contracts" />

**Vendors** (More tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists your suppliers. **Add vendor** and **Edit** take a name, optional email and phone (7 to 15 digits; spaces, dots, dashes and parentheses are fine), payment terms (**Due on receipt**, **Net 15**, or **Net 30**, the default; the same list customers use), and a **Lead time** in days. The lead time is the vendor's, not the material's. When Planning lists a material short and it resolves to this vendor, its buy-by date is the needed-by date less this lead time. The list is a table: each vendor's **Status** (Active or Inactive), its typed **Lead time** (**Not typed** when blank), its **Observed lead time**, and how many **Contracts** it holds, with **Edit** at the end of the row. Select a vendor's name to open its page. Once a vendor has received orders, **Observed lead time** shows the average from sent to last receipt, with how many orders it rests on (**over 3 orders** is weak evidence) and how they were sent (**sent from a mail client** or **sent outside MGR**), because a date someone typed for an order phoned in is not the same evidence as one MGR stamped.

<Screen name="Vendor detail" />

A vendor's page shows its status, email, phone, terms, typed lead time and observed lead time. Below them it lists the vendor's **Contracts** with the same four numbers as the Contracts list. Contracts are read-only here: add or edit them in the Contracts list on **Vendors**. Admin and Warehouse also see **Open purchase orders**: the vendor's draft, sent and partially received orders, each opening its own page. Brewer cannot read purchase orders, so the section is not shown. **Default materials** lists the materials whose default vendor this is, with inactive ones marked **Inactive**. **Edit** on the page opens the same **Edit vendor** sheet as **Edit** in the list, and **Deactivate vendor** is in that sheet.

The optional vendor **Phone** accepts 7 to 15 digits with spaces, dots, dashes, parentheses, and a leading **+** for international numbers. Letters and extension notation are not accepted. An invalid phone is highlighted after you leave the field, and **Save vendor**, **Deactivate vendor**, and **Reactivate vendor** wait until it is corrected or cleared.

Saved vendors show **Vendor status** in their Edit sheet and **Active** or **Inactive** in the list. **Deactivate vendor** opens a confirmation before saving; **Reactivate vendor** makes an inactive vendor available again. The confirmation also saves any changes in the form. Deactivation removes the vendor from **New purchase order** choices, not from existing materials, contracts or orders. It does not cancel existing orders or remove other vendor links. Vendors cannot currently be deleted.

Above the list, **Search vendors** finds vendors whose name contains what you type, a moment after you stop typing, and **Contracts** below then lists only those vendors' contracts; **No vendors match** means nothing does, and **Clear search** below the box shows every vendor again.

Vendor dialogs say **New vendor** or **Edit vendor**, and contract dialogs say **New contract** or **Edit contract**. When there is exactly one vendor, a new contract selects it for you; editing a contract keeps its saved vendor.

Below the vendors, **Contracts** lists commitments: one vendor, one material, a quantity in purchase units, an optional price, window, and **Contract number**. A contract with a number shows it as the row's label; without one, the row falls back to the material name. A contract is not an order. You order against it all year, so each row shows four numbers: **committed**, **received**, **on order** (placed but not yet arrived), and **available**, which is committed less both. Available is the one to decide against; received is the one accounting reconciles. A contract never stops you ordering: exceed it and the extra lines are ordinary spot lines.

**Add contract** needs a vendor and a material to exist first. With no vendors yet, the form's **Vendor** picker says **No vendors yet. Close this form and choose Add vendor.** With no materials yet, its **Material** picker says **No materials yet.** with **Add a material**, which opens **Materials**.

**Quantity** must be above zero and at most 9,999,999,999.9999, as a whole or decimal number without exponent notation. **Unit cost ($)** is optional and must be from $0 to $21,474,836.47 when entered. After you leave either field, invalid entries show **Enter a quantity above zero.** or **Enter a cost of zero or more.** beside it. **Save contract** stays unavailable while required fields are missing or either number is invalid, including exponent notation such as `1e3`.

The quantity is in the material's purchase unit and may be fractional, such as **1.5** or **0.001**; it is stored to four decimal places. A unit cost with more than two decimal places is rounded to the nearest cent, so **1.234** saves as **$1.23**. **Starts** and **Ends** are optional. An end date before the start date shows **Enter an end date on or after the start date.** under **Ends**, and **Save contract** stays unavailable until you fix it. The same day for both is allowed. A chosen date shows **Clear start date** or **Clear end date** under it; clear it and save to remove that date from the contract.

In **Edit contract**, **Delete contract** (Admin, Warehouse, and Brewer) asks for confirmation and removes an unused contract. A contract linked to a purchase order cannot be deleted; MGR asks you to set an end date instead, so the purchase order keeps its contract history.

### Materials

<Screen name="Materials on hand" />

**Materials on hand** (Beer tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) is a table of every material, including inactive ones: its **Type**, **On hand** summed across every bin in its base unit, **Lots** (**Tracked** for a lot-tracked material), and **Status** (Active or Inactive). With no materials yet, the list says to add one first, then receive a purchase order to put it on hand. A material that isn't lot-tracked can also be counted in. A lot-tracked material comes on hand only by receiving a purchase order, because a count needs an existing lot. Each row offers **Edit**, which changes the material itself, beside **Count**, which records what is on hand at one bin. **Add material** and **Edit** take a name, a kind (malt, hop, yeast, adjunct, chemical, packaging, other), **Base unit** (what recipes and counts use), **Purchase unit** (how you order it) and **Base units per purchase unit**. For hops bought in 44 lb boxes, set Base unit to *lb*, Purchase unit to *each*, and Base units per purchase unit to *44*. Base units per purchase unit takes at most four decimal places, so a whole-unit receipt always converts exactly: *453.5924* grams per pound saves, *453.59237* is refused with **Base units per purchase unit takes at most four decimal places.** The form also takes whether it is **lot-tracked**, an optional **default vendor**, for malt and adjunct an optional **Extract potential** (specific-gravity style between 1.000 and 1.050, so 1.037 for a malt of 37 points per pound per gallon; recipe predictions read it, and leaving it blank on an edit keeps the one saved), and whether it is **Active** (available to recipes and purchase orders; on by default). Units are refused once anything stores a quantity of the material: movements, a recipe version's ingredients, a format's packaging materials, a purchase order line, a contract, or a transfer line. Each of those was written in the units the material had then, and a recipe's predicted gravity would otherwise change under it. A lot-tracked material asks for a lot code at every receipt of a counted quantity; an untracked one never does. **Lot-tracked** cannot be turned on while the material has stock recorded without a lot: saving says "material has stock without a lot: count it to zero before turning on lot tracking". Count that stock to zero at each bin first, then turn tracking on.

The create dialog is titled **Add material**; an existing material opens **Edit material**. **Kind** starts with **Select kind**. Kind choices use readable names such as **Malt** and **Hop**. Unit choices spell out the measure and keep its abbreviation, such as **Pounds (lb)** and **Gallons (gal)**, or **Each** for an individual purchase unit. The empty materials list links Admin and Warehouse to **Purchase orders** to order and receive stock. A Brewer without either role instead sees guidance to ask an Admin or Warehouse teammate to order and receive it.

Above the list, **Search materials** finds materials whose name contains what you type, a moment after you stop typing, and **Type** shows only one kind, such as **Malt** or **Hop**; the search keeps the type you chose. **No materials match** means nothing does; **Clear search** below the box clears the text search, and **All types** removes the type filter.

Stock totals include every recorded material-and-bin balance, even in a large inventory. If MGR cannot read the complete list, reload before using its totals. A failed stock read is not a zero balance.

**Count** on a material row opens a cycle count for one bin: pick the location and bin, type what you counted in base units, and **Record count**. The count is always recorded, even when it matches. Only the variance posts, as a count adjustment. When the material holds several lots, a shortage comes off the earliest best-by lots first and an overage lands on the newest lot; a shortage crossing two lots writes one adjustment per lot. The live preview shows the system total and variance for the selected bin. Enter base units only: automatic whole-roll conversion is not available.

**Count sheet**, beside **Add material**, counts several materials in one pass. Pick the location and bin; the sheet lists every material on the list that has stock in that bin. Type a count for each material you counted and leave the rest blank: a blank line is not counted and not recorded. A search or **Type** filter on the list narrows the sheet too. One preview covers every counted line, and one **Record count** saves them all as one count. When the bin holds none of the listed materials, the sheet says so; count a material new to that bin from its row's **Count**.

### Purchase orders

Admin and Warehouse can use **Cancel purchase order** with a **Closure reason** on a draft or sent order with no receipts. It sits below the order's main button, folded under its title: select **Cancel purchase order** to open the reason and the button. **Close short** folds the same way under **Close remaining quantity**. Until a reason is typed, the button says *Still needed: a closure reason.* After a receipt, use **Close short** with a reason when the vendor will not supply the remainder. The order's status then reads **Closed short** followed by the reason, and a cancelled one **Cancelled** followed by the reason. The reason, actor, and closure time remain recorded. Closed orders leave the open Work and purchasing lists, and their unreceived remainder stops counting as on order. Existing receipts and stock remain unchanged; receipt corrections do not reopen the order. Corrected receipt expectations still use the ordered amount less other effective receipts, not the closed order's zero operational remainder. New receipts on a closed order are refused.

**Purchase orders** (Work tab; Admin and Warehouse) lists open orders: drafts, sent, and partially received. Select anywhere on a row to open its order. Each row's status reads in sentence case, as on the order's page: **Draft**, **Sent** with its dates, **Partially received** with the lines still due, **Received**, **Cancelled**, or **Closed short** for an order closed before every line arrived. An open row also names its next step: **Mark sent** opens a draft's send confirmation, and **Receive** opens a sent or partially received order. A received or cancelled order has no next action.

**New PO** opens a full-page draft with the vendor and optional expected date first, followed by material lines. Choosing a vendor fills **Expected** with today plus the vendor's **Lead time**. A vendor with no lead time typed leaves it blank. You can change or clear the date. An expected date before today, in the brewery's time zone, shows a warning; the PO still saves with it. Each line keeps its material, labeled quantity in purchase units, and optional **Unit cost ($)** together, without a surrounding card. On a phone, the material sits above quantity and cost; the narrowest screens stack all three. A lot-tracked material also shows an optional **Expected lot**, the lot the vendor named when you ordered. When the chosen vendor has exactly one contract for a line's material in force today, picking that material fills **Unit cost ($)** with the contract's price, and the line draws that contract down; you can still type another cost. With no contract in force, or several, the cost stays blank and no contract is drawn down. Use the quantity stepper or type a decimal quantity. **Add line** adds another material; with more than one line, **Remove** below a line drops it.

Every line needs a material and a quantity above zero, up to 9,999,999,999.9999, and a unit cost, when entered, from $0 to $21,474,836.47. Type ordinary numbers, not exponent notation such as `1e3`. Once you leave a line's quantity or unit cost, a quantity of zero or less is marked with *Enter a quantity above zero.*, a negative unit cost with *Enter a cost of zero or more.*, and a value past its limit with the limit, such as *Enter a quantity no greater than 9,999,999,999.9999.* **Save draft** stays unavailable until you fix or remove that line, and the line beside it names what is still missing, such as *Still needed: a vendor.* With no vendors or materials yet, the **Vendor** or material picker says so and links to **Vendors** or **Materials**. Changing a line's material clears its previous expected lot. The expected lot is advisory: it creates nothing, and receiving prefills from it. Once you choose a vendor, the materials whose default vendor it is are listed first in each line's material picker; every other material follows. **Save draft** numbers the order PO-0001 upward and returns to the purchase-order list. Saving does not send the order or add stock. **Save and mark sent** saves the same draft and then marks it sent from your mail client, as **Mark sent** below does, and opens the new order. If marking it sent fails, the order stays a saved draft: the message says so, both buttons stay unavailable, and **Open the draft** opens it so you can select **Mark sent** there. To record a phoned, faxed or portal order, use **Save draft** and then **Mark sent**.

A draft's page offers **Mark sent** first, then **Edit draft**, then **Cancel purchase order** folded below. **Edit draft** opens the New PO form on the saved vendor, expected date, and lines. Change any of them, add or remove lines, and select **Save draft** to return to the draft, or **Save and mark sent**. The draft keeps its number and note. A sent order cannot be edited. For **Mark sent**, choose how it went out, **From my mail client** or **Phoned, faxed, or vendor portal**, and confirm. MGR does not send email: it records that you sent the order today. The status then reads **Marked sent** with the date, never *Delivered*, because nothing here can know that. Marking is one-way; a sent order cannot go back to draft.

A sent order's page is the receiving form. Choose the location and bin everything lands in and the received date. The bin starts on the one holding the most of the order's first material still due, or on the first location's first bin when none is on hand. Then, for each line still due, type what you **counted**. Lines default to what is still due; type over them. Over and short are both allowed and both recorded. A lot-tracked line asks for the **lot code off the package** (prefilled from the expected lot when the order named one; if the vendor shipped another lot, type what the box says) and an optional best-by date. A lot-tracked line counted 0 needs no lot code, and no lot is recorded for it. A line left blank is not part of this receipt. A count of 0 records that line as short, but only beside a line counted above zero: a receipt of only zeros records nothing, so **Still needed** asks for *a count above zero on at least one line*. A negative or non-number count is never dropped: **Still needed** beside **Receive purchase order** names that line, along with a missing location, bin, received day, or lot code. A preview shows counted base-unit quantities and over/short differences before you commit. **Recent lots** is marked unavailable; read the lot from the package rather than expecting suggestions. **Receive purchase order** writes the receipt, its lines, the lots, and receipt movements into that bin in one step; only what you counted posts to inventory. The saved **Receipt** page opens after receiving and survives refresh. It shows posted base-unit quantities, any recorded lot reference, over/short amounts, and the current **Still owed** balance across all receipts; its back link names that purchase order (for example **PO-0042**) and returns to it. Open an earlier receipt from the purchase order's **Receipts** list. New receipts preserve the material name, units, conversion, and lot facts recorded at receipt; older receipts explicitly mark missing snapshots. The order becomes **partially received** until every line is met, then **received**. What each line still owes is worked out from the receipts every time, so a second receipt against the same order simply closes the remainder. **Show received and cancelled** at the foot of the list reveals finished orders.

Receipt counts, including corrections, accept at most four decimal places in purchase units; a fifth decimal is refused with **A count takes at most four decimal places.** The converted base-unit amount must also fit four decimal places. Because a material's **Base units per purchase unit** is itself kept to four decimal places, a whole-unit count always fits; only a fractional count of a material with a fractional conversion can fall outside. MGR refuses a count that would need rounding, so the receipt, remaining order quantity, and material stock stay in agreement.

Saved receipt headings name the purchase order and receipt date. **Status** shows the order's current state, including **partially received**, and **Still owed** names the remainder. Purchase-order submit and closure buttons sit at the bottom right on wider screens. Selection prompts such as **Select vendor** and **Select material** are hints, not choices.

Open a saved receipt and choose **Correct receipt** (Admin or Warehouse). Enter a **Correction reason**, every corrected counted quantity, and the actual lot code and best-by date. Choose the receiving location and bin; they start on the saved receipt. The receipt date stays fixed. The original receipt remains in history; MGR compensates its exact stock movements and appends a replacement receipt. Only the latest revision contributes to **Still owed**, so reducing a completed receipt can reopen the PO as partially received. **Original receipt** and **Corrected receipt** link the history. Correcting an older receipt does not make its cost newer than a later delivery's cost.

**Unit cost ($)** on receiving and **Correct receipt** records the actual price per purchase unit, including zero for free goods. Receiving starts on the ordered price; correction starts on the receipt's frozen price. Enter a missing invoice price or fix an incorrect one without reopening the sent order. A blank cost keeps the existing price, and a changed cost applies to this receipt only, not the sent order or other deliveries. The replacement receipt and its stock movements use the corrected price; the original facts stay in history. Cost corrections must preserve nonnegative stock and lot provenance.

Used stock does not by itself prevent correction. The corrected counts must leave every affected source and destination bucket nonnegative. A used lot's identity cannot change, and changed best-by facts cannot rewrite a used or shared lot. If consumed stock leaves the original bucket short, correct its count in that bucket rather than moving the receipt to another bin. Correction is also refused if another correction already replaced the receipt. Reload to see the current revision. If another inventory operation keeps material stock busy, MGR says the stock is busy; retry the correction. Historical receipts still need a known purchase-unit conversion. A zero replacement count is allowed when it leaves valid stock balances and does not detach a used lot.

### Post a receipt Bill to QuickBooks

Admin can choose **Post Bill to QuickBooks** on the latest saved receipt. MGR posts one Bill for that receipt, not the purchase order. Each positive counted line uses its recorded receipt movement's unit cost times its base-unit quantity, rounded to cents. The PO's ordered quantity and later price changes do not replace those receipt costs. A missing cost blocks posting; enter it while receiving or use **Correct receipt** to record the invoice price before posting the latest receipt's Bill.

If QuickBooks returns unauthorized for a credential that appeared current, MGR refreshes once and retries the identical saved body and request ID. An unavailable or expired refresh credential leaves the push pending for honest recovery. If any other response is uncertain, retry the same action so MGR sends the identical saved body and request ID. Once that request has completed, retry by the same currently authorized staff member returns its durable result without contacting QuickBooks again, even if the connection, mappings, catalog, or invoice status changed later; the remote document ID already stored on the attempt remains authoritative. A disconnect blocks a pending retry until the same company is verified and reconnected; then MGR resends the frozen request through the preserved logical connection. A different-company reconnect cannot retarget or finish the old request. A rejected push remains rejected on exact replay even after its mapping changes or a later corrected attempt succeeds. It needs an explicit corrected attempt after its mapping is fixed. Recreating a document is available only after sync confirms that the prior QuickBooks document was deleted; both cases keep the prior attempt in history.

**Sync now** also checks pushed credit memos in the connected QuickBooks company. A confirmed deletion stops that credit's beer money from reducing newly generated state totals and CSV exports. A deleted keg-deposit refund also stops reducing deposits held in Customer keg balance and Keg report; the separately recorded physical keg return stays unchanged, so a mismatch may become visible. Recreating the deleted credit in QuickBooks makes its original credit money count again in current totals. Its original lines and amounts, push history, physical returns, and already filed reports stay unchanged. An unavailable or malformed QuickBooks response does not prove deletion; retry the same sync. Applying a credit or leaving it with zero balance is not cash collected and does not mark it paid or voided.

QuickBooks and Square requests stop waiting after 20 seconds rather than leaving an action stuck. A QuickBooks sync also stops reading after three minutes, records a failed attempt, and leaves the last successful sync unchanged. No partial invoice observations are applied. Retry the same sync request after a timeout; a timed-out provider write may still have succeeded, so keep its saved attempt identity rather than starting a duplicate.

For an eligible live invoice with a current positive QuickBooks balance, the customer payment handoff reads a fresh QuickBooks link only after checking the signed-in customer, invoice, current company connection, payment settings, accounting permission, credential generation, and original pushed document. MGR checks the same connection snapshot and positive balance again after the QuickBooks read. A reconnect, credential rotation, or loss of accounting permission while the read is in flight makes that link unavailable. A zero or unknown current balance is also unavailable; a reversed payment with a new positive balance is eligible, and the next live sync clears its payment-confirmation marker. The link is never stored or shown in an MGR page; an unavailable or unverified link leaves the customer on a **Payment unavailable** page with instructions to contact the brewery.

## Sale channels [#channels]

<Screen name="Sale channels" />

<Screen name="Channel" />

*Admin only. Everyone who records or reads a movement sees the channel names; only Admin can add, rename, or remove one.*

A sale channel is a named way your brewery sells beer. Every brewery starts with four: **Wholesale**, **Taproom**, **DTC**, and **Export**. Open **Sale channels** from the **More** navigation to see each channel and its explicitly labeled **Tax treatment**, so a channel named **Export** is distinct from its **Tax treatment: Export**. Only Admin adds, edits, or deletes channels; Sales and Warehouse see the list read-only, and their back link returns to **More**.

1. Select **Add channel**, enter a **Channel name**, choose a **Tax treatment**, and select **Save channel**.
2. To rename a channel or change its treatment, select **Edit** on its row. The sheet is titled with the channel's name, for example **Edit channel · DTC**. Change the fields and select **Save channel**.
3. To remove a channel, select **Delete** on its row and confirm. **Delete** appears only on a channel that can be removed.

**Save channel** stays disabled until the name contains something other than whitespace. Names are saved without surrounding whitespace and must be unique within your brewery, ignoring case: **dtc** cannot be added beside **DTC**. A duplicate appears as an error under **Channel name**; change the name and save again. Renaming a channel only to change its capitalization is allowed.

The tax treatment (**Taxable**, **Export**, **Vessel supplies**, **Research**, or **Transfer in bond**) is the default for removals recorded on that channel. **Add channel** starts with **Taxable** selected, and the sheet explains every selected treatment:

- **Taxable**: beer removed with federal excise tax due.
- **Export**: sold for shipment outside the US, not taxed.
- **Vessel supplies**: supplies for ships or aircraft in foreign trade, not taxed.
- **Research**: used for lab analysis, research or testing, not taxed.
- **Transfer in bond**: sent to another brewery in bond; that brewery pays the tax.

The treatment is separate from the channel's name, so a channel named Export can still carry any treatment. A customer may override it on their own record, and the treatment that applies is frozen onto each movement the moment it is recorded. Editing a channel later never restates a month you have already filed; the channel sheet says so under the treatment.

Every channel can be renamed: no name is load-bearing, because an order carries its own channel from the moment it is created.

Some channels cannot be removed, so their rows have no **Delete**. The **Delete** confirmation states the rule: A channel used by any movement, customer, order, price cell or Square menu cannot be deleted. The system Taproom channel can never be deleted. Taproom counts and taproom removals are recorded on it, even after you rename it. Rename a channel instead, or clear its cells, move those customers, and pick another channel for that Square menu first.

## Errors and corrections [#errors-corrections]

A bookmarked staff page that your role cannot read opens **No access**, names the required roles, and offers **Back to Today**. Order controls follow your role: Sales confirms, adjusts and cancels; Warehouse picks, ships and puts back; Admin can do both.

**A form reports an error.** Read the message, fix the visible fields, and submit again. Your entries stay until the form succeeds or you close it. Permission errors need an administrator. **That name is already used** means another record of that kind already has the name, such as a second location named the same. The customer, vendor, brand, format, water profile and price group forms show it under the name field. Choose a different name or edit the existing record. **That already exists** means another record already uses some other value (for example, a second SKU for the same brand and format); change it or edit the existing record. **A number is out of range** means a figure is too large or too small for its field; enter a realistic value. A number larger than MGR can store, such as a water ion above 999,999.9 ppm or a price or cost above $21,474,836.47, is refused with a message that names the field and its limit.

**MGR says there are too many requests.** Wait for the stated retry time, then retry. Ask MGR messages count toward the same limit as other actions; after the stated time, select **Try again** in the panel. If the action's result was uncertain, use **Retry saved request** to recover the frozen input and original request ID safely. The same action on the same record waits until the original outcome is recovered or you discard the saved request.

**Your signed-in account or brewery changed while an action was pending.** MGR refuses the action before it writes anything. If the earlier response was uncertain, return to the original account and brewery and use the unchanged retry; do not start a replacement request until you have checked the record.

**A list says it "changed while loading".** A long list, such as Transfers, Packaging runs, the Pick sheet or Batches, loads in parts. If someone saves a change to it while it loads, MGR shows "… changed while loading. Reload and try again." instead of a list with rows missing or doubled. Reload the page.

**A page fails to load.** Select **Try again** once. If it still fails, give your administrator the brewery or customer name, page, approximate time, and the action you tried. When a **Reference** appears under the error, include it: it matches the server log for that failure.

**A record seems missing.** Confirm the brewery shown in the navigation, clear any order-status filter, and check the exact brand, SKU, customer, or location name.

**A page says "Not found".** A mistyped or unknown staff address, or a record link that matches nothing in your brewery, shows a Not found page with MGR navigation and **Back to Today**, not an error. Its browser tab reads **Not found · MGR**, including linked record actions and histories. The record was removed, the link is wrong, or it belongs to a brewery you are not signed in to. Check the address, switch brewery, or ask the person who sent the link.

**Inventory is wrong.** Do a physical count and figure out what happened. Record an adjustment only for a verified count difference. Never disguise a sale, sample, loss, destruction, festival removal, return, or transfer as another type. Put a note on the correction.

**A saved Taproom count was too low.** Ask Admin to open the latest saved count and use **Correct count**. Keep the signed compensation and replacement entries with that count; do not add a generic inventory adjustment.

**A shipped order or invoice is wrong.** Do not add an unrelated inventory movement. Use **Return** on the invoice so the credit and the returned stock stay linked to the shipment history.

## Import CSV [#import]

<Screen name="Import" />

Admin opens **Settings → Import** or **Import CSV** on the setup checklist. Under **What are you importing?**, choose the kind. The import has four steps: **Upload**, **Match columns**, **Review** and **Import**. Upload a CSV with a header row and 1–5,000 data rows, match its columns to MGR's fields, review the rows, and import them. Before you upload, the page lists friendly field labels beside their CSV column keys and marks each one required, optional, or required for a US address. A reference column, such as **Sale channel** (`saleChannelId`), is marked **name or ID**: it takes an existing record's name or its ID. **Download template** saves a descriptively named CSV, such as `ship-tos-template.csv` or `products-and-skus-template.csv`, with those unchanged column keys and one incomplete example row. A required cell is deliberately blank, so the example cannot import as downloaded. Delete that row and add your own records; reference cells need your existing names or IDs. A header named exactly like a listed CSV key, with the same capitals, is matched on its own. Match any other header in the **Match columns** step. **Review 1 row** or **Review N rows** stays unavailable until every required field has a valid column match, including **State** when any row uses a US address. An optional field left at **No column** stays empty. Quoted commas, newlines, escaped quotes, a quote inside an unquoted value (such as `16" keg`), blank lines, Excel's `sep=,` first line, and a leading byte-order mark are supported. Malformed quoting, duplicate or missing headers, unequal row widths, and rejected files show an error under the **CSV file** field before anything is staged. An empty file asks for a header and at least one data row. A header row with no records below it asks you to add at least one record. A blank or repeated header asks you to give every column its own name. A file containing binary NUL bytes says it is not a CSV and asks you to save it as CSV in Excel or Google Sheets.

The complete API request is limited to 8 MiB. Most 5,000-row files fit; files with unusually large notes or other cells may need to be split into smaller batches.

When every required column already matches by name, Upload offers **Review N rows** directly, skipping **Match columns**. Select **Columns differ?** to match columns yourself instead.

The lookup lists show each existing record's label and ID. Match these exact CSV column keys (optional fields follow the semicolon). The page shows a friendlier label for each, such as **Brand** for `product`, **Customer type** for `type`, **Unit price (cents)** for `unitPriceCents` and **Quantity** for `qty`, and its messages use those labels:

| Kind | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Customers | `name`, `type`, `saleChannelId`, `state` (US only); `licenseNumber`, `paymentTerms`, `country`, `alias` |
| Ship-tos | `customerId`, `label`, and for a US address `state`; `address1`, `address2`, `city`, `zip`, `country` |
| Products / SKUs | `product`, `formatId`; `sku_name`, `style`, `abv`, `upc` |
| Channel prices | `saleChannelId`, `priceGroupId`, `formatId`, `unitPriceCents` |
| Opening balances | `skuId`, `locationId`, `binId`, `qty`; `note` |

Reference cells accept an existing record's name or UUID in the same ID column, including `formatId`. Names are trimmed and matched case-insensitively and exactly. Missing or ambiguous names block the row; use the UUID when several records share a name. A bin name is looked up within the row's location, and a channel-price pour format within the row's price group. Product imports use packaged formats only. Create missing reference records first: an import does not infer or create them. Customer `type` is distributor, retailer, brewery, or other; `paymentTerms`, when given, is `due_on_receipt`, `net15`, or `net30` (an empty cell means net30). Capitals and extra spaces in these two cells do not matter, so `Retailer` and `NET30` are accepted. **Match columns** lists each choice by its label, such as **Net 30 (net30)**, and **Edit row** offers the same labels as **New customer**. state is two uppercase letters. `country` is a two-letter country code such as JP; leave it blank for a US row. A row outside the US has no state, and a ship-to row outside the US needs only its customer and label. Prices are nonnegative whole cents. Opening quantity is a positive count of SKU units with at most two decimal places, and the bin must belong to the location. An existing product is reused without changing its metadata. Duplicate customer or SKU creation is blocked; channel prices update the existing price cell. Rows import in parallel, except where two rows could collide: rows with the same customer name, the same product, or the same price cell are applied in file order. For the same customer name or product, the earlier row wins. For the same price cell, the last row sets that cell's price. A Products / SKUs file with any UPC is applied entirely in file order. A Channel prices file is also applied entirely in file order when a reference column mixes names and UUIDs, so a name and its UUID cannot reorder writes to the same cell.

The **Review** step shows a compact table under the four steps: **Row**, **Record**, **Problem** (the row's errors, or **Validated**) and **Status** (**Ready** or **Blocked**). A row whose name cell is blank reads **(no name)**, greyed, in **Record**; its editor keeps the kind, as in **Edit row 2 · Customers**. Expand **Edit row** to correct that row's fields on either phone or desktop, or go **Back to matching columns**. The import kind is fixed after upload; go **Back to upload** to choose another kind. **Import N rows** (or **Post opening balances**) imports the ready rows and stays disabled if every row is invalid. Only rows the review marks ready are sent; rows it marks blocked stay out of the batch, so fix them before importing or import them later. Each sent row commits independently, and a row the server still refuses comes back blocked with its error. Results read **N imported · N blocked**. Each result row shows its review number, the record's name, and **Imported** or **Blocked** with its error, not an internal record ID. An imported customer's name opens that customer, a ship-to's opens its customer, and a product/SKU's opens its brand at **Sells as**. Channel prices and opening balances have no record page, so their names are plain text. After a reload, the record name is blank because the file is no longer staged. The batch's request ID sits under **Batch request**. Skipped blocked rows leave gaps in the numbering. A failed product/SKU row leaves none of its new dependent records behind.

For customers, a later ready row with the same name is blocked as a duplicate of the first ready row in the file. Names match exactly, including capitals, after whitespace at either end is removed; changing the type or alias does not make the name distinct. Fixing a row recalculates which rows are ready. In **Edit row**, choose **Type** and **Payment terms** from their allowed options.

Keep the page open until results are recovered. If the connection fails, some rows may have committed: **Retry same batch**, shown until results come back, returns the original committed and blocked outcomes without posting opening stock twice. **Correct blocked rows in a new batch** carries only blocked rows back to an editable **Review** step. Even if lookup data changes, retrying the unchanged old batch returns its original blocked result. Never upload committed opening balances as a new batch; that would add the stock again. Reloading this browser tab restores the unresolved batch and its original row numbers. Retry is always explicit. If you have checked the imported records and the batch cannot be recovered, select **Discard saved request**, then **Confirm discard**; MGR drops the saved batch without sending it and returns to **Review**, or to **Upload** after a reload. Check which rows were saved first, and never re-import committed opening balances. Closing the tab clears its recovery data.

Once results show, **Import another file** returns to upload so you can import the next kind, such as ship-tos after customers or opening balances after SKUs. It clears the finished batch, file, and column matches, and reloads the reference ID lists so records the last batch created can be used. It appears only after results are recovered; an unresolved batch must be retried or discarded first. Any blocked rows from the finished batch are left behind, so correct them with **Correct blocked rows in a new batch** before moving on.

## Closing your account [#closing]

To close a brewery account, an administrator first disconnects QuickBooks, Square and Slack in **Settings**, then asks MGR support to close it. Closing ends sign-in for every staff member and buyer and cancels open invitations. Your records are not deleted: MGR keeps them for 3 years after closing, for brewery recordkeeping. Ask for an export of your records, one spreadsheet file per kind of record, before or after closing.

## Actions not yet available [#unavailable]

Do not hunt for hidden controls for these:

- public account sign-up
- resending or revoking a buyer (portal) invitation
- editing a purchase order after it is sent, or sending one by email from MGR
- deleting a customer that has history, a single ship-to, inventory history, orders, invoices, or credit memos

Ask the person who configures your brewery when one of these is required.

### Portal fulfillment warehouse [#portal-fulfillment-warehouse]

For cross-location stock transfers, receiving shows the recorded lots at each source bin. Enter the quantity from each source so it totals the received line; finished goods and material transfers preserve that lot on both legs. Manual inventory movements also offer the recorded lot in the selected SKU/bin. Select it for samples, losses or other lot-specific changes; historical untracked balances stay untracked.

For a material **Cycle count**, choose the location and bin and enter the counted quantity in the material's base unit. The count starts on the bin holding the most of that material; with none on hand, it starts on the first location's first bin. **Preview count** waits for a location, a bin and a counted quantity of zero or more, and the line beside it names whichever is missing. Once all three are set, MGR previews the count by itself a moment after you stop typing; **Preview count** runs it again. The preview shows every affected lot, its before-and-after quantity, and the signed adjustment. Shortages use earliest best-by lots first; overages go to the newest lot. **Record count** saves exactly that reviewed allocation. A matching count still records the observation without adjustment movements.

A **Count sheet** works the same way over several lines. It starts on the bin holding the largest balance of any listed material. **Preview count** waits for at least one counted quantity, and every typed one must be zero or more. The preview shows each counted line's lots and signed adjustments. Changing any line's quantity, or the bin, clears the preview for the whole sheet.

Editing the quantity or bin clears the preview. If stock or lot details change, preview again before recording. A busy stock message means another inventory operation is active; retry the preview. If recording receives no trustworthy response, the form freezes its quantity and allocation. Use **Retry unchanged count** to recover the original result. Counts do not convert rolls, cases or other purchase units, and lot allocation cannot be edited manually.

Customer account details and trading terms are edited directly on **Customer detail** with **Save customer**. Staff without edit permission see the same fields disabled. **Units** uses the shared choice buttons; selecting a unit saves it, and a failed save restores the previous choice.

### Recover an uncertain form submission

If a response is lost, **Unresolved requests** shows the saved input and **Retry saved request**. This includes staff and buyer invitations. Retry recovers the original operation; it does not submit subsequent field edits. A saved request blocks the same action on the same record. Row actions (prices, invoice pushes, menu items, mappings, deletes, and edits to an existing row such as a ship-to, bin, license or team member) are scoped to their row, so you can still save another price cell, push another invoice, or edit another row. Other actions, such as recording a movement or creating an order, wait until the saved request is retried or discarded, even for a different bin or customer. Different actions on the same page can still run while a request is unresolved. A request still being sent from this tab is not listed until its outcome arrives. If you have checked the record and know the outcome, select **Discard saved request**, then **Confirm discard**. MGR stops offering that retry and sends nothing; the record then accepts a new submission. Check the result before discarding, because a discarded request may already have been saved. Reloading the same browser tab preserves recovery. Return to the original signed-in account and brewery to recover its requests. Nothing is sent automatically. Keep the tab open until recovery finishes. CSV imports use **Retry same batch** on the Import page. Existing order, count, completion and Ask MGR recovery controls retain their own workflows.

If Square authorization is denied or fails, **Point of sale** says it was cancelled or could not finish and that the existing connection was not replaced. Use **Connect** to try again; when a connection already exists, **Try connecting again** opens the reconnect flow.


### Batch completion in-tank acknowledgement

Completing a batch lists the beer still in each open tank. When that total reaches max(0.05 bbl, 0.5% of the batch baseline), turn on the switch that names the barrels and tanks being written off as loss; **Complete batch** stays disabled until you do, and the line beside it says that switch is still needed. Before you choose a batch, it says *Still needed: a batch.* Below-threshold residue needs no acknowledgement. If someone changes tank volume after you reviewed it, completion is refused with the new figures: reselect the batch and confirm again. Existing loss reconciliation and eight-decimal precision remain unchanged. **Retry unchanged completion** keeps the exact acknowledgement and request; this does not add reopening or undo.
