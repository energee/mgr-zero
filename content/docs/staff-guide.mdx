## Sign in and sign out [#sign-in]

<Screen name="Sign in" />

1. Enter your **Email** and **Password**.
2. Select **Sign in**.
3. MGR opens **Today** for the brewery you're a member of.

Wrong credentials show **Incorrect email or password.** You can also select **Email me a link** to receive a passwordless sign-in link without creating a new account. Signed in but not on a brewery? MGR shows **No brewery yet** with **Sign out**; ask an admin for access. On a hosted installation, **Create brewery** is also available to signed-in accounts. Entry cards sit near the bottom of a phone screen and center on wider screens. If your session ends while you work, the next action returns you to Sign in with **Your session ended. Sign in to retry.** Open **Me** in the header when you want **Sign out**.

<Screen name="Reset password" />

**Forgot password?** on the sign-in card opens **Reset password**. Enter your **Email** and select **Send reset link**; the page always says a link is on its way if that email is on an account, so it never confirms whether one is. The link in the email opens **Set new password**, which shows the account and asks you to **Choose a password** and **Save password**; a used or timed-out link lands back on Reset password saying so. Signed in already? **Change password** in **Me** opens the same page.

## Accept an invitation and create a brewery [#getting-started]

<Screen name="Accept invite" />

Open the invitation email, enter **Your name** and **Choose a password** (at least eight characters), then select **Join**. The page shows the brewery and your assigned role. Staff open Today; buyers open their customer portal. The link is single use. A used, expired, or wrong-audience link opens **Invite expired**; sign in if you have already joined, or use **Reset password** and contact the brewery if access is still missing. There is no resend or existing-account attachment action.

<Screen name="Create brewery" />

On hosted MGR, a signed-in account can open **Create brewery** from **No brewery yet**. Enter **Brewery name**, a valid **Timezone** such as America/New_York, and an optional **TTB registry number**. **Create brewery** creates the brewery and makes you its first admin, then opens Today. Validation errors keep your entries for correction. Dedicated installations hide this entry and refuse its page.

<Screen name="First-run checklist" />

An admin sees **Set up** while the brewery has neither a location nor a brand. Adding either one returns Today to its usual work list. Add locations, optionally open **Import CSV**, add a brand, optionally **Invite staff**, and record opening inventory. Importing and inviting staff are optional; they do not hold the checklist open.

## Search and pick records [#search]

<Screen name="Search" />

**Search** in the header opens one search across SKUs, orders, invoices, lots, customers, purchase orders, and batches. Type a name to match on its first letters, or a document number (ORD-0231, INV-1042, PO-0142, B-0012, a lot code) to match it exactly; an exact number sorts first. Results group by kind; use the Up and Down arrow keys to move between matches and press Enter or click to open one. You only ever see records your role may read. The sheet distinguishes loading, failed, offline cached, and valid empty results.

SKU fields in **New Order** use the same search, narrowed to SKUs. They show recently selected SKUs first, followed by the current SKU list; successful searches are cached for the current signed-in session, brewery, and term so an offline retry can show those cached matches. Reloading or signing out clears recent choices and cached matches.

## Taproom variance [#taproom-variance]

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Variance by brand** from Taproom, Beer, or Taproom Today. Choose an owned taproom location and 4 or 12 weeks. The report compares whole completed count periods whose ending dates fall in that brewery-local window, with exact exclusive starting and inclusive ending times shown. The first count has no starting observation, so its actual depletion is shown without a comparison. A period may begin before the selected window.

Actual is the saved count's depletion. Expected is reconciled POS quantity times its frozen serving size; editing today's pour or package size never changes an existing expectation. A later reconciled sale can update a closed period's expected amount and variance, but cannot change counts or stock. Variance is expected minus actual.

Without usable POS observations, comparison rows are empty and expected stays blank. A dash means a value is unavailable; read zero alongside coverage and excluded consumption. Mapped lines still contribute when other items need mapping, with coverage and mapping gaps shown. Overlapping taps split each sale equally as an estimate; shares assigned to kegs outside inventory are excluded while count-derived actual stays intact. Sales without a matching open interval remain explicitly unattributed. Guest names do not identify POS sales, so guest yield is unavailable. POS connection and synchronization are not available yet.

## Taproom physical counts [#taproom-counts]

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Taproom** under Beer to prepare, record, and read physical counts. Choose a taproom location, then enter remaining whole packaged units for every displayed bin, SKU, and lot identity, including zero quantities. A partially full keg counts as one until it is gone; estimated fill levels never change the inventory ledger.

The page uses today's date in the brewery's timezone and holds the revision and prior-count identity from the prepared count. The expected card links that captured prior count by date and ID, or says **first count**. Every displayed bucket needs its own observation. A tracked lot and **untracked** stock are separate buckets; there is no automatic lot allocation. Admin and Warehouse see existing stock labels when one is available. Taproom sees the full worksheet row number on every bucket instead of raw lot identifiers on screen.

**Print current stock labels** uses the same captured count revision for Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom. The printed worksheet includes only current positive Taproom stock, keeps each row's position from the full count worksheet, and names a tracked lot by its current code or says **Untracked**. MGR rechecks the revision immediately before opening the browser's native print flow. If stock or the prior count changed, printing is refused and the page tells you to reload and review; entries and an uncertain count retry remain in place. An empty positive-stock projection says there is nothing current to print. Full lot history remains outside this projection as Admin and Sales compliance data.

A matching count still saves its server date and time, author, and all observations. Its receipt says **matched · no movement** per unchanged bucket. Shortages post only the missing units to the exact bucket, using the Taproom sale channel; the receipt shows the movement reference and frozen depleted BBL. POS setup and sales do not affect this posting. The page lists the newest 50 saved count headers for the selected taproom and can reopen each complete durable receipt, including counts larger than catalog list limits. Stock changes or a draft held past the brewery's local midnight require **Start fresh recount**, which clears entries and loads a new revision and server-owned date. Counts above recorded stock, incomplete counts, and a second count on the same date are refused.

The page also shows expected consumption while the next count is still a draft. It uses the latest saved count as the starting observation and the server's current time as the end, and identifies that saved count and both timestamps. Expected amounts are grouped by brand because several package SKUs may belong to one beer; your physical entries remain separate by bin, SKU, and lot. As you complete every bucket for a brand, the comparison shows draft actual depletion from the snapshot's package volumes and expected minus actual. A missing bucket keeps draft actual and difference blank. This is a draft estimate; the saved count receipt remains authoritative. No saved count means there is no baseline, and no usable POS observation leaves expected blank. A complete empty observation may show zero. Coverage bounds, partial coverage, unmapped or ignored lines, shares excluded because a tap was outside inventory, and poured volume without an active matching tap remain visible. **Refresh expected** updates only this comparison; it never posts stock, allocates lots, or replaces the count form's stock revision. If that refresh finds a newer saved-count baseline, the comparison becomes unavailable and the form stays locked until **Start fresh recount** loads the new snapshot and clears entries. Late POS reconciliation may change the aligned comparison until the count is saved.

If the page cannot tell whether a submission reached the server, including a timeout or `5xx` response, it freezes every quantity and the request ID. Select **Retry unchanged count** to recover the original result without posting twice. A later refusal cannot unlock an already uncertain request. A definitive first validation error leaves the fields editable for a corrected new attempt.

Admin can select **Correct count** on the latest saved count when one or more quantities were counted too low. Enter the corrected whole units, no more than the stock recorded before the count, and a reason. The form sends only increased lines but preserves every original bucket in the effective receipt. It cannot reduce a count, exceed the recorded-before quantity, correct a historical count, or correct an earlier correction. Warehouse and Taproom can read the corrected receipt and its author, time, and reason, but only Admin sees the action.

The original header, observations, and ledger rows remain unchanged. MGR appends an exact compensation for each changed depletion and, when the corrected amount still falls below recorded stock, a smaller replacement depletion using the original package volume, channel, tax treatment, state, bin, SKU, and lot. The corrected count keeps the original observation time, appears as one logical occurrence in history, becomes the next count's baseline, and updates variance without counting unchanged lines twice. Its signed ledger entries belong to the time when Admin made the correction; an earlier filed compliance snapshot stays byte-for-byte unchanged. If the response is uncertain, **Retry unchanged correction** reuses the frozen reason, quantities, payload, and request ID.

## Tap intervals [#tap-intervals]

Admin, Warehouse, and Taproom open **Tap board** from Beer, Taproom, or Taproom Today. Choose an owned taproom location. **Tap keg** opens a keg with its packaged keg SKU, or with an explicit guest label and positive nominal size in BBL. Own keg size comes from its format and is frozen by the server for that interval. A guest always shows as not in inventory; an own keg also receives that flag when its taproom has no positive stock. Tapping never changes inventory.

Opening fill is about ¼, ½, 60%, or full. A tap number is optional and can repeat. Kick closes an interval with a reason and estimated remaining fill: empty, about ¼, or about ½. Swap closes and replaces it in one action. Omit the incoming keg to reuse the outgoing own SKU; a guest replacement always requires an explicit name and size. An omitted replacement number leaves the new interval unnumbered.

Retry an unchanged action with the same request ID to recover its original result, even after another action. A new action against an already closed interval is refused and reports the safe closer handle, or **staff**, plus the closing time. Open taps includes every interval, with unnumbered entries last; recent history returns the latest 50 closed intervals with safe @handles and times. These fill estimates do not change counts or inventory. No POS yield or remaining-volume bar is supplied.

The board refreshes its open rows and recent history every 30 seconds and when you return to the tab. A sheet with unsaved edits stays open and unchanged. If MGR cannot tell whether a tap, kick, or swap reached the server, its fields and request ID freeze for **Retry unchanged**. A later refusal cannot unlock an uncertain request. An already-closed conflict reloads recent history and shows the safe closer handle and time when available.

Guest labels are never matched to POS items, so a guest row never shows numeric yield. When there is no usable POS numerator, the board shows what is open, when, and by whom without inventing a bar. Tap, kick, and swap create no finished-goods movement; the weekly physical count owns depletion.

The brewery name in the header is the brewery whose records you are looking at. Navigation has up to four sections: **Today**, **Beer**, **Work**, and **More**. On a phone they sit as tabs along the bottom, and tapping the tab opens that group's primary page. On a wider screen they sit in a left rail.

- **Beer** opens a landing that counts each area (finished-goods shortages, open taps, tanks with beer, material shortages, kegs out) and holds **Inventory**, **Taproom**, **Taps**, **Cellar**, **Materials**, and **Kegs** as allowed by role.
- **Work** opens everything in motion: Today's rows plus the open purchase orders and delivery routes your role may open, sorted by due date, with chips (**all**, **orders**, **transfers**, **batches**, **runs**, **POs**, **routes**) that filter it. The chip you pick is remembered on that device. It holds **Orders**, **Pick**, **Replenishment**, **Transfers**, **Batches**, **Packaging**, **POs**, and **Deliveries**.

Taproom does not have Work. Its Beer section opens the live Taproom count, Taps board, and brand variance pages, and an empty Today links to those same jobs. Links your role cannot use are simply not shown. Planned areas such as Menu stay hidden until they are built, so a tab with nothing you can open does not appear.

Admin opens **Import** from **Settings** or the first-run checklist. See [Import CSV](#import).

## Catalog: brands, formats, and SKUs [#catalog]

*A brand is the beer, such as Hazy IPA. A format is the physical shape it ships or pours in, such as a half-barrel keg or a case of 16 oz cans. A SKU is one brand in one packaged format, the thing inventory, pricing, and orders refer to. Admin and Sales can create brands, formats, and SKUs. Warehouse has no link here (the planned **Menu** entry is hidden until built), though the page is readable at its address. Warehouse reads catalog facts without mutation controls. Catalog is not reachable by Brewer.*

### Create a brand

1. Open **Catalog** and select **New Brand**.
2. Enter **Name** (required), **Style** (optional; a new style is added to the brewery's list), and **ABV** (optional percentage; type `6.5` for 6.5%). **Description**, **Category**, and **Hops** are optional notes. **Price group** is the row of the [price grid](#pricing) this beer sits on; leave it **Unpriced** and none of its SKUs can be ordered until you choose one.
3. Select **Create**. Brand names must be unique within the brewery. The brand lands alphabetically and starts as **No SKUs yet**.

Use **Edit brand** beside an existing brand to change its name, style, ABV, description, category, price group, or hops. The editor starts with all saved values; changing one field keeps the others. Select **Save brand**.

### Create a format

1. Under **Formats**, select **New Format**.
2. Enter **Name**. This form creates a packaged format that holds stock.
3. For a packaged format choose **Package type** (`keg`, `can`, or `bottle`), the **Keg size** for kegs, and optionally **Units per case** and **BBL per unit**.
4. Select **Create**. Packaged format names must be unique within the brewery.

For a glass served at the taproom, select **New pour** beside its brand in **Catalog**. Enter a **Name** (such as Pint or Taster) and a positive size in **Ounces**, then **Create pour**. Each brand owns its pours: two brands can both have a Pint with different sizes, but names must be unique within each brand. The saved row shows the brand, name and ounces. **Edit pour** starts with the saved values; select **Save pour** after changing the name or size. Admin and Sales can create and edit pours. Pours never hold stock and cannot be SKUs, components or packaging BOMs.

Select **Open format** on a format row to see its components and **Package BOM**. Admin and Sales can **Replace components** or **Replace BOM**; Warehouse can read them. Each save replaces that entire list. Add rows, choose distinct items, enter positive quantities, and remove rows you no longer need. An empty list requires checking the explicit clear confirmation before saving.

Components must be other atomic packaged formats with a typed volume. To create a composed format, leave **BBL per unit** blank when creating it, then open it and add components. A format with a typed volume, a poured format, or a format already used as a child cannot have its components replaced here. Clearing components leaves no volume, so the format cannot hold stock until components are added again.

For packaging materials, quantities use the material’s displayed base unit. Choose **Consumed** or **Return to stock** for what happens when the package is broken open. Existing inactive materials remain visible. Changes apply to this format’s complete list; if a different package needs its own list, create a separate format. Correcting an existing definition affects future calculations and open plans. Recorded movement volumes, closed packaging yield, and consumed material quantities stay as recorded.

### Create a SKU

1. Find the brand and select **New SKU**.
2. Choose a packaged **Format**. **Name** is optional and defaults to *Brand · Format*; **UPC** is optional.
3. Select **Create**. A brand has at most one SKU per format. After that, the SKU is available in inventory, pricing, and order forms.

**Edit SKU** beside a SKU changes **Active** and **UPC**. Deactivating it removes it from New Order choices and the portal catalog, and prevents adding it to new orders; recorded inventory and order history remain. Reactivate it to sell it again at its current grid price. UPC is unique within the brewery; clear the field to remove it. The brand and format stay fixed, and provider mappings are preserved.

**BBL per unit** is the barrel volume of one inventory unit and lives on the format, so every SKU in that format shares it. One US beer barrel is 31 gallons (3,968 fl oz). Half-barrel keg = `0.5`. Sixth-barrel keg is about `0.16666667`. A 24 × 16 oz case is `384 ÷ 3968`, about `0.09677419`. Set it on the format before you record inventory.

Catalog shows this as **Volume** in brewery units: common keg fractions stay in bbl, smaller exact totals use whole gallons, and anything under a gallon uses fluid ounces. So `0.5` reads as **½ bbl**, a 24 × 16 oz case as **3 gal**, and a can as **16 oz**. A volume that converts to no whole unit stays in bbl. MGR still stores and calculates all beer volume in bbl.

An empty Catalog says **No brands yet.** and an empty Formats section **No formats yet.**

Wholesale Shop shows the packages the brewery has designated for wholesale — ½ keg, ⅙ keg, case, bottle — grouped under the beer name. Buyers do not see warehouse counts. Staff make that designation on the packaging plan. Prices come from the buyer's [sale channel](#pricing) and the beer's price group; a package with no price for that buyer does not appear on Shop.

## Inventory and movement history [#inventory]

<Screen name="Finished goods" />

*Admin, Sales, and Warehouse can view inventory. Admin and Warehouse can record movements and reverse eligible standalone adjustments and losses.*

<Cards>
  <Card title="On hand">The sum of recorded movements for one SKU in one bin at one location. ATP stays per SKU.</Card>
  <Card title="ATP">Available to promise: on hand minus open reservations for that SKU. It can go negative.</Card>
  <Card title="Movement log">Entries show time, type, SKU, location and bin, signed quantity, frozen barrel volume, destination state or channel when present, and the movement/source references. **Older movements** and **Newer movements** page through the history.</Card>
</Cards>

**Bins.** A bin is a named place inside a location: a walk-in, a cold room, a dry rack. Every location starts with three, **Cold**, **Dry**, and **Walk-in**, and always keeps at least one; add, rename, or remove them from [Locations](#locations). A bin that has ever recorded stock cannot be removed, even at a zero balance, because the movement history still names it; rename it instead. When shipping, choose the actual source bin and lot for each quantity; tracked lots stay identified through shipment and returns.

**Review** on an inventory row opens its SKU detail, separate from **Edit SKU** in Catalog. It shows on-hand by location, global ATP and allocations, and immutable paginated movement history, including inactive and zero-stock SKUs. Locations and bins retain their recorded identities; untracked lots are labeled **Untracked**.

**Reverse movement** is available to Admin and Warehouse only for a standalone `adjustment` or `loss` that has not already been reversed. Select the exact history entry, enter a **Correction note**, then **Confirm reversal**. MGR appends the exact opposite quantity and frozen barrel volume in the original bin and lot, preserving package and reporting classification. The original remains; **Reversal recorded** and **Original movement** link the two entries. Removing a prior positive adjustment is refused if that exact bin/lot no longer holds enough stock. Retry an unchanged failed form to recover the same result.

Sales sees history without correction controls. Shipments retain **Return shipment** through their order workflow. Taproom count depletion uses **Correct count** on the eligible latest saved count; other compound entries keep their original correction owner. A generic adjustment is not a count correction.

### Record a movement

<Screen name="Record movement" />

1. Select **Record movement** from Today, or open **Inventory** and select **Record movement**. The Today shortcut opens the same form.
2. Choose a **SKU**, **Location**, and **Bin**. Picking the location preselects its first bin alphabetically; change it if the stock sits elsewhere.
3. Choose a **Type**. The default is **Opening balance** (`opening_balance` in the API).
4. For `depletion`, pick a **Channel**. The list is your brewery's own — every brewery starts with **Wholesale**, **Taproom**, **DTC**, and **Export**, and you edit that list under [**Sale channels**](#channels). **Taproom** is preselected. The channel decides how the removal is taxed, and that treatment is frozen onto the entry: editing the channel later never rewrites what is already recorded. `sale_removal` takes a channel too, but shipping sets it, not this form.
5. Enter a positive **Qty** in SKU units, to two decimal places if needed. The type decides whether stock is added or removed; for `adjustment`, choose **Add stock** or **Remove stock**. Zero is rejected. Samples and festival removals require a two-letter **Destination state**. Check the signed quantity and estimated volume in the preview.
6. Add an optional **Note**, then select **Record**.

| Type | Use | Normal sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `opening_balance` | Initial stock when you start MGR | Positive |
| `production_in` | Finished beer in from production | Positive |
| `adjustment` | Difference found by a verified physical count | Positive or negative |
| `depletion` | Taproom sale or consumption | Negative |
| `destruction` | Beer you meant to destroy | Negative |
| `loss` | Unrecoverable finished-goods loss | Negative |
| `sample` | Beer pulled as a sample | Negative |
| `festival_removal` | Beer pulled for a festival | Negative |
| `return_in` | Sellable beer back into inventory | Positive |
| `sale_removal` | Wholesale shipment from the order workflow | Negative |
| `taproom_transfer` | Transfer shipment: a negative source entry paired with a positive destination entry | Negative and positive pair |

`sale_removal` and `taproom_transfer` are written by shipping, not by this form.

**Movement recorded** appears only after the server confirms the save. It shows the committed quantity, frozen barrel volume, bin, state/channel, note, timestamp and reference. A failed or uncertain response does not show this receipt; retry the unchanged form to recover the same save. **Done** returns to the ledger. Open the SKU’s inventory **Review** page to reverse an eligible standalone adjustment or loss; other entries retain their correction workflow.

An assisted **Record movement** proposal is still only a proposal. Review its signed quantity, frozen barrel volume, selected bin and lot stock before and after, channel tax treatment, destination warning, and available correction, then confirm it in the same conversation. The confirmation is bound to you, this brewery, that conversation, and the exact proposed fields. MGR locks the inventory ledger and the displayed catalog and location facts while it checks the preview; changed stock, package volume, labels, tax, or registration warning rejects the save and asks for a new preview without recording anything. A retry of the same confirmed request shows the original result once in that conversation, even after the preview expires or stock changes, and does not record the movement again.

The composer at the bottom of the staff shell currently offers explicit structured actions; free-form language and voice are not connected. Admin and Warehouse can choose **Record inventory movement**, then select the exact SKU/package, type and direction, location, bin, tracked lot or explicitly untracked stock, quantity, channel, and destination state when required. Every unanswered field appears as a question and no commit button exists until the server returns a canonical preview. Editing a field invalidates that preview. **Dismiss** records no movement, **Open as form** carries the known fields to the ordinary form, and **Commit movement** sends the bound request once. Dismissing, opening the form, closing the composer, or choosing another action retires the preview, so returning to the movement requires a fresh preview. Admin, Sales, and Warehouse can also read current ATP with the observation time; it does not promise future stock. **History** reads only this signed-in person’s conversations in the current brewery and clears from the rendered shell when the person, brewery, or role changes. Press Command-K or Control-K to focus the action picker.

**Outbox** shows only fermentation readings captured by this signed-in person for the current brewery. A reading is saved locally with its exact observed time, parsed values, tank, role and request ID before MGR tries the network. Retry sends that exact attempt; returning under another account or brewery never sends it, and a role change disables Retry. **Fix as new reading** keeps an uncertain original while opening its fields for review under a fresh request ID. **Discard** asks for confirmation naming the reading and removes only the selected row; bulk discard names every row. Inventory movements, picks and transfers need current server state, so MGR reports an offline failure and never queues them.

MGR freezes barrel volume when each entry posts. Ordinary entries use quantity × the format’s BBL per unit; exact reversals negate the original stored volume, and shipment returns retain their source volume. Movements cannot be edited or deleted. See [Errors and corrections](#errors-corrections) before you try to fix inventory.

Dates use **Sep 10, 2026** in records, day headers use **Thu, Sep 10**, and timestamps use **Sep 10, 2026, 8:10 AM** without seconds.

Empty sections say **No inventory recorded yet.** or **No movements recorded yet.**

## Customers and ship-tos [#customers]

*Admin and Sales can create and edit customers and ship-to addresses. Warehouse can read these pages at their addresses; customer, ship-to, and invitation controls are hidden. The list shows name, type, home state, sale channel, and payment terms.*

### Create or edit a customer

<Screen name="Customer detail" />

1. Open **Customers**. Select **New Customer**, or **Edit** beside an existing customer.
2. Enter **Name** (required).
3. Choose **Type**: `distributor`, `retailer` (default), `brewery`, or `other`.
4. Enter the required two-letter **State**. MGR uppercases it as you type.
5. Choose a **Sale channel** (required; Wholesale is preselected). It decides the customer's prices and where shipped orders are recorded.
6. Optionally enter **License number** and **Payment terms**, such as `net30`.
7. Select **Create** or **Save**.

Customer detail shows **Tax treatment**. Choose **Inherit sale channel** to use the channel default, or select a customer override; editing another field preserves the selected treatment. **Orders** opens only this customer’s orders, and status filters keep that customer selected. **Clear** returns to all customers.

### Create or edit a ship-to

## Price groups [#pricing]

<Screen name="Price groups" />

<Screen name="New order" />

## Invoices and credit memos [#invoices]

<Screen name="Invoices" />
<Screen name="Invoice" />

*Admin and Sales open the Invoices list from **More**. Warehouse can open it by entering the address directly and sees the invoice created by a wholesale shipment from **View invoice** right after shipping. Only Admin and Sales can return beer against an invoice or mark a buyer's question answered.*

The list shows each invoice or credit memo with its customer, total, due date, and current QuickBooks state. **Open** an invoice to see the customer, line quantities, captured unit prices, amounts, total, mapping status, and available accounting action. A portal order with returnable kegs keeps the deposit price the buyer reviewed. If Admin or Sales later adjusts its lines, a retained keg line keeps that frozen deposit price while its deposit quantity follows the new order quantity; a newly added keg line freezes the deposit configured at adjustment time. A pool with a zero deposit remains a valid no-charge keg, while a returnable SKU missing its required keg format or pool is refused before the order changes. Its invoice adds a separate deposit line for each charged returnable-keg quantity actually shipped. An empty list says **No invoices yet.** Payment is display-only; there is no record-payment action.

Admin and Sales can run the QuickBooks invoice sync. It freezes the eligible invoices for that request, reads each one in the same connected company, then updates the whole batch together. If one read or update fails, none of that batch is applied; retrying the same request reads the same frozen invoice set, and a completed retry returns its recorded result. If two syncs overlap, a result from the older read cannot replace the newer invoice state. The sync updates current balance, total, tax, payment state, and edit marker. Staff and portal screens show that current QuickBooks total, while the original local lines and pushed snapshot remain unchanged as audit history, so an edited total can differ from the visible line sum. A partial payment stays due; a payment that is later reversed makes the invoice due again. MGR reads linked Payment details once per batch and records only positive cash that QuickBooks assigns to that invoice. A zero-dollar Payment created by applying credit records no cash and shows the invoice as settled rather than paid; a single-invoice mixed cash-and-credit settlement records only its cash portion. If one Payment covers multiple invoices and its cash is less than their combined applications, MGR records no cash from that ambiguous Payment instead of counting the same cash more than once. Voided and deleted invoices keep any earlier paid date as history but no longer appear paid. MGR compares only fields it originally sent, so a QuickBooks default such as a derived due date is not reported as an accountant edit. MGR reports a real accountant edit and stops; it never overwrites or pushes the invoice automatically.

Admin opens **Settings → Accounting** to connect, reconnect, inspect safe connection health, manage mappings and choose whether future QuickBooks invoices allow bank transfer, card, or both. Admin and Sales can open **Fix mapping** from an invoice and enter an exact customer or item ID they verified in the connected QuickBooks company; MGR never selects a record from a matching name. After a mapping saves, reopening it shows the ID the server retained. The payment choices are copied when the next invoice is pushed and do not change invoices already there. Every push, exact retry, corrected push, or re-push first opens a confirmation that names the invoice and the operation. Closing it sends nothing. Selecting its **Confirm** action invokes the registered command; an exact retry still uses the previously saved request and replay identity. QuickBooks payment, edit, void, and deletion status remains visible after disconnect. After sync confirms an invoice was voided or deleted in its original QuickBooks company, Admin may mark it **Written off** with a reason even while QuickBooks is disconnected. Credit memos never offer write-off. That status records who acted and when inside MGR; it does not change QuickBooks, record cash, or create a credit.

When a buyer asks about an invoice from the portal, the question appears on the invoice as **Buyer asked about this invoice** with their words, and as an **Answer** row on Today for Admin and Sales. Reply by phone or email, then select **Mark answered**; that clears the Today row and nothing on the invoice changes.

Returns preserve the selected shipment lot and its original recorded volume even if the format definition later changes. A damaged return writes that same volume back out as loss atomically.

### Return beer and correct the invoice

<Screen name="Return and credit" />

1. Open the invoice and select **Return**.
2. Enter the quantity coming back for each applicable line. Leave unrelated lines at zero. A return cannot exceed the remaining uncredited quantity.
3. Split the returned quantity across the original shipped sources shown under the line. Their sum must equal the line quantity; a source cannot be returned more than was shipped. Choose **Return to location**, the destination **bin**, and the **Reason**: *Unsold* and *Wrong item* put the beer back as sellable stock at that location; *Damaged* puts it back and writes the same quantity to loss in the same step, so broken beer never lands on a pick list.
4. Select **Return shipment**.

For older invoices that have no shipment, choose the return location; MGR records untracked stock in its first bin. Shipment-backed invoices always require the original source selection.

MGR creates a separate credit memo at the prices frozen on the invoice lines, never today's price, records the return-in (and, for damaged, the loss) against that credit memo, and notes the correction on the originating order's timeline. A paid invoice can still be returned; the credit sits as available credit. Credit memos cannot themselves be credited.

## Stock transfers [#transfers]

## Compliance [#compliance]

<Screen name="Compliance months" />

**Compliance** (More; Sales and Admin) lists the last three months and every month already filed. A month marked *not filed* opens for review; a filed month shows its filing date and taxpaid barrels. Below the months sit the **Compliance registry** link and, under **Lot trace**, every packaged lot. MGR reports the TTB month here; other jurisdictions and date ranges are available through the API.

<Screen name="Monthly compliance" />

A month is generated from the inventory ledger when you open it, so nothing is stored until you file. The table shows each package class (kegs, cans, bottles) in barrels: begin + in − out = end, and every class must balance. Zeros print 0.00. Beer in process is what the tanks hold now, not at the period end. Below the table, **Packaged** is production into finished goods, then each removal class (taxpaid, export, samples, losses, and so on) by the tax treatment recorded when the beer moved, so editing a sale channel or format later does not change historical classification, and taxpaid removals by destination state.

**Review auto-reconciled losses** lists each loss posted in the month, with its batch close time, original generic loss, prior allocations, and exact remaining barrels. Admin and Sales can reattribute part of the remainder to **Sample**, **Taproom**, or **Destruction**. Samples require the two-letter destination state. Taproom freezes the current Taproom channel tax treatment even if its display name changes. Keep an uncertain form open: the amount, target, state, and request stay frozen until **Retry unchanged reattribution** recovers the saved result. The original remains in history and total volume does not change. The separate **Cellar removals breakdown** is explanatory and is already included once in the removal totals; never add it again. Corrections post in the period when they are saved and never rewrite an earlier filed snapshot.

**Save filed snapshot** stores the figures as they stand, with an optional note, and marks the month filed; reopening it shows the snapshot rather than regenerating. MGR does not transmit anything: file with the TTB or the state yourself and record it here. The button stays off while a class does not balance. Direct cellar Taproom volume also blocks filing because its external filing-line mapping has not been approved; MGR does not guess that mapping or offer an acknowledgment bypass. The report itself is a fixed shape, so a movement recorded later into a filed month appears in the next generation of a *different* month, never in the snapshot.

## Keg fleet [#kegs]

<Screen name="Keg fleet" />

**Kegs** (Beer tab; Warehouse and Admin) is the brewery's returnable keg fleet: pools and where their kegs are. One-way kegs are materials and do not appear here. The page shows **No keg pools yet** until you add one.

### Keg pools

A pool is one group of kegs you count together: **Owned**, **Leased**, or **Pay per fill**. **Add keg pool** takes the name, the kind, the vendor for a leased or pay-per-fill pool, the per-fill cost (required for pay per fill), and the deposit charged per keg. **Edit** on a pool row changes its name, vendor, costs, or deposit, or takes it out of service; a pool out of service accepts no more events. The kind cannot change after creation, and a per-fill cost or vendor once set can be changed but not removed.

### Record a keg event

**Record keg event** posts one line to the keg ledger. Choose the pool, the size, the reason, the location and bin the kegs are at (the first bin is preselected), and how many kegs:

- **Acquired** and **Retired** add kegs to or remove them from the fleet at that bin. No customer.
- **Shipped** and **Returned** move empty kegs to or from a customer; the customer is required. Shipped names the bin they left from, Returned the bin they came back into. The fleet total does not change.
- **Lost** removes kegs from that bin; name the customer when they were lost out at a customer, which also reduces that customer's balance. **Found** adds kegs back to a bin and never names a customer: a keg found at a customer is a shipped keg.

The quantity is always positive; the reason is the direction. Retired, shipped, and lost cannot exceed what the bin holds, so a typo is refused rather than driving a bin negative. A **Note** is optional and shows next to the event in Keg event history. Kegs moving between bins or locations are recorded through **Transfers** or a bin move, not here. Empty kegs only: beer coming back with a keg, and its credit, is [Return shipment](#invoices). A keg deposit refund is a separate credit memo.

**Record keg event** only appears once the brewery has at least one location and one pool in service; retire every pool and it disappears until you add one back or reactivate one.

Each pool row shows the kegs each bin holds per size, so 36 in the taproom and 40 in storage read as two rows; kegs out at customers have left their bin and are listed under **Customer keg balance** for each customer holding some.

<Screen name="Customer keg balance" />

**Customer keg balance** lists, for one customer, the kegs out per pool and size and the deposit invoiced for them (zero when no deposit was ever invoiced). Kegs out is shipped minus returned minus lost. It shows **No kegs currently out** once everything is back.

<Screen name="Keg event history" />

**Keg event history** is the ledger newest first. The pool and customer filters combine, and show **No matching events** when a combination finds nothing. Nothing here can be edited; a wrong entry is corrected by recording the opposite event.

## Recipes [#recipes]

<Screen name="Recipe" />

<Screen name="Fermentation reading" />

*Admin and Brewer.*

Open **Cellar** from the **Beer** navigation to see every occupied tank: its vessel, the brand it holds, the barrels in it, and how long it has been there. Select **Transfer** for a **From** occupancy, a **To** vessel, the **Barrels moving**, and an optional **Loss (bbl)**. Moving into an empty vessel opens a new occupancy there; moving into an already-occupied vessel blends into it, keeping that vessel's existing batch identity — a blend is never a new batch. A source occupancy closes automatically once it empties.

Select **Complete batch**, choose the batch, and review its server-calculated **Baseline**, **Frozen packaged**, **Prior attributed**, **Threshold**, and **Predicted residual**. The final action accepts no amount or cause. MGR recalculates under the cellar lock, refuses while any packaging run for that batch is open or when packaged and attributed volume exceed the baseline, and then closes the batch and every open occupancy that still belongs to it at one time. A residual at or above the greater of 0.05 bbl or 0.5% of baseline becomes one automatic generic loss for later compliance review; a smaller residual closes without an adjustment. Packaged volume comes from the frozen finished-goods movements written when runs closed, so later format edits and the run's barrels-drawn figure do not rewrite it. If the response is uncertain, keep the form open and select **Retry unchanged completion**; a confirmed response is shown as saved before the page refreshes.

Select **Reading** on a tank to open its reading log and record the next one: **Observed at** and **Temperature** are required; **Gravity**, **pH**, and a note are each optional, because a quick temperature check is a legitimate reading on its own. Saving freezes those parsed fields and the observation time before transport. If the connection or response is lost, **Retry exact reading** keeps the original time and request ID; it never substitutes the reconnect time. If the tank closed before the first send, choose **Fix as new reading** only after reviewing a fresh observation. A reading that the server already committed still replays its one original result after the tank closes. Readings never overwrite one another — each is its own row, newest first. The gravity field is labelled and read in whatever unit you chose under [Gravity units](#gravity-units); MGR stores degrees Plato either way. If what you type there is not a gravity MGR can read, the field says so and **Save reading** stays disabled until you correct it or clear the field — a mistyped gravity is never quietly dropped from an otherwise saved reading.

## Packaging [#packaging]

<Screen name="Packaging runs" />

<Screen name="Schedule packaging run" />

<Screen name="Close packaging run" />

*Admin, Brewer, and Warehouse.*

Open **Packaging** from the **Work** navigation to see every run by planned date, newest first, with its next action named on the row. Select **Schedule run** for a **Brand**, a **Planned date**, an optional **Source tank**, and planned output lines (a SKU and a quantity each) — a run is identified by the brand it packages from the moment it is planned; the tank can be picked later.

A run without a tank shows **Pick source** on its page: choose a **Source tank** and confirm. Once it has a tank, **Start** stamps it started — both picking the tank and starting require one, so an unsourced run can never begin. A started run's page becomes the close form: **Barrels drawn**, the **Actual outputs** for each planned SKU (defaulting to the planned quantity), a **Lot code**, **Packaged on** and an optional **Best by** date, and the **Finished goods location** and bin the packaged units land in — all four of the last group are required, because a vessel has no location of its own for the goods to appear at. Select **Close packaging run** to write the lot, the finished-goods stock, and the packaging materials consumed in one step. The tank itself stays open afterward; emptying it is a separate Cellar transfer.

**Repack** (Admin and Warehouse only — a Brewer does not see this button), next to Schedule run, breaks a composed unit (a case) into the units it is made of at one location and bin — a shape change, never a production or a removal. Choose the **Location** and **Bin**, the SKU you are **Breaking** and its quantity, and the SKU you are packing it **Into** and its quantity. It is refused unless the two SKUs share a brand and their formats are joined by exactly one composition record, and unless the volumes on both sides match exactly.

## Purchasing: vendors, materials, and purchase orders [#purchasing]

<Screen name="Purchase orders" />

Purchasing is four pages. **Vendors** and **Materials** hold the definitions, **POs** (Work tab) is where an order lives from draft to received, and **Planning** shows what committed work is short of and drafts the orders to cover it.

### Vendors and contracts

**Vendors** (More tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists your suppliers. **Add vendor** and **Edit** take a name, optional email and phone, payment terms (due on receipt, net 15, net 30 default), and a **Lead time** in days. The lead time is the vendor's, not the material's: it is what Planning subtracts from a needed-by date to get a buy-by date. Once a vendor has received orders, the row also shows the observed average from sent to last receipt, with how many orders it rests on (n=3 is weak evidence) and how it was sent, because a date someone typed for an order phoned in is not the same evidence as one MGR stamped.

Below the vendors, **Contracts** lists commitments: one vendor, one material, a quantity in purchase units, an optional price, window, and **Contract number**. A contract with a number shows it as the row's label; without one, the row falls back to the material name. A contract is not an order. You order against it all year, so each row shows four numbers: **committed**, **received**, **on order** (placed but not yet arrived), and **available**, which is committed less both. Available is the one to decide against; received is the one accounting reconciles. A contract never stops you ordering: exceed it and the extra lines are ordinary spot lines.

### Materials

**Materials** (Beer tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists material definitions with on hand summed across every bin, including inactive materials, marked **inactive**. **Add material** and **Edit** take a name, a kind (malt, hop, yeast, adjunct, chemical, packaging, other), the base unit you consume in, the purchase unit you buy in and how many base units one purchase unit holds (a 44 lb hop box is purchase unit *each*, 44 base units), whether it is **lot-tracked**, an optional **default vendor**, and whether it is **Active** (available to recipes and purchase orders; on by default). Units are refused once the material has movements, because every ledger row was written in them. A lot-tracked material asks for a lot code at every receipt; an untracked one never does.

**Count** on a material row opens a cycle count for one bin: pick the location and bin, type what you counted in base units, and **Record count**. The count is always recorded, even when it matches. Only the variance posts, as a count adjustment. When the material holds several lots, a shortage comes off the earliest best-by lots first and an overage lands on the newest lot; a shortage crossing two lots writes one adjustment per lot. Count label stock as whole rolls and leave the open roll out; its remainder falls into the variance and self-corrects at the next count.

### Purchase orders

**Purchase orders** (Work tab; Admin and Warehouse) lists open orders: drafts, sent, and partially received. Each row names its next action. **New PO** takes a vendor, an optional expected date, and lines: a material, a quantity in purchase units, an optional unit cost, and on a lot-tracked material an optional **Expected lot**, the lot the vendor named when you ordered. The expected lot is advisory: it creates nothing, and receiving prefills from it. **Save draft** numbers the order PO-0001 upward.

A draft's page offers **Mark sent**. Choose how it went out, **From my mail client** or **Phoned, faxed, or vendor portal**, and confirm. MGR does not send email: it records that you sent the order today. The status then reads **Marked sent** with the date, never *Delivered*, because nothing here can know that. Marking is one-way; a sent order cannot go back to draft.

A sent order's page is the receiving form. Choose the location and bin everything lands in and the received date, then for each line still due type what you **counted**. Lines default to what is still due; type over them. Over and short are both allowed and both recorded. A lot-tracked line asks for the **lot code off the package** (prefilled from the expected lot when the order named one; if the vendor shipped another lot, type what the box says) and an optional best-by date. **Receive purchase order** writes the receipt, its lines, the lots, and receipt movements into that bin in one step; only what you counted posts to inventory. The order becomes **partially received** until every line is met, then **received**. What each line still owes is worked out from the receipts every time, so a second receipt against the same order simply closes the remainder. **Show received and cancelled** at the foot of the list reveals finished orders.

### Planning

<Screen name="Planning" />

**Planning** (More tab; Admin, Warehouse, Brewer) lists each material that committed work is short of. Demand is what unbrewed batches need through their recipe and what open packaging runs need through their format's bill of materials; supply is on hand plus what is already on a sent purchase order, so a gap is never ordered twice. A draft is not supply until it is marked sent. Each row shows need, on hand, on order, the needed-by date, and the vendor the gap resolves to (a contract with commitment still available, else the material's default vendor). A resolved vendor with a typed lead time also shows the **buy by** date; one with no lead time typed says so instead, and the row still drafts, just with no date to judge it against. A row past its buy-by date is out of reach and is left out of the draft; a material with no contract and no default vendor cannot draft at all until you set one.

**Draft N purchase orders** (Admin and Warehouse) writes one draft per vendor for every buyable row, quantities rounded up to whole purchase units, priced from the contract for what its available commitment covers and left blank beyond that. The label says how many before you commit, and the page then opens the POs list so you can review and mark them sent.

## Team [#team]

<Screen name="Team" />

Admin opens **Team** from **Settings**. Each member shows as an **@handle** (the first part of their email) with the email and role; your own row is marked **you**. Open another member's row to change their **Role** and **Save role**, or **Remove** them, which ends their membership at this brewery and leaves their sign-in account alone. MGR keeps at least one admin: the only admin cannot step down or be removed, and nobody can remove themselves. **Invite staff** opens the same form as the first-run checklist: enter **Email**, choose one **Role** (Warehouse, Sales, Brewer, Taproom, or Admin), and **Send invite**. Success says **Invite sent**. The roster shows memberships, without a pending-acceptance status. After an error, keep the page open and retry unchanged details to finish the same invitation. Editing the email or role starts a new request; a successful invite clears the form. Closing and reopening the form on this page preserves a failed attempt, but reloading the page does not recover it. Existing accounts cannot be attached, and there is no resend or invitation-revocation control. Multiple simultaneous staff roles are unsupported. Non-admin staff are sent to **No access** if they open the address directly.

## Settings [#settings]

<Screen name="Settings" />

Admin opens **Settings** from **More** to edit the brewery's basics in one form: **Brewery name**, **Timezone** (a zone name MGR knows, such as America/New_York), **TTB registry number**, **PA license**, the **Customer-facing phone** the portal prints when online payment is unavailable, and **Reading overdue after (hours)**, which sets when a fermentation reading counts as overdue on Today. **Save brewery** writes them together. Below the form, rows open **Locations**, **Team**, **Accounting**, **Chat**, and **Import**. Source water and Point of sale remain unavailable.

QuickBooks connection setup requires the server-only `QBO_CLIENT_ID`, `QBO_CLIENT_SECRET`, exact registered `QBO_REDIRECT_URI`, and `QBO_API_BASE` for the matching Intuit sandbox or production API. Admin can connect, reconnect, or disconnect; Admin and Sales can see the safe connection status needed for invoice work. An Admin who returns from QuickBooks without a brewery cookie uses their verified default brewery membership; a stale or foreign brewery cookie is refused. After code exchange, MGR makes an authenticated CompanyInfo read through the callback company's realm-scoped URL before claiming it; a denied request or invalid CompanyInfo response reports QuickBooks unavailable and stores no connection. A disconnect stops local work, releases the QuickBooks company for another brewery, and removes the local credential before MGR attempts remote revocation. It keeps the disconnected brewery's history and that company's customer, item, and invoice mappings so reconnecting the same verified company while it is unclaimed can resume without remapping. That reconnect preserves the logical connection identity while rotating the credential version, so an older refresh cannot overwrite the new credential. Connecting a different company creates a new identity and clears the old mappings and invoice sync fields. Historical portal quotes keep their original company identity as stale audit evidence and cannot use the replacement company's credential. One current QuickBooks company connection cannot belong to two MGR breweries. If Intuit is unavailable, health says the connection is disconnected and remote revocation is unresolved. If OAuth completion does not finish, MGR reports QuickBooks unavailable and requires a fresh reconnect instead of replaying the one-time code.

The optional server-only `QBO_TAX_API_BASE` enables QuickBooks' GraphQL sales-tax transport. MGR requests the restricted tax-calculation scope during a new connection only when that URL is configured. Portal review uses it only when the connection actually granted that scope and the customer, every ordered SKU, any keg deposit, and the shipping-source address are mapped for the same QuickBooks company. If entitlement, mapping, address, or the provider is unavailable, buyers see **Tax pending** and can still order; the later invoice remains authoritative.

Admin and Sales can map each customer and SKU to its ID in the connected QuickBooks company; Admin also chooses the QuickBooks item used for returnable-keg deposit charges and refunds. Pushing an invoice freezes its customer, address, email, line quantities, captured prices, exact cent amounts, payment-method defaults, mappings, company connection, request body, and Intuit request ID before contacting QuickBooks. Credit memos use QuickBooks' CreditMemo document with positive credited quantities and amounts, including keg-deposit refunds through the configured deposit item. MGR refreshes an expired access credential before sending; if QuickBooks returns unauthorized for a credential that appeared current, MGR refreshes once and retries the identical saved body and request ID. An unavailable or expired refresh credential leaves the push pending for honest recovery. If any other response is uncertain, retry the same action so MGR sends the identical saved body and request ID. Once that request has completed, retry by the same currently authorized staff member returns its durable result without contacting QuickBooks again, even if the connection, mappings, catalog, or invoice status changed later; the remote document ID already stored on the attempt remains authoritative. A disconnect blocks a pending retry until the same company is verified and reconnected; then MGR resends the frozen request through the preserved logical connection. A different-company reconnect cannot retarget or finish the old request. A rejected push remains rejected on exact replay even after its mapping changes or a later corrected attempt succeeds. It needs an explicit corrected attempt after its mapping is fixed. Recreating a document is available only after sync confirms that the prior QuickBooks document was deleted; both cases keep the prior attempt in history.

For an eligible live invoice with a current positive QuickBooks balance, the customer payment handoff reads a fresh QuickBooks link only after checking the signed-in customer, invoice, current company connection, payment settings, accounting permission, credential generation, and original pushed document. MGR checks the same connection snapshot and positive balance again after the QuickBooks read. A reconnect, credential rotation, or loss of accounting permission while the read is in flight makes that link unavailable. A zero or unknown current balance is also unavailable; a reversed payment with a new positive balance is eligible even though MGR retains its earlier paid date as history. The link is never stored or shown in an MGR page; an unavailable or unverified link leaves the customer on a **Payment unavailable** page with instructions to contact the brewery.

## Sale channels [#channels]

<Screen name="Sale channels" />

<Screen name="Channel" />

*Admin only. Everyone who records or reads a movement sees the channel names; only Admin can add, rename, or remove one.*

A sale channel is a named way your brewery sells beer. Every brewery starts with four: **Wholesale**, **Taproom**, **DTC**, and **Export**. Open **Sale channels** from the **More** navigation to see each channel and its tax treatment.

1. Select **Add channel**, enter a **Channel name**, choose a **Tax treatment**, and select **Save channel**.
2. To rename a channel or change its treatment, select **Edit** on its row, change the fields, and select **Save channel**.
3. To remove a channel, select **Delete** on its row.

The tax treatment — *taxable*, *export*, *vessel supplies*, *research*, or *transfer in bond* — is the default for removals recorded on that channel. A customer may override it on their own record, and the treatment that applies is frozen onto each movement the moment it is recorded. Editing a channel later never restates a month you have already filed.

Two removals are refused, each with the reason shown beside the row:

- Every channel can be renamed: no name is load-bearing, because an order carries its own channel from the moment it is created.
- A channel a movement, a customer, an order, or a filled price cell references cannot be deleted. Rename it instead, or clear its cells and move those customers first.

## Gravity units [#gravity-units]

<Screen name="Units" />

*Everyone. Only Admin sets the brewery default; the personal preference is yours alone.*

Gravity is always stored in degrees Plato. This setting changes only how gravity is shown to you and how you type it, so switching it never moves a reading you have already recorded.

Open **Units** from the **More** navigation. **Brewery default** (Admin only) is what everyone here sees unless they choose otherwise. **Your preference** is yours: choose **Plato**, **Specific gravity**, or **Use brewery default** to go back to following the brewery. ## Link your Slack account [#slack]

<Screen name="Link your Slack" />

Every staff member can open **More → Chat** to set personal notification reasons, even before linking Slack. Those choices remain available after unlinking. For each reason, you can select one of your existing verified, active personal destinations; leaving the default keeps the existing delivery behavior. Admin, Sales, Warehouse, and Brewer can also change personal quiet hours and snooze eligible reminders; Taproom cannot. An admin also manages the brewery connection, its private operations channel, brewery quiet hours, reading cadence, linked people, and health from Chat.

To link, open MGR's private App Home in Slack and choose **Link MGR account**. Sign in to MGR if asked. Before anything changes, MGR shows the brewery, your current MGR name and email, and the Slack workspace and user identity. Choose **Link these accounts** only when both identities are yours. MGR never links by matching email, profile name, or workspace domain. A missing, expired, used, or wrong-brewery link must be restarted from App Home.

Linked App Home shows a role-filtered **Today** queue with up to 20 current items, the same work your MGR Today tab shows for your role. Admin sees all live items; Sales sees submitted orders to review and buyer questions about invoices; Warehouse sees confirmed orders whose requested ship date is due, orders with staged beer to put back, and their own next stop when they're a route's assigned driver; Brewer sees overdue fermentation readings. Select **Open** on an item to view its order in MGR. With no current work, App Home says **You’re caught up**.

Linked eligible staff receive private Slack messages when assigned work first becomes due. **Snooze 1 hour** delays that Slack reminder, and muting a reason stops that class of Slack message; neither changes what is due on MGR Today. Quiet hours delay personal messages, including snoozed reminders and retries, using your current personal override or the brewery window. App Home stays current. When work resolves, its message becomes **Resolved**. MGR never posts customer contacts, prices, or notes to Slack. Invoice-question reminders show the invoice number and an **Open in MGR** link; the buyer’s question stays in MGR.

The brewery digest goes only to one admin-approved private, non-shared Slack channel where MGR is already a member. Public, archived, and Slack Connect channels cannot be selected. It runs on the brewery's local morning and midday windows; the default fermentation reading cadence is 24 hours. Personal mutes, snoozes, and quiet hours affect Slack delivery only, not Today or the shared digest.

Slack is a view of current MGR work. Operational forms such as fermentation readings and order confirmation show **Open in MGR** and do not submit the domain change from Slack. MGR rechecks the current item, assignment, role, and channel before sending or updating a message.

Admins can inspect the workspace, granted permissions, destination, queue state, recent callback/delivery times, and a safe error code on **Chat health**. **Disable** stops delivery but keeps the connection for later. **Disconnect** stops delivery and unlinks people immediately, then removes the stored Slack credential; if that cleanup fails, the page offers **Retry cleanup**. Reauthorization and cleanup are serialized so an old cleanup cannot remove replacement credentials. A rejected connection to an already-owned workspace preserves its existing connection; if saving new credentials fails, reauthorize from Chat settings. Staff can unlink their own account; an admin can unlink any linked person.

## Errors and corrections [#errors-corrections]

A bookmarked staff page that your role cannot read opens **No access**, names the required roles, and offers **Back to Today**. Order controls follow your role: Sales confirms, adjusts and cancels; Warehouse picks, ships and puts back; Admin can do both.

**A form reports an error.** Read the message, fix the visible fields, and submit again. Your entries stay until the form succeeds or you close it. Permission errors need an administrator.

**MGR says there are too many requests.** Wait for the stated retry time, then retry. If the action's result was uncertain, keep the form unchanged so MGR reuses the same request ID and can recover the original result safely.

**Your signed-in account or brewery changed while an action was pending.** MGR refuses the action before it writes anything. If the earlier response was uncertain, return to the original account and brewery and use the unchanged retry; do not start a replacement request until you have checked the record.

**A page fails to load.** Select **Try again** once. If it still fails, give your administrator the brewery or customer name, page, approximate time, and the action you tried.

**A record seems missing.** Confirm the brewery shown in the navigation, clear any order-status filter, and check the exact brand, SKU, customer, or location name.

**A page says "Not found".** Opening an order, customer, or invoice link that matches nothing in your brewery shows a Not found page with a link back to Today, not an error. The record was removed, the link is wrong, or it belongs to a brewery you are not signed in to. Switch brewery, or ask the person who sent the link.

**Inventory is wrong.** Do a physical count and figure out what happened. Record an adjustment only for a verified count difference. Never disguise a sale, sample, loss, destruction, festival removal, return, or transfer as another type. Put a note on the correction.

**A saved Taproom count was too low.** Ask Admin to open the latest saved count and use **Correct count**. Keep the signed compensation and replacement entries with that count; do not add a generic inventory adjustment.

**A shipped order or invoice is wrong.** Do not add an unrelated inventory movement. Use **Return** on the invoice so the credit and the returned stock stay linked to the shipment history.

## Import CSV [#import]

<Screen name="Import" />

Admin opens **Settings → Import** or **Import CSV** on the setup checklist. Upload a CSV with a header and 1–5000 data rows, choose the kind, map columns, and review the editable preview. Quoted commas, newlines, escaped quotes, and a leading byte-order mark are supported. Malformed quoting, duplicate or missing headers, unequal row widths, and empty files show an error before anything is staged.

The complete API request is limited to 8 MiB. Most 5,000-row files fit; files with unusually large notes or other cells may need to be split into smaller batches.

The lookup lists show each existing record's label and ID. Map these exact fields (optional fields follow the semicolon):

| Kind | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Customers | `name`, `type`, `state`, `saleChannelId`; `licenseNumber`, `paymentTerms` |
| Ship-tos | `customerId`, `label`, `address1`, `city`, `zip`, `state`; `address2` |
| Products / SKUs | `product`, `formatId`; `sku_name`, `style`, `abv`, `upc` |
| Channel prices | `saleChannelId`, `priceGroupId`, `formatId`, `unitPriceCents` |
| Opening balances | `skuId`, `locationId`, `binId`, `qty`; `note` |

Use lookup IDs for references, including the format. Create missing formats first: an import does not infer or create them. Customer `type` is distributor, retailer, brewery, or other; state is two uppercase letters. Prices are nonnegative whole cents. Opening quantity is a positive count of SKU units, and the bin must belong to the location. An existing product is reused without changing its metadata. Duplicate customer or SKU creation is blocked; channel prices update the existing price cell.

The preview marks ready and blocked rows. Fix cells or mapping before committing; **Commit** stays disabled if every row is invalid. On a mixed batch, valid rows commit independently and each blocked row reports its error. A failed product/SKU row leaves none of its new dependent records behind.

Keep the page open until results are recovered. If the connection fails, some rows may have committed: **Retry same batch** returns the original committed and blocked outcomes without posting opening stock twice. **Correct blocked rows in a new batch** carries only blocked rows into a fresh editable preview. Even if lookup data changes, retrying the unchanged old batch returns its original blocked result. Never upload committed opening balances as a new batch; that would add the stock again. Reloading or closing the page loses the wizard's retry state; there is no automatic recovery after reload.

## Actions not yet available [#unavailable]

Do not hunt for hidden controls for these:

- public account sign-up
- attaching an existing sign-in account, resending or revoking an invitation, or assigning multiple staff roles
- cancelling a purchase order, editing one after it is saved, or sending one by email from MGR
- deleting customers, ship-tos, inventory history, orders, invoices, or credit memos
- recording payments
- a printed lot trace packet and the customers who received a lot
- an order-confirmation warning from the compliance registry, or deleting an approval, registration, or license
- filing a state or other non-TTB report, or a period other than a calendar month, from the interface (the API accepts any jurisdiction and range)

Ask the person who configures your brewery when one of these is required.

This manual describes controls that exist now. If the application and this guide disagree, report the screen, action, and exact message to your MGR administrator.

### Portal fulfillment warehouse
